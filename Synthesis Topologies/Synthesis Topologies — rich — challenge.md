## One loop, two instruments — § What the Family Shares — **remake**

1. **Test or decoration?** Steps 3 and 4 test the page's sentence, both halves of it. At a whole length the table and the loop null (worst 7.8e-11 in the maker's sweep; I get 5.7e-11, −203 dB, for the saw at 44.1 kHz with my own floating counter, and exactly 0 with a counter that steps `n mod N`). At N + ½ they part, −14.5 dB at 0.05 s to −3.2 dB at 3 s (N = 100, read off the live page), which is the "only when" half the sentence implies. The per-harmonic decay is a real measurement against a prediction (worst 0.12 dB/s off, caption says within 0.15). That is a test.
   Step 5 is not. A straight-line read at d = ½ computes (1 − ½)·a + ½·b, and Karplus-Strong computes ½·(a + b). In IEEE doubles, halving is exact, so both are the same rounded sum: 0 mismatches in 10⁷ random pairs. Both functions also fill their first N + 1 samples from the same array. The null is exactly 0 by construction and could not come out otherwise at any shape or length, so "at every shape and length tried" presents algebra as if it were an experiment. Step 5 is a demonstration of an identity, and the piece's own equation line already says it in full.
2. **Faithful?** The piece's first four steps serve the page's sentence exactly. Step 5's claim, that the half-sample loop *is* Karplus-Strong, is not on the page. It is the maker's offer 1, and it is labelled as the test. Two smaller things. The caption says the string is "the Karplus-Strong string the Waveguide Synthesizer page builds". That page builds it with ρ = 0.996 in front of the average (`Projects/Waveguide Synthesizer/Waveguide Synthesizer — rich — karplus-strong walked up.html:183`), and its string would not null against this loop. The null holds for the bare average, ρ = 1. And the floating counter's residue on step 3 is honest and explained in the meter. Still, the Wavetable Oscillator face being made beside this one shows the same null as "0, exactly", because its counter is `i % P`. Both are true. A reader moving between the two faces meets two numbers for one identity, and the hint is the only bridge between them.
3. **Walks up?** Yes, and it does it as well as any I have read: table alone, loop alone, the two through their difference, then the half sample. That is Loudon's own outline for the table that travels.

**Remake, in one round:** (a) retitle step 5 and rewrite its say and the caption's last sentence so they say plainly that the string row is an identity: a linear read halfway is the average, term for term, so the difference is zero by algebra, shown rather than tested. Drop "at every shape and length tried". (b) Make the step-5 table lead with the page's own test: the whole-length null against the half-sample parting. Those two rows are already there; only the order and the heading move. (c) Say "Karplus-Strong's average (ρ = 1)" wherever the piece names the Waveguide page's string. Optional, taste: step 3 could use the exact counter so its number matches the Wavetable Oscillator face's "0, exactly", and keep the floating counter's 200 dB residue as the hint. Nothing in the engine needs to change.

## Vibrato into sidebands — § The Family (### FM) — **keep**

1. **Test or decoration?** Test. The sidebands' places and levels are set against |Jₖ(β)| three ways. The closed-form engine (0.0003 dB) is Jacobi–Anger, so it is really a check of the measurement. The phase-summing oscillator (0.0057 dB in this run; the commit body says 0.013) and the Web Audio graph rendered offline (0.0038 dB) are independent ways of making the sound, and either could have failed. Live on the page at β = 2.405 and 130 Hz the engine and the graph columns agree with |Jₖ| to 0.01 dB on every row.
2. **Faithful?** Positions, Bessel weights and the carrier's drop-out are the page's own claims, and the piece holds them. The piece goes further than the page in one place. Steps 3 and 4 say the lines "were there all along" and that the turn "is a fact about the looking, not about the sound". That is offer 2, and the caption keeps the ear's number as the page's. It is honest about it.
3. **Walks up?** Yes: one sine, a slow vibrato, the long look, the rate pushed, then the test.

**Notes, not remakes:** (a) At β = 2.405 the test table drops the k = 0 row, because `fmLines` hides any line under 1e-4 and J₀(2.405) = 9.1e-5. At the one moment the step is about, the carrier is shown by its absence rather than measured. Always keeping the k = 0 row, reading −81 dB or "silent", would make the drop-out visible in the table. (b) The check's "at β = 2.4048 the carrier line is −80.7 dB" is right as a number and wrong as a reading. At fm = 110 the k = −8 line sits at 440 − 880 = −440 Hz and folds onto +440. J₈(2.40483) = 9.22e-5 = −80.7 dB, and the carrier itself is J₀ = 0. At fm = 137 I measure the 440 Hz line at −180.9 dB, and at 130 Hz at −195.1 dB. The check should use a rate that doesn't fold onto the carrier, or the commit and any later caption should say the −80.7 is the folded k = −8 line. The piece's own caption never quotes −80.7, so the piece stands.

## Offers

**Offer 1 (What the Family Shares, claim-change): true, and it is an identity, not a finding.** A straight-line read halfway between two samples is their average, so the loop at N + ½ read linearly is Karplus-Strong's string at ρ = 1, term for term. It cannot fail, and "differ by exactly 0 over 4 s, 4 shapes × 5 lengths" is the algebra run, not evidence for it. As an addition to the page it is modest but real. It names the seam between "the loop, stored" and "the loop, that loses" in one move, and that is the section's own subject. The Waveguide face reaches the same fact from the other side: the average adds half a sample, so the pitch is fs ⁄ (N + ½), measured within 0.02 cents (`Projects/Waveguide Synthesizer/Waveguide Synthesizer — rich.json`, the karplus-strong caption). The two faces agree. The suggested sentence needs one qualifier to be true: "read by a straight line". An allpass in the same slot is also half a sample longer and loses nothing. The piece's own step-5 links say so, and so does the Wavetable Oscillator face's third mode. As written, "the loop half a sample longer is already Karplus-Strong's string" overstates it. Better: "…and a loop half a sample longer, read halfway by a straight line, is Karplus-Strong's string."

**Offer 2 (The Family § FM, claim-change): the physics is right; the page is not wrong.** Recomputed: a 5 Hz, ±30 Hz vibrato is β = 6. Lines with |Jₖ(6)| > 0.05 number 17 (k = −8…8, 99.9 % of the power). Above 1e-3 there are 23, above 1e-4 there are 27. So "17 lines" is a count at an unstated −26 dB cutoff and should say so. The look-length numbers are the Hann window's resolution. With the piece's 3 dB-dip criterion at β = 1, 50 ms resolves from 49 Hz (2.45 ⁄ T) and 120 ms from 21 Hz (2.52 ⁄ T). A rectangular window of the same length resolves from 28 Hz and 12 Hz (about 1.4 ⁄ T). 1 ⁄ T itself is 20 Hz and 8.3 Hz, the bin spacing and neither threshold. The piece uses Hann throughout (`lookAt`, `separatesFrom`, the long look). So the turn really is a fact about the look, and the 120 ms case lands near 20 Hz by choice of window, which the caption says.
The page's sentence, though, is perceptual from its first verb: "stops being heard as motion and turns into new partials" reads most naturally as one clause about hearing. It is compressed, not wrong. The offer's wording, "is heard instead as the partials it always made", is clearer and keeps the claim. One caution for whoever carries it home. Near 20 Hz the ear's usual report is roughness, not separate partials; sidebands are heard as their own pitches only once they stand about a critical band apart. That is standard psychoacoustics, not checked here, and the piece's own step-4 "wobble, then a buzz, then a new tone colour" already holds it better than either wording. Recommend it as a no-claim clarity edit, with "partials" softened to "tone colour" or "sidebands" if Loudon wants the ear's middle stage kept.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt, not just busy. The page says the family is three shapes. These two pieces take the two shapes a sound can show you in a minute: the loop, where a wavetable and a string turn out to be one ring read two ways, and the wiring, where a vibrato and a set of sidebands turn out to be one sound looked at two lengths. Both walk up exactly as Loudon asked. Both carry a real test of the page's sentence, and both find something the page compresses. What keeps the face from being finished is honesty of framing, not engineering. The loop piece's last step dresses an identity as an experiment, and the FM test hides the carrier row at the one index where it matters. Both are fixed in words. The crowd, the third shape, is still unmade, as the manifest's forward vector says.
