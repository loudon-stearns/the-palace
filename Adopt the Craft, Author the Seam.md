# Adopt the Craft, Author the Seam

![[Adopt the Craft, Author the Seam — hero.png]]

When you drop an AI generator into an established human craft, the temptation is to invent a fresh process around it. The discipline is the opposite: **adopt the craft wholesale, and author only the *seams*: the points where a reader with *taste* is replaced by a reader with *defaults*.** Exact input is the price of overriding the defaults. A century of codified practice you take off the shelf. The only genuinely new engineering lives where a human-to-human handoff becomes a human-to-machine handoff.

The realization arrived mid-[[BLUELINE]]: we kept "discovering" staging fundamentals — the Loomis head sphere, the box of the torso, shot grammar, Disney's *Staging* principle, McCloud's panel transitions — and re-naming them under our own words, until we caught ourselves reinventing film-craft. BLUELINE does not need a new pipeline; it needs the established one (anime as the skeleton, comics as the skin, the animated-feature pipeline as connective tissue, the music video as the clock) with a render-AI dropped in. The novelty is two seams, and nothing else.

The page uses *seam* in two senses, and they are not one claim. The first is the swap above: a point in a pipeline where the reader changes, and the only place new engineering lives. BLUELINE's two seams below are both of this kind. The second is the edge of a craft's norms, where the author works. It needs no machine, and it was there before one arrived. Both are authored, which is why the title holds for each. How they meet is in § The edge of the norm.

## The two seams (the worked example)

A conditioned pipeline has seams exactly where the downstream stops being a human:

1. **Board → layout (2D → 3D)** — a *handoff*, solved by design. There is no "3D version" to keep in sync with the "2D version": there is **one board record rendered at two fidelities** — comic (cheap) and [[Shop/Blender]] grey-box (exact). You tune *parameters, not pixels*; the one thing 2D can't pin down — depth — round-trips back to the record. No re-tuning, because there is no second artifact.
2. **Layout → render (staging → conditioning)** — the *frontier*. Turning exact blocking into exact OpenPose/ControlNet keypoints, which no human pipeline ever had to solve because its renderer read loosely. This is [[Blocked, Not Prompted]].

The first seam you control with discipline; the second is where the real R&D lives. **Telling them apart is the design.**

## One vocabulary, three jobs

The seams are tractable because a single staging vocabulary — facing, eyeline, left/right laterality, the shoulder–shoulder–pelvis torso frame, shot size, camera grammar — does three things at once. It is the **schema** of the board record, the **conditioning keypoints** the AI consumes (seam 2), *and* the **lossless transfer format** between 2D and 3D (seam 1). Build the vocabulary once and all three pay off together. That convergence is the signal you have found the right seam: the same abstraction serves the human read, the machine, and the handoff between them. This is what [[Simondon]] calls concretization: one feature doing several jobs at once, the way an engine block can also be the chassis and the heat sink.

## The generalization

The move is not BLUELINE's. Any AI placed inside an established practice has this shape: music production, code, illustration, editing. The swap is always where a collaborator who *interpreted* is replaced by one that falls back on its defaults, and you pay in exactness to override them. Find that point; it is your only new engineering. Everything upstream and downstream is someone else's solved problem. Use it. Music is the first craft past BLUELINE where the move has been tried, in the groove test below.

## The edge of the norm

Loudon's reading, when the groove test was first played (2026-10-19): a craft's norms are how art communicates clearly. Risers, the ii–V–I, the plagal cadence, the comic frame. The artist's voice lives in breaking those norms, at the craft's edges and where crafts join. Break too far and the communication fails; break too little and it isn't art. Authorship is the zone between the norm and the breaking point. The rules of harmony are historical studies of what people did, so you read the rules on purpose, to find where authorship can happen.

That is the second sense of seam: not a point in a pipeline but an edge in the material. It changes what *adopt* means. You take the norms off the shelf to find their edges, not to obey them. [[Comic and Cinema — Two Ways of Seeing]] already uses the word this way, for BLUELINE's join between the comic and the film.

The two senses meet in the groove test. A chart says *swing, laid back*. A drummer reads it with taste, and the lean they bring, how uneven the eighths and how far behind the beat, is their place between the norm and the breaking point. A machine plays the same chart straight, because nothing in it reads swing. So the swap forces the edge onto paper. Two numbers, the swing ratio and how far the backbeat lays back, are the authored seam in both senses at once. Push them past the usual range and the groove limps, then comes loose: the breaking point becomes something you can play. What the drummer carried as taste, the author now has to write down. [[Quantization ↔ Precision vs Flow]] leans the same way at the scale of one beat: a deviation from the grid can carry intent.

They stay two claims. The first says where a machine makes new engineering; the second says where the voice was all along. A craft with no machine in it still has the second. And the first can be met without the second: write down a textbook swing and the machine plays it correctly, with no one's voice in it.

## The tension it lives in

This `contradicts` the autodidact's [[FOUR PILLARS]] instinct — *make first, learn through friction*, build from scratch. The resolution is not compromise but precision: **build the seam from scratch (that friction teaches); adopt the craft (that friction was already paid, decades ago).** Reinventing the adopted half is not learning — it is tax. The contrary is generative: it tells you *where* to spend your make-first energy.

## Forward Vectors

I want to be tried in a third craft, after BLUELINE and music: name both seams there, ask where the reader changes, and ask where the edge of the norm sits. I carry an open question: is there a *third* swap, neither handoff nor conditioning, where the AI's output must feed *back* into the craft's human stages (a render that informs a re-board)? The edge of the norm does not answer it. It is a second sense of seam, not a third swap.
