# Making a Palace Citizen — Context

## Born as two files

The page was deposited on 2026-07-01 together with a second file in its bundle, `Making a Palace Citizen — context.md` (lowercase). The deposit message calls it a card and lab-companion split under SCHEMA §6: the card is the stable method, the companion the running experiment log, seeded with the Spinoza and Meadows validation, an experiment queue and a rollout tracker (`917bdaae`). The lab's own first forward vector said what the split was for: gather evidence until the card's open questions resolve, one by one, into the card (`917bdaae`). So every run's findings went to the lab, and the card changed only when a finding settled into method.

The name was right when it was given: until v1.28 the schema's `context` row meant a long-running session-history companion, and the lab was one. The v1.28 Schema Ceremony gave `context` its present meaning, why a page is shaped as it is, and sent what each run taught to a ledger (`9f9d1cf1`). On 2026-11-02, on Loudon's word, the lab log was renamed [[Making a Palace Citizen — tuning]] and became the page's tuning ledger; its lowercase name had also collided, on a case-insensitive volume, with this file's (`9e63fc52`). This file is a new companion, not the old one renamed. It holds no run findings; those stay in the tuning ledger.

## What came into the card

Three things came into the method on 2026-07-02, each logged in the lab as it landed.

**Voice fidelity.** The fourth layer and two procedure steps came in together (`22a9a5d7`). The procedure grew from seven steps to eight. "Write the dossier first" lost its "first", though the step had always allowed the body first. The test-enchant check gained two tests: catchable on its blindspot, and sounds like them. The same commit renamed "The three layers" and records the alternative that was weighed and refused: writing the body in first person.

**Audio and video.** This was Loudon's note, put in the three places he named: an open question and a line in the voice layer here, the forward vectors of the Eno and Cage speech files, and an item in the lab's queue (`e639d3d7`).

**When a citizen may edit itself.** After the Eno and Cage Dialectic, Loudon held every proposed write-back on a principle: a citizen earns self-edits across several remembered encounters, not one (`d054a26d`). The card's growth step still said each encounter flows straight back into the page. That is how Spinoza and Meadows had both had their vectors rewritten after a single run. The step was not rewritten then. Instead two comments were left beside it: a `CLAUDE → LOUDON` flag to revise the step, and a note that the archived transcripts were the citizens' memory in embryo (`d054a26d`). When the memory mechanism was built, the second was rewritten to describe it: perspectival, kept in the bundle, append-only (`f53791ba`, `41979c9b`). The memory file's name changed from "context companion" to "diary" in the v1.28 Schema Ceremony (`9f9d1cf1`). The growth step itself was rewritten only in the 2026-10-24 Weave, to propose per encounter and apply once the same drift repeats, and both comments came out then (`a75350ec`). What the memory comment said now lives in the `diary` row of [[SCHEMA — Reference]] §8.
