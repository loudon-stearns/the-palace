# Light Enrichment

One small thing set beside one sentence of a page, in its rich face: a picture, a diagram, a short loop, a drawing made from the palace's own records, a short sound. It makes that sentence clearer or more felt, and it asks nothing of the reader. Nothing to operate, nothing to learn first.

Loudon, 2026-11-10: "I'd like to propose also lighter enrichment, where we just add an image, diagram or sound here and there that help to explain a concept … to make rich faces feel more alive without creating more burden to work with as a reader/visitor."

It is the small sibling of [[Enrichment]], not a replacement. A heavy piece walks a reader up to a test; a light piece only has to make one sentence land. Everything Enrichment says about the rich face holds here: the text is never written, every section lands as a proposal, and approval is Loudon's alone, section by section, often when [[The Daily Page]] brings him to it ([[Review Layer]]).

One tension is held, not settled. Enrichment went to depth because "five small cards spread across many entries never transformed one" ([[Enrichment — tuning]] item 1). A light round is breadth again. What it has that the old queue lacked is a challenger who recomputes.

## Read the page as a first-time reader

Loudon, 2026-11-11: "light enrichment should be aware of what is already on a page and attempt to connect between what is already there... reading as if it were a first-time reader, considering how one would build understanding and filling in gaps and helping bridge concepts."

So the work starts with the whole page, not with a sentence found by search. Read it as someone meeting it for the first time, and take stock of what it already shows: the figures and diagrams in its text, the pieces already in its rich face, its hero. Then ask how understanding builds as the page goes, and where it does not: a gap a reader has to cross alone, two ideas the page holds and does not join. A light piece belongs there, filling the gap or bridging the two, and it connects to what the page already has.

The first round did not work this way, and it shows. One page got a second diagram of a sentence that was already the caption of a diagram.

## Choosing what to make

**The sentence chooses the form.** A sound if the idea can be heard. A diagram if it is a structure. A count if the palace's own records hold it. A loop if it is a change over time.

**Let a neighbour leak in, when it helps.** Loudon, 2026-11-10: "allow nearby pages and concepts to 'leak' into the rich faces, like the pages are trying to speak to each other." Open the neighbour, find the sentence being borrowed, and name it.

**Avatars and heroes are a material, not a target.** Loudon, 2026-11-11: "don't eliminate it, but don't look for it, just reach for it when needed." They fit when the idea is about pages themselves.

**A picture still has to earn its place.** Take it away: would the reader lose something the words cannot give back ([[Image Embedding Standard]])? A piece that is only a chart is to be suspected ([[Enrichment — scroll]], Standing Orders).

## The round

1. **An elder opens a workshop**, `Enrichment/light-round-YYYY-MM/`. Everything is made there.
2. **Choose pages.** Pages with no face first. Leave any page a heavier effort has claimed, the pages every agent is born with unless Loudon names them, and anything whose record is private.
3. **Read the page whole, as a first-time reader, and take stock of what is already on it.** Then pick the place: a gap in how understanding builds, or two things the page already holds that a piece could join. The sentence there is the anchor. Write the claim the piece will make before making it, and what on the page it connects to. If the claim is not on the page, it is the maker's, and the caption says so.
4. **Make it with a script that stays** beside its output and its data. A count names its source and its date. Randomness is seeded per piece, or thrown once and kept.
5. **Write three lines.** A caption that states the claim, not a description of the picture. What is real and what is a choice. Which neighbour leaked in, if one did.
6. **Look at it at the size it will be shown.** Check a sound by measurement, and say nobody has heard it until somebody has.
7. **A challenger reads it before it is committed.** Batches of about ten, grouped by the kind of checking they need, each read by an agent that neither made nor placed them. It reads the page whole, as a first-time reader would, and recomputes every number. It asks whether the piece helps that reader across a gap, and whether it connects to what the page already shows or only repeats it. It returns keep, remake or retire with a reason, and any error it found on the page itself.
8. **Remake once.** The maker fixes what was found.
9. **An elder places it as a proposal**: the file in the page's bundle under its `— rich —` name, in the band, sounds as m4a, the manifest carrying the verdict and what changed (`_ops/rich-face/README.md` § The manifest). Stamp, run `conform.mjs`, commit by path.
10. **Carry the page findings to Loudon.** A stale number or a missing link on a page is an offer for that page, and is often worth more than the picture.
11. **Loudon reviews in the palace**: approve a section, or leave a note for the next round.

## What goes wrong, and the guard for each

From the first challenge, which found fault with three pieces in five.

- **It repeats what the page already shows, or sits beside nothing it connects to.** Take stock of the page first: its figures, its face, its hero.
- **The caption says more than the page.** Write the claim first and find it on the page.
- **The honest line owns too little.** List every choice a reader could take for a finding: the selection, the scale, the threshold, the mapping.
- **A count is off by one or two.** Read frontmatter as YAML, not by pattern, and count again a second way.
- **The measure is diluted.** Measure what the page measures, with the page's own tool where it has one.
- **The picture invents something.** Draw only what a page describes; label anything else as the maker's.
- **The leak is not on the neighbour.** Open the neighbour and find the sentence.
- **It cannot be read.** Place it in the band, and keep words inside the picture few and large.
- **It names the person in the room.** A face a reader meets does not call that person "he"; the reader will not always be Loudon.

Two things to keep: every sound carries a picture drawn from what the sound did, and the challenger is asked to recompute, never to judge whether a thing is plausible.

## The first round

November 2026: seventy pieces on sixty-eight pages, made before most of this page was written. What it did, what its challengers found, and what it cost are in [[Light Enrichment — Context]]; the workshop is `Enrichment/light-round-2026-11/`.

## Where this is headed

Loudon, 2026-11-11: "We should encourage the light enrichment to use more of the shop, and of our projects, this will keep the shop and projects active and help push it forward and will expand the rage of expression." (He means range.) And of that: "this can be a part of the page's forward vector/plan."

So it is a direction, not a step. A round may reach into [[The Shop]] for a Specialist whose craft a piece is, or to a project for an instrument the palace has already built, and each time it does, that Specialist or project gets work. The first round did neither. It is slower than a script of one's own, and a light piece is meant to be quick; that cost is real and is weighed piece by piece.

## Open questions

- Does a remake get a second verdict? Enrichment says yes; the first round's remakes have had none.
- Who listens to the sounds, and when? None from the first round has been heard.
- May a light piece sit on a page that already has a heavy face?
- How often, and how many pieces to a round?
- Does this page want a trigger of its own, so that saying its name begins a round?
