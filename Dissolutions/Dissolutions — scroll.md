### 2026-10-18 — One curve, three readers: the projection picture

The second rough, rebuilt from what the first one taught. One curve sits in the middle. Its reader runs along the bottom, and what comes out is drawn to the right, so the dissolution is a picture of who is reading, not a claim. Slow the reader down and the curve is an envelope; speed it up and it is a waveform. Around 20 Hz the output crossfades from a change in loudness to a sound, which puts the seam in perception on screen where you can play it. For the oscillator, a button lets the reader run back and forth instead of jumping back to the start, so the wrap's discontinuity no longer swamps the spectrum. Harmonic bars stand beside the straight line, so the shape of the curve and its spectrum can be seen together.

### 2026-10-18 — One curve, three doorways: envelope, oscillator, effect

The morning's first rough: one curve, used three ways. As an **envelope**, the knob read as duration rather than loudness. As an **oscillator**, changing the curve was barely audible, because the jump where the ramp wraps dominates the spectrum and hides the curve's shape. As an **effect**, it gave a lovely timbre change, but one that needs a picture to be understood. Taken whole, the sameness came across as an argument, not yet as a picture; the proof needed to show it, not just say it. Those notes shaped the second rough above.
