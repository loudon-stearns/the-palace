One field, two hands

One stored field, a landscape of heights over a square. A synthesizer reads it with two phasors, and the point it reads traces a figure across the square. An effect reads the same field at two places the input itself chooses: where the signal is now, and where it was a moment ago. Line the two hands up and they null. Then let the effect’s lag do what no phasor pair can.

What the proof measures, live

The test a Hammerstein structure must pass: the difference tone stays at 30 Hz while both tones slide up. A static curve followed by a filter can only give this line one height, because its filter only ever hears 30 Hz. (The gap is 30 Hz so that no other mixing product lands on it anywhere in the range.)
The same reading written as a Volterra kernel, second order. Its axes are lags, not heights: every weight sits at 0 or τ. The field and the kernel are an exact translation of each other, between two different spaces.
The question this asks the page

Press Line the hands up and play their difference: silence, one field read by two hands along one path. Then give the effect two tones and slide the lag. The phasors have nothing like it: a lag is memory, and it makes how loudly the tones mix depend on which tones they are. Is the synthesizer and the effect one instrument? And is the Volterra kernel that same instrument, or a faithful map of it drawn in another space?

Two lines, because the page makes two claims and they test differently. The synthesizer and the effect are one stored field and one lookup, with only the hand changed: when the hands trace the same path the outputs are the same numbers. The field and the kernel are not one object. The field’s axes are heights of the signal; the kernel’s are lags. They map onto each other exactly, which is an isomorphism, and whether that keeps the member in the catalogue is a question for Loudon. The test also corrects a sentence on the member page: the diagonal keeps intermodulation. What it loses is intermodulation that depends on the tones that made it.
