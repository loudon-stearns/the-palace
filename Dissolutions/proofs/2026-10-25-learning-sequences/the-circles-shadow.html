The circle's shadow, step by step

Seven short steps up to the proof. A shadow alone, a stack of cosines alone, a bent curve alone, the curve that gives back one harmonic, then the curve and the stack set against each other. Then the reach, alone and on the drawbars. Each step has one thing to hear. Nothing plays until you press Play.

Step 1 of 8 · the shadow alone

A point going round, and its shadow

Send a point round a circle at a steady speed and look at its shadow on a straight line. The shadow slides left and right, and its place on the line is cos θ, where θ is how far round the point has gone. Played as sound, that sliding is the plainest tone there is: one frequency, nothing else. The shadow lingers at the two ends, where the point is heading straight up or down and its shadow barely moves.

TryPlay it and slide the speed: one pure tone, higher as the point turns faster. Then look at the sixteen grey dots. They are evenly spaced round the circle, but their shadows bunch at the ends of the line. Every later step uses this shadow as its reader.
Step 2 of 8 · the stack of cosines alone

Many points, each going round faster

An organ builds a tone another way. Each drawbar is its own point going round: the first at the note's speed, the second twice as fast, the third three times, and so on up. Each one's shadow is a cosine, and the drawbar sets how big its circle is. Add the shadows up and you have the tone. Nothing is bent here: it is only cosines, added.

TryPress First alone and play: step 1's tone. Now raise the drawbars one at a time, left to right. Each one adds a tone a whole number of times the first, and the sum grows brighter and buzzier. Press Saw for all ten.
Step 3 of 8 · a bent curve alone

A curve that bends the shadow

Back to one point and one shadow. This time the shadow doesn't go straight to the speaker. It slides along under a curve, and the curve's height above it is what you hear. That is a waveshaper. A straight line hands the shadow through unchanged; bend the line, and every cycle is reshaped the same way, so the tone changes colour but keeps its pitch.

Step 4 of 8 · the curve that gives back one harmonic

Bend it just so, and the shadow goes n times as fast

Here is the fact the proof stands on. For every whole number n there is one curve, Tₙ, the nth Chebyshev polynomial, that turns the slow shadow into the shadow of a point going n times as fast. No oscillator runs at the higher speed. The curve has n − 1 bends, and as the shadow sweeps across them the height rises and falls n times a turn. Each curve is built from the two before it, by the rule written below, with no cosine anywhere in it.

(curve n, read at the shadow's place) = cos((n) × (how far round)) · (the next curve) = 2 × (the shadow's place) × (this curve) − (the curve before it)
TryPlay it and step n from 1 to 10. You hear the harmonic series, 110, 220, 330 Hz and up, every note made by the same 110 Hz shadow read through a different curve. Watch the gold trace lie exactly on the blue at every n.
Step 5 of 8 · the curve and the stack, through their difference

Write the drawbars as one curve

Now put steps 2 and 4 together. Weight each curve Tₙ by its drawbar and add them into one curve. Read that one curve with the slow shadow; build the stack of cosines from the same drawbars; the two never share a line of code. Then flip one upside down and add them. Studio engineers call this a null test: if two paths cancel to silence, they are giving you the same numbers.

TryPlay the difference: silence. Drag the drawbars about while it plays, and it stays silent. Then switch to plain powers and play the difference again. That loud leftover is what the null catches when the curves are only nearly right.
Step 6 of 8 · the reach alone

Don't swing the whole way

So far the shadow has always swept the whole line, from −1 to 1. Turn the input down and it only sweeps the middle, from −L to L, as if the point went round a smaller circle. On a waveshaper this is the drive. The identity needs the full sweep, so below it the curve stops giving back one clean harmonic. It follows a rule instead: harmonic n keeps only Lⁿ of its level, and the rest falls to harmonics below it of the same kind, odd to odd, even to even.

Tₙ(L × cos θ) = Lⁿ × cos(n × θ) + (lower harmonics of the same parity)
(curve n, read with reach L) = (the reach to the nth power) × (harmonic n) + (only harmonics below n, odd with odd, even with even)

For an even n, a curve read with less than full reach also makes a steady offset, harmonic 0. It is not a sound, so it is taken out of what you hear; the bars still show it.

TryLeave n on 3 and play at full reach: a pure 330 Hz. Pull the reach down slowly. Nobody retunes anything, yet the weight sinks to 110 Hz, because the middle of T₃ is nearly a straight slope. At reach 0.6, harmonic 1 is 1.152 and harmonic 3 is 0.216, which is 0.6 × 0.6 × 0.6.
Step 7 of 8 · the reach, put into the combination

Drive becomes a colour the stack doesn't have

Set the drawbars again and give the one curve its reach. At full reach the curve is the stack, as in step 5. Below it, every drawbar leaks downward at once, so the curve's colour depends on how hard you drive it. The stack has no input to turn down: it can only get louder or quieter. Swell the reach and you hear what a waveshaper is for.

Step 8 of 8 · the proof

Now the whole thing, on one page

The proof puts every step on one screen, in one picture. Here is where each one is waiting for you there.

  • The reader pane, bottom left, is step 1: the point going round, squashed to fit, its shadow on the line, and the sixteen ticks bunching at the ends.
  • The stack of cosines tab, with the drawbars under the picture, is step 2.
  • The curve pane, top left, and The bent curve tab are step 3: the dotted line climbs from the shadow to the curve, then across to what comes out.
  • The first equation under the picture, Tₙ(cos θ) = cos(n·θ), is step 4. The Third alone preset puts T₃ by itself on the curve.
  • Their difference tab and the box What the proof measures, live are step 5: silence, with the difference given in dB below the tone, and the gold bars filling their outlines.
  • The reader's reach slider is steps 6 and 7: the blue stretch on the curve shrinks, and the box names the harmonic that misses most.
  • One thing the proof does not have is step 7's swell. Move the reach slider by hand while it plays to hear the same thing.

It ends on a question. Put Third alone on the drawbars and pull the reach down: only the curve changes. Is the reach a seam in the identity, or a knob the curve has that the stack never will?
