Step 0 · the two things, before anything joins them

Analysis and synthesis, each on its own

This sequence takes two jobs done to a voice, taking one apart and putting one together, and tests whether they are one equation solved two ways.

The first thing

Analysis: linear prediction

Take a recorded voice, a list of numbers called samples, 16,000 a second here, and guess each sample from the few just before it, as a weighted sum: so much of the last one, so much of the one before. Choose the weights that make the guesses as close as they can be. What the guess still misses is called the residual. The weights end up holding the slow resonances that make the vowel; the residual holds what they could not predict, sample by sample.

Where you meet itLinear predictive coding, LPC, inside speech codecs since the 1970s: send the weights and a little of the residual instead of the whole wave.

Steps 2 and 3.

The second thing

Synthesis: a source and a filter

Make a voice from scratch out of two parts. A source: a buzz, like the vocal folds snapping shut over and over, or a hiss for a whisper. A filter: something that rings at a few frequencies and lets the rest fade, as the mouth does. Those ringing frequencies are the formants, and they make a vowel a vowel. The source sets the pitch; the filter sets the vowel.

Where you meet itVocoders, talk boxes, and the speaking toys of the late 1970s, the Speak & Spell among them.

Step 1.

The claim that joins them

Both jobs are one line: a sample equals the weighted guess from the samples before it, plus the residual. Analysis solves the line for the residual. Synthesis solves the same line, with the same weights, for the sample: the residual is the source, the weights are the filter. Feed the residual back in and the voice returns, every sample of it (step 4).

Three kinds of sameness. Identity: one object, read two ways. Isomorphism: an exact map between two things that stay two. Analogy: a likeness that breaks when you push it.

Identity, of the equation. What is one is the line and its numbers, not two machines. Solved for the residual, the line is a filter that takes the vowel out; solved for the sample, it is a filter that puts it back. Two filters that exactly undo each other, made from one line.

Where it holds

The round trip closes to rounding error at every order, every number of weights, from 1 to 32 (step 5), when the same weights and the residual go back exactly as they came out. The order never decides whether it closes, only how the voice is shared between the weights and the residual.

Where it stops

Exactness needs the weights and the residual handed back untouched: a codec that rounds them to save bits gives back a voice close to the original, not the same one. And the weights hold over a short window only, 25 ms here, because a voice keeps changing; the method assumes the mouth holds still that long, and takes a fresh set every 10 ms. What the round trip never promised: swap the residual for a plain buzz and the line still runs, on a different source. The vowels survive and the life goes: the robot voice of LPC-10, the 1970s secure-voice standard (the proof).

Step 1 of 6 · the source and the filter

A buzz, shaped by a mouth

A voice is two things. A source, which is a buzz: the vocal folds snapping shut over and over, here a plain click 125 times a second. And a filter, the mouth, which rings at a few frequencies and lets the others fade. The buzz sets the pitch. The mouth sets the vowel.

The proof’s voice starts from a smoother pulse, closer to air through real vocal folds, with a little breath. The click is the plainest buzz there is, and it is the kind the proof’s A buzz button uses, there following the voice’s pitch.

TryPlay the buzz alone: a harsh, nasal tick-tone with no vowel in it. Switch to the mouth and flip between ah, ee and oo. The pitch never moves; only the curve does. Then look at the mouth’s equation: its output now is the buzz plus a weighted sum of its own last outputs. Hold on to that shape.
Step 2 of 6 · prediction, by hand

Guess the next sample from the last one

Now take a finished voice, the proof’s, singing ah, ee, oo, and try to guess each sample before it arrives. The simplest guess: the last sample, times one weight. What the guess gets wrong is the miss. Set the weight by hand and listen to how loud the miss is.

Hear
Left · the guessThe last two dozen samples of the voice. Gold dot: the guess, the last sample times the weight. Blue dot: what the sample really is. The white line between them is the miss.
Right · how loud the miss isAgainst the weight, in dB below the voice. The gold dot is your weight. The dashed line is the best one.
e[n] = x[n] − a × x[n − 1] loudness of e ÷ loudness of x = 1 − 2a × ρ + a²
(the miss now) = (the voice now) − (the weight) × (the voice one sample ago) · (how loud the miss is) ÷ (how loud the voice is) = 1 − 2 × (the weight) × (how alike neighbouring samples are) + (the weight)²
TryStart at zero: the guess is nothing, so the miss is the whole voice. Slide right and find where the miss is quietest. It gets thin and bright there, because one sample ago is nearly the same as now for the slow, low parts of the voice, so the guess cancels the lows and leaves the highs.
Step 3 of 6 · the residual

Sixteen weights, and what they cannot guess

One weight can only cancel the lows. Give the guess sixteen weights, reading the last sixteen samples, and let the machine choose them, a fresh set every 10 ms as the vowel changes. Now the guess catches the vowel itself. What it still misses is called the residual, and it sounds like the buzz you started with.

Hear
Left · the guessThe voice’s recent samples, with the sixteen weights as gold stems under the sixteen newest. Gold dot: their weighted sum, the guess. Blue dot: the truth.
Right · where the voice livesBlue: the voice’s spectrum at this moment, with the vowel’s peaks. Grey: the residual’s, on the same scale. The peaks are gone; what is left is nearly flat.
e[n] = x[n] − Σₖ aₖ × x[n − k] k = 1 … 16
(the residual now) = (the voice now) − Σ (weight k) × (the voice k samples ago), for k from 1 to 16

The weights are chosen by a recipe called Levinson-Durbin, which finds the sixteen that make the miss smallest over each 25 ms stretch. This sequence does not teach the recipe. It does what your hand did in step 2, sixteen weights at a time.

TryPlay the voice, then the residual, turned up so you can hear it. The vowels are almost gone. What stays is the pitch, gliding, and the breath: a bright buzz, close to step 1’s click. The weights took the mouth; the residual kept the source.
Step 4 of 6 · the same line, solved for the voice

Residual in, voice out

The line that gave you the residual has three parts: the voice, the guess, the miss. Move the guess to the other side and the same line, with the same weights, makes a voice from a residual. Each new sample is the residual plus the guess from its own last sixteen outputs. That is the mouth’s shape from step 1. Feed it the residual and the voice comes back, every sample.

Each weight shrunk a little, the far ones more. The vowels stay, but the numbers are no longer the same numbers.
solved for e: e[n] = x[n] − Σₖ aₖ × x[n − k] solved for x: x[n] = e[n] + Σₖ aₖ × x[n − k]
(the residual) = (the voice) − (the guess from the voice’s past) · (the voice) = (the residual) + (the guess from the voice’s own past, as it is being made)
TryPlay the difference: silence, all the way through ah, ee, oo. Then nudge the weights and play it again. A small change in the numbers is enough to make the null speak. It is the same line with the same numbers that makes the round trip close.
Step 5 of 6 · the order

Where the voice is split

The number of weights is called the order. It never decides whether the round trip closes: it closes at every order. It decides how the voice is shared between the two halves. The weights hold the shape of the mouth, and the residual holds whatever they could not reach. Drive the weights with a plain buzz and you hear what they hold; play the residual and you hear what they left.

Hear
Left · the weights’ filterGold: the curve the weights make at this moment. Faint blue: the voice’s own spectrum. More weights, more of its peaks the curve can follow.
Right · what the residual keepsGrey: the residual’s spectrum, on the voice’s scale. Faint blue: the voice. Whatever the curve missed is still here.
x[n] = Σₖ₌₁ᵖ aₖ × x[n − k] + e[n]
(the voice now) = Σ (weight k) × (the voice k samples ago), for k from 1 to (the order) + (the residual now)

The buzz here is the proof’s: one click per period at the voice’s own pitch, turned up or down every 10 ms to the residual’s loudness.

TryStart at order 2 on what the weights hold: a buzz with a dull or bright tilt and barely a vowel. Raise it to 8, then 16, and the ah, ee, oo arrive. Switch to the residual and go back down: at low orders the residual still sounds like a voice, at high orders it thins to buzz and breath.
Step 6 of 6 · the proof

Now the whole thing, on one page

The proof puts every step on one screen, around the same made voice. Here is where each one is waiting for you there.

  • The voice tab is the voice of steps 2 to 5, and the A buzz button is step 5’s what the weights hold: step 1’s kind of click, now at the voice’s own gliding pitch and loudness, in place of the residual.
  • The reader, bottom left of the picture, is steps 2 and 3: the recent samples, the gold weights under them, the gold guess, the blue truth, the white miss.
  • Solved for the miss is step 3: the residual. The bottom-right panel, where the voice lives, is step 3’s spectra.
  • Solved for the voice and Voice minus that are step 4. The first line of the measurement box is the round trip, closing to rounding error.
  • The order slider is step 5, and the measurement box’s second line is how many dB the weights catch. The top-left panel, the numbers, draws the weights as the filter they make, as step 5 did.
  • One thing the proof adds: A whisper. Noise in place of the residual. The vowels come back whispered, with no pitch at all, because the pitch lived in the residual and noise has none to give.

It ends on a question. With its own residual the voice comes back whole; with a buzz the vowels survive and the life does not. Are analysis and synthesis one thing, or one equation with two kinds of thing in it, a model and what the model cannot reach?
