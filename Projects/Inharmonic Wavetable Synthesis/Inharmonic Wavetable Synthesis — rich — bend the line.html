Bend the flat line

Three Kinds of Warp ends on a fact: whatever you do to a one-cycle wavetable, its sound stays on the harmonic lines. This walk starts there. It rebuilds the same tone as a bank of sines, one per line, so each line can be moved on its own. Wavetable B says how far each one moves, in cents. The readout under the spectrum is the same one that walk used: the share of the energy that lands between the harmonic lines.

Measured on an exact lap: 256 samples a cycle of the fundamental, 32 cycles long, so a harmonic is an exact bin and anything between the bins is real. The sound plays at the pitch named under the spectrum. The measured curves in step 5 are medians over each recording's live frames, from the project's trajectory library; the stiff-string family uses each string's own fitted B.
fn = n · f0 · 2(D · Δn ⁄ 1200)
(partial n's frequency) = (n times the fundamental), raised by (the depth knob times that partial's value on Wavetable B, in cents). Every Δn = 0, or depth 0, and every partial sits on a harmonic line. The page calls the depth knob B; this piece calls it D, to keep B for the string's stiffness below.
Δn = 1200 · log2 √(1 + B n²)
(the stiff string's bend, in cents, at partial n) grows with (the stiffness B) and with (the square of the partial number): flat near partial 1, steepening to the right.
