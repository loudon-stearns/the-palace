Play the edge

One tone whose partials hang off a single phase, stretched away from whole-number ratios and pulled back by an outside ensemble tuned to the plain harmonic series. Somewhere along the stretch the tone stops holding and tears. This study keeps you there: it knows where the edge is for every note, partial count and coupling, and can put the stretch just under it. The engine is the 2026-10-17 frustration sweeps' toy model, run live, with the same random detuning the renders used. Trust the shapes and the directions, not the exact values.

Keys a s d f g h j k play E1 to A4; space strikes. A MIDI keyboard plays any note, its mod wheel rides coupling and its pitch bend rides stretch.

Gestures as modulators

Do the field guide's gestures play live, and does a strike's pitch drop follow how far past the edge it is thrown?

Breathe and flutter swing the stretch up from where you set it, in decades. A strike throws it to the set height above the edge and lets it decay. The sequence's eight steps are set relative to the edge (the middle of each slider is the edge itself), so the same pattern plays the threshold as a rhythm on any note. Ride swings coupling between its two values with the stretch held still.

Real materials at the edge

Where does a measured material sit against the edge, and how hard would a harmonic ensemble have to pull to hold it?

The measured piano sets its stretch from the note you play, using the string stiffness the trajectory library measured across the keyboard; the slider then scales it (1× is the real string). The modal objects bend each partial by the library's rank-filed cents, the k-th mode in slot k, scaled the same way. They have few modes, so they snap rather than fray.

Let the voices re-pair

If each partial may be gripped by whichever voice is nearest, does the bar lock to the ensemble again?

Fixed pairing is the sweeps' model: voice n pulls partial n wherever it has gone. Nearest pairing lets a partial be gripped by whichever voice it lands near. With the ensemble holding its note, play other notes over it, and the consonant intervals find voices to lock to. Nearest pairing switches on by itself while the ensemble holds.

Two tones pulling on each other

When two stretched tones pull on each other instead of on a fixed ensemble, what interval do they agree on, and where does it tear?

The second tone has the same stretch, partial count and source as the first. Each partial is paired with the nearest partial of the other tone, both ways, so the pairs change as the tones stretch.
