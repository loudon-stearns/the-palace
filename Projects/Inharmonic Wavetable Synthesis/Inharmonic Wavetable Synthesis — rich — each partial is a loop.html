Each partial is a loop

From the side a spectrum is a row of bars. Turn it, and each bar is a loop: one partial, going round. A loop that closes after one lap of the fundamental stands still. Push a loop off its place and it stops closing. It crawls, and you hear it leave the chord. Take hold of it. The trace in the corner is their sum over that one lap: the single cycle a wavetable stores. It holds still only while every loop closes; after that no one cycle is the sound.

Sideways moves a partial; the stem under each loop still stands on the harmonic it was filed under, so a leaning stem is a partial out of place. Up and down is how loud. Shift-drag swaps your grip. The pad under “when you let go” is the return itself: left to right is how long it takes (0.05 s to 20 s, and the last strip is “stays”), bottom to top is how fast it wobbles on the way (none, then 0.3 to 12 Hz); the trace in it is the path a loop will take. Let go while your hand is still moving and the body is thrown: it carries on, then comes back. The keys play notes; with “held” they latch, so build a chord and then pull on it. The corner trace is drawn from the same places, levels and phases as the loops, for the first sounding voice. The crawl is what a strobe tuner shows: the loop lit once per lap of the fundamental, slowed so the eye can follow.

Further down this face. What you just did by hand, the sections below take apart. The Architecture measures the flat line: the same bank of sines, with the energy between the harmonics counted. What This Sounds Like sets this putty's return beside a measured piano string, whose bend holds still after the strike while its loudness fades. The Cube Architecture lays several of these bodies side by side as unison voices. At the Coupling Edge lets voices pull on the loops until the tone tears.

fn = n · f0 · 2(cn ⁄ 1200)
(partial n's frequency) = (n times the fundamental), raised by (that partial's bend, cn cents). This is the project's engine, and cn is Wavetable B. Here your hand writes it: where the loop sits along the object is fn ⁄ f0, and the engine is sent the cents that put it there.
u(t) = e−t ⁄ τ · swing at w wobbles a second
(how far a loop still is from home, t seconds after you let go) = (a fade with time constant τ, the pad's left-to-right) times (a swing at w Hz, the pad's bottom-to-top; none at the bottom, where it only fades). Each loop does this on its own; the tilt slider gives the top loop a τ up to four times shorter or longer than the bottom one.
turn per lap = fn ⁄ f0 − nearest whole number
(how far a loop misses closing, each time the fundamental goes round once) = (its place in harmonics) minus (the nearest whole number). Zero, and the loop closes and stands still: it is a harmonic, of whichever number it landed on. Anything else, and it crawls at that rate.
