Two ways to settle

The page’s settling piano bows Wavetable B hard at the strike and flattens it over half a second, so the bend itself relaxes. Here that recipe sits beside a real piano string, measured partial by partial from a recording. The line on the left glows where its partials are loud. The curve on the right is the bend you hear: each partial’s distance from the flat line, averaged by how much of the sound it carries.

The string is a Grand Piano A2, 110 Hz (fitted f₀ 110.25 Hz), from Ableton’s Core Library, tracked into 16 frames from 0.03 s to 8 s by the project’s steward on 2026-10-14; only its numbers are here. Between frames the line is interpolated. The page names its recipe at “B ≈ 0.002 at partial 20”; the piece reads that as stiffness B = 0.002 and lets you move it.
heard bend(t) = Σ an(t)² · |Δn(t)| ⁄ Σ an(t)²
(the bend you hear at time t) is (each partial’s distance from the flat line, in cents), weighted by (how much power that partial carries right then). It falls if the line flattens, and it also falls if the bent partials go quiet.
string: Δn(t) ≈ 1200 · log2 √(1 + B n²), B steady · recipe: Δn(t) = 1200 · log2 √(1 + B n²) · max(0, 1 − t ⁄ 0.5 s)
(the real string’s bend) stays on one stiffness curve the whole note, give or take a few cents at the lowest partials in the first 60 ms; (the page’s recipe) shrinks that curve to the flat line over half a second.
