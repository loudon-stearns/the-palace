## Plan

<!-- scroll:plan:start -->
**Where this is going.** A pipeline that turns a plain description of a sound into a playable instrument for any modular or DSP environment. English goes in, a target-neutral patch language (PDL) sits in the middle, and each target has one verified vocabulary on the way out. VCV Rack is the first target, and the whole chain has worked there since May 2026. Loudon's ear met it on 2026-10-24, when he called the house bass alive. From here every patch should be as quick to hear as that one, then play more than one note at a time, then prove the pipeline is target-neutral on Loudon's own Mac: first as an app, then as a plugin Live loads.

**The moves ahead**

1. **Make every patch quick to hear.** Every emitted patch opens on the computer keyboard and the MacBook speakers, with each modulator on the row under the module it moves (the Standing Orders). Built 2026-10-26: thirteen patches in `proofs/2026-10-26-ready-to-play/`, checked by `verify_standing_orders.js`. It passes when Loudon opens one in Rack, presses a key, and hears it without touching a menu.
2. **Hear the archetypes in Rack.** Loudon plays a few of the ready patches and says whether the archetypes sound usable and the layout fits his hands. What he hears steers the archetype library.
3. **Play chords.** The voice count is said in PDL (`# voices: N`) or in the archetype, and the MIDI module carries it. It passes when the warm pad plays a chord from the QWERTY keyboard. Built 2026-10-26: six voices; not yet heard.
4. **Prove it on a second target: Loudon's Mac** (Loudon, 2026-11-04: "move to VST or full MAC app"). The same PDL that makes a VCV patch makes C++ from one module kit, whose curves are read from VCV Fundamental's source. First as a Mac app on the QWERTY keyboard and the MacBook speakers. Built 2026-11-04: `native/`, four apps in `proofs/2026-11-04-native/`, 83/83 with every PDL going through both targets. That part passes when Loudon plays one and it feels like its Rack twin. Then the same patch code goes inside a VST3 plugin, and that passes when a generated instrument plays on a MIDI track in Live.
5. **Make knob settings target-neutral.** Regions, archetypes and seeds are worked out inside the VCV emitter today, and the native target borrows the numbers. Move that into a step of its own, so a third target costs only a vocabulary and an emitter.
6. **Max, after the patching standards** (Loudon, 2026-10-29: Max, not Pure Data). Until the standards exist, this makes no `.maxpat`, gen~ or RNBO work. It builds the Max vocabulary from patches Max itself saved and writes down what the emitter will need. No Pure Data.

Side moves, whenever there is a spare hour: a search box in the renderer that lights up modules by feel words (it passes when 'bright' lights the oscillator and filter, 'breathing' the LFO and envelope, and 'kick' lights nothing yet), and a page of its own for the PDL grammar. Deferred and not allowed to hold anything up: recounting the oscillator and filter parameters against Rack's source, since patches Rack re-saves carry more parameter slots than the vocabulary lists.

### 2026-11-04 — cycle 5 — The house bass is now a VST3 and AU, built by VST Template's machinery, passing every check. It's installed on your Mac; Live just needs a rescan.
> shipped · plugin installed, all checks pass · nobody has played it in Live yet

The plugin. A new script, native/emit_plugin.js, makes a plugin folder with VST Template's new-plugin.py, then fills it from a PDL file. It copies in the patch, the module kit and the voice engine the Mac app plays (unchanged), and adds a knob table that turns every knob of the patch into a host parameter, grouped by module, with the filter's FREQ and RES as the two large knobs, plus a Level. VST Template compares every plugin against a reference sketch. Here the reference is the same engine driven outside the plugin (reference/ref.cpp), so the exact check proves the plugin drives the patch the way the app does, and the plugin check proves the real audio callback, MIDI timing and knob smoothing keep it. Results: the engine matches the reference exactly (0 difference) on all three test phrases, and the plugin's loudness is within 0.13 dB on the cutoff sweep and within 0.000002 dB elsewhere. pluginval passes at strictness 5, auval passes, and the host check passes for the VST3 and the AU; turning the cutoff to 0.9 moves the brightness centroid from 201 Hz to 3222 Hz. To share the engine I made it allocate no memory while playing (the held-note list is now a fixed array, as in Simple Synth). That change touched every target, so I checked that all 17 existing renders came out byte-identical, and the native check still passes 109/109.

The coordination with Synth Templates. Their 292 recipe cards each become a PDL file. Run through the Mac target, 195 built; the other 97 all needed one module this target didn't have, the four-channel VCV mixer (VCA Mix). Before porting it I read its source and found the parts list wrong: it said the audio inputs were sockets 5 to 8 and the level controls 1 to 4, and Rack's source (v2.5.1, the version on your Mac, and the current one) says the opposite. So every Rack file made with that mixer put audio into control sockets. None of my own patches use it. Their card house bass does. I fixed both copies of the list (on-disk file and the copy inside the renderer, now v2.7, with only that block changed), and all four renderer checks still pass. I added the mixer to the Mac kit from the same source lines, and now all 292 cards build there.

### 2026-11-04 — cycle 3 — The same patch description that makes a VCV patch now makes a Mac app you play from the QWERTY keyboard through the MacBook speakers.
> shipped · 13 patches through both targets, 83/83 · four apps built, none heard yet · steward leans the plugin next

How it works. Every cable carries volts, just as in Rack: one volt per octave, 10 V gates, ±5 V audio. That way a patch means the same thing in both places, and the archetypes' knob values need no translating. I cloned VCV Fundamental's source (commit 10dd016) and copied each module's curves from Rack's own lines. The oscillator's tuning, the filter's cutoff and resonance scaling, the envelope's 1 ms to 10 s exponential stages, the VCA's exponential response and the LFO's offset and invert all come from the source, and each struct names the lines it follows. The keyboard copies Rack's QWERTY map from its source: Z is C4, Q is C5, and ` and 1 shift octaves. Notes take turns across voices the way Rack's MIDI-CV module rotates them. The app picks the MacBook Pro Speakers by name and falls back to the system output. Its probe found them.
