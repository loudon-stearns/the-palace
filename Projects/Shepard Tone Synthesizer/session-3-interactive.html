The glide, and why the stack moves as one

Stage 2 climbed the endless staircase in steps. Here it slides. Eleven sine waves, an octave apart, glide together under a fixed bright window. As long as every octave rides one shared pitch path, the ear hears a single note sliding up forever. Give each octave a mind of its own and the note comes apart.

Four things to try

  1. Climb forever by hand. Leave the drift at 0 and play C, E, G, then C again, and keep going. Every C is "higher" than the last, but the window never moves. On the circle, all the dots stay on one spoke.
  2. Pull the octaves apart. Raise the drift to 20 %, then 50 %, and play the same notes. The spoke fans out on every move, and the readout tells you by how many cents. Listen for the moment the one note becomes a chord of slightly wrong octaves.
  3. Make the classic mistake. Set the drift back to 0 and switch to Hz slew. Every voice now moves the same number of Hz per second, so the low voices land almost instantly and the high ones crawl. Each move becomes a smear that sweeps through the stack.
  4. The surprise. Press endless up with the drift at 30 %. You might expect the error to show only while the stack changes notes, but the glide never stops, and voices with different glide times trail the target by different amounts. The octaves stay out of tune for as long as the climb lasts.

The math, twice

One smoothing step per sample, run on pitch in semitones, not on frequency. Every voice adds its own whole number of octaves to the same smoothed path.

p[n] = a · p[n−1] + (1 − a) · target[n] , a = e^(−1 / (τ · sr))
(pitch now) = (smoothing) × (pitch a sample ago) + (1 − smoothing) × (target pitch) , smoothing = e^(−1 / (glide time × sample rate))
f_k[n] = 2^( log2(f_C4) + p[n] / 12 + k ) → every pair of neighbours stays exactly 2 : 1
(frequency of voice k) = 2^( (C4 in octaves) + (pitch now) ÷ 12 + (voice k's octave number) )
error_k ≈ rate × (τ_k − τ_shared) × 100 ¢ on an endless glide
(how far voice k sits off its octave, in cents) ≈ (glide speed in semitones per second) × (voice k's glide time − the shared glide time) × 100

The third line is why drift never heals on an endless climb. A voice with a longer glide time follows the moving target further behind. At 2 semitones a second, a 100 ms difference leaves that voice 20 cents flat for as long as the climb lasts.
