# Frequency-Time Duality — Context

## Why the page walks

Until October 2026 the page was a concept with a list: the continuum, the boundary, the recursion. In April 2026 the same idea was designed a second time as a project of its own, the Frequency-Time Explorer: a standalone web app that would walk the axis in five movements and open three doorways. Nothing was built.

The first Daily Page morning dealt the Explorer on 2026-10-13. Loudon played a rough of it on three axes and read the design as this page's rich face. The deposit of 2026-10-21 folded the walk in as the order of the page's sections. The reason is mechanical as much as it is about meaning: the rich face lays its pieces beside the text heading by heading, so once the text walks, the face can walk too.

## What the morning changed

Three findings came from that morning. The first Loudon brought from his own teaching, answering the page before the rough had a polyrhythm axis: the walk had none, and that crossing is one of his best classroom instruments for the idea. The other two came from playing. The crossing is a zone, whose top, he said, "should be lower in almost all cases" than the 40 Hz the rough drew. And once the drums fuse, their sound and the start point of the sample shape the timbre. One more note was about the source: for the modulation axis, a sine instead of noise, which he called "a much better choice." That morning's record is [[The Daily Page — record — 2026-10-13]].

## What the page left out

The Explorer's tool design composted with it; git keeps the page. The parts the text did not take:

- A universe view: an axis from 0.1 Hz to 100 kHz with five rows (period, frequency, tempo, MIDI note, subdivision at a reference tempo) and five coloured zones. The face's spec keeps a smaller version as the axis every piece shares.
- A lock that kept free exploration and a calculator strip shut until the walk was done. A rich face is a page you scroll, so nothing locks.
- A switchable test signal (click, noise burst, sine, your own sample) and saved positions.
- Six build questions: sample rate, upload size, phones, recording a learner's path, where the app would live, and whether it took the BBS palette.
- Its audience, musicians and sound designers who are already curious, not learners from zero. The face's first audience is Loudon's class.

Loudon wondered on 2026-10-13 whether the Explorer "perhaps needs a rename." It composted instead; this page keeps its name.
