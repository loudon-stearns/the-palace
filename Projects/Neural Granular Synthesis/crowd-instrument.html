The Crowd

Neural Granular Synthesis · a playable population · study · cycles 10–12

No neuron here is the sound. A crowd of simple neurons fires at audio rate, and every spike is a tiny action potential, so the crowd's firing rate is the pitch. You don't play the neurons. You play two things about the crowd: how tightly it listens to itself, and how many of its members ignore it.

What you're hearing

Each neuron is a phase that runs at its own natural rate, pulled toward the crowd's average phase by the coupling (the Kuramoto model). When the phase wraps, the neuron spikes. With no coupling, 128 spikes a cycle land at random: a hiss with a pitch smear around 110 Hz. Raise tightness past 1 and the crowd snaps together. The raster shows the stripes lining up and you hear a buzzy tone that gets brighter as you keep going, because the spikes stack more exactly.

A locked crowd with no pacemakers is dead still. Every period repeats the one before. Pacemakers fire at their own rate and never listen, but the rest of the crowd still hears them, so the locked core gets pushed around. That push is the wobble meter: vibrato and a chorus halo. The teal line on the pad is the prediction that pacemakers only thin out the pull, so the tone breaks at tightness 1/(1 − share). Cycle 9 confirmed it across 153 crowds.

Something to try

Sit at 40 % life and slide tightness right. Past the edge, the wobble grows as you tighten: the more closely the core listens, the harder the pacemakers pull it around. In this instrument coupling is the vibrato depth as well as the tone switch.

Then try the edge itself at two different lives. With no pacemakers, sliding tightness across the teal line is a switch: hiss, then tone. At two-thirds life the same slide is a slow crossfade, and the tone starts gathering well before the line. The edge doesn't move, but pacemakers soften it (cycle 12, edge-softening.png).

Honest limits

The shading comes from one random crowd at σ 0.10. The instrument rescales tightness by σ, which cycle 8 showed holds well but not exactly. Levelling rides the loudness so you hear texture rather than volume; turn it off to hear how much louder a locked crowd really is (about √N times). There's no noise and no plasticity yet: the only life is the pacemakers.
