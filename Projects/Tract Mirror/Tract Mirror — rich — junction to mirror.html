Tract Mirror · from one joint to the mirror

One joint, then the whole mirror

The page says the tubes on screen are the filter, not a picture of it. Five steps build that up: one joint between two tubes, a straight tube that rings, a shaped tube that says a vowel, a morph between shapes, and then the test, the machine run backward, from the sound to the tube. Each step plays. You can jump to any of them.

1 · One joint

Where a tube changes width, part of a wave turns back. How much turns back is set by the two areas and nothing else: that number is the joint's reflection, k.

k = (A₁ − A₂) ⁄ (A₁ + A₂)
(how much of the wave turns back) = (area before − area after) ÷ (area before + area after)
k² + (1 + k)² · A₂ ⁄ A₁ = 1
(share of the power turned back) + (share carried on, in the new tube) = all of it

The area before the joint is fixed at 4 cm². The tubes are drawn with their widths squeezed so that 0.04 and 400 cm² both fit; the numbers are not squeezed. In the sound, the click goes in at once and the echo comes back 0.4 s later, slowed down so you can hear it apart: its loudness is |k|, and a widening joint sends it back upside down.

2 · A straight tube, closed at one end

Line up 22 equal sections. Inside, every joint has k = 0 and nothing turns back. Only the ends reflect: the nearly closed glottis sends the wave back the right way up (+0.97), the open lips send it back upside down (−0.90). Between them the tube rings at its own notes.

3 · Shape the tube, and it says a vowel

Now narrow it in places. Every joint gets a k of its own from the two areas beside it, the same rule as step 1, 21 times. The notes move, and the moved notes are the vowel.

4 · The morph

Move from one vowel to the next by blending the shapes, not the numbers: the areas mix, and then every k is worked out again from the blended tube. Watch the notes travel through vowel space as the tube changes.

5 · The mirror: read the tube back out of its sound

Everything so far went one way, from shape to sound. Linear prediction goes the other way. Give it only the tube's answer to one click, and Levinson-Durbin peels a ladder of reflections off that sound, one rung at a time. Turn the ladder back into areas and you have a tube. The analysis runs at order 44 for 22 sections because each section takes one sample each way, so a round trip is two samples: every odd rung comes back zero and the even rungs are the ladder. Both tubes in the sound play through the analysis model, with no radiation filter on the lip reflection, which is the model the formant table is measured on, and the piece's level follower plays them at one loudness, so the A/B compares the shape of the sound, not its level.
