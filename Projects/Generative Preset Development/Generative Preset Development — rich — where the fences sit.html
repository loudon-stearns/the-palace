Where the fences sit

The page says a knob's numbers hide what it sounds like, and that named regions (closed, dark, warm, open, bright) are what keep a generated preset from wearing the same blank face. For Vital the project has already drawn those regions, before anyone listened: it split how 480 presets set the knob into fifths. These are the project's own renders, one knob changed at a time on Vital's init saw, and a blind test of whether those fences sit where your ear hears the sound change. Nothing plays until you press a rung.

Step 1 of 4 · one knob, numbered

Filter cutoff, fourteen settings, nothing else changed

This is the project's cutoff ladder, rendered by vital_measure.py: Vital's init saw holding C3 through its analog 12 dB low-pass, with the cutoff set to fourteen values. The knob counts in semitones on the MIDI note scale, so an equal step on the knob is an equal step in pitch, and the cutoff in hertz doubles every twelve. Press a rung. These play as rendered.

Step 2 of 4 · one switch

Match the rungs for loudness, so only the colour moves

Asked where the sound changes, an ear will put a fence at the fade, and the fade is not what a region names: a preset sets its level somewhere else. So each rung was measured for loudness (the K-weighting of ITU-R BS.1770, over the same one second of sustain, 0.6 to 1.6 s into the note, where the project measures brightness) and turned to meet the loudest. The bottom rung comes up 29.1 dB. Switch between the two.

All of this is one note with key tracking off; on another note the same knob numbers fall in a different place against its partials.

Step 3 of 4 · the test

Where does the sound stop being closed, dark, warm, open?

The rule, before the first rung. Mark four fences: the first rung that sounds dark rather than closed, then the first warm, the first open, the first bright. Two fences may share a rung. A fence agrees with the project's when it lands within one rung of it. All four agree: the project's fences sit where you hear the change. Two or three: partly. None or one: they miss. Chance: of the ways to place four fences in order on these fourteen rungs, a random one has all four agree of the time and three or more . The rungs play loudness-matched and unnumbered; the numbers come back when you ask for the project's fences.

Step 4 of 4 · the same test, another knob

Amp attack: where does the start stop being a click?

The same test on envelope 1's attack, which the page calls punchy, soft and pad and the project names clicky, punchy, natural, soft and swell. This knob is not in seconds: the time is its number to the fourth power, so 0.5 on the knob is 62 ms and 1.0 is a whole second. Each rung holds the note five seconds. Every rung's sustain already measures the same loudness to within 0.01 dB, so nothing is turned.

The rule, the same. Mark the first rung that sounds punchy rather than clicky, then the first natural, the first soft, the first swell. Two fences may share a rung. Within one rung agrees; all four agree, they sit where you hear; two or three, partly; none or one, they miss. Chance: of the ways to place four fences in order on these twelve rungs, a random one has all four agree of the time and three or more .
in ladder order, short to long · each rung 5.6 s
