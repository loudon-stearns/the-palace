How many copies before it stops sounding like a chorus?

Open Questions asks how many pitch-shifted copies the listener needs before they hear shimmer rather than chorus. Every take here is May's droplet cloud: the phrase copied many times, each copy played a few cents fast or slow, started somewhere in the first 300 ms and panned somewhere left to right. We start with one voice and end on a square that sets the number of copies against how far apart they are tuned.

One voice

The phrase, dry: Kokoro reading "the prism, and the cloud." Its pitch sits near 200 Hz (measured: 202 Hz median). Every cloud on the next steps is made only of copies of this.

More copies

Now copies, all tuned within about 5 cents of the voice, the set May made to answer this question. Each copy starts at its own moment in the first 300 ms, so the number of copies is also how many onsets arrive per second. Thirty copies is a hundred onsets a second. An old rule for reverbs (Schroeder, 1962) puts a smooth, flutter-free tail at about a thousand.

onsets per second = N ⁄ T
(how many copies begin each second) = (the number of copies) ÷ (the window they start in, here 300 ms)

One copy is the dry voice: step 1.

Wider spread

Hold the count at 400 copies and widen how far apart they are tuned, from about 1 cent to about 75. Two copies a few cents apart beat: their sum gets louder and softer as they drift in and out of step. A crowd of copies does the same, all at once, and how fast the level swirls depends on how far apart they are tuned. The swirl is measured on May's engine with a steady 200 Hz tone in place of the phrase, since the phrase's own pitch moves too much to measure it.

swirl rate ≈ f · (ln 2 ⁄ 1200) · (2 ⁄ √π) · σ
(how often the crowd's level rises and falls) ≈ (the pitch) × (how far one cent moves a pitch: 0.058 %) × 1.13 × (the spread in cents). At 200 Hz and 5 cents: 0.65 times a second.

One cent is close to step 2's tight tuning: the same crowd, its swirl slowed to almost nothing.

Which dial makes the chorus?

The question asks for a number of copies. Here are two counts and two spreads, as a square. Down the square, the count goes up 83 times. Across, the copies are tuned five times further apart. The same steady-tone measurement across every take on these steps: from 2 copies to 2500, the swirl's depth stays between 0.44 and 0.53 (a crowd of random phases tends to 0.52) and its rate between 0.65 and 0.87 times a second; at 400 copies, 1 to 75 cents moves the rate from 0.23 to 9.4 times a second.
