White light through two kinds of lens

Cross-Domain Parallels says a prism disperses white light into a spectrum, and Shimmer Cloud disperses audio into a spectrum of pitch shifts. A prism doesn't change any colour. It sends each one out at its own angle and its own time. So strike each kind of lens with audio's white light, a click, and watch when its lows and highs come out. We start with the click alone and end on a cloud of each kind.

strike it with

White light

A click is every frequency at once, all at the same instant: audio's white light. On the picture, time runs across and pitch runs up, so a click is one thin vertical line. The plucked note is a fainter comparison: harmonics stacked on 220 Hz, all starting together.

A pane of glass

Delay the click. Every frequency waits the same time, so the line moves right and stays a line. Glass in a window does this to light: it slows every colour about the same, and nothing fans out.

A pitch copy

Now copy the sound a little fast or slow, the way each droplet in May's cloud does. Try the plucked note: its harmonics all move up or down together. Then the click: there is nothing to move. A click played faster is still a click. Whatever the shift, lows and highs land at the same moment.

rate = 2c ⁄ 1200
(how fast the copy is played) = 2 to the power of (the shift in cents ÷ 1200): +1200 cents plays it twice as fast, an octave up.

0 cents is step 1.

One prism

Now one lens the way the bundle's third model and the steward's pushed lens build it: every frequency passes at full strength, but each waits its own time. The wait climbs evenly per octave from 80 Hz to 11 kHz. Struck with a click, it comes out as a sweep: the click's frequencies, sorted in time. Turn it around and the highs come first.

τ(f) = D · u(f),   u(f) = log(f ⁄ 80) ⁄ log(11000 ⁄ 80)
(how long frequency f waits) = (the lens's strength D) × (how far f sits up the range from 80 Hz to 11 kHz, from 0 to 1, by octaves). Turned around: D × (1 − u). Its gain is 1 everywhere; it only delays.

A cloud of each

The claim, tested. Two clouds of 600, each struck the same way, each scattered over the same 25 ms and panned across the field. The first is pitch copies, spread about 25 cents: the "spectrum of pitch shifts". The second is prisms, strengths from 150 to 600 ms, highs late: the steward's pushed cloud. If a spectrum of pitch shifts disperses like a prism, the two should fan the strike the same way.
