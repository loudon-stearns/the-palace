The order as a knob — when does Jn arrive?

The Bessel function itself, played as the waveform: x becomes time. As a sound, one unit of x is a 110 Hz cycle over 2π; slowed, one unit is 25 ms, and Jn shapes the loudness of a 440 Hz tone so you can hear its timing. The panel draws Jn(x) from 0 to 30. Walk from J0 to the order as a knob; the last step tests what the section says about where the peak falls.

The Bessel function of order ν is a sum of powers of x, the first of them x to the ν. That is why a higher order stays near silent at first: for small x, xν is tiny. ν need not be a whole number, so the order can turn like a knob.
Jν(x) ≈ √( 2 ⁄ πx ) · cos( x − νπ⁄2 − π⁄4 )   for large x
Far out, every Bessel function is a cosine with its phase moved by a quarter turn per order and an eighth more, fading as one over the square root of x. Its zeros settle to a spacing of π.
Every zero and peak in the readouts is found on the engine's own values, not typed in. Each sound is scaled to the same peak, so you compare shapes, not levels.
