The carrier null — is it exactly Jn?

A carrier at 1100 Hz and a modulator at 100 Hz, eleven to one, so the sidebands have room to spread on both sides without folding back onto the carrier or each other. Only the index β moves. The upper panel is the Bessel functions themselves, drawn against β; the lower is the sound's spectrum, measured from the engine's own samples, with Jn(β) outlined over it. Walk β up; the last step goes to the first zero of J0, where the section says the carrier must vanish.

(the modulated carrier) = the sum, over every whole number n, of (the n-th Bessel function, at the index) × (a sine at the carrier plus n modulators). The carrier itself is the n = 0 term, so its loudness is J0(β) and nothing else, as long as no other sideband lands on its frequency.

The first index at which the zeroth Bessel function crosses zero. There the carrier's share of the sound is nothing; all of it has gone to the sidebands.
