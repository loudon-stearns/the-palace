## The verdicts

**The window you can hear** (made, FILO Circular Buffer RMS) — **keep.**

1. *Test or decoration?* A test. The section says 100 samples is twitchy, 1000 sluggish, 500 in between, and that the number becomes "time they can hear". Step 5 plays the three windows blind, level-matched to −20 dB RMS, and asks the reader to label them before the reveal. If the ear can't sort them, or sorts them otherwise, the page is wrong for that ear, and the reveal says so in as many words. The two measures beside it, the gain's wobble on a 55 Hz note and the time to grab a sudden tone, are not the test; they say what changed, after the ear has spoken.
2. *Faithful?* Yes, on every number I reworked (below). Step 1 shows the order of the ring and says "the page calls this buffer FILO … the order usually called FIFO": it shows the disagreement and leaves the text alone. One sentence goes past the page: the lead says "this compressor measures loudness with a ring … and nothing else", and step 3 says the window "cannot set [attack and release] apart". That is true of the piece's model. The page never says the Gen~ build has no attack and release stage, and the gathered proof's Moment 2 runs that compressor at "attack=0ms, release=10ms". Note for the maker (not a remake): say "the compressor here" in the lead, so the model's limit isn't read as the build's.
3. *Walks up?* Yes. A sixteen-slot ring you can count, then the sum read as a level, then attack and release as one straight shape beside slide~, then the window as a knob, then the blind test. One idea per step, and the test is last.

**One ring, two clocks** (made, FILO Circular Buffer RMS) — **keep.**

1. *Test or decoration?* A test of the section's last paragraph, "below perception here, fully perceptual there". Step 3 hides the ring's length, the reader drags it up from 64 slots and marks where the slot that leaves becomes a separate repeat, and the reveal compares the mark with 500 and 44 100. A mark at or under 500 makes the page wrong for that ear, and the piece says that too. On a snare, where a delayed copy can separate from the original in well under 20 ms, that can really happen.
2. *Faithful?* Yes, and it holds the section's tension instead of smoothing it. At 500 slots step 1 shows what leaves the ring is audible, as a colour (a comb, notches at 44.1 Hz and every 88.2 Hz), while the page calls the window "below perception" and also "audible enough". The caption names both.
   *Can it test the page's ~20 ms?* Only in part. What the piece measures is one listener's echo threshold on one phrase: where a delayed copy stops fusing with the original. That is one reading of the page's "perceptual attack threshold of ~20ms". The other reading, whether 11 ms of lag in the gain's onset can be heard in a compressor, belongs to the window piece and is not measured anywhere. Note for the maker (not a remake): have the reveal set the mark beside 882 samples (20 ms) as well as 500 and 44 100, and say in one clause that the mark is an echo threshold. Then the piece puts a number from the reader's own ear beside the page's 20 ms, without claiming to settle it.
3. *Walks up?* Yes. What leaves the ring, then the stretch from 64 slots to a second, then the hidden test. Step 1 holds two things, the delay and its comb, but the comb is just how that delay sounds at 500 slots, so it reads as one idea.

**Three teaching beats** (gathered, `proofs/Compressor Design — proof — pedagogical-retrospective.md`, margin) — **remake** (caption only).

1. *Test or decoration?* Neither, and it doesn't need to be. It is a record, and the page's forward vector asks for exactly this, a teaching retrospective. It is the one thing in the bundle that speaks to "time they can hear" from a classroom, and the caption holds its disagreement with the page (it calls 1024 samples "smooth and natural"; the page calls 1000 "sluggish").
2. *Faithful?* The caption is not honest about who remembers. It opens "**The window heard in a room, as remembered**" and says "it was reconstructed from memory". The proof itself says students "*reportedly*" heard these things, which is not how a teacher writes about their own class. It came in with the May proof batch (born 2026-05-04, first committed in `a5ca537b`, listed in the 2026-05-04 Proof Showcase), and nothing on record says whose memory it is. The fix: replace "as remembered" with "as reconstructed", and replace the sentence "It was reconstructed from memory, not recorded, and it says so." with "It says it was reconstructed, not recorded; it came in with the May 2026 proofs, and whose memory it draws on is not on record." Optional, for the same caption: its Moment 2 sets attack and release separately, which the window piece's model can't do.
3. *Walks up?* Not applicable. It is a margin record at the section's end, read after the walk.

The manifest gives it no `contract` block. Enrichment step 3 says a gathered proof carries one, but this is the first gathered `.md` piece in any face, and it needs nothing from its host. The renderer showed it in the margin, and conform doesn't read `.md`. It doesn't block anything.

**The knee in the spectrum** (made, Soft Knee Mathematics) — **remake** (one line, and a check for it).

1. *Test or decoration?* The strongest test on the face. The page says the quadratic knee "has bounded Fourier content". The piece puts a tone through the page's own curve, asks the reader whether the knee's harmonics stop, and only then shows they don't, beside a cubic that does. If the page were right, the numbers would have come out the other way.
2. *Faithful?* Its numbers are, and it says plainly that the detector has been taken out, so the curve shapes the waveform directly. That is the strictest reading of the page's sentence, and the piece names it. **One defect, and it lands on the piece's own subject.** The tone it plays is `Math.round(2 * 44100)` = 88 200 samples of a 400-sample cycle: 220.5 cycles. The loop flips the tone's polarity at every seam, every two seconds. The wave comes down to zero and starts up again instead of going negative, which is a jump in slope. Measured: the second difference at the seam is 4.4e−3, against at most 3.5e−5 anywhere inside, 127 times larger. So every step, the "pure" straight line of step 1 included, carries a corner, the very thing the piece teaches you to hear, at 0.5 Hz. An agent can't say how loud that tick is, but it shouldn't be there. **Fix:** in `KN.tone`, `const n = 400 * Math.round(sec * SR / 400)` (88 000 samples, 220 whole cycles), and add a check to `check.mjs` that the tone's length is a whole number of cycles.
3. *Walks up?* Yes. The straight line, the corner, the knee, the four edge cases, then the question. The edge cases sit between the knee and its test, which is a small detour. Taste, not a remake.

## Offers

All five hold. I reworked each one myself: `recompute.py` (numpy, a 2^18-point FFT, separate from the piece's own transform) and a Node read of the piece's core.

1. **FILO → FIFO (no-claim-edit): confirmed.** The page itself says the buffer "adds the current sample and subtracting the oldest", so the oldest leaves first, which is FIFO. The check pushed 384 samples through a 16-slot ring, and every one that left was x[n−N]². Renaming the heading changes this face's section key (`"heading": "FILO Circular Buffer RMS"`), so the manifest heading must change in the same commit, then re-stamp. Nothing else links to the heading (`git grep "Compressor Design#"` finds nothing). One other page uses the word: `Projects/Retrospective Delay/Archive/Retrospective Delay — Staging.md:42` says "uses a FILO circular buffer". It is an archive, so leave it or fix it on the same yes.
2. **Bounded Fourier (claim-change): confirmed, with the range made wider.** Hard corner: −11.6 and −10.8 dB/oct at peaks −6 and −12 dB (1/k² is −12). Quadratic knee, 16th to 255th: −15.6 to −19.3 dB/oct, the same as the maker. Over the 64th to the 1023rd it is −14.8 to −17.8 (1/k³ is −18). The knee's harmonics stay above −140 dB to the 323rd–737th, depending on width and level, so "near the 255th" is the top of the piece's chart, not the end of the knee's spectrum. The cubic stops at the 3rd. Two nuances for the suggested wording. Say "about 18 dB per octave", since 18 is the limit and the measured falls run 15 to 19. And the measurement is the curve acting directly on the signal; in the compressor a detector smooths the level first, so the curve shapes how the gain moves rather than the wave itself. The page's sentence is about the curve, so the offer stands. The page's "a sharp discontinuity" is also the wrong word: the hard knee's curve is continuous; its slope jumps. The offer already says so.
3. **"gain =" → "output =", levels in dB (no-claim-edit): confirmed.** The region lines give the output level, the same as the code block's `output =`. Boundary-Crossing Instruments' landed face makes the matching offer (slope is 1/R only on decibel axes).
4. **R = ∞ (no-claim-edit): confirmed, one nuance.** At −6 dB in, out is −18 dB, the threshold: a clamp, not a step. Its slope is 1.000 below and 0.000 above. With a knee wider than 0 the slope runs from 1 to 0 across the knee, so it is a step only at W = 0. Suggested: "with a hard knee, its slope becomes a step function".
5. **R < 1 is upward expansion (claim-change): confirmed.** With R = 0.5 and T = −18: +12.0 dB at −6 dB in, 0 dB at −30 dB in. Below T − W/2 the output is the input for every R, so no setting of R reaches a downward expander. R → 0⁺ sends the gain to infinity, which is the page's own R = 0 case, so the edge cases agree with each other.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt, not busy. Each section gets one walk that ends in a question the reader answers with their own ear or their own guess before the numbers show. The window's length, the ring's two readings, and the knee's spectrum could each come out against the text, and the knee does. The face also does what the page asks of its students, "make the invisible visible", and turns it on the page: the FIFO order, the slope that becomes the step, the expander that only goes up. Two fixes before it lands. The knee's loop seam is small, but it puts a corner into the piece about corners. The proof's caption is a matter of who said it. Neither needs more than one round.
