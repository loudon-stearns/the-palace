The chase room

A show on GoButton's TIMECODE track, sent down one cable to three desks that chase it. Two songs: 01:00:00:00, then 02:00:00:00. Three cues. Pick a lesson, or set it up yourself, and see which cues land, which come late, and which fire into a silent room. Everything about the timecode is GoButton's own ltc.js: its Pre-Roll, its encoder, its reader.

the desks — made up, each set the way a real one can be

Real desks call these settings lock frames or chase threshold (how many good frames in a row before it believes the clock), freewheel or drop-out time (how long it runs on alone when the clock goes quiet), and offset (whether it adds the frame every reader is late). The numbers here are not any brand's; check your own desk's manual. Measured in Node over the same code, every reader in the hostile folder names its first frame two or three frames after the signal starts, and finds the time again the same two or three frames after a dropout of any length (hostile/chase.mjs).
