A counter that skips to stay honest

NTSC video runs at 30000/1001 frames a second, a little under thirty, but its timecode counts to thirty. Five steps: counting at thirty; the same count at 29.97, falling behind; skipping two labels every minute; the exception at every tenth minute; and last, a whole day of labels walked one by one. The rule, before the run Every frame of a 24-hour day at 29.97 drop-frame is given its label, and every label is turned back into its frame number. Pass: no label is emitted twice, no label the rule forbids is emitted (00 or 01 in a minute not ending in zero), every label goes back to the frame it came from, and after one hour the label is within one frame (33.4 ms) of the wall clock. Fail: any one of those. The page asks whether long runs, over an hour, keep frame accuracy; the same walk prints how far the label has moved from the clock at 1, 10 and 24 hours. A walk that only counted labels could not fail; this one checks each against the rule and against its own inverse.
