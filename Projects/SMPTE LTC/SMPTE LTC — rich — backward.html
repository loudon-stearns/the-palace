SMPTE LTC · the sync word · four steps

Backward, and at any speed

The page says LTC reads in reverse and at varispeed, and that the sync word is why: it tells a reader where a frame is, which way the tape runs, and how fast. Four steps: the sound itself and where its energy sits; the sync word forward and mirrored; the tape run backward and at other speeds, by ear; and last, a reader that knows neither speed nor direction. Nothing plays until you press a play button.

The rule, before the run Fifty frames, plus one either side that is not scored, go through the same audio path every time: a 10 Hz high-pass and noise 30 dB under the peak, sampled at 48 kHz. The reader is told neither speed nor direction; it guesses the cell length from the gaps and looks for the sync word both ways. First, a reversed recording: one made at 1× at each of the six rates, its samples reversed, then sent through the path. Then speeds: 25 fps, rendered by time-scaling the tape, so each sample is the tape averaged under the head. That is an ideal anti-alias, kinder than a real varispeed, so beside each row the same speed is also read from a 1× buffer through a plain linear resampler, printed and not judged. A row passes when all 50 frames come back with no wrong timecode and the direction right on every one. The page’s claim fails if the reversed recordings or the backward rows read nothing, or read wrong timecodes. The speed is read from how far apart the sync words come, against the rate the tape was made at; it is printed beside each row. Without the rate, a reader could not tell a fast 25 from a 30. Chance: none; a frame needs eighty bits right and a sync word in place. The limit: at 12× and 16× a half cell is one sample or less at 48 kHz, which is Nyquist. Those rows show where the sample rate runs out; they are not counted either way. The control: a forward-only reader on the backward rows. If it read nothing there, the direction comes from the asymmetric sync word, not the bit code. The shuttle: 2× forward, slowing through a stop, back at 2× in reverse. Printed, not judged.
