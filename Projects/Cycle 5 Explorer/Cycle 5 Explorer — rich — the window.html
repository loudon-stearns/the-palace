A seven-note window on the line of fifths

The explorer's one idea, built up from nothing. We start with a strip of named notes and end on the section's claim, that each step darker lowers exactly one note, tested on every tonic the explorer allows and then on your ear. Silent until you press something.

The line of fifths

Every note has a place on one line, an integer k, each place a fifth above the one to its left: … F C G D A E B F♯ C♯ … with C at 0. The name comes from the place, never from a list of twelve, which is why G major will always say F♯ and never G♭. Press any note to hear it. The two notes seven places either side light up in blue: they have the same letter, one a half step from the next. Press seven apart to hear one of them and then the note seven places to its right.

letter(k) = “FCGDAEB”[ (k + 1) mod 7 ],   accidentals(k) = ⌊ (k + 1) ⁄ 7 ⌋
(the letter) = count places from F and go round the seven letters; (sharps, or flats when negative) = how many whole laps of seven you have made. Every lap of seven adds one sharp to the same letter.
k + 7  →  the same letter, 7 × 7 = 49 semitones higher = four octaves and a half step
(seven places right) = the same letter, raised one half step, once the octaves are folded away.

A window of seven

A key is seven neighbours on that line, a window [L, L + 6]. Seven in a row cover every letter exactly once, so a key never spells a letter twice and never skips one. There is no home note yet; the window is only a set. Slide it with the arrows (that is the explorer's note ring, changing key) and play its seven notes in the order they sit on the line, rising by fifths.

The line is step 1's; the window only marks seven places on it.

key = { L, L + 1, …, L + 6 }
(a key) = seven neighbouring places on the line; L is the window's left edge, its flattest note.

A tonic inside the window

Now pick a home. The window stays where it is (the notes of C major) and the tonic moves inside it: at the window's flat end it is Lydian, at the sharp end Locrian. Each note's degree, in amber above it, is its distance from the tonic. With the tonic at the flat end every other note sits sharpward of home, and that is the brightest mode; walk the tonic right and more of the window sits flatward of it, darker each time. Press a mode to hear its scale. Watch the line: no note moves. Only home does.

The window is step 2's, held at the notes of C major.

T = L + m,   d = k − T,   degree = step (4d mod 7) + 1 with ⌊ (d + 1) ⁄ 7 ⌋ sharps
(the tonic) = the window's left edge plus the mode number, 0 for Lydian to 6 for Locrian; (a note's degree) = its distance from the tonic in places, read as a scale step and an accidental. Six places right of the tonic is ♯4; six places left is ♭5.

Hold the tonic, slide the window

Now the other way round, the explorer's same tonic switch. C stays home and the window slides one place flat under it, each slide one mode darker. One note falls off the window's sharp end; seven places to the left, the same letter comes in a half step lower. Press darker: you hear the note that left, then the one that came, and the line marks both. Walk from Lydian down to Locrian.

At Ionian this is step 3's window, with C at home.

[L, L + 6]  →  [L − 1, L + 5]:   out goes L + 6, in comes L − 1 = (L + 6) − 7
(one step darker, tonic held) = slide the window one place flat. The note that leaves and the note that arrives are seven places apart: step 1's same letter, a half step apart.

The test: exactly one note, every time?

The section says each step darker lowers exactly one note. Here every darker step on every tonic the explorer allows, C♭ to C♯, is counted: the scale before, the scale after, and how many of the seven notes sounded a different pitch. The explorer's header switch decides what a mode change keeps, so the count is made both ways. Press any cell to hear the two scales, before then after.

Each cell is one press of the explorer's darker button on that row's tonic, with the switch set as above; same tonic is step 4's.

Your ear: which degree went down?

The grids make the one note visible; the page says the sound makes it audible. This asks your ear. Two scales play on a random tonic, the second one step darker with the tonic held. Press the degree you heard go down. I could not check what a person hears; only you can.
