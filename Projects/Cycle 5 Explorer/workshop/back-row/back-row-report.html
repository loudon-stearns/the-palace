Cycle 5 Explorer — the back-row test

How big each label on the page looks from the back of a classroom, measured in headless Chromium, not in a room. The angle a capital letter makes at your eye: 5′ is the smallest letter 20/20 eyes can make out, about 10′ reads, 16′ and up is comfortable. The back row sits 8 screen-heights from the screen; a small room, 6.

What it found. On a 1280×720 projector only the tonic and mode name read from the back row; almost everything else sits between 4′ and 5′. On a 1920×1080 projector it is worse, because the page uses the extra pixels for space, not size. And the wheel at 1280×720 does not fit the screen: its bottom ring is cut off. Pressing ⌘+ makes letters bigger but stacks the page into a column, so the wheel alone is taller than the screen from 150% on (table at the foot).

What I built. Room view: press R, or open the page with ?room. The wheel takes the screen's height and sits whole beside the inspector on one screen; the type around it is one and a half times larger, twice on a 1080p screen; the four-dialect table and the ground switch step aside. Nothing changes until you press R.

The honest limit: room view lifts most labels from about 5′ to about 7′. The wheel's notes, the tonic and mode, the chord symbol and the staff read from the back row; the grids read from the middle of the room or a small room, not the back of a big one. A grid that reads from the back would need a screen of its own.
