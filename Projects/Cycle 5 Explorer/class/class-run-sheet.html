A fifty-minute class built from the page as it stands, in the order your brief gives: emotion, then mode, then the cycle and its progressions, then modal interchange, with the four notations along the way. Every key and button below was pressed on the real page by a script before it went on this sheet, and the chords, feel words and notes quoted are what the page showed back. Blue boxes are what you do, red lines what you might say, dashed boxes a question for the room. Change anything with a pen.

Before class 2 min

Open cycle5-explorer.html. On a dark projector press P for Phosphor. Press S once as a sound check. The page opens on C Ionian, with a mode change keeping the same notes. T flips that.
Know this before class: the grids hide their last column. Rehearsing turned it up. At 1280, 1440 and 1920 pixels wide, the Scale Tones and Chord Tones grids each scroll sideways and hide their seventh column. In Scale Tones that is the 7 and ♭7 column, the one note that turns Ionian into Mixolydian. The Chord Function grid does the same at 1024 to 1440 and hides its VII column. No width I tried shows all three in full, so zooming won't save it. Until the page is fixed, scroll a grid sideways when you reach its last column.

1 · Emotion first 10 min

Press T until the header says same tonic. Then click Bright to dark, one note at a time, or walk it by hand with ↑ to Lydian and ↓ one mode at a time, S for each scale.
Before you reveal the page's word: what does this one feel like? Collect the room's words for two or three modes, then show the inspector's.
Every step darker lowers exactly one note. Watch the red box move in the Scale Tones grid.

2 · Same notes, new home 8 min

Press ↑ twice to get back to C Ionian, then T for same notes. Click Same notes, new home, or press ↑ once and walk ↓. The white keys stay; the home moves:

F Lydian → C Ionian → G Mixolydian → D Dorian → A Aeolian → E Phrygian → B Locrian

It's one seven-note window on the line of fifths. Turning the degree ring moves the tonic inside the window; turning the note ring slides the window. That's the whole decoder ring.
Drag the note ring yourself, slowly, and ask: which note just changed? Each slot is one new sharp or one new flat.

Your 2026-10-27 video says the line-of-fifths jig "isn't incredibly useful for the students to see". The page shows it anyway, under the hood at the bottom. Skip it here, or use it once to answer whoever asks why F♯ and not G♭. ☐ skip  ☐ show once

3 · The cycle, and four ways to write it 12 min

Press ↑ five times to come home to C Ionian. Click Around the cycle, which plays the diatonic cycle of fifths. Then press N three times while it loops, so the room watches one progression in each notation:
BerkleeImaj7 Cmaj7IVmaj7 Fmaj7VII–7(♭5) B–7(♭5)III–7 E–7VI–7 A–7II–7 D–7V7 G7Imaj7 Cmaj7
Jazz shorthandIΔ7 CΔ7IVΔ7 FΔ7VIIø7 Bø7III–7 E–7VI–7 A–7II–7 D–7V7 G7IΔ7 CΔ7
Lead sheetImaj7 Cmaj7IVmaj7 Fmaj7VIIm7♭5 Bm7♭5IIIm7 Em7VIm7 Am7IIm7 Dm7V7 G7Imaj7 Cmaj7
ClassicalIM7 CM7IVM7 FM7viiø7 Bø7iii7 Em7vi7 Am7ii7 Dm7V7 G7IM7 CM7
Same chords, four dialects. Berklee writes uppercase numerals and puts the quality on the end; classical lets the case of the numeral tell you major or minor.
Then try the presets II V I and I VI II V, and press 1 to 7 to play single chords. The inspector shows each one's tensions and avoid notes.

4 · Borrow one chord 14 min

Click Borrow one chord. It puts the page on Ionian and plays the preset IV to minor IV:

Imaj7 Cmaj7IVmaj7 Fmaj7IV–7 F–7 from AeolianImaj7 Cmaj7

The minor iv comes from the parallel minor, the same tonic with a darker window. The dashed outline in the lane marks a borrowed chord.
The students make one. Hold same tonic. Click Clear, turn on + Build from clicks, and press 1 6 4 5:

Imaj7 Cmaj7VI–7 A–7IVmaj7 Fmaj7V7 G7

Clear again. Press 1, click the ♭VI in the Aeolian row of the Chord Function grid, then 4 5. Play both versions back to back:

Imaj7 Cmaj7♭VImaj7 A♭maj7 from AeolianIVmaj7 Fmaj7V7 G7

Now press → twice. The progression moves to D, and the borrowed chord keeps its colour:

Imaj7 Dmaj7♭VImaj7 B♭maj7 from AeolianIVmaj7 Gmaj7V7 A7

Which single chord would you borrow into your own song, and from which mode? Have two or three students build theirs on the projector.

5 · Walk the keys, and close 4 min

Click Walk the keys. It plays a tonic chord up the sharp side from C to F♯, then down the flat side from C to G♭.
The one question to end on: how do you feel, and which mode felt most like you today?

Afterward the page is one file with nothing to install, so a student can keep it and open it in any browser.
