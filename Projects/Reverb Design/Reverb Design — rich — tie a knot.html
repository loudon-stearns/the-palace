Reverb Design · the part the dish does not have

Tie a knot in the thread

Knock on a grown dish and for the first fifth of a second you can still pick out single echoes. Then it blurs. A room does not make you wait that long. The fix dates from 1961 and it is a knot: a place where the sound goes round a small loop and leaks out a little each time. One knock goes in. A cloud comes out. Nothing is lost.

walk
y(t) = x(t) + r² · g² · y(t − 2L)

Start where the dish starts. One hypha, two ends: the knock runs down, turns, comes back. What you hear now is the knock plus what you heard one round trip ago (2L), made smaller by the two ends it turned at (r, twice: each end keeps a fifth) and by the way there and back (g). Every echo is a copy of the one before, the same shape, a fixed time later. Engineers call this a comb, because drawn against pitch its response has teeth. You can count every echo, and that is the trouble with it.

y(t) = −g · x(t) + x(t − D) + g · y(t − D)

Now the knot, alone, with no thread after it. What comes out is a little of the knock upside down at once (−g), then most of it one turn of the loop later (D), then what is still going round, smaller each turn. Schroeder and Logan's trick, 1961, is that first upside-down piece: it is sized so that every pitch comes out exactly as loud as it went in. A comb colours the sound. A knot only smears it in time. That is why it is called an allpass.

Put the knots on the thread the knock comes in on, then let it into the thread from step 1. The echoes still come every round trip, but each one is now a small cloud, and four knots turn one knock into dozens of pulses. Each knot multiplies what the one before it made. Untie them and it is step 1 again.

The test. This is a colony grown by the dish's own engine, fed the same plucked strings its check uses. With no knots, find the moment its echoes become a wash. Then tie knots in front of it. The dish's check already showed that growing the colony does not move that moment much. Here you move it by hand.

Nothing sounds until you click.

The knots here are four loops of 4.8, 3.6, 12.8 and 9.4 ms that keep 0.7, 0.7, 0.6 and 0.6 of what goes round. Those numbers are this piece's own, picked so the loops share no common beat; they are not taken from any published reverb. The moment of “a wash” is the dish's own measure: in each 20 ms window, the share of samples louder than the window's own average, against the share noise would give (its check names Abel and Huang, 2006, as the source of that measure; this piece did not re-check the paper). The engine is the dish's, read from its page when this one opens.
