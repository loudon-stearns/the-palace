### 2026-11-07 — cycle 13 — Slime Mold Delay is a plugin now, installed under Loudon Live: five bands of your sound feed a mold, and its tubes are the delay lines.
> shipped · every check passes · not yet played in Live · steward leans you put it on a send

**Start with [the dish, playing a performance](Projects/Slime Mold Delay/plugin/proofs/dish-performance.html).** Press play. It's thirty seconds through the plugin's own processor, picture and sound together. Bass plucks, and the mold covers the dish as a mesh. After six seconds the flow carves it down to the bass roads: from 138 tubes to 12 in three seconds. Then the music moves to the middle bands and the bass roads let go while tips go out looking. Then the highs take over. Then every band plays at once while Feed sinks from eating to starving, and the colony thins to 8 tubes.

**The screen** is the petri dish seen at a tilt, not a scope. Each yellow filament is a tube, and so a delay line, standing up off the agar as thick as the flow law makes it. Beads stream along each tube at the Flow speed knob's rate, so a bead takes exactly as long to cross a tube as its echo does: you watch the sound travel the network. Flakes swell when their band plays. A tube that dies leaves a brown slime trail on the agar for a while, the way a real plasmodium leaves its trail and then avoids it. Drag up or down on the screen to feed or starve the colony.

**The knobs:** Feed and Flow speed are the big two. Clock sets how fast the dish thinks (0 freezes the roads), Budget how much road it can afford, Dish picks Five flakes, Ring or Scatter, Cover again lays a fresh mesh over the carved roads, Regen sets how much sound recirculates, and Dry/Wet sets the mix.

**How the sound works:** each tube carries sound both ways. Where tubes meet, the sound is summed, softly saturated and sent on down every other tube. Tubes that reach the listener are panned by where they came from. The plugin runs the same 'compete' flow law as the browser dish.

**Checks, all passed** (`./plugin.sh all`): four test performances match the numpy sketch sample for sample to under 1e-7 of peak. The built plugin's loudness stays within 0.31 dB of the sketch every quarter second. Codesign, pluginval at strictness 5 and auval all pass. The installed VST3 loads the way a DAW loads it, and each knob changes the sound (table). One thing didn't work: the real editor's photo came back with no picture, the same as for the other template plugins on this Mac. So the editor image is the template's preview page.

### 2026-10-18 — cycle 11 — The dish now starts as a mesh over the whole agar and carves it down. On the clustered dish the network got leaner: 1.73× the shortest network → 1.28×.
> shipped · start-dense-then-thin is the page default · steward leans: pull tubes taut next

**Open [the flow dish](open:Projects/Slime Mold Delay/slime-mold-flow-dish.html) and press THE MUSIC MOVES.** The dish fills with a fine gold lattice, and within a few seconds it thins to a few roads. SPREAD A MESH floods it again whenever you want it to rethink. Two new dropdowns put cycle 10's mold back so you can compare by ear. [The strip](open:Projects/Slime Mold Delay/headless/2026-10-18-mesh-dish-carving.png) shows a browser run at 1.5, 9, 14 and 29 seconds.

**A mesh alone made it worse.** On the clustered dish (listener at the left, four flakes fanned out on the right) a mesh with the old law went from 1.73× to 2.0× the shortest network. Drawing it showed why. The old law lets a tube's thickness level off as its flow rises, so two parallel roads that split a load both stay full and neither has to lose. The mesh carved down to one separate road per flake.

**Two changes made the roads compete.** First, thickness now grows faster than the flow it carries (Tero's power law), so a road carrying twice the flow ends up more than twice as thick, and parallel roads fight until one wins. That alone gave a rake: each flake kept its own road and they met only at the listener. Parallel roads sit at the same pressure, so the cross-links between them carry nothing and die before the roads can merge. Second, the flakes take turns, the trick in Tero's Tokyo model: the flake that just sounded pushes, and the other flakes that are still sounding draw half the flow alongside the listener. That puts pressure between neighbouring roads, so roads can merge into trunks. Two smaller changes go with it. Under the new law a road feeding one flake is thin by design, so a tube is pruned when it carries almost no flow, not when it's thin, and its gain follows its flow so that flake isn't buried. A flake that falls silent stops drawing, so its road can let go when the music moves.

**What changed, on 40 seeded dishes per row** (table). The round dish now feeds all five flakes in 30 dishes instead of 10. In 5 of them the network uses less tube than the shortest straight-road network, which means real shared trunks, the way the mold does it. The cycle-10 mold never did that.

1. **The old roads kept all their push.** Each reached flake was judged only against the *other reached flakes*. When the music left, the nearly silent left flakes still pushed 100% until a tip found the right. Now a flake's share is judged against all the sounding food in the dish, so the old roads lose their push the moment the music moves.

That tube carried no flow, so it pulled back in about a second, and the next tip from that spot built an island the listener couldn't hear. Now a tip that crosses a bare spot keeps growing and leaves a branch point behind, the way a real tip does. As a side effect, roads now bend through branch points, so echoes take multi-hop paths and right-side roads share a trunk before they fork.

A third, smaller fix: the dish is round now, so tips turn back at the glass instead of wandering past the rim. The table has the sweep. The share rule is a dropdown on the page, so you can switch back to the cycle 8 rule and hear the difference.
