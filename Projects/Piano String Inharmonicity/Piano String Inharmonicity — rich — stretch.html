Where the partials go

A string at A3, 220 Hz, made stiff by one number, B. We start with one partial and end on the section's claim that the stretch grows with the cube of the partial number. Silent until you press a button.

One partial

B is the string's stiffness as a single number; B = 0 is a perfectly flexible string. Start with the first partial alone. Drag B across its whole range and watch how little it moves: less than two cents. It does move, though, because f₀ in the formula is the pitch the string would have with no stiffness at all, not quite the pitch you hear.

f₁ = 1 · f₀ · √(1 + B)
(the first partial) = (the stiffness-free fundamental) × the square root of (one plus the inharmonicity coefficient).

The whole row

Now twelve partials, each with a ghost line where a flexible string would put it, at n × 220 Hz. Raise B and the row fans out to the right: the low partials hardly move and the high ones run sharp. At the top of the slider the twelfth partial runs past the thirteenth's ghost.

fn = n · f₀ · √(1 + B · n²)
(the nth partial) = (the partial number) × (the fundamental) × the square root of (one plus the coefficient times the partial number squared).

B = 0 puts every partial on its ghost; the first partial is step 1.

A partial beats against its ghost

Play one partial together with its ghost, at equal level. They beat, and the beats per second are the gap between them in hertz: the stretch, made countable. Pick a partial, listen, then try the one with twice its number.

beats per second = fn − n · f₀ ≈ B · n³ · f₀ / 2
(how fast they beat) = (the partial) minus (its ghost), which for small stiffness is about (the coefficient) × (the partial number cubed) × (the fundamental) ÷ 2.

B = 0 and the two are one tone: no beats. Partial 1 is step 1 with its ghost beside it.

Is it a cube?

The section says the stretch is about B·n³·f₀/2, so it grows with the cube of n: double the partial number and the stretch should grow eight times. Here the exact formula is measured against that, on a log–log plot, where a cube is a straight line of slope 3. The table reads the slope between doublings. The button plays partials slow enough to count, each double the last, against their ghosts, so you can hear the eightfold jumps.
