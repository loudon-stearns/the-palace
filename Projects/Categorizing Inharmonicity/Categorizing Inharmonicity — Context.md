## The stretched-tuning fix

On 2026-10-24 the weave rewrote § 1 Stretched Tuning (`a75350e`). The reason was a factual slip, not a matter of style. Three pages disagreed about why a stiff string is inharmonic. This page said "restoring force increases with displacement (nonlinear potential)". [[Piano String Inharmonicity]] and [[Quantum Synthesizer]] both say the stretch comes from a linear stiffness term (`_ops/swarm/sessions/weave-2026-10-24/coherence-findings.json`). The finding called it a slip, not a productive tension, and said not to retype any link to `contradicts`.

What went with the fix:

- **The physical-origins list.** Stiff strings as a nonlinear potential, bending coupled to tension in beams, and geometric nonlinearity in prestressed membranes were replaced by one line of origin.
- **The formula.** The small-B form `1 + B·n²/2 + higher order terms` was replaced by the `√(1 + B·n²)` form that Piano String uses.
- **The synthesis-mechanism list.** Four routes went: a polynomial in n, distorted oscillator potentials, pitch-dependent FM, and the cubic spring `k·x + λ·x³`. The finding had proposed keeping the cubic spring on a line of its own, as a different effect: amplitude-dependent pitch. The writer did not keep it, and it has no other home.
- **"Drift inward".** The perceptual line had the upper partials drifting inward. They drift sharp.

The fix is half done. The Quantum Synthesizer bullet under Cross-Domain Resonance still says the quantum potentials are "the same nonlinear springs that produce stretched tuning in acoustic strings". The writer saw it and left it (`_ops/swarm/sessions/weave-2026-10-24/writers/log-set-3.md:62`). It is the next edit this page is owed.

The same pass deleted the closing italic quote, which had no attribution, and annotated the Interpolation question as progressed by Quantum Synthesizer's round trip (`a75350e`). A week earlier the Kuramoto question had been given a pointer to the 2026-10-17 sweeps. That commit says the question itself "stays open as written" (`5769a93`).
