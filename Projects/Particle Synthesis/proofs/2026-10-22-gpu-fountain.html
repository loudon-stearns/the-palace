The fountain on the GPU

Particle Synthesis · 2026-10-22 · up to 1024 particles, each one thread, each one moved and heard at 48,000 steps a second

press start

What is running. A compute shader gives every particle its own thread. For every sample of audio the thread moves its particle one step under gravity and air drag, and when the particle hits the ice it starts one of four chirp voices that the same thread keeps singing. A second pass adds all the particles together. Nothing on the CPU knows where any particle is; it only receives finished sound.

The switch. Both modes use the same particles, the same throws, the same strikes. Heard through the ice lets each strike travel through a stiff plate to two pickups, so the highs arrive first and the lows trail, and a far strike sweeps slower than a near one. Mapped grains is the ordinary way: each strike triggers a little decaying sine whose pitch is set by how far away it landed. Same information, chosen by a rule instead of falling out of a law.
