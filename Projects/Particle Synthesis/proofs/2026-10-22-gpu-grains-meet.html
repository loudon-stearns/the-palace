Grains that meet, on the GPU

Particle Synthesis · 2026-10-22 · two fountains aimed at each other · up to 1024 ice grains, each one thread · every pair checked for contact 1,500 times a second

press start

What is running. Two nozzles 3 m apart, 8 m from the pickups, throw ice grains at each other in pairs. Every grain is one GPU thread that moves its grain once per audio sample. Every 32 samples the threads stop, and each grain checks itself against every other grain in the air. If two are touching and still closing, each takes its half of the bounce. That pass is where the 1024 threads stop being separate. A grain that gets hit sings the clack itself: its own shove, heard as a small loudspeaker in the air. When the two grains' sounds add up, the line of impact is loud and the side is quiet. Nobody wrote that pattern in.

The aim knob is the scatter on every throw, from 6° down to 0.1°. Loose aim and the streams cross like rain. Tight aim and the grains meet, deflect, land somewhere new, and sometimes knock the next grain too. Pass through makes exactly the same throws with collisions switched off.
