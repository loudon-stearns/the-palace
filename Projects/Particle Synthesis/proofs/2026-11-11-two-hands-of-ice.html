Two hands of ice

Particle Synthesis · 2026-11-11 · the GPU fountain made playable · every key throws real grains; the sound is where and how they land

press start, then play the three rows of your keyboard, or a MIDI keyboard

How to play. There are two nozzles, 3 m apart, aimed at each other. The bottom row of your keyboard (Z to /) throws from the left nozzle, the top row (Q to P) from the right, and the middle row (A to ;) from both at the same instant. A key sets how hard the throw is, from 4.2 m/s on the left of the row to 8 m/s on the right. Hold a key and it pours. On a MIDI keyboard, the octave from C3 throws left, the octave from C4 throws from both, the octave from C5 throws right. How hard you strike sets the handful.

What you hear is only the landing. Nothing sounds when you press. A grain flies for half a second to a second and a half, then strikes the ice, and the ice carries the strike to the pickups as a chirp, highs first. A harder throw lands at a different distance, so its chirp sweeps differently. The delay between your finger and the sound is the flight. That's the instrument's latency, and it's physics, not lag.

The middle row is the one that meets. Two grains thrown at the same speed from both nozzles at the same instant arrive in the middle together and clack, then fall somewhere new. Two fingers can do it too, sometimes, and the arc decides when. Throw a middle key (about 6 m/s) and the two grains cross near the top of their arc, where they are hardly falling, so a 20 ms gap between your fingers still meets. Throw the softest key and they cross on the way down at more than 3 m/s, so 20 ms apart puts one grain about 7 cm below the other and they miss, and at 10 ms only the bigger grains still touch. The middle row leaves both on the same sample, so it always meets. A handful from the middle row is a chain: the leading pair meets, bounces back into the grains following it, and three grains a side make five clacks inside 20 ms, only one of them between partners. Tighten the aim to hear more meetings; loosen it and the streams cross like rain.

The pickups knob moves the two pickups toward the landings. Closer, the chirps get shorter and brighter, because the ice has had less distance to spread the strike out by frequency. Record keeps everything you play and hands it back as a WAV.
