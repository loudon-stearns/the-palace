One plane

The section builds the instrument on one plane: a field of waveforms underfoot, a flock walking it, every bird a grain reading the waveform beneath it. Here that plane is built up one layer at a time, ending on the section's claim about the Cohesion knob, tried on the page's own flock. Nothing plays until you press sound.

Step 1 of 4 · a field of waveforms

Every point on the plane is one waveform

Start with the ground alone. Each corner holds a single cycle of sound: sine at top left, saw at top right, square at bottom left, an inharmonic bell at bottom right. Every point between is a blend of the four, mixed by how near it sits to each, so moving across the plane changes the timbre smoothly. Drag across the field and hear the point under your finger as a steady tone.

Step 2 of 4 · a bird reads the ground

A bird is a stream of grains reading the waveform under it

Release one bird. It plays the waveform under it, chopped into short grains, each with a smooth rise and fall. Where it is sets the timbre. How high on the plane sets the pitch: the top is higher, across a span of 19 semitones. How fast it flies sets how many grains a second. This is the mapping the section describes, the instrument's first build. The bird here flies alone, speeding up and slowing down, so only the ground and its own pace change what you hear.

Step 3 of 4 · sixty birds, sixty grain voices

Where the flock is, is what the cloud is

Now sixty birds flocking, each the bird from step 2. Nobody sets the cloud's spread of pitches or timbres. It is wherever the flock happens to be: spread over the plane, many pitches and many waveforms at once; gathered in one spot, few. The bars at the right count the birds at each pitch, which is the cloud's pitch spread, read straight off the flock. This flock runs at the instrument's defaults: cohesion 1.0, separation 1.5, disorder η 0.06.

Step 4 of 4 · the test

Does Cohesion morph a pitched tone into a broadband cloud?

The section says it does: clustered birds read nearby waveforms at nearby pitches, one tone; scattered birds read waveforms all over the plane, a cloud; and the morph is a flocking parameter, not a crossfade. Here is the Cohesion knob over the instrument's whole range, 0 to 3. The curves are the page's flock settled at each setting, four starts each, measured: how spread the flock is on the plane, and how spread its pitches are. The dot is the live flock.
