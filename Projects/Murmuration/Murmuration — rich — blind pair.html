The blind pair

The project bets that a grain cloud grown by a flock sounds more alive than one dialed in by hand, and says the honest test is blind. Here are the two takes its steward rendered for that test: one performance, the same 60 grains, the same mapping. One take is the flock. The other copies from the flock everything a hand could copy. The key stays shut until you answer. Nothing plays until you press play.

Step 1 of 4 · one take, and what it follows

Forty seconds: melted, gathering, ordered, a scare, melting again

Start with one take alone, and the score both takes follow. The grey line is the Disorder slider. The bright line is φ, how much the flock agrees on a heading: near 0.1 when every bird goes its own way, near 1 when they all fly together. In this mapping a bird's heading is its pitch, one octave around, and a bird held steady by its neighbours sings long grains while a tumbling one clicks. So as φ climbs, listen for the cloud to gather toward one pitch.

Step 2 of 4 · the other take

Copy everything you can measure, and leave out who listens to whom

The other take is the hard version of a hand-dialed cloud. Frame by frame, sixty times a second, it is handed the flock's numbers and told to match them, each grain wandering on its own smooth random path. What it copies, and what it can't:

where the cloud's centre is
how far it spreads, and in which direction
how fast the birds go, and how much that varies
how much they agree on a heading, φ
the mix of long singing grains and short clicks
which bird steers by which neighbour

Step 3 of 4 · one switch

The same moment, either take, at the press of a key

Both takes now run together and the switch chooses which you hear, so you compare the same second of the performance, not your memory of it. They were matched in loudness before they were written: one take was turned down by 0.36 dB to meet the other.

Step 4 of 4 · the test

Which one is alive?

Answer from the listening you just did. "Can't tell" is a real answer. Once you choose, the key opens: which take was the flock, what Loudon answered when the steward asked him the same question, and what a measurement of the two takes shows.
