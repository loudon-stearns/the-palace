The logo, heard

The Lissajous-in-sphere is three oscillators drawn instead of played. Here they are played too, on the same clock as the drawing.

What you are looking at

Three voices, one figure

Each axis of the trace is one oscillator. Left–right is voice a, up–down is voice b, and how near the trace is to you (brightness and weight) is voice c.

x = sin(a·t)   y = sin(b·t + δ)   z = sin(c·t + 2δ)
(left–right) = sin((ratio a) × (time)) · (up–down) = sin((ratio b) × (time) + (phase offset)) · (depth) = sin((ratio c) × (time) + 2 × (phase offset))

The logo uses a : b : c = 3 : 4 : 6. As a chord, 3 to 4 is a fourth and 3 to 6 is an octave: an open, suspended sound with nothing pulling it anywhere.

Why it moves

The drift is a beat

The canonical sketch does not use 3, 4 and 6. It uses 3.011, 4.013 and 6.007, "slightly off integers so the figure precesses." Played, that same small mistuning makes the chord beat. Here the figure is drawn on the sound's own clock, so the shape wobbles at the rate the chord beats.

beat rate = |2 × a − c| × f₀
(how often the near-octave pulses per second) = |2 × (ratio a) − (ratio c)| × (fundamental)

Press Snap to whole. The figure freezes into a closed knot and the chord goes still. Whatever life the logo has, it gets from being a little out of tune.

The question for the page

Does the sigil teach?

Loudon Live says the public self is itself a teaching artifact, and the Prism essay says the logo reads at four depths, starting with "an oscilloscope trace." The live sketch has never made a sound. If hearing it is what makes the first depth land, the smallest public thing Loudon owns could be the first proof of the biggest claim.
