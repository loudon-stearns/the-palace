Bayesian Granular Synthesizer · the grain spec

Every grain carries the same energy

The spec sets each grain's amplitude in proportion to √(f ⁄ cycles), and says that without it long low grains dominate the mix. Three steps: one grain, then two set against each other, then the whole cloud with a bass note held, rendered both ways and measured.

1 · One grain, and what it weighs

A grain's energy is how loud it is, squared, times how long it lasts. Its length is counted in cycles of its own pitch, so a 128-cycle grain at A1 lasts more than two seconds and a 4-cycle grain at C7 lasts three milliseconds. Hold amplitude fixed and the long one carries hundreds of times the energy. The rule makes amplitude pay for length: every grain lands on one diagonal of equal energy.

(the grain's amplitude) grows with the square root of its pitch over its length in cycles, so (its loudness squared) × (how long it lasts, N cycles of 1 ⁄ f seconds) comes out the same for every grain

The engine divides by N + 1, counting the two one-cycle fades as one cycle of full level; their true weight is 0.75 of a cycle. That leaves a 4-cycle grain 0.2 dB under a 128-cycle one, which the numbers above include. "One amplitude for all" gives every grain the amplitude the rule gives a 4-cycle A4.

2 · A long low grain against a short high one

Take the two extremes a believing cloud makes: a long grain on a held bass note, A2 at 128 cycles, 1.2 s; and a click far from any note, C6 at 4 cycles, 6 ms. With one amplitude for both, the bass grain carries about 250 times the energy. With the rule, the two weigh the same, and the click is as loud as it needs to be to count.

3 · The test: hold a bass note under a chord

Now the whole cloud, sixteen seconds: two of the prior alone, then A2, E4 and C5 held. Rendered twice with the same seed, so the same grains land at the same pitches with the same lengths: once with the rule, once with one amplitude for all, set so the two clouds are equally loud while nothing is held. The belief gives each held note about a third of the grains. Does each get about a third of the sound?

Rendering the two takes…

Energy share is the power within 50 cents of each note, both channels, Hann windows of 1.37 s; grain share counts grains landing within 50 cents. "Below 250 Hz" is everything under B3. The match button plays the two takes at one loudness over the held chord, so you hear their balance; off, they play at one gain, matched over the first two seconds, so you hear the jump. Energy is not loudness: the ear needs more energy at 110 Hz than at 500 Hz to hear the same loudness, so to an ear the rule leaves a bass note a little under its share. This piece measures energy. The engine is bayes_engine.js, the October rebuild from this spec, not the lost V8.
