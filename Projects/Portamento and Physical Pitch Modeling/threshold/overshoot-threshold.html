Find your overshoot threshold

Portamento and Physical Pitch Modeling · a ten-minute listening test
A singer who lands sharp and settles is an underdamped spring: the pitch overshoots its note and rings back. Two questions the page has carried for months: how small an overshoot can you hear at all? and where does an overshoot stop sounding alive and start sounding out of tune? This page measures both, for your ears. Use headphones at a comfortable level.

1 · Can you hear it?

You hear two glides up a fifth, A then B. One lands clean. The other overshoots and rings back. Both arrive at the same speed, so the overshoot is the only difference. Pick the one that overshoots. The overshoot gets smaller when you are right twice in a row and bigger when you miss. About 40 trials.

2 · Alive or sour?

Now every glide overshoots, by amounts from just above your threshold to a third of a fifth. For each, say whether it sounds alive (energetic, a landing with body) or sour (out of tune). No right answers. Each level comes twice, shuffled.
