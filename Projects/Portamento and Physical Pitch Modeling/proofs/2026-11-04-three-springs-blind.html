Three Springs, Blind

ROUGH · DAILY PAGE 2026-11-04 · PORTAMENTO AND PHYSICAL PITCH MODELING

The page says a glide is a spring settling on a note, and that its three regimes are three sonic characters any student can name by ear after one session. Nobody has tried. This takes about five minutes.

0 · Hear the three first (not scored)

Each one glides up a fifth on the same spring. Only the damping changes.

1 · Name it — as the page states it

Nine glides, up or down, a fourth or a fifth, from different notes. The spring's stiffness wanders a little, so speed alone is not the answer, but the page's regimes are used as written (same spring, damping 2.5, 1, 0.3). Play as often as you like, then name it.

2 · The harder question — is it shape, or only speed?

Six glides, overdamped or critical only, but now each pair is matched so both reach 90% of the way in the same time. If you can still tell them apart, the page's "three characters" are three. If not, there are really two: it rings, or it doesn't, at some speed.

3 · Your own voice

The page says singers "overshoot slightly when landing on a note from above — they arrive sharp and then settle." On the page's own spring, a landing from above that overshoots arrives flat. Hear the model, then sing it.

The sung answer is not used as a reading (Loudon, 2026-11-09).
