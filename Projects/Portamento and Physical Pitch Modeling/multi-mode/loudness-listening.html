The bow sets how much of the tone holds together

In the last study every bowed clip had one fixed pull holding its partials onto the harmonic series. But that pull isn't a property of the string. It comes from the bow, and a harder bow pulls harder. So I let the locking range follow the bow: about 0.5 Hz at pianissimo, 30 Hz at fortissimo. Partials whose stiffness pushes them further off than that ring free and shimmer; partials inside it fuse into one tone.

K(t) = Kpp · (Kff / Kpp)g(t)  ·  partial n locked while Δn < K
locking_range(t) = range_at_pp · (range_at_ff / range_at_pp)^bow_force(t) · partial n holds while its detuning < locking_range

The pair · which one fuses as it swells?

Both are the same held E4 with the identical crescendo, level for level, from −24 dB to full. In one the locking follows the bow; in the other it stays frozen at its pianissimo value. Listen to the last second.

B follows the bow. As it swells, partials 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are pulled into lock one at a time, at 1.36, 2.13, 2.63, 3.06 and 3.56 s, from the bottom up. By the top the waveform repeats itself almost exactly (0.96 match a hundred cycles later). A is the control: same loudness, nothing new locks, and it still shimmers at full volume (0.79). The difference you hear is not loudness. It's fusion, and here fusion is what loudness does to a bowed tone.

The overshoot at three bow strengths

The underdamped arrival from the last study, G3 to E4 with ζ 0.2, peaking at 400 Hz, played soft, medium and loud. All three are level-matched so you compare texture, not volume.

Soft bow locking range 1 Hz · partial 2 slips, −6 dB The slip lands low, on the second partial: it lets go from 0.82 to 1.31 s and again from 1.93 to 2.31 s. The ring-back is right in the body of the tone.
Medium bow locking range 3.4 Hz · partial 3 slips, −9.5 dB The last study's clip, rebuilt by the new script as a check: the same partial slips at the same times, to within a hundredth of a second.
Loud bow locking range 30 Hz · partial 6 slips once, −15.6 dB Six partials hold through the arrival. Only the sixth lets go, once, from 0.84 to 1.27 s; the second ring doesn't reach it.

So a louder bow doesn't erase the texture cue of an overshoot. It pushes it up the spectrum, onto a weaker partial, and shortens it. For the ear-training: teach the texture cue with a soft bow.
