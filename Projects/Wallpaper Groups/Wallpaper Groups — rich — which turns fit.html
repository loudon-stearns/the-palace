Which turns can a repeating pattern hold?

The crystallographic restriction, played: turn a lattice and count the points that land, try the fifth-turn, find the one condition every turn must meet, then count what is left. Silent; nothing here makes sound.

points within 2.6
v+ + v− = 2 · cos(360° ⁄ n) · v  ⇒  2 · cos(360° ⁄ n) ∈ {−2, −1, 0, 1, 2}  ⇒  n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}
(the shortest step turned one way) + (the same step turned the other way) = (a whole lattice step lying along the shortest one, 2·cos of the turn times as long); a whole step along the shortest step is a whole number of them, and that number lies between −2 and 2; so the turn is a full turn, a half, a third, a quarter or a sixth.
Points are counted within 3.2 cell widths of the centre. The seventeen in the last step are drawn by the same engine as the piece in the Prism Machine section, from one tile. Counting by the section's kinds reads a glide as a reflection; read any other way, its four bins can't hold all seventeen.
