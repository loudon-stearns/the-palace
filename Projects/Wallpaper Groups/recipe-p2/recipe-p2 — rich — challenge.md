## First, the page's own situation

The maker's whole face refutes the page, so I rebuilt what the note says before judging anything.

- **One voice.** Ingredients (:9–11) give one cycle of 16 positions and a centre between 8 and 9. No second voice or pitch row appears anywhere. Step 4's held tone (:18) "does not transform".
- **The rule.** Step 2 (:16) says "mirror each hit's position *and* invert its placement", then gives the rule n → (n + 8) mod 16.
- **The note describes a slide three times.** Step 3 (:17) says "a displaced copy". :22 says it "echoes itself at a distance … returns in the second half, but starting from a different phase position". Only the labels are about rotation: p2, "180°", "no mirror axis" (:3), and a centre between 8 and 9 (:11).
- **"Invert its placement" has no single meaning in one voice.** I found three readings, and none needs a second voice.
  - The note's own "(or 8 steps doubled)" (:9) lays the loop out as two rows of eight. Mirror the position inside the eight, then invert which row: n → 17 − n. That is recipe-pm's map exactly.
  - Two reflections, first about 8|9 and then about 12|13, compose to n + 8. On this reading the words and the formula agree, and both describe a slide.
  - The maker's reading: placement is a second direction, low and high. That gives the true two-dimensional half-turn, which is distinct from pm.

**The note does not support the two-voice reading.** It is the maker's repair, and a good one, but it is not what the note already says. So the maker's one-voice tests do test the note's claims, and offers 1–3 stand as findings.

Their proposed texts are one of several possible fixes. The reason they stand is that on one voice no reading of the words gives "a 180° rotation with no mirror axis". The parent face's challenge said the same thing from its strip piece: "a single line can't tell a half-turn from a reversal".

## The pieces

### the recipe, cooked (Method): remake

- **Test or decoration?** Step 4 is the real finding, shown by construction. Turn the clock 180° and you get n + 8. Turn the strip about 8|9 and you get 17 − n. The test set before step 5 can't fail, though. "It is a slide if it makes the half played twice on every one" is the definition of n + 8: S ∪ (S + 8) is the half played twice for every S. Three more tallies are identities rather than measurements: "126 of 126" (17 − n equals pm's map), "1568 of 1568" (two-voice half-turn differs from pm) and "every half-turn lands on itself turned". Only the period null (pm reads 16 in 120) and the 1540 p2 count could have come out otherwise.
- **Faithful?** Not quite. The sub-line, step 5 ("the second direction the recipe's own words ask for") and the caption ("as 'invert its placement' asks") say the note asks for a second voice. It doesn't (see above).
- **Walks up?** Yes. The half, then the rule, then pm beside it, then two pictures of 180°, then the test.
- **Runs clean.** No AudioContext before the click. Play gives `running`. Hide gives `suspended`, and it stays suspended on return. Pressing play in the other piece hushes this one.

**Remake, exactly:**
1. Reword the sub-line, step 5's text and the caption so the second voice is one reading of "invert its placement". Show the one-voice reading from the note's own ":9 8 steps doubled": mirror inside the eight and swap the halves, which gives 17 − n, pm's map.
2. Replace the step-5 rule with tests that could fail on the note's own words:
   - Does the cooked loop have the Ingredients' centre (a half-turn about 8|9, which on one voice is n → 17 − n)? Yes in **6 of 126**.
   - Is there "no mirror axis" (:3)? It fails in **62 of 126**.
   - Keep the period null.
3. Label the identities as identities, not counts.
4. Optional: add a fourth map to step 5, the glide (n + 8 with the voices swapped). It has the same voice change as the half-turn with no reversal, so the ear can tell "another voice answers" apart from "it turns". As built, the half-turn is the only map that changes pitch at the half.

### the break and the clave (Where to break it): remake

- **Test or decoration?** A test with two arms, and the rule is stated first. The recipe arm is right and can't be otherwise: a cooked loop has 6 or 8 hits, a break keeps the count, and a clave has 5. The mirror arm has a working null: 4 of the 6 tresillo breaks miss a mirror by one pair.
- **Faithful?** The step-4 verdict, "The clave lives by pm, not here", says more than the arms show. The arms compare a **slide** with a **mirror**, not p2 with pm. On one voice, a strip half-turn about step 4 is the same move as the mirror through 4 and 12. So by p2's group definition (not the rule's), the son clave is an exact one-voice half-turn, and the rumba clave "breaks the rotation slightly". That makes :24 true on that reading.
- **recipe-pm can't cook the clave either.** Its mirror sits between 8 and 9 and pairs every hit, so it always makes an even count. One break of any recipe-pm loop reaches either clave in **0 of 7840** tries. The son clave's line runs *through* one of its own steps (step 4). Neither recipe's centre allows that: the nearest between-step mirror splits 3 pairs for the son clave.
- **The section's break move is real.** Write the son clave as 2-3, 3 5 9 12 15. Move the second-half hit 15 to 16 and you get the rumba clave as 2-3, 3 5 9 12 16. That is ":28 Break one hit in the second half … to something adjacent", exactly. What fails is the loop the break starts from: no recipe can cook it.
- **Walks up?** Yes. Cooked, then broken, then the clave beside it, then the test.
- **Runs clean.** Same results as the first piece.

**Remake, exactly:**
1. Rewrite the step-4 verdict. Neither recipe's centre, which sits between steps, can make a five-hit clave. The son clave is symmetric about a line through one of its own steps: a mirror, which on one voice is also a half-turn. The rumba clave is the section's own break made on it (2-3: 15 → 16).
2. Add a third arm: the nearest mirror *between* steps, the centre both recipes use. Son clave **3**, rumba **1**.
3. Drop "lives by pm, not here".

**Loudness, "beside it" (step 3).** The claves have one hit fewer per bar. That is not a leak. Nothing is identified blind, the hit count is the parity point itself, and the hint says so. Per-hit level is identical (the same `wood()` call). Adjacent hits overlap slightly in the compressor (threshold −14, 4:1) on two of the six breaks, which is negligible.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt in one place: step 4 of the first piece, where the same 180° lands two ways depending on the picture. Seeing that the recipe's turn is a clock's turn, and so a slide in time, teaches more than the page's five paragraphs. Everywhere else the face is too sure of its own repair. It treats the second voice as what the note meant, and the mirror as where the clave belongs. The note supports neither. On one voice, rotation and reflection are one move, and the honest difference between p2 and pm here comes down to where the centre sits and whether a second direction exists. Both pieces are sound underneath. They need their words, not their engines, changed. They are not busy, and they share one look. Taken together, the four offers are one rewrite: decide whether p2 gets a second voice, and the rest follows.
