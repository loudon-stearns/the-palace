The bell beside the spin

One onset on a twelve-step clock, copied by the Method into everything it can make. Then the mirrors those loops carry, and the bell the section names beside them. Last, a measure of how near the bell sits to p6. Sound starts only when you press play; changing step stops it.

(how near a pattern is to p6) is (the fewest steps you would add or take away to reach a loop the Method can cook): 1 3 5 7 9 11, 2 4 6 8 10 12, or all twelve. A sixth of a turn on the clock is a slide of two steps in time, so p6's loops repeat every two steps.
100 BPM, sixteenth-note steps, numbered 1 to 12. One wooden tone at one level for every stroke; the loops differ in how many strokes they have (5, 6, 7 or 12), so the denser ones are louder per bar. A quiet tick marks step 1 of every twelve only; the tick button turns it off.
