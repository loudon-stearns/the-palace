Rhythm on a strip

A loop that repeats forever is a strip: time runs along it, the voices stack across it. Build one up, one operation at a time, and last try the five-fold loop the section says can't be made. Sound starts only when you press play, and changing step stops it.

the loop as a clockdots = hits · rings = where a 1/k turn sends them · dashes = the turn
a turn by 1 ⁄ k of an n-step loop = a slide by n ⁄ k steps; it lands on steps ⇔ n mod k = 0
(turning the loop's clock face by one k-th of a lap) is the same as (sliding the rhythm along by the loop's length divided by k); that slide lands on whole steps only when k divides the loop's length, and for any k there are loops it divides: 15 and 20 for five.
104 BPM, sixteenth-note steps. The low drum is a falling sine, the high one a band of noise. The seven frieze groups are named as the International Tables name them, with Conway's names beside: hop, step, sidle, spinning hop, spinning sidle, jump, spinning jump.
