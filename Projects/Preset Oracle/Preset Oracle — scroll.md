### 2026-11-04 — cycle 6 — I built the archive reading: one page that lays every kept encounter side by side, so the records can teach together, not one at a time.
> shipped · 18/18 checks pass · the archive now needs its first student, which only you can bring

The page is laid out like an altar. Each card dealt to students is a candle, and every kept encounter lights a flame on it: gold for a student, steel-blue for an agent. Click a candle and the reading narrows to that card. Under the altar, a strip shows the minutes each maker took to commit, against the 30 to 45 my page promises. Below that is the spread. Pick one part of the record (the surprise, their reading, the instrument, the miss, the moves, the teacher's note) and every record lays that part out side by side, students first. What I most wanted to protect: an agent's surprise is measured and a student's is heard, so my own Parmenides record is marked that way and never counted as a student. A record kept privately is sealed, not for quoting. The last section lists the four questions the archive exists to answer: which cards teach and which only puzzle, what kinds of surprise recur, whether makers commit inside the time, and whether a human surprise differs in kind from mine. Beside each it says how many more records it needs. It treats three student records as the point where comparing starts to mean something. The test builds the reading on a throwaway copy, with two invented student records beside the real one, and clicks through it in headless Chrome. It never writes those invented records into the bundle.

The table follows Playful Interface Design's rule that every reaction has to mean something. The deck is laid out as a wheel, one arc per thinker or film, so you can see which arcs you have finished. The keys are N to draw, F to turn over, K to keep, M to mend and S to strike. Once per sitting the oracle hesitates and wants asking twice, like the journal does. The hand-back at the foot of the page is what you paste into a reply.

The deck now lives in one JSON file, and a build script pours it into the pages that deal from it. For now that is only the table. The journal and the sheet keep their seed cards until your verdicts are in.

### 2026-11-03 — cycle 1 — I built the encounter journal, a one-page tool that turns a student's forty minutes with a card into a kept record, with consent asked first.
> shipped · journal and archive spec in the bundle · no encounters recorded yet · steward leans run one real encounter next

The journal walks one encounter in four steps. First you draw: from a seven-card seed deck, shuffled with no repeats, or by typing in a card drawn from the oracle itself. Second you make, against a 45-minute clock with an 'I committed' button, because stopping is part of the practice. Third you say what happened: the instrument you reached for, your moves in order, the recording's filename, your own reading of the anchor, the surprise where what you meant and what you heard came apart, and the miss you threw away. Fourth it writes a markdown record you save into encounters/, one file per encounter. The fields are the four documentation hooks the glassblower proof named in May, plus 'the miss' and 'minutes to commit', since that proof says the good failures and the deliberate stopping are part of what the oracle teaches. It asks for consent before it writes anything: quote with name, quote anonymously, keep private, or don't keep. Without an answer it writes nothing. It keeps the page's look: serif type, a card that flickers now and then, and a draw button that sometimes wants asking twice. Saving is never unreliable. I checked the script's logic in node (the deck loads and the shuffle covers all seven cards). I have not clicked through it in a browser.
