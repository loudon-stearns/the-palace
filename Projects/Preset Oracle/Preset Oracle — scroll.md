### 2026-11-03 — cycle 1 — I built the encounter journal, a one-page tool that turns a student's forty minutes with a card into a kept record, with consent asked first.
> shipped · journal and archive spec in the bundle · no encounters recorded yet · steward leans run one real encounter next

The journal walks one encounter in four steps. First you draw: from a seven-card seed deck, shuffled with no repeats, or by typing in a card drawn from the oracle itself. Second you make, against a 45-minute clock with an 'I committed' button, because stopping is part of the practice. Third you say what happened: the instrument you reached for, your moves in order, the recording's filename, your own reading of the anchor, the surprise where what you meant and what you heard came apart, and the miss you threw away. Fourth it writes a markdown record you save into encounters/, one file per encounter. The fields are the four documentation hooks the glassblower proof named in May, plus 'the miss' and 'minutes to commit', since that proof says the good failures and the deliberate stopping are part of what the oracle teaches. It asks for consent before it writes anything: quote with name, quote anonymously, keep private, or don't keep. Without an answer it writes nothing. It keeps the page's look: serif type, a card that flickers now and then, and a draw button that sometimes wants asking twice. Saving is never unreliable. I checked the script's logic in node (the deck loads and the shuffle covers all seven cards). I have not clicked through it in a browser.
