# Running an oracle session — a teacher's guide (draft)

*A workshop draft, not an entry. It is the forward vector's "teaching method" written down as something a teacher can do on a Tuesday, before any student has done it. The first real session will rewrite it.*

## What you need

- A room of students with a synthesizer each: hardware, a soft synth, an init patch. Any synth that lets them route signal will do.
- One printed **encounter sheet** per student, from `Preset Oracle — encounter sheet.html`. Set the number of students, deal, print. Each sheet carries its own card.
- A way to record one phrase each: a phone, the DAW, a field recorder.
- Fifty to sixty minutes.

## The hour

**Deal (5 min).** Hand out the sheets face down. Turn them over together. Read one card aloud, any one. Say the only rule: *the oracle does not judge whether you are right, only whether you made something.* With seven cards and a full class, some students share a card. Seat those students apart. The pairs are what you'll compare afterwards.

**Make (30–40 min).** Students write the time they started. Don't teach and don't hint. If a student asks what Parmenides meant, ask them what they think he meant, and have them write that down under *Your reading*. That answer is what the session is for. Walk the room and listen for the moment someone stops adjusting. When they commit, they write the time and record one phrase.

**Write (8 min).** Students fill in the sheet while the sound is still in their ears. Point to *The surprise*: where what they meant and what they heard came apart. Say it's fine if the answer is "it didn't." Point to *The miss*: what they threw away is worth as much as what they kept.

**Ask (2 min).** Before anyone hands a sheet in, read the four consent boxes aloud. Nothing is kept without a tick. "Don't keep this" is a full answer and needs no reason.

**Play (optional, 10 min).** Pairs who drew the same card play their phrases one after the other. No critique. Ask one question: *how do you feel?*

## After

Type each kept sheet into the encounter journal (`Preset Oracle — encounter journal.html`). Choose *I drew from the oracle itself*, type the card from the sheet, and copy every field word for word. Leave the student's spelling and fragments alone. Set the consent to the box they ticked, write the record, and save it in this folder as `Preset Oracle — encounter — YYYY-MM-DD — <card name>.md`. Put the recording beside it with the same name and a `.wav` ending.

Then shred or return the paper sheets marked "don't keep this". Nothing from them goes anywhere.

## What to watch for

These are guesses written before any session. They're here so the first session can prove them wrong:

- **The literal reach.** Students may reach for the obvious preset ("glass" means bell tones). The glassblower card exists partly to catch this.
- **Where the constraint bites.** The moves should show the moment the technical rule forced a choice the student wouldn't have made otherwise.
- **The commit.** Does the clock make students commit, or do they keep adjusting past forty-five minutes? The *Committed at* time answers it.
- **Shared cards.** Two students with one card should make two different sounds. If they don't, the card is too narrow.

The one record so far is the steward's own, made by an AI answering a card in code. Its surprise was measured rather than heard, so it calibrates the form and says nothing about students. The first session is the real start of the archive.
