How long does the delay have to be?

The plan says the latency is musical: seconds of phrase-delay, not zero-latency monitoring, with 8 seconds as the honest minimum. This walks one phrase through the plugin's timing, holds the arithmetic against two real runs, then sets the page's 8 seconds against every speed the bundle has measured since April.

A phrase has to end first

The plugin can't send what you haven't finished singing. It listens for 0.3 seconds of quiet, and only then knows the phrase is over and sends it on, quiet and all. It cuts any phrase at 8 seconds, and a phrase cut there goes with no quiet. Play the first phrase of the helper's first real run: 6.4 seconds of SoulX's example vocal.

Seconds of work

Then the model sings it in another voice, and that takes longer than the phrase itself. How much longer is its speed: seconds of work for each second of audio. Pick one the bundle measured. The plugin has to hand the host a buffer every few milliseconds, so the model can't live inside it; it lives in its own process, and the plugin waits.

c = r · (L + 0.3)
The model's work (c, seconds) is its speed (r, seconds of work per second of audio) times what was sent: the phrase (L) and the gate's 0.3 s of quiet. Each speed was measured on one phrase length, mostly 4.5 s, and is stretched here to every length as a flat rate. The real runs go faster per second on longer phrases, so a part of the work is a fixed cost per echo; step 5 shows that fit beside the flat lines.

The delay counts from the end

Now the delay knob. In both the Python instrument and the plugin it counts from the moment the phrase was sent, the end of the gate, not from your first word. The echo is due then, and it plays if the model has finished. If it hasn't, the plugin plays it the moment it's ready, late, rather than clipping its start.

due = end + D  ·  heard after L + 0.3 + D  ·  on time when c ≤ D
The echo is due a delay (D) after the phrase was sent. From your first word you wait the phrase, the gate and the delay (a phrase cut at 8 s has no gate, so just the phrase and the delay). It lands on time only if the model's work fits inside the delay.

The test: is 8 seconds enough?

The page's own arithmetic: a 4-second phrase at April's speed, 1.65, is about 6.5 seconds of work, so a delay of 8 seconds or more is "musically workable", and 8 is "the honest minimum".
