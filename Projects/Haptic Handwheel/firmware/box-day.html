Haptic Handwheel — box day

What to do, in order, on the day the parts arrive: wire it, power it up safely, feel the first two feels, then let the bench measure the real motor. Nothing here has met hardware yet. It was written before the order, from the firmware and the parts list.

The day, in order

  1. Lay it out, nothing plugged in

    Motor, encoder lead, two drivers (one is a spare), the board, the capacitor, the supply, the printed caps. Clamp the block to the desk; the base must not move when the knob pushes back. Fit the motor to the block with no cap on.

  2. Meter the three motor leads

    Ohms across each pair: 1–2, 2–3, 1–3. The shop page says 15.2 Ω across two leads. Pass: all three within about 5 % of each other. Half your reading is the per-phase number --R the bench wants (about 7.6). If one pair reads open or very different, stop: a broken winding, and the motor goes back.

  3. Prepare the Arduino IDE

    Board: ESP32 Dev Module. This needs the esp32 by Espressif core (Boards Manager), version 2.x; it is not on this laptop yet. Library: Simple FOC, pinned to 2.3.3 (2.3.4 and later refuse the 2.x core). Open firmware/handwheel/handwheel.ino.

  4. Flash it over USB alone

    Board on USB, nothing else connected. Upload. Open the console below (Connect), or the serial monitor at 921600. Pass: it says hello handwheel ESP32-DevKitC and ready. With no motor power the alignment does nothing; that's expected.

  5. If it says nothing

    Press the board's reset button with the console connected. Check the baud is 921600. On the Nano, double-tap reset if the port vanished. Close the Arduino serial monitor before using the console or run_bench.py: only one program can hold the port.

  6. Wire the encoder, then turn it by hand

    Board unplugged, wire the six encoder lines, plug back in. In the console, turn the motor by hand. Pass: the dial follows you smoothly, one turn of the motor is one turn on the dial, and it reads about 6.28 rad a turn. If the angle is stuck at 0: CSn or MISO. If it jumps around: the lead is too long or lies against the motor wires.

  7. Power, carefully

    Board unplugged, supply output off. Wire driver to board, motor to driver, capacitor across VM and GND with the long leg on + (the stripe is −). Set the supply to 12 V and the current knob to 0.5 A. Plug in USB, then switch the supply output on, then press reset. Pass: a small twitch (the alignment), then ready, and the supply shows well under 0.3 A at rest. If the current pins at 0.5 A or the motor buzzes: output off, and check the phase wires and the grounds. Your hand on the supply's output button is the stop switch for the whole day.

  8. The first feel: the centring spring

    In the console press centre. Turn the knob away and let go. Pass: it comes back to centre without wobbling, landing within a degree or two, and pushes back harder the further you turn it. Near centre you'll feel a faint notch: that is the small push that carries it the last few degrees past friction and the motor's bumps (set kick 0 takes it away, and then it stops up to 18° short). If it runs away, or pushes you away from centre: the motor's direction is the wrong way round from the encoder's. Press stop, swap any two motor leads, reset.

  9. The second feel: frets

    Press frets. Twenty-four clicks a turn. You will also feel the motor's own 84 bumps a turn underneath, as a fine grain; the bench maps them next and the firmware cancels them. Let go between two clicks: it should fall into one.

  10. Optional: the turntable, before the bumps are mapped

    Press turntable and let it spin. It should read about 33.3 rpm over half a second, but with a wobble of about ±14%: the motor's bumps go by 47 times a second, and you will feel them as a buzz under the finger. That is expected until the bench maps them; once they are cancelled the wobble falls to about ±1%. To feel it now with less buzz, set kv to 0.1 and speedTf to 0.001 (the menu under the readings): in the model that cuts the wobble to a few percent (±3–6%), but it fights a finger harder and adds encoder fizz once the bumps are cancelled, so put them back to 0.01 and 0.005 after the bench. Judge the turntable's feel only after the bench.

  11. Let the bench measure the real motor

    Close the console's connection (Disconnect). In a terminal, from firmware/bench:
    /usr/local/bin/python3 run_bench.py --R 7.6 (your meter's half-reading in place of 7.6)
    It runs the five tests in order and stops twice to ask: once to fit the 40 g disc, once to start recording the contact mic in your DAW (save it as chirp_mic.wav beside the script). About three minutes. That Python is the one on this laptop with numpy and scipy. Rehearse it any time with --fake.

  12. Hand the numbers back

    The run ends by printing the motor's numbers, the set lines to paste into the console, a picture, and a new handwheel/cog_map.h. Upload the firmware once more so it cancels the bumps. Then tell the page; the next cycle retunes the feel lab to the real motor and the touch tests begin.
