Where does the middle of a crossfade go?

The page leaves a decision open: crossfading two anchor waveforms sample by sample drops the level in between, "up to ~3 dB for orthogonal anchors, ~10 dB for the worst negatively-correlated case (sine → rising sawtooth in the diagnostic)". Two anchors alone first, then the crossfade, then why it dips, then the cheapest fix. The last step measures the real diagnostic table, row by row.

law
W(α) = (1 − α)·W₁ + α·W₂
(the waveform partway across) = a share of the first anchor plus a share of the second, sample by sample.
RMS²(α) = (1 − α)²·RMS₁² + α²·RMS₂² + 2α(1 − α)·⟨W₁, W₂⟩
(its power) = each anchor's power, weighted, plus a cross term: the average of the two waveforms multiplied together. When that average is negative, the two partly cancel and the middle sinks.
target(α) = (1 − α)·RMS₁ + α·RMS₂   ·   constant-RMS: W(α) × target(α) ⁄ RMS(α)
(the level the catalog wants partway across) = a straight line between the two anchors' levels; the constant-RMS fix scales each row back up to it, changing gain and nothing else.
The anchors are the diagnostic's own (Tools/rebuild_diagnostic.py), one cycle each, 1024 samples, played at 110 Hz. RMS is a level, not loudness: a sawtooth sounds louder than a sine of the same RMS.
