the page's pairs
Wα = (1 − α)·WA + α·WB   ⇔   cα = (1 − α)·cA + α·cB
(a height-map crossfade) is the same thing as (crossfading every ripple as a complex number, size and angle together), because the Fourier series is linear. Where both surfaces hold a ripple at opposite angles, the two cancel on the way through.
|cα| = (1 − α)·|cA| + α·|cB|,   arg cα turns the short way from arg cA to arg cB
(the spectral crossfade, as the project's own morph tool does it) blends each ripple's size, and turns its angle, so nothing cancels.
level(α) = Σ |cα|² ⁄ Σ |cA|²,   both ends scaled to the same energy
(how loud the middle of the morph is) = the total energy of the blended ripples, over the energy at either end. Two surfaces that share no ripples dip exactly 3 dB at the middle under either law.
Each surface is its lattice from the catalog WAV (the ripples holding 99.5% of its energy), scaled to equal energy, and scanned at f₁ ⁄ f₂ = φ with f₂ = 110 Hz, the ratio the catalog's own auditions used. At a ratio no fraction names no two ripples share a frequency, so the level heard is the lattice's energy, whichever surface.
