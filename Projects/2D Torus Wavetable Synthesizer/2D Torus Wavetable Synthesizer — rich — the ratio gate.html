Is the gate a line?

The page says the ratio of the two scan rates is the instrument's inharmonicity gate: a simple ratio closes the scan into a knot and the tone comes out harmonic; a ratio no fraction names never closes and the tone comes out inharmonic. It also says the boundary between the two is a slope, not a line. Five steps, one idea each, and the last one tests that.

(what you hear) = the surface's height at the point the scan has reached, where the scan runs around one circle f₁ times a second and around the other f₂ times a second.
W = Σ cmn · ei(mφ₁ + nφ₂)   ⇒   partials at f = m·f₁ + n·f₂
(the surface) = a sum of ripples, each with a count m along one circle and n along the other; scanning puts every ripple at (m × the first rate) + (n × the second rate).
f₁ ⁄ f₂ = p ⁄ q   ⇒   f = (m·p + n·q) · f₂ ⁄ q
(when the ratio is a fraction in lowest terms) every partial is a whole-number multiple of f₂ ÷ q: harmonic, with the path closing after q turns of the second circle.
f₁ = (p ⁄ q)·f₂ + δ   ⇒   each partial moves by m·δ; partials that shared a harmonic beat at multiples of |q·f₁ − p·f₂|
(just off that fraction, by δ hertz) every partial slides by its count m times δ, so ripples that landed together drift apart at a rate that falls smoothly to zero as the ratio arrives.
Surfaces rebuilt from their lattices (the cmn holding 99.5% of each catalog table's energy) into a 512 × 512 table, read bilinearly, as the instrument reads its own. f₂ = 110 Hz from step 2 on.
