The Singer

One torus voice you play from a keyboard. The note you press is the note you hear, on any lock: change the lock and the colour changes while the pitch stays. Hold space (or the sustain pedal) to let go: the voice slides off its ratio and shimmers. Release to grab it back.

the solve · symbols and words

h = (a − b) / (a + b)
(height on the sphere) = (a − b) ÷ (a + b)
f₀ = 2 · base / (a + b)
(locked period) = 2 × (base) ÷ (a + b)
E(k) = Σ |cmn|² over m·b + n·a = ±k
(energy at harmonic k) = sum of (surface coefficient)² over every (m, n) whose (m × b + n × a) is ± k
k* = smallest k with E(k) ≥ 0.3 · max E
(heard partial) = the lowest harmonic that carries at least 30% of the loudest one
base = note · (a + b) / (2 · k*)
(base) = (the note you pressed) × (a + b) ÷ (2 × heard partial)
δ = √(slip² + ((a + b)·K)²) , Δh = δ / (base · (a + b))
(mistuning) = √((wanted beat)² + ((a + b) × (grip))²) · (height offset) = (mistuning) ÷ ((base) × (a + b))

On a lock the scan closes into a knot and repeats every 1/f₀. But a surface may have nothing at the first harmonic of that period, so the ear hears a higher one. The Singer reads the surface's own coefficients, finds the lowest strong partial on each lock, and tunes the base so that partial lands on your note.

The étude did this by hand, with a different formula for each voice: base = note/6 for the hero at the pole, note/2 for the mentor on 3:2, note for the reed on 1:1, 3·note/8 for the pad on 1:2. This solve gives all four from the surfaces alone.

The pole is the lock 0:1: the second scan stops and the voice is a plain one-dimensional wavetable.
