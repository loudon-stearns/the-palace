### 2026-10-13 — cycle 19 — Étude No. 2, fifth draft: a 4:27 hero's journey in twelve chapters, with a cast of surfaces, atlas-click gestures, canons, and phrases that end as questions.
> shipped · 4:27, nobody has listened yet · steward leans listen to the whole thing once

**The cast is the surfaces.** The hero is Penrose, born at the pole where it is the plain 1D synth. The ordinary world is the one-dimensional wavetable, and the journey is the climb up the sphere. The mentor is Knot Shadow's 3:2 whistle. The guardian is a stirred Chladni growl. The shadow is Fisher Ridge, off every ratio. Two new characters come straight out of the voicing atlas:

- **The trickster** is Matérn Field clicked from lock to lock. On almost every lock it sounds the same A4 (measured 439.5 Hz after every click), but with a different set of undertones, so each click changes its colour and never its pitch. In the tests it fills the gaps in the bell pattern. In the reward it clicks on every group of the 7/8 limp.
- **The stair** is Stiff String. On every lock it sounds its x scan rate, so its nineteen locks form a scale of fractions, the Farey scale, part tonal and part not. The hero's motif A C E D is already inside it (1:1, 2:3, 1:3, 1:2, with the C 15 cents sharp). The stair clicks down that scale one lock a beat into the cave, runs an ostinato up it on the road back, and at the resurrection plays the motif as a bass from inside the strange scale.

**The weird sounds return** in the refusal (the hero slides back to the pole through every ratio in between, the smear from No. 1), at the threshold, and in the ordeal. In the ordeal the reed is torn through the atlas in leaps from 55 to 1145 Hz, the hero's dead line slows toward stillness, and then comes 1.6 s of true silence before a coupling snap starts the reward.

**Counterpoint:** a free-time canon with the mentor (the motif inverted, the hero imitating below), a stretto chase on the road back that tightens from a beat to an eighth, and a three-voice motif at the resurrection.

**Questions:** the phrases end on E, on E over G♯, on B, or climbing without landing. Only the very end arrives on A, and that ending is kept in the shape you liked.

Listen: [the fifth draft](open:Projects/2D Torus Wavetable Synthesizer/Etude/etude_two_e_heros_journey.m4a). Chapter times are in the score.

### 2026-10-13 — cycle 16 — Étude No. 2, third draft: 12/8 with uneven triplets, a West African bell, sliding tempo, and every melody sung with slides and vibrato.
> shipped · 155 s, ending kept as-is · unheard by anyone yet

**How time moves now.** The piece is in 12/8. Each beat splits into three eighths that are uneven: short, medium, long, like the stretched triplets in Malian jembe drumming. The motif A C E D becomes 3·2·1·6 eighths, so it swings by itself. There is no fixed BPM; time runs through a tempo curve. The staircase speeds up from 50 to 80, so the groove arrives inside it. The knot section holds steady and swung at 80. The let-go slows to 56 and speeds back up to catch the hands. The B theme breathes, with each two-bar phrase pushing ahead and then relaxing. The ending stays in free time. So the piece now moves between rubato, groove and rubato, which is the threshold you asked for.

**The beat.** A two-tone Chladni bell plays the West African 12/8 bell pattern (x.x.xx.x.x.x). A shaker plays every eighth and leans on the long one. The kick walks the four beats against the bell, goes half time in 3+3+2 under the hands, and becomes a soft frame drum in B. Drum hits get a few milliseconds of seeded push and pull.

**The singing.** Every melody slides into tied notes, longer on leaps, the way a violinist shifts. It scoops up into detached notes, and a delayed vibrato blooms on long notes: 10 cents in the coda, up to 32 on the whistle's reach. In a patch the vibrato is just a `[cycle~]` into `[param baseHz]`.

**The dead lines follow the tempo.** In the let-go the score sets each mistuning from the tempo, so the slip stays at eighth notes (Knot Shadow) and beats (Penrose) all the way down the slowdown and back up. Two small composing changes: a turn in bar 10, and B's answers now climb through chord tones over the moving pad (A C E, then E G B, then G B D) instead of repeating.

### 2026-10-03 — cycle 7 — The Hopf control surface is playable: drag a dot on a sphere to steer the torus scanner, with gold lock bands where the scan snaps shut.
> shipped · browser instrument + two 20 s tour renders + an unverified RNBO codebox · steward leans warps-next

The mapping isn't forced. It falls out of the Hopf map itself. A point (z₁, z₂) on the 3-sphere carries two phases, and those are the torus's two scan phases. The Hopf map keeps two things. One is the balance |z₁|² − |z₂|², which I use as height. The other is the phase difference, which I use as longitude. It discards the shared phase, and that discarded circle is the scan running through time. So each point on the sphere names one orbit of the scanner.

How it plays: height splits a fixed rate budget between the two axes, w1 = base·(1−h) and w2 = base·(1+h). At the south pole the y rate is zero, so you have a plain 1D wavetable and longitude chooses the row. That means the classic wavetable synth is one point on this sphere. The equator is 1:1. Longitude slides where the scan sits on the surface. That only matters when the orbit is closed. On an open orbit it washes out, which is itself a lesson.

I don't set the lock target with a knob. Every 32 samples the engine picks the simple ratio, a:b with both numbers at most 5, that needs the least coupling to lock. The result I didn't expect: every lock band has the same width, coupling ÷ base, in height. By Archimedes' hat-box theorem, equal height means equal area on the sphere. So 1:1 and 4:5 get exactly the same room. When it locks, the pitch is 2·base ÷ (a+b), so it depends only on the sum of the ratio's two numbers.

Checks: the engine's surfaces match the catalog WAVs to float precision (max difference 8×10⁻⁷). The measured slip matches the theory. At height 0.21, near 3:2, it drifts 22.0 / 19.6 / 9.1 / 0 cycles over 2 s at coupling 0 / 1 / 2 / 2.4 Hz, against predicted 22.0 / 19.6 / 9.2 / 0, and it snaps at the predicted 2.2 Hz. The browser page and the offline render run the same engine script, pulled out of the HTML, so they can't drift apart.

The tour, 20 s: 0–5 s at the south pole, a 1D wavetable at 440 Hz with longitude sweeping the rows. 5–12 s climbing the sphere, catching and slipping through the 1:4, 1:3, 1:2, 2:3 bands, each a stepped pitch. 12–16 s parked just off 3:2 while coupling ramps up, snapping shut at 13.5 s. 16–20 s locked at 88 Hz while longitude slides the closed knot across the surface. Same gesture on Penrose and on Knot Shadow.
