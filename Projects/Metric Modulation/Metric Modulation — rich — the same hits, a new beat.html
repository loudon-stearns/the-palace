The same hits, a new beat

The page's opening claim: a metric modulation sounds smooth because the grid doesn't stop; it reinterprets. Five steps, from one pulse to a test you take with your own hand. Every sound here is synthesised.

A pulse at 120

A kick on every beat, a hat between. Tap along on the pad, or the space bar, and the readout gives the tempo your hand found. One idea only: a pulse is something a body locks to.

silent until you press play

The dotted quarter

A bell on every dotted quarter: one and a half beats. Over the 120 groove it runs across the beat, and meets the kick every third kick (ringed). Alone, with nothing else playing, it is a pulse of its own.

♩. = 1.5 × (60 ⁄ 120) s = 0.75 s  →  60 ⁄ 0.75 = 80 BPM
(one and a half beats at 120) lasts three quarters of a second; a pulse that long is 80 beats a minute.

The pivot

Three bars at 120 with the bell running through. At 6 s the kick moves onto the bell and the hats follow it: 80. Watch the bell's lane. It never changes; only what is around it does. This is the page's the grid doesn't stop; it reinterprets, drawn and played.

Two changes, four names

The page lists four relationships. They are two tempo changes, each named from both ends. Read the arrow as the old value becomes the new one. In each door below, a figure sounds through 120 on the bell, then becomes the beat.

120 → 80: dotted quarter → quarter, and quarter → triplet quarter  ·  120 → 180: quarter → dotted quarter, and triplet quarter → quarter
new tempo = old tempo ÷ (figure, in old beats)
(the new beats a minute) = (the old beats a minute) divided by (how many old beats the carried figure lasts). A dotted quarter lasts 1.5: 120 ÷ 1.5 = 80. A triplet quarter lasts ⅔: 120 ÷ ⅔ = 180.
So the page's equivalently line holds as arithmetic and not as its gloss. Quarter = triplet quarter gives 80 when the old quarter becomes the new triplet quarter. If the triplet, two thirds of a beat, became the new pulse, the tempo would rise to 180, the second door. What becomes the pulse at 80 is one and a half old beats, as in the first line.

The test: does the running grid carry you?

The opening says the change sounds smooth because the grid doesn't stop. Here the grid is the bell. A is the pivot from step 3, the bell running through 120 and on into 80. A′ is the same jump at the same moment with the bell silent until the change. From 6 s on the two are identical, note for note; they differ only in whether the dotted-quarter grid was already running. Tap the kick from the start and keep tapping it through the change. The piece measures how soon your taps are back on the kick. It may come out either way.

Tap from the first bar: your taps before the change set how late your hand and speakers run, and that delay is taken out. Back on the kick means three taps in a row within 50 ms of it.

A glide never jumps, so a tapping hand rarely leaves it; that is why it isn't scored against the pivot. The clock can say where your hand landed. Only you can say how each one felt.
