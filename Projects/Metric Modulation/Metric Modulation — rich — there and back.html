There and back

The page's mirror: 120 drops to 90 and snaps back. Four steps: the drop alone, the return alone, the two laid over each other, then the whole trip, with how long you stay at 90 in your hands. Every sound here is synthesised.

The drop

Two bars at 120 with a bell every four triplet eighths: two thirds of a second, three bells to every four kicks. At 4 s the kick moves onto the bell. That is 90.

4 × (60 ⁄ 120 ⁄ 3) s = ⅔ s  →  60 ⁄ ⅔ = 90 BPM  =  120 × ¾
(four triplet eighths at 120) last two thirds of a second; a pulse that long is 90 beats a minute, three quarters of 120. Said the other way: the old quarter, half a second, is the new dotted eighth.

The return

Three bars at 90. In the last four seconds a bell sounds every half second, the old 120 beat, now a dotted eighth. At 8 s the kick moves onto it: 120. It is the drop's pivot with the pair swapped: there the old quarter became the new dotted eighth; here the old dotted eighth becomes the new quarter.

¾ × (60 ⁄ 90) s = 0.5 s  →  60 ⁄ 0.5 = 120 BPM  =  90 × 4⁄3
(a dotted eighth at 90, three quarters of its beat) lasts half a second; a pulse that long is 120, four thirds of 90.

Lay one over the other

Each beat drawn as a bar as long as the beat lasts: the drop above, the return below. Flip the drop end to end and it lands on the return, beat for beat. That is a picture of the mirror, not a test of it: the two were built eight beats of ½ s and twelve of ⅔ s, so reversed they must match. Drawn under the bars is the one place the mirror can break: if the return is to land back on the old 120’s bar lines, the two tempos do not offer the same places to leave from.

¾ × 4⁄3 = 1  ·  4 × 0.5 s = 3 × ⅔ s = 2 s
(the drop's ratio) times (the return's) is one: you come home to the same tempo. And four beats of 120 take as long as three of 90, so, counted from one start, the two grids meet every two seconds. A return that keeps the old 120 in step has to leave on one of those meetings; a return counted from wherever it starts can leave from any 90 beat, a little out of step with a 120 nobody is still playing.

The test: does the return arrive?

The page's choreography: build at 120, drop, let the new pulse settle, snap back. It says the return hits with recognition and a sense of arrival because you have adjusted to 90. Each trip below stays at 90 for one bar, three bars or six (about 3, 8 or 16 s), drawn at random and not shown. Every return is prepared alike: four bells on the old beat in the last two seconds. Listen to the whole trip, say how the return landed, and only then see which stay it was.
