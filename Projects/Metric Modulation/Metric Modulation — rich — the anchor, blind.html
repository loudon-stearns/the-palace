The anchor, blind

Step 3 of the page's recipe says the anchor, a kick on the dotted quarter that carries into the new tempo as its beat, is what makes the change feel natural. Four steps: the anchor alone, the change with it, the change without it, then a blind test. Every sound here is synthesised.

One kick on the dotted quarter

A 120 groove with no kick: hats between the beats, a rim on two and four. Then add the anchor, a kick every dotted quarter. It rolls across the bar line, eight kicks to every three bars, and agrees with the groove only now and then.

The kick becomes the beat

At 6 s the 120 groove gives way to an 80 groove built around the same kick. The kick never moves. What was rolling across the old bar is now the floor of the new one.

♩. at 120 = 0.75 s = ♩ at 80
(a dotted quarter at 120) and (a quarter at 80) are the same three quarters of a second, so one kick can be both.

Take the anchor away

The same change into the same 80 groove, but the 120 section keeps its kick on the beat, as any 120 groove would. Nothing before the change is 0.75 s long. The slider moves where the 80 groove comes in; at zero it lands on the bar line, where the anchored one did.

Blind

Six pairs. Each pair is the change with the anchor and without it (steps 2 and 3, the slider at zero), in an order drawn by chance. Play both, pick the smoother. From the change on, the two are identical, note for note; the only difference is what the 120 section prepared. Nothing tells you which was which until the end.
