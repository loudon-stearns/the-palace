Which knobs are which

The card says some settings make the house bass a house bass and the rest are yours to move. This piece plays the card on the project's own voice (the site's voice.js, the vocabulary's numbers, its compressor), lets you move one knob of each kind, then moves every knob on the card and asks two meters (a rule of this piece's over the project's four sidecar numbers, and the project's own comparison verdict), and then your ear, whether the sound is still that sound. The rules are written before the measurements.

1 · The house bass, untouched

One square oscillator and a sine an octave under it, through a 24 dB low-pass with moderate resonance. The filter envelope opens instantly and shuts in a short decay with no sustain; the amp envelope holds. The card's bar is four off-beat eighths at 124 bpm on C2, each a sixteenth long. Press the bar to hear it as the card writes it; press and hold hold a note to hear one note for as long as you hold it. The listening cue on the card: "every note opens and shuts in the first instant: 'ooh', not 'aah'. The body stays under it as long as the key is held."

The four numbers are the project's feature sidecar, by render-features.py's definitions as compare.js ports them: level is the RMS over the sounding region (dBFS), brightness the power-weighted spectral centroid over it, rise the time from the onset to the first point within 6 dB of the peak (5 ms RMS envelope), tail the time from the last note-off to 40 dB under the loudest point after it. Measured on this voice offline, compressor on, as the site's own Render ours does. For a bass the centroid sits low (about 100 Hz here): the fundamental and the sub carry most of the power.

2 · A taste knob alone: cutoff

The card lists the cutoff as taste, dark – mid, default dark. The vocabulary makes dark 300 Hz and mid 1200 Hz. Move it across the whole range and play. The sound gets brighter; the card's claim is that it is still a house bass. Nothing else moves.

tasteFilter 1 · cutoff frequencydark 300 Hz – mid 1200 Hz

Numbers are measured when you let go of the slider, beside the untouched sound's (in amber, the change).

3 · An essential knob alone: filter-envelope sustain

The card pins the filter envelope's sustain at zero, "the pluck lives in the filter, not the amp", and its A/B is this knob: "raise the filter envelope's sustain to full: the pluck vanishes and it becomes a plain square bass." Cutoff is back at dark. Move the sustain from zero to full and play the bar, then hold a note.

essentialFilter envelope · sustain levelzero – full (the card pins zero)

What the numbers say: with the sustain at full the filter never decays at all, it stays open for the whole note, so the A/B is plain on the bar too (brightness 102.7 Hz untouched, 157.4 Hz at full). What blurs it on the bar is a taste move: the filter decay at the far end of its taste range, medium, 0.575 s, never finishes inside a 121 ms note, so it also leaves the filter nearly open (155.2 Hz). On a held note the decay has time to finish and the two part. Step 4 measures all three.

4 · Every knob on the card, two meters, rules first

Every knob the voice models is moved, one at a time: each taste knob to the far end of its own range on the card, each essential to a value a player could set. The card predicts the taste moves keep the sound and the essential moves lose it. One disagreement first: the page's opening lists resonance amount as taste, while the card pins filter 1's resonance at moderate as essential ("the vowel in the 'ooh'"). This piece follows the card: both resonance moves are scored essential, and one of them is a blind trial in step 5. Two meters judge, on two clips: the bar as the card writes it, and one held note (0.8 s), the situation the card's cue names.

Meter A, this piece's rule over the project's four sidecar numbers. Not a meter the project has: the features are the project's, the rule is mine. The taste region of a feature is the span the untouched sound and the taste moves cover, widened by the feature's grain (level 0.1 dB, brightness 1 %, rise and tail 5 ms, the envelope's hop).
lost(m) ⇔ ∃ f ∈ {level, brightness, rise, tail} : f(m) ∉ [ minT f − gf , maxT f + gf ]
In words: a move is lost if it takes any of the four numbers outside the range the card's own taste knobs reach. A taste move is judged against the region of the other taste moves, so the taste arm can fail too, and it fails by construction whenever one taste knob is the only one to push a feature to an extreme: the four taste misses below (decay to medium on brightness, sub off on rise, sub at equal on level and brightness, release on tail) are all of that kind. The card's split holds on this meter when every taste move is kept and every essential move lost.
Meter B, the project's own: the comparison page's verdict. The site's Compare yours grades only the card's essentials, each read from the sound in the card's own words (compare.js readings), and calls a reading a match when it is the same word or the next one on its ladder (adjacent; the three-word ladders ask for the same word). Here "yours" is the moved sound and "ours" the untouched one. A move is lost if any graded essential stops matching.
Two checks on each meter. The untouched sound rendered a second time must read kept, every number unchanged, or the renders are not repeatable and the table means nothing (the voice's noise source is seeded so they are). Oscillator 1 swapped for noise, under eight noise seeds, must read lost every time, or that meter can call a sound with no square in it a match and its column is not a measure of lost.

Not moved, and why: the card's drive (the voice has no filter drive) and glide (one repeated note on a mono voice never slides), on the taste side; mono against poly (the bar's notes never overlap, so the voice count cannot show) on the essential side. The comparison page has no reading for the filter type; meter B sees the band-pass move only through what it does to other readings. What this table can and cannot say: it asks whether the project's meters see the split the card draws. It does not decide which knobs are structural. That is a teaching rule, and the ear is its judge.

5 · Your ear, blind: is it still house bass?

Twelve trials, in an order shuffled for you. Each plays the untouched house bass, then one moved sound: six taste moves and six essential, the knob never named. Answer still house bass or not house bass. The clip is the card's bar followed by one note held 0.8 s.

Before the first trial. Every moved sound is matched in loudness to the untouched one: scaled by one gain so its RMS over the sounding region (the sidecar's level) equals the untouched clip's, then both are played at the same output gain. Every clip has the same length and the same notes. Scored on both arms: the taste arm counts "still" answers on the six taste trials, the essential arm counts "not" answers on the six essential trials. Rule: an arm agrees with the card at 5 or 6 of 6, contradicts it at 0 or 1, and cannot say at 2 to 4. Chance: someone answering at random agrees on one arm with probability 7/64 (11 %), on both 1.2 %; someone who gives the same answer every time agrees on one arm and contradicts the other. Two of the twelve moves were heard by name in steps 2 and 3 (cutoff to mid, filter sustain to full); the score is also shown without them.
