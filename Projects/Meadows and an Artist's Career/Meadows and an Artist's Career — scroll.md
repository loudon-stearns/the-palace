### 2026-10-30 — cycle 13 — Worksheet v5, the journaling edition: you write your ten decisions first, and every rung opens into plain context and a question to write about.
> shipped · your note on the plan, built · steward leans you try writing in it once

[leverage-diagnostic-v5.html](open:Projects/Meadows and an Artist's Career/leverage-diagnostic-v5.html), with [a preview](open:Projects/Meadows and an Artist's Career/leverage-diagnostic-v5-preview.png) showing a sample ten and one journal entry.

What changed from v4:

- **You write before you sort.** The +/− counters are gone. A new box at the top holds ten numbered spaces: write each decision the way you'd tell a friend, saying what you decided and what it changed. Each one then gets a dropdown for its rung, and the counts follow from what you wrote. Before the ladder appears, a line explains why you write first: it keeps you honest about the decisions you'd rather forget.
- **Your own words show up on the ladder.** Next to each rung, your filed decisions appear by number and sentence, so you can see what actually sits on rung 12.
- **Every rung has context and a journal prompt.** Under each rung, 'sit with this rung' opens two or three plain sentences on what Meadows meant by it, then one open question and space to write. Rung 12, for example: 'Name the last knob you turned. What were you hoping it would change, underneath? Did it?' Rung 3: 'Finish the sentence without editing yourself: My career is for… Whose sentence is that?' Once you've written something, the rung shows 'written'.
- **Reflection after the shape.** After the reading come two prompts: which decisions you filed lower or higher than you expected, and 'How do you feel, having looked at it?' That second one is the Hilaritas question, with a line saying the feeling is information the numbers don't carry.
- **The exercise gets context too.** Each of its steps now says why it matters, and step 1 quotes your own sentences from your lowest rung back to you.
- **Saving keeps everything.** The saved text keeps v4's per-rung lines, so older pages can still read it, then adds your ten in your own words, every journal entry with its question, the two reflections, and the move. It all stays in your browser. 'Clear everything' now asks before wiping, since there's writing to lose.

### 2026-10-14 — cycle 12 — Worksheet v4: after your ten are placed, a four-step exercise walks your lowest decision one move up and saves it with your shape.

The example is a gear rabbit-hole turned into a once-a-year gear-day rule, placed on rung 5 (rules of the system).

What's new since v3:

- **The exercise, in four steps.** Step 1: name your lowest decision in a sentence. The rung fills in from your placements, and you can change it. Step 2: one real move from each of the three careers, the nearest one at least a band above yours. Step 3: write your own higher move and say which rung it lives on. Step 4: one small act this week, plus a check-back date that defaults to 30 days out. On that date, place your next ten and see whether the shape moved.
- **Gentle checks on the rung you pick.** Moving up one rung inside the same band gets a nudge to try a full band higher. Jumping from the knobs straight to the paradigm gets a reminder to check your savings and slack first (rung 11, buffers).
- **The list-order bias is fixed.** In v3, Oliveros showed up in the 'one band up' prompt mostly because she came first in the list. Now the reading uses whichever career is nearest your shape, and the exercise shows all three side by side.
- **It remembers where you left off.** Your placements and your writing stay in this browser only, and nothing leaves your machine. A ?demo= view never overwrites what a real reader saved.
- **The saved text adds a ONE MOVE UP block at the end.** The rung lines above it are unchanged, so the case-studies page still reads them. I checked: all 12 parse.

I tested it by script in headless Chrome. The script placed ten decisions, typed into each step, changed both rung pickers, saved, reloaded to confirm the state came back, and re-parsed the export. Everything behaved.

Four things the grid taught me, written on the page:

(1) Each artist has a different home rung. Eno works at the paradigm. Oliveros builds structures that let a practice reproduce itself. Martin fixed her lowest rung once (one format, one motif) and never spent attention there again.

(2) A parameter isn't low-leverage because it's small. Eno's tape-loop lengths are just a number, but they were set inside a paradigm move.

(3) Biographies record the high rungs, so none of the three comes out 'inverted' (the entry's word for most of your attention sitting on the low rungs). Some of the gap between a reader and these artists is biography versus calendar, not real difference. Rung 9 (delays: how long from idea to finished) is empty for all three, because no biography records cycle time.

(4) The high rungs cost something. Oliveros gave up a tenured professorship, which was her buffer, in 1981. Martin quit painting at her peak in 1967.

All facts come from the dossiers except Oliveros 1981, which I checked on the web (Wikipedia; the UCSD memoriam page).
