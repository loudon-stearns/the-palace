### 2026-10-14 — cycle 10 — Case studies v0.1: Eno, Oliveros and Agnes Martin, ten career moves each, placed on the twelve rungs. You can paste your own saved ten beside them.
> shipped · case studies started · steward leans fold these into the worksheet next

One page, [leverage-case-studies.html](open:Projects/Meadows and an Artist's Career/leverage-case-studies.html). It has five shape cards (the three artists, the typical artist, and you) and a 12-rung ladder with a column per artist. Click any move to see the fact behind it and why I put it on that rung. Paste the text v2's 'Save my distribution' produces and your ten fill in the fifth card and the right-hand column. I tested the paste against v2's real export format, and the page renders cleanly in headless Chrome. Average heights come out at level 5.6 for Eno, 6.8 for Oliveros and 6.1 for Martin.

Four things the grid taught me, written on the page:

(1) Each artist has a different home rung. Eno works at the paradigm. Oliveros builds structures that let a practice reproduce itself. Martin fixed her lowest rung once (one format, one motif) and never spent attention there again.

(2) A parameter isn't low-leverage because it's small. Eno's tape-loop lengths are just a number, but they were set inside a paradigm move.

(3) Biographies record the high rungs, so none of the three comes out 'inverted' (the entry's word for most of your attention sitting on the low rungs). Some of the gap between a reader and these artists is biography versus calendar, not real difference. Rung 9 (delays: how long from idea to finished) is empty for all three, because no biography records cycle time.

(4) The high rungs cost something. Oliveros gave up a tenured professorship, which was her buffer, in 1981. Martin quit painting at her peak in 1967.

All facts come from the dossiers except Oliveros 1981, which I checked on the web (Wikipedia; the UCSD memoriam page).

### 2026-06-23 — cycle 9 — Stage 2 prototype shipped: a self-diagnostic worksheet that places ten career moves on the twelve leverage points and shows the inversion live.
> sprout · Stage 1 spec done · Stage 2 worksheet sketch up

Quick catch-up: this project is the career-edition pivot of Meadows' twelve leverage points — the second pass that asks where your *career as a system* will repay a move, not where your mixdown will. The entry body has the twelve levels spec'd (Stage 1). Stage 2 in the forward-vector list was a self-diagnostic worksheet: ask the reader to place their last 10 career decisions on the hierarchy. I built it. Single HTML page, drop counts into each level, watch the distribution bars and the verdict (INVERTED / BALANCED / TOP-HEAVY) update live. The instrument is meant to perform the lesson — most readers will see their own inversion in under a minute, which is the whole pedagogy the entry argues for.
