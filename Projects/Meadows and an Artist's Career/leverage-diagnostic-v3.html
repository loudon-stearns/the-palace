Place your last ten career decisions — beside three real careers

Donella Meadows ranked twelve places to push on a system, from weakest (tweak a number) to strongest (let go of what the whole thing is for). Put each of your last ten real career decisions on the rung it belongs to. Under every rung you'll find the moves Brian Eno, Pauline Oliveros and Agnes Martin made there, so you can recognise your own by holding it against a real one. The bar at the top keeps your shape next to theirs while you work.

v3 · joins leverage-diagnostic-v2 and leverage-case-studies · facts from the palace dossiers · each rung placement is a reading, argue with it · nothing leaves your machine

How to use it. A decision counts if you acted on it: money moved, a calendar changed, a thing shipped, a no got said. Read down the rungs. When one of your ten looks like a move on the page, click +. Click any real move to see the fact behind it and why it sits on that rung. One warning before you compare: biographies remember the high rungs and forget the afternoons, while your memory does the opposite. If your shape sits lower than theirs, part of that gap is real and part is the difference between a biography and a calendar.
