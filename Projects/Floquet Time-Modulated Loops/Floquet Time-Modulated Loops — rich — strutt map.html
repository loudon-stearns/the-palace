Floquet · the Strutt diagram, heard

From one ring to the map of tongues

The page says the Strutt diagram splits the (a, q) plane into bounded and growing, and that the split is audible: silence cracking into ringing. Four steps build up to that: a plain oscillator, the pump on along one line, the whole map, and then the test, two points a hair apart on either side of a drawn edge. Every step plays; jump to any of them.

1 · One ring, no pump

With q = 0 the equation is a plain oscillator, and a only sets its pitch: the ring is at √a × 220 Hz. Keep one number in mind for later: the pump, when it comes, will always run at 440 Hz.

2 · Pump it, along one line

Now the stiffness breathes: a − 2q·cos 2t, at 440 Hz. Hold the depth q and slide a. The curve is how fast a ring grows in one pump period, worked out from the equation; above the line it grows, below it dies. Where it grows is a tongue, and its width at this q is the range of a the pump can capture.

4 · Across the edge, a hair apart

The test. Pick an edge. The piece finds where the map puts it, to many decimals, then runs the same resonator you have been hearing for three seconds at two points: 0.03 outside the edge and 0.03 inside it, same q, same noise. If the drawn line is the audible boundary, one stays hiss and the other rings.

Damping κ = 0.05 throughout, noise σ = 0.0015, the same seed on both sides. Levels are the rms of x over the last half second, before the output's soft clip.
