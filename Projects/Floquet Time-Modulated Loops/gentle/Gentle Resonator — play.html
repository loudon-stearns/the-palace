The Mathieu resonator, gentle version: damping that grows with the ring's energy

A 220 Hz resonator whose tuning is pumped. Below the tongue it's quiet. Cross into it and the noise cracks into a ring, and the ring settles where the extra damping uses up what the pump puts in, so the loudness tracks the pump depth. Click anywhere on the map to move there. Inside the tongue the pitch is half the pump rate, not the resonator's own 220.

where it settles: E = (√((q/2)² − (r/2 − 1)²) − ζ) / g, zero outside the tongue. The map's colour is this law; the meter shows what the resonator is actually doing. The law is first-order: past q ≈ 0.3 the real tongue leans toward low pump ratios, louder on the left edge and quieter on the right than the colours say.
