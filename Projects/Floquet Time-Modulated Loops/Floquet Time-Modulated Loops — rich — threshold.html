Floquet · the threshold of a pumped swing

Pushing, pumping, and the depth it takes

The page says a pumped oscillator is different in kind from a pushed one, and that pumping has a threshold: below a certain depth the damping wins and it settles, above it the ring grows exponentially. Three steps build up to that: push against pump from rest, the threshold under your hand, and then the test, the threshold measured and set beside the damping that makes it.

1 · Push or pump, from rest

A push is a force from outside; a pump changes the stiffness and leaves the right-hand side at zero. Start the same 220 Hz resonator from perfect rest. Pushed, it comes up to a steady ring. Pumped, it does nothing at all, ever. Give it the faintest nudge first and the pump takes it from there, by the same factor every period: a straight line on this decibel plot.

push: ẍ + κẋ + x = F cos t pump: ẍ + κẋ + (1 − 2q cos 2t)·x = 0
a push adds a force on the right; a pump multiplies the stiffness by something that breathes at twice the ring, and the right stays zero

3 · Where the threshold sits

The test. For the damping the project’s audio was built with, ζ = 0.025, the piece measures the growth at five depths three ways: the textbook rate, one pump period of the equation (its multiplier), and the resonator itself run for two seconds from the same noise. Then it takes the damping away.

Units: q is the page’s Stage 1 depth (q = q_depth · a, with a = 1 here), ζ the damping ratio, so κ = 2ζ. Growth is per second at 220 Hz. “Full” is within 3 dB of where the ring settles once the soft brake holds it; the brake is this piece’s, not the codebox’s tanh.
