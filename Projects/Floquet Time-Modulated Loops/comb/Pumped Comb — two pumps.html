Two pumps on one string

A plucked-string loop that dies on its own. One pump breathes its loudness once per pass and can bring it back to life; the other breathes its length and draws new partials between its harmonics. Pick a preset, press render, and watch the spectrum while you listen.

what the measurements say

Hold the string alive with the loudness pump at 1× (ε 0.1), then wobble its length. At a whole-number length rate the ring never broke, all the way to ±32 samples (±15 % of the loop). At a stray rate it breaks: the pulse riding the loudness peak arrives early or late on each pass, slips out of the window, and the string dies back to noise.

length rate × f₀breaks at, samples

One seed, 6 s runs, a break counted when the last 2 s fall below −33 dB (held level ≈ −13 dB). Halfway rates mostly hold too (0.5 and 1.5 never broke; 2.5 broke at 32). Among stray rates I found no simple rule: √2 lasted to 24, 2.1213 died at 6.
