Floquet · one period, two multipliers

Where a state lands after one pump period

The page says stability is read off two numbers, the characteristic multipliers, and that a tongue's edge is where one of them sits on the unit circle. Three steps build up to that: one period as a map of the phase plane, the two multipliers moving as you push into a tongue, and then the test, with and without damping.

1 · One period is a map

Start the oscillator from 32 different states (x, ẋ), all on one circle. Let the pump run for exactly one period, t = π, and mark where each lands. The circle comes back as an ellipse: the equation is linear, so one period is a 2 × 2 matrix, the monodromy M. Step the periods on and the ellipse either turns in place or stretches away.

2 · The two multipliers

M has two eigenvalues, ρ₁ and ρ₂: the multipliers. With no damping their product is det M = 1. Walk into a tongue and watch them: they travel round the unit circle as a pair, meet on the real axis, and split, one out and one in.
