Echo, echo, echo — or the phrase?

The page says this delay thinks in phrases: not "echo, echo, echo" but "here's what you just did, replayed", and that it stays clear because each new measure overwrites the oldest. Walk up to that claim, then test it against every feedback setting an echo has. Synthesized plucks at 120 BPM: one measure is two seconds. Silent until you press something.

One phrase, into silence

One measure of eighth-note plucks, then three measures of nothing. This is what goes into every delay that follows. The thin lines mark the measures.

Echo, echo, echo

A tapped delay hears the phrase and plays it back a half note later, then feeds that copy back in, so it comes again, quieter by the feedback each time. Each tap is a copy of the whole phrase laid over the one before. Turn the feedback up and the copies pile.

e[n] = x[n − L] + f · e[n − L],   y = x + 0.8 · e
(the echo now) = (what you played one tap ago) + (feedback) × (the echo one tap ago); (what you hear) = (what you play) + 0.8 × (the echo)

The tape, as the core architecture describes it

Now the page's device: one measure of tape, always recording, a read head half a measure behind the write head, the ghost at 0.8. The phrase comes back as one block, once. Nothing feeds it back, so it doesn't come again.

tape[w] ← x,   y = x + 0.8 · tape[(w − L) mod M]
(the slot under the write head) takes (what you play); (what you hear) = (what you play) + 0.8 × (what the tape holds half a measure back)

Is it different?

Subtract an echo with its feedback at zero from the tape, sample by sample. The read head reaches each slot half a measure after it was written, and the write head doesn't come back to overwrite it for a whole measure, so the tape never runs out and its length never shows. The steward found this first, in the witness's model; this piece runs it again.

The test: keep it, then play over it

The séance, built later, lets silence keep the past: while you play the tape writes, and while you're silent it keeps what it holds. Play phrase one, stop for two measures, play phrase two, stop. A delay that thinks in phrases should do two things: keep phrase one while you're silent, and clear it when phrase two writes over it.

Give the echo its best chance at keeping: a tap one whole measure long, so a phrase lines up with itself. Then sweep its feedback. Amber is phrase one's share of what you hear, violet is phrase two's.
