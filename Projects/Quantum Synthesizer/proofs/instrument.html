Quantum Synthesizer · Study · the instrument

One instrument, three knobs: the equation, the shape, the angle of i.

Everything the last six proofs tested, in one place you can play. Pick the rule that turns shapes into pitches (a drum's wave equation, or Schrödinger's), pick what holds the wave (a line, a square, a circle), and turn the i in the equation by a small angle to let it ring like a bell or die like a thud. Click the surface to strike it there. The keys play it as a scale.

wave:       ∂²u/∂t² = c²·∇²u  →  fₙ ∝ kₙ
(how fast the surface's speed changes) = (wave speed)² · (how sharply the surface bends) → each partial grows with its wavenumber
Schrödinger: ∂ψ/∂t = −i·e^(−iθ)·(−∇²)ψ  →  fₙ ∝ kₙ²·cos θ,  decay = 2π·fₙ·tan θ
(rate of change of the wave) = −i turned by θ · (how sharply the wave bends) → each partial grows with its wavenumber squared, and every one gets the same damping ratio, sin θ

What to listen for. Start on the presets, which are the seven strikes from drum.wav. Then hold the shape and flip the equation: the same square goes from a clangy drum that never repeats to a box that repeats exactly, an octave below its own lowest partial. Then drag θ. Near 0.01° everything rings like a bell; by 0.3° it is a short knock; past 1° it is a click. Because every partial gets the same damping ratio, the high ones always die first, so turning θ darkens the tone as it shortens it. Strike the circle dead centre and only its rings sound, nearly evenly spaced.

What is physics and what is my choice. The pitches and the loudness of each partial come straight from the shapes' modes (line: sines; square: products of sines; circle: Bessel functions), the strike point and the pickup point. The mallet is a soft roll-off on high wavenumbers, the same felt mallet as drum.py. The angle θ really is Schrödinger's i turned, as in wick.py. For the drum there is no i to turn, so the same pole angle is applied as a damping ratio, the standard way a synthesist damps a modal bank. The moving picture is slowed so the lowest partial turns about once every two seconds, its fading is sped up sixty times so you can watch it happen, and it shows only the partials slow enough to draw.
