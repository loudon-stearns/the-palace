b = | q · fhigh − p · flow | for a ratio p ⁄ q
(beats a second) = (the high note × the ratio's bottom number) − (the low note × the ratio's top number). Those are the two partials that meet when the ratio is exact: the low note's p-th and the high note's q-th. At the exact ratio they are one frequency and b is 0.
c = 1200 · log2( (fhigh ⁄ flow) ⁄ (p ⁄ q) )
(cents off) = 1200 × log base 2 of ((the interval played) ÷ (the whole-number ratio)). A hundred cents is one piano step; the piano's fifth is about 2 cents narrow, its major third about 14 wide.
What this measures is one sitting, one length of note, one register (around middle C) and one tone. Another day, a longer note or a brighter tone moves the threshold. The page draws the miss; this is the ear's side of the same line.
