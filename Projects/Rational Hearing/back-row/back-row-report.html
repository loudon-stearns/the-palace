What the back row can read

The page was opened in a browser at three projector sizes, and every label on it was measured. The back row is taken as 8 screen-heights from the screen; a small classroom is nearer 6. The rule of thumb: a letter 5 arcminutes tall is the smallest a 20/20 eye can read, about 10 reads, and 16 or more is comfortable. These thresholds are from memory, not looked up. Your room test confirms or overturns all of it.

Three findings.

1. The anchors you point at are the smallest things on the page. The ladder's edge labels (P5, M3, −6…) come out at 5.9′ from the back row in Projector mode on a 1280×720 projector. That is at the 20/20 limit. The bracket column and the letter names are smaller still. The big ratio readout is 67′, more than four times what it needs.

2. Browser zoom won't rescue the ladder. The ladder shrinks to fit its column, so zooming the browser to 200% leaves the edge labels at the same size. Fixing it takes a change on the page, not a setting in the room.

3. At ×1 the fifth's and fourth's gaps can't be seen from anywhere. Their 2¢ gap is 1.4 px on screen and hides under the 8-unit-wide bar. ×5 makes them 4 to 5 arcmin, just visible. The thirds and sixths show at ×1 from the back. ×5 is the back-row mode, as designed. On a 1920×1080 projector everything is a third smaller, because the page stops widening past about 1280.

For the room: the walk to the back

  • Projector mode on, ×5 on. Is the projector 1280×720 or 1920×1080? It changes everything above by a third.
  • From the back row: can you read P5, M3, −6 on the ladder's edges? The measurement says this is the first thing to fail.
  • The PERFECT / IMPERFECT / DISSONANT words and the short names beside them: readable, or just colour?
  • The letter names beside the numerals (C, G, E): needed from the back, or only on paper?
  • The temper strip at ×1: can you see that P5 misses at all? At ×5?
  • Is the big 3:2 bigger than it needs to be? Its space could go to the ladder.
