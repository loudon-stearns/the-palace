Doing more with less, and no brake

The page says "do more with less" has no brake: the efficiency he wins gets reinvested into more use, not banked as slack, and nothing in the story pulls the other way. Here is a world small enough to try that on: one stock that regrows, one use that draws it down, and an efficiency that rises every cycle. Five steps, one thing to see in each; the last is a test whose rule is written before it runs. Every number on this sheet is this model's, not the world's and not Fuller's. Nothing here makes a sound.

The rule, stated before the run Nothing is guessed here, so there is no chance level. The run sweeps the efficiency speed from 1% to 50% a cycle, over 300 cycles, and finds for each the smallest rebound at which the stock runs out. The page's doubt has two halves, and each can fail. One: efficiency is not a brake. It holds if, at every speed, the stock runs out at some rebound below 100%, where use is still falling. It is wrong if any speed keeps the stock alive at every rebound below 100%: then efficiency alone outruns the ceiling, and only a true backfire could empty it. Two: a balancing loop is the missing part. It holds if, at every speed, the braked world's line sits above 100%, so the brake keeps the stock where efficiency alone lets it go, even through a backfire. It is wrong at any speed where that line is at or below 100%. Where total use stops falling is not a test: it is 100% by what a rebound is, and the sheet prints it only to show its arithmetic agrees. How it can fail. Half one cannot fail by its arithmetic alone: whenever use starts above what regrows, flat use empties a finite stock, so given enough cycles the line always sits below 100% (98.08% at start use 1.3, stock 100, over 100,000 cycles). On this sheet it fails for one of two reasons the verdict names: the start use is at or below regrowth, so only a backfire could empty the stock; or the 300-cycle horizon is too short for a shallow overdraw on a big stock (start 1.05, stock 30: 101.6% at 2% a cycle). Half two fails when the overdraw is deeper than this brake can hold (start above about 1.35, stock 10).
