## The criterion, checked against Papert

**Papert's words** (web; *Mindstorms*, 1980, read from the scanned first edition at <https://worrydream.com/refs/Papert_1980_-_Mindstorms,_1st_ed.pdf>, printed page numbers):

- p. 11 defines the thing: objects-to-think-with are *"objects in which there is an intersection of cultural presence, embedded knowledge, and the possibility for personal identification."*
- Foreword, the gears: *"You can be the gear, you can understand how it turns by projecting yourself into its place and turning with it."* The power is a *"double relationship—both abstract and sensory."*
- p. 63: the Turtle circle is *"body syntonic in that the circle is firmly related to children's sense and knowledge about their own bodies,"* or ego syntonic, *"coherent with children's sense of themselves as people with intentions, goals, desires, likes, and dislikes."*

**What is not in Mindstorms** (web):

- "Public entity" is not in the book. It comes from Papert & Harel, "Situating Constructionism" (1991), read at <https://dailypapert.com/?p=1737>: *"constructing a public entity, whether it's a sand castle on the beach or a theory of the universe."*
- The page's quoted list at `Objects to Think With.md:33–34`, *"a sand castle, a machine, a computer program, a book"*, was not found in either text or by search. Its source is unverified.
- *Mindstorms* also does not ask that the learner build the object. Papert found the gears himself, and he designed the Turtle for children to "make theirs for themselves" (p. 11).

**The page's statement** (palace; `:33–36`, `:62–65`) is a fair compression of two sources: the 1991 public entity, and *Mindstorms'* body-syntonic identification. It drops *cultural presence*, the third of Papert's three terms.

**The bar used here**, one test applied to all 51:

1. **Built and public.** The entry is a made thing others can meet. An unbuilt design counts if it says concretely what a person would handle.
2. **Embedded knowledge.** An abstract idea lives inside the object, and the object exists for the idea's sake. An idea used as an implementation trick behind a product fails.
3. **Identification.** The entry gives the learner either a part of the object to project into and enact (the gear, the Turtle), or makes the learner's own body, voice or choices the part (Papert's ego-syntonic).

On gate 3, hearing or seeing the idea's effect is not identification. That is gate 2's "make the invisible visible", and it is where the strict and loose counts part.

**A finding about the page itself:** the three phrases at `:46–53` (*make the invisible visible*, *the concrete incarnation*, *not as metaphor but as isomorphism*) are all gate-2 phrases. They name the idea living in the object. Only [[Blood Compressor]], the page's "sharpest instance", names gate 3. So the page's own examples mostly sit in the can't-tell column, and its "Body-syntonic … has never been tested" forward vector (`:91`) is right in a way the count had hidden.

## Method and its limits

- Four read-only readers took 12–13 entries each, working from the criterion above and reading each entry `.md` whole. Their verdicts were then held to one bar by the catching session, which read the borderline entries itself. The eight moves are marked.
- The 26 does-nots and the strong qualifies were not re-read by the catcher. They rest on the readers' calls, with the quotes checked. A spot-check caught one reason that was wrong on the text: [[Meadows and an Artist's Career]]'s worksheet is built (`:111`), not only planned. The other does-nots may hold another miss like it.
- Only the entry `.md` was judged, not the bundles. A rich face or proof might settle some can't-tells, most likely [[Tract Mirror]], [[Crystal Synthesizer]] and [[Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis]].
- None of this tests whether any object actually taught anyone. The page's second and third forward vectors stay open.
