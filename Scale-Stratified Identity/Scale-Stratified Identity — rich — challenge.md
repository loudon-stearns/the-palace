## The piece — `Scale-Stratified Identity — rich — two scales.html` (§ The Finding) · **remake**

**1. Test or decoration?** Half a test. The network half is a real test: the rule for "completely" is set before the run from null statistics (|r| ≤ 1.96/√56 = 0.262, ≤ 63.1% of edges on their starting side, RMS move ≥ 0.9·√(1/6) = 0.367), it can pass (in order, 10 of 12 starts) and it can fail (shuffled, 0 of 12; an unchanged network reads "barely", r 1.000). The cell half is not a test, and the piece only half says so. With the slow term off it cannot fail, which the piece discloses: the baseline rule has an analytic bound of 0.149 mV against a 0.5 mV tolerance (measured worst 0.054 mV). The recovery rule was rewritten after the page's own neuron broke it, and the replacement cannot fail either: a clear spike starts with at most 4.5 mV of fast threshold, so its recovery is at most 10 ln 9 = 21.97 ms against the first spike's 10 ln 8 = 20.79 ms, a spread of 1.18 ms inside a 2 ms tolerance (measured 21.96). What the piece does not say is that with the slow term on the rule fails at any size. I swept it: 0.01 mV a spike, a twentieth of the chosen 0.2, already fails (on recovery time, at spike 13); 0.02 fails on baseline at spike 37. So the cell's verdict reports where the switch is, not what the cell does. That is honest if it is said; at present the caption says the slow term "lets the cell's 'no' fail", which reads as a test.

**2. Faithful?** Mostly. It keeps the page's two answers and its count (the watched cell's 1,000th spike), and its "whose numbers" paragraph is honest and complete: the cell and STDP constants match Song, Miller & Abbott 2000 as I know them (τm 20 ms, rest −70, threshold −54, reset −60, E_ex 0, τ_ex 5 ms, g_max 0.015, A+ 0.005, A−/A+ 1.05, τ± 20 ms); window shape Bi & Poo 1998; slow-inactivation time scale Fleidervish, Friedman & Gutnick 1996, with the 0.2 mV size named as the piece's own; eight cells, the 2 ms hold, the 4 mV/10 ms fast threshold, kicks and seeds named as the piece's own. SMA 2000 is a single cell under feed-forward input; the recurrent net of eight is the piece's, and it says so. It also says, rightly, that at g_max 0.015 the weights barely feed back on firing, so the two scales are uncoupled in this model. Three lines overreach:
- the slow-on verdict says "from spike 2 it no longer came back within tolerance; the worst baseline left was 4.68 mV". Spike 2 fails on recovery time (35.9 ms against 25.8); the baseline first goes over 0.5 mV at spike 3 (0.597 mV).
- the shuffled verdict ends "It is the pattern of firing that rewires, not the firing." That holds at the page's count and not beyond it. I ran the shuffled arm on: at 3,000 spikes r 0.871 ("partly"), at 10,000 r 0.390 ("partly", side 0.589, move 0.470). Unpatterned firing rewires too, roughly ten times slower.
- "the same spikes, shuffled in time" are not the same spikes. The arm draws new kick times and a different noise seed, and the network fires 7,953 spikes against 8,474 in order (6% fewer) over 57.0 s against 60.9 s. Rate is matched (17.44 against 17.39 Hz per cell) and the watched cell's count is matched by design (1,000 each). "Same kicks, at random times" or "same rate, no order" is the honest name.

**3. Walks up?** Yes. It builds the page's situation, a neuron firing into a learning network, before it brings in the term that argues with the page. Step 1 names "true by construction" early, which is a disclosure about the model, not a spoiler of the test.

## The offers

**1 · The Finding · claim-change ("not eroded" true only by construction) — numbers confirmed, kind disputed: a no-claim-edit.** The by-construction half is right (bound 0.149 mV, measured 0.054). The rest is the maker's chosen term played back: 4.7 mV is what 0.2 mV a spike over 2 s gives at the watched cell's 10.6 Hz (steady state about 0.2/(1 − e^(−0.047)) ≈ 4.4 mV, peak 4.68), and "back within 0.03 mV after 10 s" is 4.459 × e^(−5) = 0.0300, arithmetic of the 2 s time constant that could not come out otherwise. "From spike 2" should read "from spike 2 on recovery time, from spike 3 on baseline". Ask what kind of claim the sentence is (tuning item 12a): the page says the cell does not change across accumulated crossings and is "homeostatic by design". The maker's own model agrees: the threshold comes all the way back. The proposed wording keeps the page's claim and adds the reason (the cell's equations hold a pull back to baseline; the weights' do not). That reason is a good addition. It is not a correction. A finding that would really argue with the page is long-lasting intrinsic plasticity, threshold changes that outlast rest; I have not checked that literature, and the piece does not model it.

**2 · The Finding · no-claim-edit ("completely" holds for patterned firing) — confirmed at the page's count, with one limit.** In order: completely on 10 of 12 starts; same rate with no order: barely on 12 of 12, r 0.945 to 0.983. Does 173/200 undermine the rule? No. It is the rule's own miss rate. "Completely" is defined as "can't be told from a fresh random draw", and a fresh draw passes 86.5% of the time against seed 7's start (74% against seed 3's), so 10 of 12 (83%) sits at the ceiling these rules can see. The rule is fair to the saturated end STDP makes: an ideal sequence end passes 12 of 12, and a random 0/1 network passes 94.8%. Two limits. The control is matched on rate and on the watched cell's count, not on the network's spikes (6% fewer). And the difference is one of speed, not kind: at 10,000 spikes the no-order arm is at r 0.39. The proposed wording, "the same thousand spikes in no order leave the network nearly as it was", is accurate because it names the thousand. Keep it that way.

**3 · Instances Across the Palace · claim-change (a locked oscillator doesn't keep its own period) — numbers confirmed, kind disputed: a no-claim-edit (tuning item 12e).** My integrator agrees: ±23.90° at K = 2Kc with r 0.969, and every running speed equal to the group's. But the sentence says each oscillator "maintains its own natural frequency at the mechanism level". In the model the natural frequency ω is a fixed parameter, and nothing ever changes it. That the locked state carries it as a phase offset, sin φ = ω/Kr, is the textbook locked solution, and Kuramoto Coupling.md:122 already states its edge case (π/2 at the critical coupling), with :66 already saying the locked oscillators keep "individual character while achieving collective coherence". The landed Kuramoto face does not draw the offset, but its page states it. The one loose phrase is the parenthesis "(its individual period, …)", which a reader could take for the running period. The proposed wording ("a property, not its running period … how far ahead or behind the group it sits") fixes that phrase and is good. It restates a definition; it does not correct a claim.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt on the network side. Watching r fall to 0.15 in one run while the same rate with no order sits at 0.96 makes "same at which scale?" concrete: the network's identity follows the pattern of firing, and the walk earns that by building the learning rule before it asks. The cell side is quieter than its caption suggests. It shows what a restoring term looks like and why the weights lack one, which is the face's best idea and the right gloss on "homeostatic by design". But it cannot tell the page it is wrong, because its verdict is set by a switch. Say that plainly, trim the three overreaching lines, and the face is honest throughout. Neither claim-change offer corrects the page. Both sharpen it, and the sharpening is worth Loudon's look.
