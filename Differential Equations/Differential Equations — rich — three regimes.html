One equation, three regimes

A rule for how fast something moves, given where it is. Then the same rule with weight, so it can swing. Then one number, ζ, that decides whether the swing rings, stops dead, or crawls. Silent until you press a button.

A rule, not a number

A differential equation doesn't give you a value. It gives you a rule: wherever the level is, here is how fast it moves. Every short line in the field below is that rule, read at one spot. Any curve that follows the lines everywhere is a solution, and the starting point picks which one. Click anywhere in the field to start a curve there.

τ · dy/dt + y = u
(the time constant) × (how fast the level moves) + (the level) = (where it is being pulled). The page writes it dy/dt + a·y = b; it is the same rule with τ = 1/a and u = b/a. The farther it still has to go, the faster it goes, so it never quite arrives.

τ only stretches time. The field looks the same at 5 ms and at 500 ms; you hear the difference. This is an RC charge, an envelope's stage, a smoothed control.

Give it weight

Now let the rule set how fast the speed changes, not the speed itself. A second derivative is inertia: the level arrives at its target still moving, so it goes past and swings back. Strike it and it rings. With almost no friction, as here, that swing is a tone.

d²y/dt² + 2ζωn · dy/dt + ωn² y = ωn² u
(how fast the speed changes) + (friction, 2ζωn, times the speed) + (the spring, ωn², times the distance) = (the spring, pulling toward u). Here ωn is 220 Hz and ζ is 0.01.

Take the weight away and the rule is step 1's again: it can only approach, never pass.

Turn ζ

ζ is the friction, measured against the spring. Turn it and the same equation does three jobs at once. Struck at 220 Hz, it is a pluck. Fed noise, it is a resonant filter at 660 Hz. Slowed to 3 Hz and fed a held note's on and off, it is an envelope on a tone. Try each job at a few values of ζ: below 1 all three ring, at 1 all three stop clean, above 1 all three go slow.

ζ < 1 rings  ·  ζ = 1 critical  ·  ζ > 1 sluggish
(below one, the spring wins and it swings) · (at one, the fastest return that never passes) · (above one, the friction wins and it crawls).

The filter is held at one loudness so a ringing ζ doesn't blast; a low ζ makes it much louder than this, by about 1/(2ζ) at its peak (Resonance and Damping measures that).

Where does ringing stop?

The section makes four claims about this equation. Below ζ = 1 it oscillates. At ζ = 1 it is the fastest approach with no overshoot. Above 1 it is slower. And a struck one rings "at its natural frequency ωn, damped at rate ζ". Each is measured below on the engine you have been hearing: every ζ pulled from 0 to 1, and struck at 220 Hz.

Settling is read as the last moment the curve is more than 2% from its target, at steps of 0.002/ωn. The ring's pitch is counted from its zero crossings, its fade from the slope of its peaks; a heavily damped swing passes too few crossings to count, so those cells stay empty.
