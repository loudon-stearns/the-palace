One grain

The bench's NPR ink, ported from stylize.py: a grey puff of smoke broken on a cold-press paper grain. The puff sways; the grain is made fresh every frame, made once, or carried with the puff. Shown enlarged so the grain reads. Silent; nothing here makes sound.

meters/ 255
ink = clip((m − 0.5·τ) / 0.7),   paper = 0.985 + 0.015 (τ − ½)
stylize.py's NPR: m is how much smoke a pixel holds, levelled and curved; τ is the paper's tooth there. Where the tooth is high a thin edge of smoke breaks to paper; a dense core stays solid. The white keeps a faint grain of its own.
boil = 255 · mean |Ft − Ft−1| where the puff never reaches
slide = 255 · mean |Ft − I(x − dt)| where it does
Boil is change on the plain paper, where nothing moves. Slide compares each frame with the puff inked once at rest, I, moved by the same sway: zero if the marks travel with the smoke, more than zero if the smoke moves past marks that stay put.
