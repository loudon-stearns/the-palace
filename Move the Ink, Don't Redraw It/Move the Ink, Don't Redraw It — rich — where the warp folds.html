Where the warp folds

A small ink drawing (a ring, two blobs and a curl) pushed by one ripple, harder and harder. Watch where the ink smears, where the warp folds back on itself, and what that does to the drawing's islands and holes. Silent; nothing here makes sound.

Measured in Python on the bench's own fields.py at full plate size (832 × 1216), each field times its plate's mask and anchor as warp.py applies them, over the 60 frames of a loop; det J by central differences. "Push that folds" is the factor on the field's amplitude at which det J first reaches zero somewhere in the loop; below 1 means the default push has already crossed it there.
s(x) = x − d(x),   det J = det ∂s/∂x
Each pixel x takes its ink from s(x), the place its arrow points back to. det J says how much of the still one pixel of the frame reads: 1 is a plain move, near 0 one sliver of ink is smeared across many pixels, below 0 the frame reads the still backwards.
d = A · sin(2πx / λ),   det J = 1 − (2πA / λ) cos(2πx / λ),   folds when A > λ / 2π
For one ripple the edge has a number: the push can grow until it equals the ripple's length divided by 2π. Up to there the warp only rearranges the drawing; past it, it folds.
