Try to break the lattice

A phasor runs round a lap of length T and reads a wavetable as it goes. The theorem says that whatever you do to the table, and whatever you do to how the phasor moves inside the lap, the sound's energy stays on the lines k/T. The readout below the spectrum counts the energy that lands between those lines. Walk up to the warp you can draw, try everything, and then take the one step that does get off the lines.

Measured on an exact lap of 256 samples, 32 laps long, so a line on the grid is an exact bin and anything between the lines is real. A digital oscillator at an arbitrary pitch also folds harmonics above half the sample rate back between the lines. That is aliasing, an error of the sampling, not a new partial of the warp.
