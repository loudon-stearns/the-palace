Two tongues, and what damping does to them

One resonator, tuned to 220 Hz, and a pump that can sit at two different rates. Each run starts from a whisper of noise. Silent until you press a button.

The first tongue: the pump an octave above

Pump the stiffness at 440 Hz, twice the resonator's own frequency. The tone that grows out of the noise is at 220 Hz, half the pump: the swing repeats once every two pump cycles. Turn the pump on as a quiet reference tone to hear the octave between them.

The second tongue: the pump in unison

Now the pump runs at 220 Hz, the resonator's own frequency. The tone that grows is still 220 Hz, now in unison with the pump: the swing repeats every pump cycle. At the depth that bloomed at once in step 1, listen to how long this one takes, and raise the depth until it comes quickly.

a ≈ 4   pump = 220 Hz   multiplier > +1
(the stiffness) is about four, because the pump is now half as fast against the same resonator; (the growth per cycle) is positive, so the motion keeps its sign and repeats with the pump.

The first tongue is one step back, at the same resonator.
