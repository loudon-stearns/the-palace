The breathing spring

A mass on a spring, tuned to 220 Hz. Then the spring's stiffness is made to breathe, in and out, and the only question is how fast. Silent until you press a button.

A mass on a spring

Pluck it and it rings at its own frequency, ω₀, here 220 Hz, and fades slowly because a little friction is left in. Nothing else happens. This is the oscillator the Mathieu equation starts from.

ẍ + ω₀² x = 0
(how the mass speeds up) + (the spring's stiffness) × (how far it is stretched) = 0. The stiffness, ω₀², is a fixed number.

Let the spring breathe

Now the stiffness swings up and down four times a second. Pluck it: the pitch wobbles with the breathing, and the ring still fades as it did. A slow breath changes the pitch and nothing else.

ω₀²(t) = ω₀² (1 − 2ε cos Ωt)
(the stiffness now) = (its resting value) × (one, minus twice the depth ε times a cosine at the breathing rate Ω).

Depth at 0 puts you back at step 1.

Speed the breathing up

Nobody plucks it now. All it gets is a whisper of noise, far below hearing. Drag the breathing rate up from 4 Hz. At most rates the whisper stays a whisper. Find the rate where it grows into a tone by itself, then look for a weaker one.

a = (2ω₀ / Ω)²   q = a · ε
Write the same system in the Mathieu equation's own time, and the breathing rate turns into a: (twice the natural frequency ÷ the breathing rate), squared. The depth turns into q.

A rate of 4 Hz puts you back at step 2, without the pluck.

Where does it grow?
