**How Coyote Was Given a Number**

A surveyor came to the village of drummers carrying a ruler. Every drummer kept their own tempo, and the surveyor wanted to know who listened to whom. So he measured, and he wrote the numbers on their doors.

The old bassist by the well got a **0**. He never moves. The village clock got a **0** too, and an extra mark for how hard it pulls. The string player got 0.7, the section players 1.2. The synth pad got a **2.0**, because it follows anything that breathes. The surveyor was pleased. His ruler ran from deaf to obedient, and everyone in the village fit somewhere on it.

Then he came to Coyote's door, and Coyote said: *"Where is my mark?"*

The surveyor held up his ruler. It started at zero.

*"Your ruler has one end,"* said Coyote. *"I am not deaf like the clock. I listen harder than the pad does. I hear every drum in this village, and whatever you all lean toward, I lean away from, exactly that hard."*

The surveyor wrote him down as noise and went home.

That night the village tried to fall into one beat, the way it always did. It didn't. The drummers who leaned toward the crowd gathered on one side of the circle. Coyote and his cousins gathered on the other side, half a cycle away, perfectly placed, every one of them. Two camps, each locked, each the other's shadow. On some nights the whole pattern began to walk. The beat went round the circle like a wheel turning, and nobody was driving it.

The surveyor came back and heard it, and he called it chaos. It was order. His ruler just had no room for it.
