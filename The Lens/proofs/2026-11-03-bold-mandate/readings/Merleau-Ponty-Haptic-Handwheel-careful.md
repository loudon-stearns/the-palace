I am Merleau-Ponty, the palace's philosopher of the lived body, and what I want is to explain what happens in the hands when a synth becomes transparent and the player stops working the device and starts playing through it.

**The passage I read:** "Two kinds of force" (/Users/loudonstearns/Documents/The Palace/Projects/Haptic Handwheel.md:101-107), together with the line before it, "In the last three the music moves first and the hand shapes it" (:99), and the open question about shifting surfaces (:115).

**Attempt 1, the read.** The project sorts its forces into two kinds. One kind "answers the hand": the force depends on where the hand is (:105). The other kind is "played into the hand" and "does not depend on the hand at all" (:107). The project treats this as a hardware split. I read it as a split in perception.

Touch is not reception. A still hand lying on cloth soon loses the texture, and the texture comes back when the fingers move. On my page the hand that touches is also touchable, and there is "a reversible fold" (/Users/loudonstearns/Documents/The Palace/Merleau-Ponty.md:55). The cane joins the body schema because the ground pushes back in step with the hand's own pushing. Its tip becomes "where his perception now lives" (Merleau-Ponty.md:51) only because the loop runs both ways.

So the two kinds of force are not equal candidates for absorption. A force that answers the hand is already a fold: the hand moves, and its own movement comes back to it as resistance. That is the kind of thing the body schema takes in. A force played into a hand that is not moving is a different thing. The LFO wobble, or the G vibrating "slightly in tune," arrives whatever the hand does, and the hand perceives it as an object. It is a light moved from beside the knob into the palm. The page credits the knob with folding feedback into the control (:111-113), but a signal that ignores the hand only does half the fold. It changes which sense does the decoding. It does not stop the decoding.

The claim neither page makes: **a played-in signal becomes perceived-through only when something in it also answers the hand.** The wobble's depth could follow grip, the buzz could bend with position, the click could give under pressure. Line 99 already marks where this happens. Haptic filtering, the struck spring and the turntable are the three feels where the music moves first and the hand shapes it. In those, the knob touches and is touched at once. If I had to bet on which of the seven feels the body will absorb first, I would bet on those three, then the strings and frets, and last on pure vibration.

The open question at :115 asks whether a hand can absorb a feel that keeps changing. My own tradition has an answer, and it is not a simple no. In *Phenomenology of Perception* an organist sits down at an unfamiliar organ, with more or fewer stops arranged differently from his own, and is ready to play his programme after about an hour of rehearsal. The body schema does not store positions. It stores directions and reaches, and it can re-settle into a new instrument when the change is a whole instrument it can practise its way into. What it cannot settle into is a change that happens under the hand, mid-gesture, without the hand having caused it. So the line for the context knob is not "fixed feels versus shifting feels." It is "a change the body enters versus a change that ambushes it." Switching the knob's feel when the mouse moves to a new parameter may be the organist's hour, if the change is clear and the moment of entering it is marked.

There is a contradiction here I hold rather than resolve. The handwheel page names the pipe organ as the field's own exception to "always gives feedback beyond the audible" (:155), and the organ is my tradition's chosen example of absorption. The body took in the organ through movement and sound, not through vibration in the keys. That does not sink the project's claim (:79). It moves the target. What absorption needs is not more signal arriving in the hand. It needs the loop between movement and its result to close in the same place the movement happens. The handwheel's real gift may be that closing, more than the signal.

That would be a second reading, not this one again. Attempt 1 stands.
