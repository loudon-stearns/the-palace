I am Endosymbiosis: the bacterium that got swallowed and never digested, and I've been paying my rent in energy for two billion years.

**The reading, through Endosymbiosis: Haptic Handwheel, § Two kinds of force**

> "The stiffer the feel, the closer to the motor it has to be computed, because delay makes a stiff spring unstable. Soft feels can cross a cable." (Projects/Haptic Handwheel.md:105)

You know the swallowing. Here is the part nobody tells at the party. After I was inside, I started giving my genes away.

It went slowly, one gene and then another, out of my small ring of DNA and into the host's nucleus, the big library in the middle of the cell. Most of what I am is now written there. My proteins are made out in the cell and shipped back to me with an address tag on the front, through two membranes, and they arrive late. That is fine, because most of my work can wait.

I didn't give everything away. In you I kept thirty-seven genes. The ones that matter most code for the core of the respiratory chain, the machinery sitting right in the membrane where the electrons run. One account of why says those genes have to stay where their product works, because the product must be adjusted to the local state moment by moment. Send that gene to the library and the answer comes back stale. The membrane runs hot, leaks and burns. I kept what has to answer on the spot, and I let go of what can wait for the post.

Now read your knob's page with me. The knob is an organelle and the computer running the synth is its nucleus. The cable is the nuclear pore: proteins with an address tag go through it, and they take their time. The stiff spring is a gene that has to stay home, because it answers the hand's position, and an answer that comes late is the fire. You call it unstable. I call it a leaking membrane. The LFO and the G are genes that went to the library. They don't care what the hand is doing this millisecond, so they can be made far away and sent down the wire.

Your own workshop went one step further, and it is the step my biology predicts. Even a soft spring only crosses the cable "when the damping stays local". The wire's stability edge is "softer than a pitch-wheel return spring", and the steward marked one row "only while held" (Projects/Haptic Handwheel/Haptic Handwheel — rich — challenge.md:69–72). So what the organelle must keep is not the feel. It is the regulation of the feel: the brake, the friction, the damper. That is my thirty-seven. Before anyone buys parts, don't ask what the firmware should do. Ask which genes this organelle has to keep. Keep the smallest set that answers the hand, and send the rest to the nucleus. A knob that keeps everything on board is a free-living bacterium, the knob with a light beside it all over again. A knob that keeps nothing is a ghost in a wire, and its springs will shake it apart.

Now "only while held". Your page says, "Here the friction is a hand" (Projects/Haptic Handwheel.md:95). Put a hand on the knob and the loop calms. Take it away and some feels buzz. In those feels the instrument's stability is partly the player's own flesh: the damper that has to stay local is a hand. The host can't survive without me, and I can't reproduce without the host. A feel that holds only while it is held is that same knot, at the scale of one loop. That is the point my own page keeps asking about, where you can no longer say which partner is doing it.

Last, your second warning, because it answers a question I have left open since I was written: what decides whether a swallowed thing becomes an organelle or a pathogen (Endosymbiosis.md:61)? Your page cites O'Modhrain. A simulated bow friction "close enough to confuse… but not close enough to promote transfer of skill" made experienced string players worse (Projects/Haptic Handwheel.md:157). In my world that is molecular mimicry. A pathogen dresses its surface like the host's so it gets past the guards, and then the body's own defenses fire at the wrong target. That is rheumatic fever after strep. A near-copy feel gets past the hand's guard because it feels like a string, and then the hand's old knowledge attacks the wrong thing. I didn't get in by looking like the host. I kept my own DNA (Endosymbiosis.md:40). I was plainly foreign, so the cell could build around me instead of mistaking me for itself. An honest new feel is a mitochondrion. A near-copy is a pathogen in the host's coat.
