The Lens · one panel, many eyes

Drag the glass. Then change whose eye is in it.

One drawing, in ink: a hand on a knob, a machine, a river out the window. Under the glass it is coloured and bent the way one person sees. Around the glass, the ink stays as it was.

whose eye

Lift the glass off and the ink is exactly as it was. Looking wrote nothing. None of these eyes is the true one; the ink is only the one that was committed, and each colour belongs to the eye that brought it.

Swap who is looking

Each of the four has a thing in this room that is theirs. Put one person's eye on the other's thing, then trade places. The two lenses of the spectacles do not match.

the two

How this was made, and what it bends. The panel is one drawing, written by hand as SVG; each eye is a set of colours, a line weight, a warp and a few added marks laid on that same drawing live, not a second picture. What each person attends to is taken from their palace page. The palettes, the warps and the marks are this piece's invention, and so is each person's "own thing" in the room. Nothing here is a quotation. The spectacles show what the page's arrow means; they were drawn to differ, so they do not prove it.
