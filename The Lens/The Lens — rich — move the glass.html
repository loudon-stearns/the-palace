The test, blind

One glass, Kuramoto Coupling, three hidden rules behind ◇ 1, ◇ 2 and ◇ 3: one reading written for each paragraph, one fixed formula, one that borrows the paragraph's words. You haven't seen any of them, and the three are the same length, give or take a tenth, and open the same way, so neither length nor form gives a rule away. Three trials, each held on a different paragraph, with the rules shuffled and the formula and borrower worded afresh each time. First every trial at one look, the glass held; then all three again with the glass free to move, changing any mark you want.

Before you start: chance and the rule. Guessing gets one mark in three right on average, so 3 of the 9 one-look marks is what luck alone scores. The page's test is to move the glass. My reading of it, not the page's words, is that one look may not be enough and moving should be. The verdict counts all nine marks: moving helped if your after-moving total beats your one-look total by 2 or more; worse if it falls by 2 or more; anything closer is no clear difference at this size.
