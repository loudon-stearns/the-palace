## The pieces

### switch or scan · The morph is a 2D linear interpolation — **keep**

1. **Test or decoration?** A test, twice. Step 3 renders the same sweep both ways with the engine it plays and measures energy between the harmonics. If picking and blending came out alike, the page's switcher/scanner line would be wrong. Step 5 measures what the blend costs at the middle of each real pair and splits it into crossfade arithmetic and phase. That could have found no dip, or found it only in very different frames.
2. **Faithful?** Yes, and where it disagrees with the text it says so and holds the disagreement as an offer (1), not as a smoothed line. Three things the next round should fix in words:
   - The caption says the moved saw's "middle loses every odd harmonic". That is only true moved half a cycle. Say "moved half a cycle".
   - Step 5's headline says "the top sits under the ends, by 1.7 to 3.5 dB: the dulling the page names, measured." The ruler counts a drop in level, arithmetic included, not a tilt. In cheeze → aguitar the bottom dips 4.17 dB against the top's 3.04; in birds → vgsqu, 2.69 against 2.67. Those middles are thinner, not duller. The paragraph says so further down; the headline should not claim more than that.
   - The "measurement's own floor" (−122.5 dB held) and the 20 dB gap hold at the pitch the piece plays, fs/512. There the 1024-sample frame is read every second sample, so `readFrame` never interpolates, and every alias lands on a harmonic line. The ruler is blind to the read's own aliasing by design. That is a good choice for isolating Position, but it should be said where the numbers are quoted (see offers 2 and 5).
3. **Walks up?** Yes. One frame alone, then Position picking, then Position blending, then the phase toy, then the test. Each step adds one idea.

Runs clean, hushes when hidden, sound on a click only. The step 4 readout (−6.08 dB over harmonics 1–40) differs from the caption's −6.02 (all 511) only by band, and it is labelled.

### which order is brightness · Frame ordering carries the axis's meaning — **remake**

1. **Test or decoration?** A test. Step 2 puts the axis to the ear with the names hidden and shuffled. Step 4 measures, along the whole knob, whether the sorted order only rises and how evenly. Either could have failed.
2. **Faithful?** Not yet, in two places:
   - Step 4's *do* says "sweep slowly through its fourth fifth, from birds to vgsqu". Sorted, the order is cheeze, aguitar, birds, vgsqu, saw, bitreduced, so birds → vgsqu is the **third** fifth. The check asserts index 2 (`check.mjs:102`). The line sends the listener to the wrong stretch of the knob. Fix: "third fifth".
   - The finding says the waveform walk is "the smoothest sweep in sound … as the page expects of a walk". What it measured is the smallest steps sample by sample (1.86 against 3.00 rms) and the shallowest middles (−1.17 against −2.33 dB). It did not measure smoothness as heard. By brightness, the walk is the roughest of the three orders. Its fifths move 0.57, 3.16, −0.28, −4.63 and 3.92 octaves of centroid, 12.6 octaves in all, against the sorted order's 4.9 (1.19, 0.57, 2.16, 0.72, 0.28), with a 4.6-octave fall inside one fifth. The page's TSP order is "perceptually close" (`1D Wavetable Scanning.md:58`). Rms distance between samples is not perceptual: a saw and the same saw moved half a cycle are far apart by it and the same by ear, which is the piece's own sister step. Fix: in step 3's *say*, quote the page ("perceptually close") and then say this walk measures closeness sample by sample instead. In the finding, replace "the smoothest sweep in sound" with "the smallest steps in the waveform and the shallowest middles", and add the brightness the walk travels against the sort's.
3. **Walks up?** Yes. Brightness along one blend alone, then three hidden orders by ear, then named, then the test.

Runs clean, hushes, sound on a click only. "Where the blend overshoots a frame and settles back" fits the first fall (p 0.175–0.201, centroid 5.19 → 4.93, just before aguitar). The second is a dip just after vgsqu (p 0.600–0.629, 32.74 → 32.24). That is close enough not to need a change.

## The offers, recomputed

1. **The dip is phase.** Numbers confirmed by a second formula. Per harmonic, the middle's magnitude is |a + b·e^{iφ}|/2. The AKWF saw moved 0.1 cycle: −1.36 dB at the middle (an ideal 1/k saw gives −1.37). Moved 0.5: −6.02 (ideal −6.03). The top band (h8 up): dips 3.04, 2.18, 2.67, 1.70 and 3.54 dB; phase 0.35, 1.91, 0.99, 1.34 and 3.22; arithmetic 2.69, 0.27, 1.68, 0.36 and 0.32. **Disputed in framing.** The page does not say the dip is *limited to* very different frames. It says the blend "dulls sharp transients between very different frames" (`:48`), which places the cost without saying it happens only there. The offer argues against a restriction the page doesn't state. Its strongest evidence is also not the one it leads with. aguitar → birds is the closest pair here, but two unrelated instruments are still very different. The moved saw is the clean case: it has no spectral difference at all and still loses 1.36 dB. Lead with that. The last point stands: what the blend smooths is the step in Position, not a mismatch at the wrap. bitreduced's own wrap jump (first sample 0.249, last −0.065) is in the frame and in every blend that contains it. The suggested sentence is fair once "dulls" is read as "loses level": the ruler measures level, not tilt.
2. **Zipping measured.** Confirmed: 5 s at 48 kHz, pick −26.5, pick at the cycle's start −26.5, blend −69.1 dB (44.1 kHz: −26.2, −26.2, −67.5). The page's claim is about **continuity**, "zipping between sounds and morphing through them" (`:46`), steps against a morph, not zipper noise from a stepped control. The measure is a fair proxy for the step: it counts the energy each step throws off the harmonic lines. It does not count the plateaus a listener hears in a slow switcher sweep. Two caveats for the sentence. First, it holds at the pieces' pitch, fs/512 (93.75 Hz), where the read's own aliases sit on harmonics. At 440 Hz and 48 kHz I get pick −33.1 against blend −48.4, 15 dB apart, not 42, because the read's aliasing now lands between the harmonics too. Name the pitch. Second, "at the cycle's start" is not a zero crossing for vgsqu (first sample 0.469) or bitreduced (0.249), so the careful switcher still jumps samples there. The result stands, since a step costs the same wherever in the cycle it falls, but the sentence shouldn't suggest the careful switcher was click-free.
3. **Monotonic but uneven.** Confirmed: two falls, the larger 0.073 octave; 44.0% of the climb in birds → vgsqu; 6% in the last fifth. The page's claim, a brightness knob (`:56`), **holds**. The 44% is a fact about the maker's six frames. "Sorting gives the order, not the spacing" is true of any library by construction. This is an added cost, not a correction, and it is worth adding as such.
4. **TSP.** Numbers confirmed: 1.862 against 3.003 rms walked, 38.0% less; middles −1.17 against −2.33 dB; the feature walk equals the centroid order. **Disputed as a change to the page.** The page claims the TSP pattern exists, with "perceptually close" as its criterion (`:58`), and names no distance. Walking over raw waveforms by rms is a design choice, and not a perceptual one (see the remake above). By brightness this walk travels 12.6 octaves to the sort's 4.9. The feature walk collapsing to the sort is predicted by the page's own line 62: centroid and flatness "are measuring overlapping things". The suggested "over the waveforms, not over features" would swap the page's criterion for one the piece shows is rougher to the ear by brightness. Decline that half. The other half is a real addition worth carrying: the order decides which in-between sounds exist, because the blend makes them from neighbours.
5. **Lost branches.** The principle is confirmed and the number disputed as evidence. Catmull-Rom across frames against a straight line, 5 s sweep: −110.9 against −69.1 dB, confirmed. But the held blend's −122.4 dB comes from fs/512, where any held periodic read sits on the harmonics, aliased or not. It cannot see aliasing. At 440 Hz and 48 kHz, held: vgsqu −52.7, saw −36.7, the blend at 0.5 −43.6 dB between harmonics. The blend lands between its two frames and adds none. That is the evidence the offer needs. Use those numbers in place of −122.4. The suggested sentence stands.

**Against the landed Wavetable Oscillator face.** The two faces agree on what a moving Position does. Held, Position puts nothing between the harmonics: −122.5 here, and "nothing lands between" in "position as a second clock". Swept slowly, it moves the harmonics' strengths, with new lines inside f0/4 that both rulers leave uncounted (the Oscillator face puts them a quarter of a hertz off). Faster, it spreads: here −57.1 at 2 s and −39.2 at 0.5 s, pointing on to the Oscillator's second clock. Driven at an unrelated rate there, −7.02 dB, the blend's own algebra. No contradiction. Switch or scan's step 3 points across to it in so many words.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt rather than busy. Every section that carries a piece carries one, and each made piece walks from one concept to a test that could have failed. Switch or scan is the strongest thing here: it puts the page's central sentence, Position picks the blend, under the ear and under a ruler, and its phase toy, a saw blended with itself, is the clearest reason the middle of a morph is a sound neither frame has. The ordering piece's hidden-names step is the right instinct, the ear before the eye. Its finding reaches past what it measured, calling the waveform walk smooth in sound when by brightness it is the roughest. One round fixes that. The offers are mostly real and well measured. Two need their pitch named, one leans on a number that can't see what it claims, and one would trade the page's perceptual criterion for a sample-by-sample one. Not verified: nothing here was heard. The level, the switcher's steps and the blend's dip by ear are untested, and so are Safari and Firefox.
