# Challenge: State Machine's rich face, proposal round 2026-11-03

Challenger: claude-opus-5-5. I did not make this face. I judged it from the page, the one made piece, the one gathered figure and the maker's commit body (`94738e6a`), and used nothing else of the maker's.

## The pieces

### four problems, one neuron: The Core Pattern. **Remake** (small, one round)

1. **Test or decoration?** A test, reached at the last step. Steps 1–4 show the four problems one at a time. Step 5 puts three one-line bugs into the same engine, and each could have come out differently if the section were wrong. One already did: the latch put on the edge the section names plays sharp. The piece says plainly that the section's ranking ("most common", "second most common") can't be tested. It doesn't fake a test for it.
2. **Faithful?** Mostly. It says where it disagrees with the text ("In the three-state Stage 3 … there the sentence is right; in this four-state machine the latch belongs on recovery → charge"), and the disagreement is held as offer 1, not smoothed over. The walk takes the problems as memory, behaviour, transitions, actions, but the page numbers them memory, transitions, behaviour, actions. That's a taste note: behaviour before exits is the easier climb. Two of the piece's own claims are wrong in places, and that's why this is a remake:
   - **The ambiguous transition is not inaudible just above its floor.** The verdict says "Below 248.7 Hz every note plays 248.7 Hz" and "Above … the bug can't be heard". Both are wrong in a band from 248.1 to 249.3 Hz (0.1 Hz scan, 48 kHz). In that band the threshold and the stray recovery exit open on the same sample, so `go = 2`, the machine jumps from charge straight to fall, and the note plays 319.7 Hz, +435 cents. The slider's log scale steps about 0.5 Hz there, so a reader can land on it. If they do, the verdict prints "at 248.5 Hz this one plays 319.7 Hz" right after saying every lower note plays 248.7. **Fix:** in the ambiguous verdict, name the band: two exits true at once, so the machine skips the rise, which breaks the strict progression step 3 promised. It is the clearest case of "ambiguous" the piece has. Add 248.5 Hz to the check (`checks.mjs` tests only 260, 300 and 380 above the floor).
   - **The silent readout doesn't say "from rest".** `measureMachine` always runs from rest. At τ = 0.1 ms that matters. Starting from rest, the first charge has the whole period to fill, so λ^m drops below half an ulp and nothing sounds below 317.5 Hz. A machine already running gives the charge only what the spike and recovery leave (121 samples fewer), and with τ turned down to 0.1 ms while it plays it keeps sounding down to 176.5 Hz. In step 5, then, a reader who drags τ to 0.1 ms at 200 Hz while it plays reads "silent: the charge stops 2.2e-16 short of threshold" and hears 200 Hz. The "what you hear" line shows the mismatch. **Fix:** label the silent line "from rest", as the line that says it plays already is, or say both.
3. **Does it walk up?** Yes, well. It opens on one integer and a counter (a pulse; take the History away and it sticks in LOW). Each step adds one problem, and each step's toggle takes you back to the step before ("With the latch on, this is step 3's machine exactly"). The test comes last and the step rail stays open.

Also checked: it runs clean, makes no audio context before a click, and suspends when the page is hidden. It needs no files, licenses nothing, and is sized by the kit (1272 px at 760, 1282 at 1130). One more note, not a remake reason: the readouts are computed at 48 kHz and the sound runs at the device rate. At 44.1 kHz the heard floor is 246.4 Hz, not 248.7.

  - **Bug 1, ambiguous: 248.70 Hz, derived.** With `(phase == 3)` gone, the recovery's exit, `(t+1) ≥ refr ∧ V > 0.3·depth`, can fire in any state once that state has run `refr` samples. The rise (15) and fall (34) end sooner. The charge starts above 0.3·depth, because the recovery only hands over when it is, and climbs, so its voltage half is always open. So the charge is cut at ⌈refr⌉ samples, and the slowest the machine can go is ⌈refr⌉ + ⌈rise⌉ + ⌈fall⌉ + ⌈refr⌉ = 72 + 15 + 34 + 72 = 193 samples: 48000/193 = **248.70 Hz**. At 110 Hz that's +1412.3 c. What sets the floor is the **refractory timer**, not the membrane's time constant. refr 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.5 ms gives a floor of 331.0 / 248.7 / 166.1 Hz, predicted and measured, and τ = 20 ms changes nothing. At 44.1 kHz it's 179 samples, 246.37 Hz. The exception is the 248.1–249.3 Hz band above.
  - **Bug 2, missing guard:** a 1 ms pull to τ = 0 makes V NaN at sample 24029. The machine is stuck in phase 3 with 0 firings after, and V stays NaN once τ is back (`V·λ` of NaN). With the guard, the same pull gives 154 firings in the next 1.4 s. Guarded, τ = 0.1 ms at 110 Hz is silent, 2.2e-16 short, with no NaN. The APO face's stage 4 is silent there too: 0 firings in 2 s. The first note that sounds from rest is 317.5 Hz. Turned down while running, the lowest that keeps sounding is 176.5 Hz.
  - **Bug 3, latch:** on the fall's end, +97.4 / +146.3 / +148.5 / +124.9 / +77.4 c at 55 / 110 / 165 / 220 / 300 Hz. No bug: −0.5 / +1.4 / −0.5 / +1.4 / 0.0 c. Over 55–300 Hz the range is **+77 to +149 c**. The commit body's "+97 to +149 c from 55 to 300 Hz" is wrong at the top end, but the offer text gives +77 at 300 Hz correctly. As a behaviour: silent at 55, 110, 220 and 380 Hz, still in charge after 20 s, 1.3e-14 short. I checked the piece's step-5 algebra for this case, 1 − Vₙ = (λ − λᵐ/(τ(1 − λᵐ)))·(1 − Vₙ₋₁), from the drive formula with τ(1 − λ) = 1, and it holds.

## The offers

**1 · The Core Pattern, no-claim-edit: confirmed, and the reading is fair.** Numbers as above. Is it literal-minded? I don't think so. The sentence isn't loose prose. It names a variable, an edge and a purpose, and it comes four lines after the same section numbers the oscillator's four phases with Phase 3 as the spring. Read in that section's own numbering, "when Phase 2 ends" is fall → recovery, and the latch there costs +146 c at 110 Hz. The palace's other account puts it elsewhere: Action Potential Oscillator.md:70 says V_start_actual is captured "at each cycle boundary", which in four states is recovery → charge, where neuropulse.dsp:113 latches it. My guess at where the wording came from: Stage 3, where Phase 2's end *is* the cycle boundary, or a merge with the separate "latch pattern" at "the spike fall → next phase transition" (Action Potential Oscillator.md:71). The March Gen~ Stage 4 code isn't in the palace, so I can't check what it did. The offer changes a fact about the oscillator, not the section's claim about actions and behaviours, so "no-claim-edit" holds, but it should be shown to Loudon with the Stage 3 / Stage 4 split spelled out, as the proposed sentence does.

**2 · Boxes and Lines, claim-change: confirmed.** The Max reference (gen common operators, `?`/`switch`): "If the first argument is true, the second argument will be output. Otherwise, the third argument will be output." That's C's and JavaScript's `cond ? a : b` order. The Faust manual: `_,_ : select2(s)` with "`0` for the first signal, `1` for the second one", so `select2(cond, if_false, if_true)`, and the compile in checks.mjs gives `select2(c,10,20)` → `c ? 20 : 10` and `ba.if(c,10,20)` → `c ? 10 : 20`. The page's "opposite of most programming languages' ternary operator" is wrong about gen~. The reversed one is Faust's `select2`, which neuropulse.dsp:104–113 uses for every transition. "Verify it every time" should stay.

**3 · Best Practices, claim-change: confirmed, with one correction to scope.** The NaN hold is real (above). The silent floor agrees with Signal-Rate CV Architecture's landed finding. Same mechanism: λᵐ under half an ulp, a drive aimed exactly at threshold, and rounding decides. Same verdict at 110 Hz: silent. The edges differ by engine, as rounding-decided edges will: the plan's Stage 2 is silent to 282.8 Hz (Signal-Rate challenge.md:70); the real neuropulse.dsp in double is silent to about 294 Hz and plays from 311 (challenge.md:77); this engine, from rest, plays from 317.5 Hz. The correction: "nothing sounds below about 318 Hz in this engine" is true **from rest** only. Running, the edge is 176.5 Hz. The proposed sentence ("A guard keeps the numbers finite; it does not keep a transition reachable…") stands as written. Worth adding from the Signal-Rate record: Faust's default single precision is silent through 440 Hz at this floor (challenge.md:78), and 0.1 ms is the instrument's own knob minimum (neuropulse.dsp:51).

**4 · Open Questions, claim-change: confirmed for what it measures. The question also reaches things the test doesn't.** 6.0e-8 is right (the figure; scroll.md:206; synchrony identical). It is also about 2⁻²⁴, the size of float32 rounding, and the harness reads both outputs as float32 and applies the codebox's 0.4 gain after that rounding (check_codebox.py:22–29). So the residual is probably the output format, not the machines. That's my inference; I didn't run it. The test isolates exactly what Q3 asks, threading versus branching, at equal precision with inputs moving inside the render. For this oscillator that's notation. What it doesn't reach is what comes with the Faust toolchain rather than the functional style. Single precision by default: samples part after 9 ms, the τ floor goes silent through 440 Hz. Sliders at block rate: compiled neuropulse.dsp computes its hslider math once per block (`fSlow`), so a CV on a Faust slider is not per-sample unless it comes in as an audio input. And the codebox ran in a Node shim, not in RNBO. Vectorisation is Q1's business and is about cost, not sound. Proposed: keep the question and add the answer scoped: "for the Action Potential Oscillator, at the same precision, the two agree to 6e-8 over a 30 s sweep; what differs in practice is Faust's defaults (single precision, sliders read once a block), not the threading." The Q1 pointer is fair, but population_audition.py:84–101 masks a two-state machine (charging, refractory), not the four-state one.

## The face as a whole

It makes the page's idea felt. One neuron carries all four problems. The diagram and the code light up as it runs, and the slowed view runs the same engine as the sound, so what you see is what you hear. The last step does what the section only asserts: it moves a bug one line and lets you find where it shows, always at an edge. It isn't busy. Each step has one thing to hear, and the controls are only the ones that step needs. It also pushed back on the page three times (the latch edge, the gen~ order, the guard's limit), and all three hold up. The remake is small and about the piece's own honesty: name the band where the ambiguous bug skips a state, and say "from rest" on the silent line. The gathered figure answers the third open question for this oscillator. Its caption should say "at the same precision".
