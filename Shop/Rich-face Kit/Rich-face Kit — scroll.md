### 2026-11-15 — What a piece can reach from inside its frame

The kit's zoom-out. The starters were written against the browsers of 2023 and 2024, so this page asks the browser it runs in which newer features a piece can reach from inside its frame: WebGPU, import maps, a worker from a Blob, OffscreenCanvas, WASM SIMD, WebCodecs, an audio render size, View Transitions, anchor positioning, and a few more. It makes no sound; one line opens a silent audio context to read its size, and closes it.

Headless Chromium 147 said yes to nearly all of them, in a frame and alone. One thing differed: a module worker from a Blob fails in an opaque frame (a piece that declares nothing) and works in a same-origin one. The audio render-size option was accepted and ignored; cross-origin isolation is off, so there is no SharedArrayBuffer. Your own Chrome on the scroll may answer differently from headless, so press the button there.

**For Loudon: of the features your own browser says yes to here, which one would you most like to see a piece try next?**

Nobody has seen this outside a headless browser.

### 2026-11-15 — A piece that plays a second file

Until now a maker synthesized every sound inside the one HTML file, to stay inside the rule. This proof declares a wav beside it in its head (`piece:files pluck.wav`), plays it on a click, and beside it plays the same pluck made in the page, sample for sample.

Framed on a real piece origin, the way a scroll frames it, the file loaded and the two nulled to −91.5 dBFS, the wav's own 16-bit rounding. An undeclared file beside the piece was refused. The kit's checker could not see any of this: it frames every piece opaque, so it reports the piece's own declared file as blocked. That is now on the card.

**For Loudon: on your scroll, does "Play from the file" sound at all, and does it sound the same as the one made in the page?**

Nobody has heard this.
