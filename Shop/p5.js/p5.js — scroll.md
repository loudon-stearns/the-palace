### 2026-11-15 — The same sketch in p5 1.10.0 and p5 2.4.0, side by side

The zoom-out. p5 2.4.0 came out on 2026-11-11 and the shelf still holds 1.10.0, so one sketch is written twice and run side by side in one page: a closed curve that leans toward your pointer, then a warp shader that ripples it by how far right the pointer is. Each half uses its own version's words. In 2.x the setup is async, the closed curve is `splineVertex` with `endShape(CLOSE)` instead of `curveVertex` with three points repeated, and the shader is written in JavaScript (p5.strands) instead of a GLSL string. The page lists every change in a table under the panels.

Measured headless: both versions load in one page and draw within 0.06% of the same pixels at the same pointer, and the warp changes 4.1% of the pixels in each. A forced frame took a median 19.6 ms in 1.10.0 and 3.0 ms in 2.4.0, on a software GPU, so that is a comparison, not a speed on the Mac. Two things 2.4.0 does on its own had to be worked around. It fetches a translation file beside the page as it loads, which fails with two console errors. And its `redraw()` has to be awaited, so the card's headless seam changes under 2.x. Both are on the ledger, owed a review. Nobody has seen this.

**The question: do the two panels look and move the same to you, and if one is better, which one, and what do you see?**

Whether the pin moves to 2.x is your call, not this proof's. WebGPU mode, also new in 2.x, was not tried.

### 2026-11-15 — A WebGL field that a mouse or a finger can press

Two items from the card's "Not yet shown to hold" line in one proof: WebGL mode, and touch on a phone. It is a lit field of boxes seen from above at a tilt. The boxes under the mouse, or under each finger at once, sink and warm, then spring back when you leave. It is still until you press start.

Measured headless: the canvas is WebGL2, and the mouse presses the boxes under it. On an emulated phone, two fingers were both seen in p5's `touches` and pressed two places at once. A finger dragged on the field left the page still (0 px of scroll), and the same drag on the text scrolled it, which shows the check can fail. Take the canvas's `touch-action: none` away and the field drag scrolled 584 px. The fingers were emulated, sent through Chrome's own input pipeline. No real phone or real finger has touched it. Nobody has seen this.

**The question: does a finger press the field the way the mouse does, the same weight and no lag, with the page holding still under your finger?** On the Mac, the mouse half alone is still worth a look. A phone may not reach the piece origin, which runs on the Mac.
