## Cold start

COLD START — this work has not begun; no prior state, no tried-and-rejected. The ten points below are the framing Loudon gave in the sit, carried in his sense. They are the content of the rewrite, not a scope limit; how the page is shaped around them is the catcher's craft, shown to Loudon before it is written.

1. **Captions: replace Hard Rule 2 with four rules.** They came from a sorting game he played (Caption Court; a rough, not kept).
   - Invite, don't tell: questions that challenge the viewer to try something with him. His example: "Can you hear 58% swing? Let's try to hear swing together."
   - No absolutes ("every", "never", "all", "none") unless absolutely true.
   - No hooks about the maker ("I almost didn't post this").
   - Lift, don't shame: never assume what the viewer can or can't hear.
   - Over all four: honesty is the floor; above it, playful, odd, funny and irreverent are welcome.
   - The mechanism is in [[Playful Interface Design]] (`Playful Interface Design.md:71`, Loewenstein: curiosity dies if the answer never comes). Bait opens a gap it never closes; an invitation opens one the video closes with the viewer. Hard Rule 1 (no withheld payoff) already says the closing half. That is a candidate link to Playful Interface Design, with that reason, and Loudon asked to question how the two pages intersect, not just link them.
2. **Hard Rule 5 is from the brand era.** The footer "Loudon Live · Autodidact Polymaths" was retired in 2026-07 (`_ops/loudon-live/design-system/SKILL.md:49`), and house style held as law contradicts his 2026-11-08 sentence on [[Drafting Table Design System]] ("which look is best for this exact context… I am an artist trying to explore", `Palace development/Drafting Table Design System.md:77`). The rule becomes: ask which look fits this context.
3. **Many kinds of posts, not one 60-minute stream.** The workflow, outputs and promotion unit all assume one long stream. A new format he wants: a one-on-one student session, recorded with the student's consent ahead of time, with focused re-shot "asides" edited in that show each concept cleanly, so the viewer sees how he works with a student and also gets the clean version.
4. **Consent and privacy, as a hard rule.** A session with another person is used only with their yes, and never shows their name or likeness unless they ask. Whether their yes covers publishing their voice or music is their separate question. Session recordings, transcripts and any student audio never enter the palace, which is a public repo.
5. **Say uncertainty out loud.** Or check the manual and make a small on-camera test. When hunting for a control, make a little test to confirm.
6. **Tradeoffs, not right and wrong.** Frame them as fidelity against creative flow, leaning toward flow and joy. Link candidate: [[Quantization ↔ Precision vs Flow]]. [[Sidechain ↔ Conversation]] is a natural neighbor for sidechain lessons.
7. **Connect each concept to its neighbors.** Masking leads to Bregman's auditory scene analysis; attack and release lead to synth envelopes and RMS. The Post-producer flags the details a teacher skipped by leaning on a student's knowledge. In the dry run those were: sidechain input, envelope follower, masking, buses and dry/wet, Q, reading a spectrum, threshold.

baton and the entry, not this ceremony — so the catcher's obligations live where

A pickup has two beats: **claim** it when you catch it, **close** it when the move lands. The card stays visible in between — a claim that ages with no close is how a dropped baton (a "fumble") surfaces instead of vanishing. (A parent-entry baton that was never announced on the board has no card; skip the board posts — just remove the pointer and delete the file at close, step 8.)
