Standing on a shake

The page's first example of Floquet engineering: a pendulum balanced upside down falls, but shake its pivot up and down fast enough and it stands. Four steps: the fall, a slow shake that changes nothing, a fast one that holds it up, and the test of "fast enough" against the multipliers of one shake period. The rod is 20 cm; there is no friction anywhere in this piece.

1 · Balanced, and falling

Stand the pendulum on its pivot, six degrees off straight up, and let go. Nothing holds it there: every degree of lean makes gravity pull it further. Read once every fiftieth of a second, the lean is multiplied by the same factor each time. That factor is a multiplier, and it is outside the unit circle.

the pendulum
the multipliers of one fiftieth of a second
θ'' = (g / L) · θ ⇒ ρ = e^(±√(g/L) · T)
the lean grows at a rate set by gravity over the rod’s length; over any stretch of time T, one multiplier grows by e to that rate times T and the other shrinks by the same

2 · A slow shake

Now move the pivot up and down, 2 cm each way, five times a second. When the pivot accelerates upward the pendulum feels heavier; downward, lighter. At this rate gravity still wins every cycle.

the pendulum · h = 2 cm, 5 Hz
the multipliers of one shake period
θ'' = ((g − hΩ² cos Ωt) / L) · θ
the pull that tips it now breathes with the shake: gravity, less the pivot’s own acceleration, over the rod’s length

3 · A fast shake

Same stroke, faster. Somewhere past fifteen shakes a second the pendulum stops falling and wobbles about the top instead. Nothing in the static pendulum has a stable upside-down state; the shaking made one. With no friction here it never settles, it just wobbles, and its two multipliers sit on the unit circle, not inside it.

the pendulum
the multipliers of one shake period
(h · Ω)² > 2 g L
Kapitza’s rule: it stands when the pivot’s top speed, squared, beats twice gravity times the rod’s length

4 · How fast is enough?

The test. Every stroke and every rate, each decided by the multipliers of one shake period: shaded where they sit on the circle and the pendulum stands. Over it, Kapitza's rule, the curve where hΩ = √(2gL). The page says "fast enough". The map says whether that is the whole condition.
