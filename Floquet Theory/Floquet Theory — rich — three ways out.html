Floquet · stability boundaries and bifurcations

Three ways out of the circle

The page says a stability boundary is where a multiplier crosses the unit circle, and that the kind of crossing, at −1, at +1, or as a pair off the axis, sets the kind of thing that happens, each with its own sound. Five steps build up to that: one damped ring and its two multipliers, the pump pushing one out at −1, then at +1, then a pair leaving together, and last the test. Every run is rendered here and played as drawn.

1 · One ring, two multipliers

A resonator struck once, with no pump yet. Watch it once per pump period (440 times a second) and each look finds the ring smaller by the same factor and turned by the same angle. Those two numbers are the multipliers, a pair, and with damping they sit on a circle a little inside the unit circle. The pitch sets their angle: at 220 Hz, half the pump's rate, the pair sits together near −1; at 440 Hz, the pump's own rate, near +1. The plane is drawn with its radius stretched about the unit circle, ten times, so a difference in the third decimal shows.

2 · Out at −1

Now pump the stiffness at 440 Hz, twice the ring's 220. The pair that sat together near −1 splits along the real axis: one multiplier slides toward 0, the other toward −1. Below the threshold the pump only slows the fade; at it the ring hangs; past it the ring grows out of the whisper. The stroboscope shows why this is period doubling: each look finds the state on the other side.

3 · Out at +1

Tune the ring up to 440 Hz, the pump's own rate, and pump again. Now the pair splits near +1, and the one that leaves does so through +1. It takes a much deeper pump with the same damping, because this is the second tongue. Past it the ring grows again, but the stroboscope tells a different story: each look finds the state on the same side.

4 · A pair off the axis

The third way out is a pair leaving together, off the real axis. Set the ring between the tongues, at 341 Hz, and push the pump as hard as you like: the pair turns, but it never moves out. Their product is fixed by the damping alone, and a pair off the axis shares one size, so the pump can't touch it. Only one thing moves it: turn the damping negative, a ring that feeds itself. Then the pair leaves together and the ring grows at its own pitch, owing nothing to the pump.

5 · What each crossing makes

The test. For each way out, the multiplier the monodromy predicts against the growth the engine measures, the pitch against the one the crossing calls for, and what settles once the soft brake catches the ring: eight runs from eight different whispers, read on the stroboscope. One point that flips side each look is a ring at twice the period. One point that stays put, but on a side the whisper chose, is two mirror rings born at once. A closed curve of points is a ring on a torus, keeping a pitch of its own.
