Floquet · Floquet and Bloch

Same equation, read twice

The page says Floquet's theorem and Bloch's are the same theorem with time in place of space. For one pair it is more than that: the Mathieu equation is word for word the wave equation of an electron in a cosine lattice. Four steps: a wave in the lattice, the same numbers read as a pumped spring, the bands beside the tongues, and the test, where the two sides are computed by methods that share nothing.

1 · A wave in a lattice

A row of identical cells, each with the same bump of potential, 2q cos 2s, one every π along the line. Send in a wave of energy a. At most energies it travels: it wobbles with the lattice but keeps its size, cell after cell. At some it can't: the wave either dies away or, followed the other way, swells, cell after cell, by the same factor. Those energies are the gaps.

the potential (grey) and the wave across 12 cells
−ψ''(s) + 2q cos(2s) · ψ(s) = a · ψ(s)
the wave’s bend, plus the lattice’s bump times the wave, equals its energy times the wave

2 · Read it as time

Move one term across and nothing changes but the name of the letter. Call s time instead of place, and the lattice becomes a spring whose stiffness is pumped, one pump period per cell. The energy becomes the spring's pitch squared. The curve below is the same array of numbers either way; the switch only relabels it. Look at the wave once per cell, or the spring once per pump period, and each look turns by one fixed angle: Bloch's crystal momentum k, Floquet's quasi-frequency.

3 · Bands beside tongues

Left, the crystal's band structure: every allowed energy a at every momentum k, found from plane waves, with no integrating at all. Right, on the same vertical scale, the spring's growth per pump period at each a, found by integrating one period, with no plane waves at all. Drag the level across both.

4 · Is it the same theorem?

The test. If the two readings are one theorem, every gap edge the plane waves find at k = 0 or k = 1 is a tongue edge the integrator finds where half the trace of M reaches ±1, and inside a band, half the trace is cos kπ at the k the plane waves used. Two methods that share no code.

ρ = e^(μT) , μ = i·k ⇔ tr M / 2 = cos(kπ)
the multiplier is the exponent raised over one period; inside a band the exponent is i times Bloch’s momentum, so half the trace of one period’s map is the cosine of k times π
