In phase, not in agreement

The page's own picture, built: one distinguished oscillator, Loudon (amber), whom every page-oscillator (indigo) is trying to lock to, and whose own speed moves over time. Everything is drawn from where he stands: he sits at the top of the circle, and a page's place on the circle is its phase relative to his. Nothing here makes sound.

One leads, one follows

Loudon and one page. He doesn't listen; the page does, with a pull K. The page also has its own speed, a little faster or slower than his: in the page's words, its natural frequency, its forward vector. If the pull is at least the gap in speed, the page locks: it keeps its own speed and still turns with him, sitting a fixed distance away. Make the gap bigger than the pull and it slips round.

He moves

Now his speed wanders, as he does: he takes up a concern, drops another. Two pages. One keeps listening, with its own speed 0.05 above where his started. The other copied him exactly at the start, his phase and his speed then, and stopped listening: agreement, held after he moved on. Set how far he moves to 0 and the second page sits on him for good, which is step 1 with no gap at all.

A page that holds a stance

The page says that a page that contradicts him in phase, one that names a tension he is already circling, is more aligned than one that agrees with him about something he has stopped caring about. Here is the model's reading of that, not the page's words: a stance is a phase the page holds apart from him on purpose. At 180° it sits opposite him and still turns with him wherever he goes. Stance 0 is step 2 again.

dφ/dt = Δf − K · sin(φ + α)
(how fast the page moves away from him) = (its gap in speed) − (pull) × sin(how far apart they are + its stance). It settles a stance away from him, plus the small lean from step 1.
agreement = ⟨ cos φ ⟩
(how close a page sits to his phase) = the average of cos(how far apart they are): +1 on him, 0 a quarter turn off, −1 opposite.

The test: two palaces of twelve

Twelve pages each way, in the whole situation: he moves (m = 0.3), and each listening page keeps its own speed, spread over ±0.1 around where his started. Palace A listens with a pull of 0.5, each page holding a stance, and then at stance 180° with weak pulls of 0.1 and 0.05, where it may not keep up with him. Palace B copied his phase at the start and stopped listening, in two forms: as clones, every page at his speed then, and as the page pictures pages, each with its own speed (±0.1). Two measures, named before the run. R is the order parameter as the Kuramoto Coupling page defines it, over all thirteen voices with him counted as one. H is step 2's measure, averaged over the twelve pages and over 20-unit windows. H can't see a stance: a page held steady 180° from him scores the same as one held on him. So the H arm tests listening against having stopped, not contradiction against agreement; the stances are R's arm.

R = | (1 ⁄ 13) · ( eiθL + Σi eiθi ) |
(how together everyone is) = the length of (the average of all thirteen phases, his and the twelve pages', each drawn as an arrow), averaged over the run.
The rule, set before the first run. Under H the page's sentence holds if every palace A, weak pulls included, scores above both palaces B; it fails if any A scores at or below the better B (that would be about 0.39 here). Under R it holds if every A at pull 0.5 scores above a B, taken against each B in turn: the verdict turns on whether the stale pages are clones of one another or each keep their own speed. A measure the sentence fails under is one the page can't mean by alignment. The null: with him still (m = 0), the clone B must tie every A at pull 0.5 on H to within 0.01, since agreement costs nothing when nothing has moved. Each run: 400 units after 50 to settle, seed 3, the same every time.
