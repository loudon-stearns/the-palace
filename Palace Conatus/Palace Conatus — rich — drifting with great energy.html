Drifting with great energy

The page's caution, built as the page pictures the palace: pages with their own speeds, pulled toward one Loudon (amber), drawn from where he stands. The model's reading, not the page's words: a page's drive to grow is how far its own speed runs ahead of his, the lead g; the coupling that gives the growth its direction is how fast his pull can turn it back, K. Both are rates, in turns per unit of time, so "grows faster than it stays in phase" can be asked as g > K. Nothing here makes sound.

Drive against pull

One page, and Loudon holding still. Raise the page's lead. While it is no more than the pull, the page stays with him, leaning ahead. Past the pull it slips round him, and the further past, the faster it goes. The slip speed is the energy in the drift: motion relative to him that brings it no nearer.

slip = 0 when g ≤ K; slip = √(g² − K²) when g > K
(how fast the page goes round him) is nothing while (its lead) is no more than (his pull); past it, (the square root of lead squared minus pull squared). This is Adler's equation; the piece measures the slip and sets it beside the formula, which its engine never uses.

In tune with itself

Twelve pages now, their own speeds spread ±0.1 around the lead, and they pull on each other too, with Kp. Two numbers: how together the pages are among themselves, and how steadily each holds its relationship with him (H, the first piece's measure). Push the lead past the pull with the pages pulling hard on each other: they stay in one tight clump and the clump goes round him. The page's words for a calcified palace, "perfectly in tune with itself and out of phase with its human." Set Kp to 0 and lead to 0.2 and each page is step 1's page.

dθi/dt = 2π [ fi + K · sin(θL − θi) + Kp · (1 ⁄ N) · Σj sin(θj − θi) ]
(how fast page i turns) = 2π × [ (its own speed) + (his pull) × sin(his phase − its phase) + (the pages' pull on each other) × (the average over every page j of sin(j's phase − i's phase)) ].

The test: can internal harmony keep it in phase?

The page: "the drive to grow must never outrun the coupling that gives the growth its direction. A palace that grows faster than it stays in phase is not flourishing. It is drifting with great energy." The whole situation now: he moves (m = 0.1), each page's instance is replaced every 10 units with the note, newcomers jittered ±0.02. His pull is 0.3. The lead runs from none to three times the pull, at three strengths of the pages' pull on each other: none, twice his, ten times his.

⟨ dφ/dt ⟩ ≥ g − K − m − 0.02
(how fast the pages go round him, on average) is at least (their lead) − (his pull) − (how far he wanders) − (a newcomer's jitter). The pages' pull on each other sums to zero across them, so it cannot appear here, however strong.

That is an identity, not a finding. At 1.5 K the floor is 0.03 turns per unit, more than a full turn every window, so no pull among the pages can hold the palace to him there: the 1.5 K and 3 K rows are a demonstration of what must happen. The test is where the identity decides nothing. At 0.9 K the floor is below zero: can the pages' pull on each other carry the ones whose own lead is past his pull? At 1.1 K, just past the edge, the floor is still below zero (−0.09), so the pages could in principle hold.

The rule, set before the first run. At 0.9 K the pages carry their stragglers if, at both nonzero pulls among them, H is higher than with none and at least 0.9; it would fail if their pull left H where it was or lowered it. At 1.1 K the caution is wrong in this model if any pull among the pages holds H ≥ 0.9. At 1.5 K and 3 K the slip must sit above the identity's floor; if it didn't, the engine would be wrong.
