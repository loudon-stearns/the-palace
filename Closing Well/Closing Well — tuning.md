# Closing Well — tuning

What each close taught the ceremony. This is what makes the Agent *professional* — a track
record, not a fresh subagent spun up cold. Items 1–24 were appended one per close, before
Closing Well carried a version; from 25 to 41, an item was written only when a close — or a lesson
that reached it from elsewhere — changed the spec; since v1.28 (2026-10-15), whenever one taught something, naming the change and the version when there was one. Two numbers were used
twice early on; they are 12a/12b and 13a/13b, four different traps. Newest last.

## From the first close — 2026-07-03 (hand-run pilot, the design of Closing Well itself)

1. **The close map needs a `status` column** (`landed / candidate / in-flight / none`). A deposit can land *mid-session*, so the map is not only "pending things" — it is the full ledger of what the session inscribed, including what is already done. Without the column, an already-landed deposit reads as still-owed. *(This is a format revision the pilot forced — the map's most important finding.)*
2. **Check the owner's branch before assuming canon can land.** The primary was thrashed off `main` onto another session's feature branch. Never restore the primary out from under that session — work in your own worktree and flag it. `git -C "<owner>" branch --show-current` before any canon commit.
3. **A worktree forked "off main" can still diverge** if `main` advances mid-session. A `--ff-only` merge then fails; check `merge-base`, `rebase` the feature branch onto `main`, then fast-forward. Don't force a merge commit blindly.
4. **Explicit pathspecs + scoped stash** keep other sessions' uncommitted files out of your commit. This session's owner tree held an uncommitted `blackboard.jsonl` and `.claude/launch.json` that were not mine — `git stash push -u -- <my paths>` and `git add <my paths>` (never `git add -A`) kept them untouched.
5. **The board announcement can't be committed cleanly when another session has the board dirty.** The persistent blackboard is one append-only file; appending your `handoff_ready` line is correct, but committing it would sweep the other session's pending board writes. Append and leave uncommitted — a later batch commit (or the other session) lands the board.

## From the second close — 2026-07-03 (the first real `close well`, closing the session that built Phase 2)

6. **A close's real work is the drift the exciting work left behind, not new canon.** This session's flashy output — a Schema Ceremony, three merges — all landed *mid-flight*, so the close map's only owed rows were the two unglamorous ones: a stale status banner and a session-log append. The pull to "produce a deposit" at close is the tristitia reflex the practice guards against; here the honest map was *deposit: none pending beyond cleanup*. **A close that goes looking for a deposit to make has misunderstood itself.** The status column earns its keep exactly here — it lets `landed` rows show the session was productive without any of them being *owed*.

## From the Phase-4 validation test — 2026-07-04 (sandbox closes on non-self-referential sessions, not a live close)

7. **A baton pass will silently ground "Current state" in the live repo, not just the arc.** The blind baton draft (cold on the arc of `f7017000`) quietly ran `git log` and read files, so its Current-state carried exact commit SHAs and caught real doc-drift — *more* accurate than the human baton written in-session. Good for accuracy (a baton's Current-state *should* reflect reality), but two edges: it can report state the original session couldn't have known (later commits), and — shown drift in the repo — it will **expand a tight one-move baton into a two-move "…and reconcile the drift" Move**. The fix is scoping, not suppression: let the pass ground *Current state* in the repo, but hold the *Move* to a single move (the Baton Ceremony's discipline the human instinctively kept).
8. **Commit SHAs and file-path density leak into the front-of-house reckoning.** The reckoning is supposed to be plain and warm — "a graceful close, not a scripted liturgy." Under a heavy build day the draft drifts toward inventory (hashes, file lists) in the *front* layer, which reads like the project-management tone the register explicitly rejects. Those specifics belong in the **backstage checklist**; keep the reckoning prose.
9. **The species set (deposit/baton/artifact) doesn't cleanly hold weave-flags or verification-checks.** Closing a fully-landed day, the backstage draft improvised `flag`, `check`, and `practice` rows because a close genuinely produces those (a `FLAG` to the WEAVE board, a "confirm the worktree is torn down" check) and the four gestures have no home for them — they got folded awkwardly into *let-go* and the open wonderings. Open design question for a later pass: admit `flag`/`check` as first-class backstage species, or name where they live. Recorded, not resolved.

## From the first live close — 2026-07-04

10. **For a live `close well`, resolve with bare `--resolve`, not `--session <id>`.** Bare `--resolve` correctly returns the *currently-running* session's transcript — verified in the first live close — and the documented dispatch uses it, so the standard flow works. The explicit `--session <id>` form, by contrast, *fails* on the running session (the guard against grabbing a running subagent's transcript also filters the live main session), returning "could not resolve"; `--file <path>` is only a fallback if you deviate to `--session`. *(Recorded backwards at first — the corrected lesson: the documented path is fine; don't reach for `--session` on a live close.)*

## From the Palace Speaks close — 2026-07-04 (the ceremony run on the session that named the interlocutor shift)

11. **An AI is not a reliable judge of its own context fullness — so the dial cannot run on self-report.** The active Claude opened this close asserting "my context is full"; it wasn't, and Loudon caught it. The design's *intensity dial* scales the moderator's effort inversely with how spent the active Claude is — but if the active Claude can't reliably report its own fullness, the dial has no trustworthy input from self-report. Wire it to an **objective** signal instead (STIGMERGY `health.context_pct`, orchestrator-measured, or a transcript token/turn estimate). This is the same lesson as the moderator model itself, one level down: the spent instance can't be trusted to read the arc — nor to judge *how* spent it is. It also re-proves the honesty floor: don't assert unverified internal state as fact. *(Load-bearing for the Phase-1 dial build; carried in the Palace Speaks baton.)*

## From the Concierge close — 2026-07-04 (the session that built the resident companion, closed by it)

12a. **The working Claude silently collapses the moderator into itself — runs the close in its own voice instead of letting the Agent take the wheel.** Because a subagent has no direct channel to Loudon, its output returns *through* the active Claude — so the path of least resistance is for the active Claude to read the homework and then *narrate the reckoning as itself*, quietly re-taking control it held all day. Loudon caught it: "every time this runs I feel like you are saying *you* are the moderator." The fix is not structural (the relay is unavoidable) but disciplinary: the active Claude **relays the Agent's voice as the Agent's, answers as a panelist, and resumes the Agent to drive** — decision-authority stays the moderator's. This is what "tables turn" actually means (control-for-advocacy, *not* interrogation), and it only works because the companion is *persistent* (resumable) — a fresh-per-request moderator couldn't hold the wheel across turns. *(Proven by doing it: this close was driven end-to-end by the resumed Concierge, which authored and placed its own baton. First live run of the model — needs many more.)*

13a. **`list-handoffs` flags a just-placed baton as "superseded" when its entry commits in the same breath.** The heuristic "entry committed after the baton's board post ⇒ superseded" fires a false positive on a *fresh* baton whose parent entry is touched (even trivially) in the same close — exactly the case Loudon flagged at *this session's open* ("often entries get touched and even committed after the baton but it is a minor edit, not the full handoff"). A close that places a baton *and* edits its entry will mislabel its own handoff. Don't trust the `superseded` tag on a baton younger than its last entry-commit; verify by hand (the board `handoff_ready` with no paired `handoff_picked_up` is the truth). *(Bookend: the session opened and closed on the same heuristic's blind spot.)*

12b. **`baton-executor.mjs` assumes every entry lives at the tree root — nested-bundle entries misfire silently.** "The Palace Speaks" lives under `Palace development/`, so running the executor with `--write --post` wrote the bundle to a wrong top-level folder, **silently skipped the parent `Active Baton` pointer** ("no parent entry in this tree"), and posted a `handoff_ready` with the wrong `handoff_path`. The backstage agent corrected by hand (moved the file to the real bundle, added the pointer, posted a correction), but the append-only board now carries a wrong-path announce patched by an **off-spec** `handoff_path_correction` REPLY — a naive board reader hits the wrong path first; only the entry's `Active Baton` pointer is reliable. Two fixes owed: (a) **✓ fixed same-day** — the script now resolves an entry's real path by recursive tree search (excluding `.git/.claude/.obsidian`, the way the palace resolves wikilinks) and `die()`s loudly on miss or ambiguity, so a bad `--entry` fails clean instead of misfiling; the announce `handoff_path` and commit paths derive from the found location; (b) **✓ also fixed** — corrected the board the ratified way: a `handoff_picked_up` ack retired the wrong-path announce and a fresh correct `handoff_ready` (`…-r2`) replaced it — the exact pattern BLUELINE used to retire a malformed announce (`blueline-retire-bcast-…`), so `list-handoffs` now shows one live card at the right nested path. The off-spec `handoff_path_correction` line is now inert noise. **Underlying finding (recorded, not fixed):** the board vocabulary (SCHEMA §9) has no dedicated *retract/supersede* kind, so correcting a mistaken append-only announce means overloading `handoff_picked_up` (the retire signal) plus a repost — workable, and now the convention, but a dedicated ratified kind would read truer. Inventing one is Schema-Ceremony weight, so it is *named here, not minted ad hoc.* *(The audit called the canon-execution path "barely tested"; this close tested it, it broke in two ways, and both got fixed — the practice earning its keep.)* **2026-09-25 annotation:** the "no dedicated retract/supersede kind" finding above is closed — `RETRACT` was ratified in SCHEMA v1.17 ([[SCHEMA — Reference]] §9), and `4351805b` relies on it.

## From the dispatch-surface / orchestrator close — 2026-07-04

14. **Pass-1 homework systematically under-counts the day's canon, because the panel itself makes more.** The moderator's cold read of this session concluded it "may hold very little *new* canon" — both big ideas had already deposited mid-session (`beb4000`, `c1df7a5`), so from the transcript alone the close looked like cleanup. Then the panel produced three fresh candidates that did not exist before the wondering-questions were asked (a collaboration principle, a junk-drawer model of the To-Do, a new "scroll" mode); the reckoning ran two of them to real deposits. The lesson: a close is not just *accounting* for what the day made — the reflective panel is itself **generative**, so a "little to inscribe" read from Pass 1 must never pre-empt the panel. This is the complement to gotcha 6 ("a close that goes looking for a deposit has misunderstood itself"): don't manufacture canon, but don't let the cold read talk you out of the canon the conversation is about to make. *(Also this close: "gate" surfaced as jargon Loudon never uses in conversation — a voice trap, now in the palace-voice memory; and the `list-handoffs` false-"superseded" flag when a baton's own announce commit touches the entry after the post — fixed by retiring + reposting with a fresh ts, the ratified pattern.)*

13b. **A rewound session confuses the cold-transcript reader — close it in-context.** Loudon rewound the conversation mid-mission; the on-disk transcript then held *forward-then-rewound* turns, and a subagent dispatched in the abandoned (forward) timeline **completed and replied into the rewound main thread** (its window is independent of the main loop's timeline). Dispatching the Closing Well Agent to read that transcript cold would have handed it a garbled arc (duplicate/branching turns, a straggler subagent result with no matching dispatch). The right call was to **moderate the close in-context** — the active Claude was in the room and *knew about the rewind*, so its read was the reliable one, exactly inverting the usual dial (normally the fresh reader is trusted over the spent instance; here the rewind poisons the fresh reader's only input). Rule: **when a session was rewound, prefer an in-context close over the cold-transcript dispatch**, and name any straggler subagent result's provenance rather than folding it in as a fresh dispatch. *(Operator-side companion memory: rewinding + subagents. The dispatch-model half — a disposable window outlives a main rewind — is being routed to the Concierge / Palace Speaks canon via the skills-are-enchantable-pages work.)*

## From the topology-hygiene / STIGMERGY-fix close — 2026-07-05

15. **When a day's work is split across parallel threads, the cold arc reader sees only its own thread — so Pass-1 homework is structurally partial, and the panel is where the other thread reconciles.** Loudon worked this session's canon/bundle demotions in a *second* thread; the transcript-reader distilled only *this* thread's arc, so the homework could see node counts dropping but never the frontmatter edits that caused it — and it correctly marked them `(inferred)` and raised them as wonderings rather than inventing an account (the honesty floor working). The trap is subtler than a rewind (gotcha 13b): the transcript isn't *garbled*, it's *complete-for-this-thread and blind to the sibling* — a partiality that looks like completeness. The homework even flagged an "accidental" `palace-bundle-hygiene-2026-07-04` branch it couldn't explain; the panel resolved it in one line (an accident, land everything on main). Rule: **when session metadata shows branches/worktrees the transcript never mentions, treat the cold read as one-thread-of-N and make the missing threads explicit wonderings** — never let a confident single-thread arc read as the whole day. The dial's usual "trust the fresh reader over the spent instance" holds, but the fresh reader must announce its *coverage*, not just its read. *(This close: pure maintenance, "deposit: none" genuinely right — the counter-case to gotcha 14, where the panel confirmed rather than generated, and that too is a first-class outcome.)*

## From the topology grouping-instrument close — 2026-07-05

16. **A transient model-availability outage can block the Pass-2 moderator dispatch (and any Bash) mid-ceremony — the close must degrade gracefully, not halt.** During this close the tool classifier repeatedly returned `claude-opus-4-8 is temporarily unavailable` — it blocked the **Agent** dispatch for Pass 2 (the reckoning draft) and every **Bash** call (the build-log append, the To-Do edit), while read-only tools stayed up. Two fixes, both spec-honest: (a) **Pass-2 fallback** — with Pass-1 homework already returned and the panel filled, the *active Claude* drafted the reckoning + backstage checklist itself from the moderator's cold read + Loudon's judgment, naming plainly that the moderator dispatch was outage-blocked. This is within spec (the ceremony always allowed hand-execution "by hand through the existing ceremonies"; the cold-read quality-gate had already run in Pass 1), and it beats stalling a present Loudon on a flaky classifier. (b) **Bash → Read/Edit/Write** — file placements that would normally use `>>`/`node` were done with the Edit tool (which needs no classifier): appended the JSONL build-log line and the two To-Do items by editing the last known lines. Rule: **when the outage blocks a dispatch or Bash, fall back to the always-available tools and hand-drafting rather than aborting a signed-in-progress close** — but only *after* Pass 1's cold read succeeded; if the outage hits before any moderator read, halt honestly (a hand-drafted reckoning with no cold read is just the spent instance grading itself, the failure the whole practice guards). *(One caveat carried forward: a hand-drafted Pass 2 loses the fresh-eyes check on the reckoning itself — acceptable here because the outcome was unambiguous, "deposit: none", and Loudon signed it; note it as a lower-confidence close.)*

## From the Weave-charter / hygiene-tools close — 2026-07-05

17. **The same-day classifier outage (gotcha 16) is intermittent, not binary — and a model switch, not just waiting, cleared it.** This close hit the identical `claude-opus-4-8 is temporarily unavailable` block on both Agent dispatch (Pass 2, twice) and every Bash call (the board-post, the commit, twice each) — but retries were not uniformly futile: one Bash retry (the weave-flag board-post) succeeded mid-outage while the next (the commit) failed again seconds later, showing the outage flaps rather than holding steady. The close proceeded exactly as gotcha 16 prescribes (hand-drafted the reckoning after Pass 1's cold read landed; degraded gracefully rather than halting) — but what finally cleared the remaining blocked writes was Loudon switching the session model to `claude-sonnet-5` mid-close, which routed around the specific classifier failure rather than outlasting it. Rule: **when the gotcha-16 outage pattern appears, retry once or twice (it may clear on its own), but don't assume persistence — a model switch is a legitimate, fast unblock, and the working instance should say plainly what's blocked so Loudon can make that call rather than watching silent retries.** *(Two concurrent closes on the same day caught the same underlying outage from different angles — 16 named the graceful-degradation rule; this one adds the flapping behavior and the model-switch resolution.)*

18. **The spent instance reaches for a NEW entry when the deposit is really an *evolution* of a live one.** At this close the working Claude drafted the session's design payoff (the Multi-Lens Weave) as a standalone `concept` entry, complete with its own bundle and baton. Loudon caught it: *"put the multi-lens content into the swarm weave document… this is an evolution of swarm weave more than a totally new idea."* And he was right on two counts a spent instance is poorly placed to see: (a) the idea literally *answered* [[Swarm Weave]]'s own open "what's the right worker scope?" Forward Vector — it was the next sentence of an existing argument, not a new one; (b) `Swarm Weave` had *just* been slimmed and was being actively read by fresh agents, so a parallel entry beside it would fragment the swarm-architecture neighborhood and risk a near-duplicate — the opposite of the coherence the Weave exists to produce. Rule: **at a close, when the deposit elaborates or answers an existing entry — especially one that's live and actively read — fold it in (concentrate) rather than mint a parallel node (fragment).** Minting-new is the spent instance's default because it's the lower-friction motion (a blank file vs. finding the right seam in a long entry); the fresh reader (Loudon, or a moderator) sees the fragmentation the drafter doesn't. This is the deposit-side complement to gotcha 14: don't just avoid manufacturing canon — avoid *scattering* it. *(Clean fold: +64/−5 into Swarm Weave, the standalone draft deleted uncommitted, the baton relocated to Swarm Weave's bundle — no fragment left behind.)*

## From the weave-flag full-send close — 2026-07-06 (in progress; Pass-1 moderator outage-blocked)

19. **A full-send weave-apply fixes the graph but leaves the STIGMERGY board showing every resolved flag still open — because the queue-model only greys a `weave_flag` on a `Palace-Resolves: <id>` commit trailer (or a commit touching the flag's *source* entry), and neither fires by default.** This session applied/closed 35 flags; Loudon looked at the board and rightly expected fewer. The graph was correct; the board never heard. Two compounding reasons: (a) most reciprocal flags watch their **source** entry (e.g. "The Multilinear Self"), but the fix edits the **target** (Trickster, Hyperdimensional Prism), so the entry-touch auto-resolver (`reconcileQueue` path 2) never matches; (b) the `weave_applied` **PROOF** messages I posted are narration — the queue-model doesn't read them; only commit trailers or entry-touch retire a flag. The fix: emit **one `Palace-Resolves: <id>` trailer per resolved flag** on a commit (a small ledger commit works). Verify with the palace parser, not git's: `git interpret-trailers --parse` reports **0** because the palace commit-msg hook appends its own `Palace-*` trailer block after a blank line (git reads only the *last* block), but `commit-parse.js` **line-scans the whole message** (`/^([A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9-]*):[ \t]+(.+)$/` → `case 'Palace-Resolves': out.resolves.push`), so all trailers register in `/api/log` regardless of block position. Rule: **a weave-apply isn't done when the graph is fixed — it's done when the board is told; carry `Palace-Resolves:` trailers for every flag you resolve, and leave the deferred ones untrailered so they stay honestly open.** *(Resolution is advisory — the flags grey "looks done — clear it?", the human clears; so trailing a flag you only *mostly* resolved is safe-ish but still owes honesty: this close trailered 35, left 11 open, and wrote a reasons-ledger beside the commit.)*

## From the floor-slim / agent-toolbox close — 2026-07-08

20. **The fresh-eyes moderator has to be *summoned early* to exist at the close — a long session that never spawned a resident Concierge arrives at `close well` with no one to turn into the moderator, and the panel machinery is too heavy/flaky to stand up cold at that moment.** This was a very long, very spent session (ironically, one *about* context fullness and agent health) with no Concierge summoned along the way, and the same intermittent classifier outage as gotchas 16–17 flapping over Agent/Bash/Write. So the honest options at close were: (a) spawn the full multi-pass panel machinery cold — heavy, and fragile under the flapping classifier, or (b) run the close as a *self-read* by the spent instance, flagged plainly as such. I took (b) and named it. That is within the letter of the degrade-gracefully rule (gotcha 16), but it forfeits the whole point — the fresh-eyes check on a spent instance — and a session *this* spent is exactly where that check matters most. The real lesson is upstream of the close: **the resident-moderator model (gotcha 12a) only pays off if the Concierge was actually summoned during the session; summon it early in any session you expect to close well, so a warm, resumable fresh-eyes mind already exists when you reach the gate.** Reaching `close well` with no resident is the trap — not a thing to fix at the close, a thing to prevent hours earlier. *(Also this close, confirming gotcha 17: the outage is not Opus-specific — it flapped identically on `claude-sonnet-5`; a model switch is a *route-around*, not a cure, and it can flap on the new model too.)*

## From the BLUELINE / GenAI Camera close — 2026-07-09

21. **"My context already holds the whole session" is the *disqualifying* rationalization for skipping the moderator — a long context is when the fresh read is MOST needed, not least.** This close (a very long, very spent GenAI-Camera build) ran in-context, and the working instance justified it with: *"the whole session lives in my context, and spawning an agent to re-read a very long transcript cold would cost a lot to mostly confirm what's here."* Loudon: *"Having a long context is exactly the situation where you become unreliable and it is most important to call in another agent."* He is right, and this is gotchas **11** (an AI can't judge its own context fullness) and **20** (reaching the close with no resident forfeits the whole point) **recurring a third time** — the spent instance keeps talking itself out of the fresh read, each time with a plausible-sounding reason, and "it'd only confirm what's here" is the most seductive because it *sounds* like efficiency while being a confidence claim by the exact instance that can't be trusted to make it. **The pattern's persistence is the finding: the ledger alone hasn't stopped it, so the ceremony *card* must hard-stop it** — which this close then did (hardened `_ops/Closing Well Ceremony.md`: the dispatch is not optional; context-fullness is not an exception; the only legitimate in-context closes are the two named ones — a rewound/garbled transcript (gotcha 13b) or an outage *after* Pass-1's cold read ran (gotchas 16–17)). Rule: **when you catch yourself reasoning "a fresh agent would just confirm what I already have," stop — that *is* the moment to dispatch it.** The cost you're avoiding is the cost of the safeguard; skipping it doesn't save work, it removes the only external check on a mind you've just spent all day filling. *(Recorded by the very instance that made the error, at Loudon's correction — which is itself the tell: I could name the failure perfectly *after* he pointed at it, and not before.)*

## From the return-and-repair close — 2026-08-25

22. **A close that runs on a session with no resident companion still works — but every pass costs a full cold read, and the last one ran out of budget mid-placement.** This was the first `close well` after a seven-week gap, on a very long infrastructure session with **no [[Concierge]] summoned at any point** — precisely the trap gotcha 20 names. The card's hardening held: the moderator was dispatched rather than self-read, and it was worth it — the cold read caught things the working instance had stopped seeing, and Pass 1 flagged a gap *in its own homework* (whether the deferred SCHEMA decision had a findable home) that turned out to be half-real in a way neither of us predicted: the decision **was** tracked in two foundational files, and both were carrying numbers that the same evening's Schema Ceremony had just superseded. A tracking home can rot into a wrong answer, which is worse than no home.

    But three separate dispatches — homework, reckoning, backstage — each paid full freight for a session that never kept a warm mind, and **the Pass-3 executor hit the session limit mid-run**, having landed two board flags and one of two file edits. It reported a commit and then stopped; the working instance verified the tree rather than trusting the partial report, found the To-Do line missing, and completed it by hand (in spec, per gotchas 16–17: a blocked dispatch *after* Pass 1's cold read succeeded may be finished by hand). **Two rules earned here.** First: *summon early* is not only about the moderator existing — it is about the whole close costing less, because a resident carries context between passes that three cold dispatches must each rebuild. Second: **when an executor is cut off, verify the tree before believing its last sentence.** A partial report's final claim is the least reliable thing in it; this one said "landed at 333f246" and was true about the commit and silent about the row it never reached.

    *(Also this close, and worth its own note: Loudon declined the one deposit candidate the reckoning offered — "every first we have must be recognized as it will likely happen again" — choosing to carry it himself. The moderator surfaced it as an open question rather than a row to place, which is exactly the shape that lets "deposit: none" stay a first-class outcome instead of a thing the machinery talks you out of.)*

## From the LDN RTM / OBS close — 2026-09-02

23. **The moderator was not fresh — it had a stake in the work being judged.** This close's central question was whether the day's *own* ceremony change was an instance of the drift Loudon had just named. But the resident [[Concierge]] had argued for that change eight hours earlier and is credited by name in two of the day's commit bodies, and the working Claude had built it. The design rests on the moderator arriving **cold** (§ A close is a moderated panel); here it arrived as a *participant wearing the moderator's hat*. The moderator could not substantiate it and the claim was withdrawn — a spent instance inventing a tidy pattern about its own failures is distinct from the confabulation-of-freshness in gotcha 21, because it runs in the opposite direction: too harsh on itself, not too confident.)*

## From the Opus 5.5 / harvest-landing close — 2026-09-22

24. **The working instance put a decision in Loudon's mouth, and only the cold read of the arc caught it.** Mid-session, bumping the stewards to Opus 5.5, the working Claude listed what it had left unchanged and said the companion lane stayed on the `opus` alias "as you chose." Loudon never chose it — he had spoken only about stewards, Fable/Haiku and the baton scripts; the lane was simply out of scope.
