the mixer
u₁ = m₁₁·s₁ + m₁₂·s₂ , u₂ = m₂₁·s₁ + m₂₂·s₂ , sᵢ[n] = ρ · ½ · ( yᵢ[n] + yᵢ[n − 1] )
(what goes back into each loop) = a mix of (what came out of both loops, softened by Karplus-Strong's two-point average and a loss ρ = 0.996 per pass). Loop i is a delay of dᵢ = fs ⁄ fᵢ − ½ samples.
crossfeed: M = [ 1 − c , c ; c , 1 − c ] lossless: M = [ cos(c·π⁄2) , sin(c·π⁄2) ; −sin(c·π⁄2) , cos(c·π⁄2) ]
crossfeed: each loop keeps (1 − c) of itself and takes c of the other, so the two loops' sum passes and their difference is lost. Lossless: the pair is turned by a quarter-turn times c, and no energy is lost. Either way, c = 0 keeps them apart and c = 1 swaps them on every pass.
lossless modes: cos( π·f·(T₁ + T₂) ) = cos( c·π⁄2 ) · cos( π·f·(T₂ − T₁) )
the pitches the coupled pair can ring at are the f where (a wave over both loops' periods together) equals (the coupling's cosine) times (a wave over their difference). Both sides move smoothly with c, so the roots do too. At c = 0 it gives each loop's own harmonics; at c = 1 the odd harmonics of 1 ⁄ (2·(T₁ + T₂)).
at c = 1: f_S = f₁·f₂ ⁄ (f₁ + f₂)
swapped on every pass, the two loops are one loop as long as both together, and its fundamental is (the product of the two pitches) ÷ (their sum): lower than either. The crossfeed lands on its whole harmonic series; the lossless mixer, which flips a sign on the way round, on its odd harmonics of f_S ⁄ 2.
A linear model: the loops never saturate and nothing in them is a self-sustaining oscillator.
