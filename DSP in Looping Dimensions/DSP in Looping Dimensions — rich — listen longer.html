Does it ever become noise?

The Honest Limit says that however many loops you add at irrational ratios, the spectrum stays a set of separate lines, and a continuous spectrum like noise stays out of reach. Four steps, one idea each. The last one measures what tells the two apart.

y(t) = Σₖ cos( k·θ(t) + φₖ ) ⁄ √k , θ(t) = 2π·f₁·t + β · Σᵢ₌₂ sin(2π·fᵢ·t) , k = 1 … 5 , β = 2
(the tone) = a small table of five harmonics, each a little quieter, read by loop 1, while (every other loop) pushes the reading point back and forth, each by up to β radians. Taken as a function of all N phases it is one surface on the N-torus, W = g(θ₁ + β·Σ sin θᵢ), and its lines fall on k·f₁ + Σ mᵢ·fᵢ: every one a single frequency. Its envelope stays level, so more loops make it denser, not clickier.

each loop's rate, added in this order: no two related by a ratio of whole numbers.
share = (energy in the loudest 10 % of bins) ÷ (all the energy from 100 to 4000 Hz)
how concentrated the sound is. Noise spreads its power over every bin at random, and its loudest tenth holds about a third, (1 + ln 10) ⁄ 10 ≈ 0.33, at any window length. Lines put their power in a few bins, and once the window is long enough to part them, the share climbs.
Checked offline on this page's own engine (the script marked engine), windows of 21 ms to 5.5 s averaged over six seconds of sound: noise stays between 34.5 and 36.2 %; one loop is past 99 % by 43 ms; two loops reach 99.9 % by 0.68 s; six loops start at 40.7 % at 21 ms, a few points off noise, and climb, unevenly, to 51.2 % at 5.5 s. With six loops, 136,862 lines within 60 dB of the loudest fall between 100 and 4000 Hz, against 21,299 bins in a 5.5 s window, so no listen this long can part them all. Not heard by an agent.
