How many clocks does a sound need?

The section says the rank N is measured: the fewest base frequencies whose whole-number combinations account for a sound's partials. A harmonic tone fits with one, a piano string or a bell needs two or more. Five steps, one idea each. The last one runs the fitter on seven measured instruments and sets each against chance.

N = clocks + partials left alone, at the cheapest naming
(the rank) = (how many frequencies the cheapest naming has to give outright). A partial no clock reaches is its own generator, so it counts. The fitter is a greedy search, so the N it reports is an upper bound: a cleverer search could find a cheaper naming, never a dearer one. It is not the page's "smallest set" itself, which needs a tolerance and a price on steps before it is a number at all; the price is a choice, and it moves N (most above 10 cents, where this piece's slider stops).
Search: the first clock is tried at every loud partial divided by 1 to 8, then scanned finely (±1 % in steps of 1⁄20000 of itself), the second at what is left of an unreached partial after a few steps of the first, divided by 1 to 3; each is refined by least squares on the partials it reaches. Steps: 0 to 64 along the first clock, −8 to +8 along the second. Two clocks at most, the most a 2D torus can play; a third never paid in the offline check on any source here at 2, 5 or 10 cents. Chance: the same count of partials with the same levels at random frequencies, evenly spread in log frequency between the source's lowest and highest partial, those two kept; twelve draws, seeded, fitted the same way. A source counts as more than chance when its clocks save more bits than they save on every draw. In a prefix code, d bits are saved on a random frequency with odds of at most 2⁻ᵈ; the fitter's choice of clocks among many tries is what lifts chance's best above a few bits. Measured sources: one frame 0.19 s after the strike from the steward's tracks in Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis's trajectory library (Ableton Core Library samples, measured in place; the tracker looked for each piano partial near the stiff-string prediction). What you hear is those partials as sines, not the recording.
