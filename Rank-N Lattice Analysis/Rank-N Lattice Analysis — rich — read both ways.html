Is what was heard what will be played?

The section says the analyzer's output, a field W and its clocks ω, is exactly the object the 2D torus synthesizer plays, so one stored field is both what was heard and what will be played back. Here the fit is written onto a 256 × 256 torus and read back by two phasors at the fitted clocks. Four steps, one idea each; the last one runs a measured note through it.

(the stored field) = a sum, over the partials the clocks reach, of (that partial's level) × a cosine that turns mₖ times across the torus one way and nₖ times the other. The fit's steps decide where each partial sits on the torus; its level decides how strongly.

With the steps priced, a fit of rank two closes whole (one clock and one lone partial take one axis each); with free, long steps, two clocks can place every partial of anything, and the field then costs more bits than the list of partials it plays.
Field: 256 × 256 samples, read with bilinear interpolation at 48 kHz; random phases. The round trip is measured on the read-back itself: each reached partial's level at its lattice frequency through a Hann window 0.68 s long, against the level written. A smaller table reads worse: at 32 × 32 the piano A2's reached partials, up to 13 steps of its clock, come back as much as 5.0 dB off. Fit: the rank fitter of the piece under "Rank as a measured property", two clocks at most; when it names one clock, its loudest lone partial is written on the second axis as a clock of its own. Free steps: two clocks searched with steps up to 64 and ±16 and no price, then priced afterwards to compare with the list. The level match tests the table and its interpolation, which is exact by construction for any partial the clocks reach; the claim is tested by what is left over and by the bits. Measured notes: one frame 0.19 s after the strike from the steward's tracks in Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis's trajectory library (Ableton Core Library samples, measured in place; never copied). "What was heard" is those partials as sines with one shared decay, not the recording; the read-back wears the same decay.
