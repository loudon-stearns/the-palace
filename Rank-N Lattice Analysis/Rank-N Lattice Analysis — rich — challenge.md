# Rank-N Lattice Analysis — rich — challenge

Challenger: claude-opus-5-5, 2026-11-03, against the maker's commit 74e4ab51 (branch enrich/rank-n-lattice-analysis). Judged from the page, the two made pieces, the gathered sheet, the manifest and the maker's commit body. Verdicts: **0 keep, 3 remake, 0 retire.**

## The fitter, first

The page defines rank as the size of "the smallest set of base frequencies … whose integer combinations account for this sound's partials" (Rank-N Lattice Analysis.md, § Rank as a measured property). Taken strictly, that number is degenerate both ways: with exact reals a stiff string's partials are linearly independent over the rationals, so its rank is the partial count; with a tolerance and free integers, two clocks reach anything (Dirichlet). The maker sees the second half and answers it with a price: a free frequency costs log₂(band ⁄ 2τ) bits (10.22 at 5 c), a step costs its Elias-gamma length, and the fit is the cheapest naming the greedy search finds. The reported "rank" is the clocks plus the partials left alone in that naming.

So the fitter measures something close to the page's rank but not the same thing, and the difference shows in three places:

1. **It minimises bits, not generators, and the search misses.** For piano A2 at 5 c the fitter reports one clock at 110.519 Hz reaching n = 2–11, 211.4 bits, rank 15. A fine scan of one clock finds 110.620 Hz reaching n = 2–13 for 204.9 bits, rank 13, saving 40.5 bits instead of 34.0. The maker's "ranks are upper bounds" (commit body) covers this, but the offers quote 15 as the measurement.
2. **The price, not the source, moves the rank as τ widens.** The free name gets cheaper as τ grows (8.22 bits at 20 c) while a step code doesn't (9 bits for steps 15–30, 11 from 31). A perfectly harmonic tone of 24 partials reads rank 1 at 5 c, 3 at 10 c, **11 at 20 c**; of 32 partials, rank 2 at 5 c; of 64, rank 6 at 5 c and 32 at 10 c. The measured A2 reads rank 15, 12, 12, 12, 12, 15 at τ = 5, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20: widening the tolerance from 18 to 20 c raises its rank. Both pieces put a 1–20 c slider in the reader's hand and tell them to watch the piano's rank move.
3. **Inside its range it is fair.** The harmonic and two-clock tones (12 partials) come out 1 and 2 at every τ from 1 to 20, so it does not make everything look high-rank. And at 5 c with 24 partials the piano's climb is the bend, not the price: an ideal stiff string n·110·√(1 + B n²) with the library's own B = 8.301 × 10⁻⁵ (piano-A2.wtb.json) fits rank 15 at 5 c, 12 at 10 c, 18 at 1 c, exactly the measured note's numbers, while 24 perfect harmonics fit rank 1 at 5 c.

The bits-against-chance comparison is a fair test: the same search runs on random partials of the same count, span and levels, and 40 seeded draws (I ran 40 where the piece runs 12) never reach the piano's saving. Its weakness is only that "chance's ranks" set the rank beside a range it shares with the sources, which is the maker's own point.

### read both ways · The synth as its own oscilloscope — **remake**

- *Test or decoration?* Half and half. The level match (0.002–0.004 dB) is guaranteed by construction: buildField writes each reached partial as a Fourier mode of the torus and two phasors read it back, so what is measured is bilinear interpolation, not the page's claim. The finding that matters, that a measured note loses what the fit left alone, is real.
- *Faithful?* Its conclusion is contradicted by its own numbers. "One stored field is both readings only for a sound whose rank is two or less": but the vibraphone (rank 2) comes back 4 of 5, the marimba (rank 2) 3 of 4, and step 3's harmonic with the fifth partial +5 c (rank 2) 5 of 6, because the piece writes only the fit's clocks and leaves the torus's second axis empty when the fit has one clock. Writing the loudest lone partial on the second axis would bring every one of those back whole. "A field that held them would need a torus of 15 dimensions" is wrong too: with steps unpriced, two clocks (44.154 and 54.887 Hz, steps up to 64 and 15) place all 24 of A2's partials within 2.13 c, and the piece's own 256 × 256 field plays them back, levels within 1.9 dB (bilinear roll-off at step 64). What a two-clock field cannot do is hold the piano *compressively*: that field costs 259.1 bits against 245.4 for naming the partials outright. And "a wider tolerance lets more partials onto the lattice" fails at the top of its own slider (A2 reaches 13 at 18 c, 10 at 20 c).
- *Walks up?* Yes: one clock as an ordinary wavetable, two clocks filling the square, a partial snapped or lost, then the measured note. Good order.
- *Remake, exactly:* (a) when the fit has one clock, put the loudest lone partial on θ₂ (or the best second clock), so a rank-2 sound closes on its own steps; (b) at the test step, add the dense reading beside the compressive one (free steps: every partial placed, the field longer than the list), so the claim reads "closes compressively only on a two-generator lattice"; (c) drop "15 dimensions", qualify the tolerance sentence, and say in the caption that the level match tests the table, not the claim.

## The offers

1. **Rank as a measured property — "a stiff string is one clock and a bend": confirmed in substance, numbers soft.** f_n = n f₀ √(1 + B n²) is not a lattice of any rank below the partial count; "N ≥ 2" is true and says almost nothing, and the page's "two or more incommensurate generators" draws the wrong picture. At a tolerance the reach is set by B: partials within τ of one stretched-out comb, about n ≤ √(2τ ⁄ (866 B)) ≈ 11.8 at 5 c, so the rank climbs with what is counted. The ideal string at the library's B reproduces the measured ranks exactly (15, 12, 18 at 5, 10, 1 c). But 15 is a greedy upper bound: one clock at 110.62 Hz gives rank 13 for fewer bits, and the climb 1, 1, 4, 7, 11, 15 is an upper-bound sequence. Suggested wording holds with "13–15, upper bounds".
2. **Rank as a measured property — "rank only means something at a stated tolerance and count": confirmed, and the price belongs in the list as a choice, not as the cure.** Free steps: two clocks reach 11.3 of 12 random partials on average, all 12 in 15 of 40 draws (reproduced), and all 24 of A2's at 5 c. Priced, the rank still moves with the price: 24 perfect harmonics read 1, 3, 11 at 5, 10, 20 c. So the offer's "once the integers are priced so that a dense lattice can't fit anything" should become "at a stated tolerance, count and integer price, each of which moves it".
3. **Cross-Domain Resonance — piano 15 and bell 18 both inside chance; only bits saved tells them apart: confirmed.** Over 40 draws: A2 saves 34.0 bits (40.5 at the better clock), chance mean 8.7, max 19.3, 0 of 40 reach it, chance ranks 13–22; A1 saves 40.5, chance max 20.3, 0 of 40; the bell saves 11.3, chance mean 8.8, max 18.0, 11 of 40 reach it, chance ranks 15–22. A bent line and a cloud can share a rank; the margin parts them.
4. **The synth as its own oscilloscope — the round trip closes only for rank ≤ 2: disputed.** The reported numbers reproduce (two partials and the two-clock tone within 0.002 and 0.004 dB; A2 10 of 24, 98.7 % of energy, moves ≤ 4.62 c; bell 8 of 24). The condition does not: three rank-2 fits on the piece's own engine fail to close (vibraphone 4/5, marimba 3/4, +5 c snap 5/6) because the second axis is left empty; the piano's lost 14 do have a place on a two-clock torus once the steps are long (all 24 within 2.13 c), which only costs more bits than the list; and the 0.004 vs 0.053 dB gap tracks step size, not rank. What holds: the round trip closes *exactly and compressively* only for a sound on a two-generator lattice; any sound can be laid on two clocks to within τ, but then the field is longer than what was heard.

**Against Three Kinds of Warp.** The two faces agree that a stiff string lies on no lattice (Three Kinds of Warp.md:93, "not on any rational lattice") and that a torus read at fixed rates, coupled or not, plays a two-generator lattice (its landed typology offer: every line on m·ρ₁ + n·ρ₂). They disagree on what that lattice can hold approximately. Three Kinds of Warp.md:95 says a two-generator lattice is dense, so "prominent partials can be placed near piano-stretched positions … approximation rather than exact reproduction". This face's test step says the piano's other 14 partials "have no place" and would need fifteen dimensions. The recompute sides with Three Kinds: they have a place, at long steps and a bit cost above the list. The read-both-ways remake should bring this face into line.

## The face as a whole

The face makes the page's idea felt, and more than that, it finds the page's weak joint: the walk from one sine to a measured piano is the clearest way I know to show that "rank" is only a number once you say what an integer costs, and the bits-against-chance strip is a real test that the piano passes and the bell fails. That is a good find, and it should go home. Where it overreaches is in letting its own instrument stand in for the page's concept. The fitter's rank is a description length, priced by one integer code, found by a greedy search; read inside 1–10 c and up to about 30 partials it tracks the page's rank and the piano's bend exactly, and outside that it measures its own price. The oscilloscope walk turns one choice (write only the fit's clocks) into a law about two dimensions, and its own bars contradict the law. Both are one round of fixes, not a rethink. The face is not busy; it is two honest walks that each need their last step tightened.
