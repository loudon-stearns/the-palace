## The offers

1. **Three Meanings: sharp goes with highs first** — confirmed. +196.4 ¢ / 31.4 ms and −181.1 ¢ / 42.7 ms reproduced, and the convexity argument holds. One caveat for the suggested text: a free bar's partials (2.76, 5.40 …) also depend on its ends, not only on the curve. "A law that bends up sends partials sharp" is right for strings pinned at both ends. Bars agree in direction.
2. **Three Meanings: "couples nonlinearly", "room modes"** — confirmed. Every law in the piece is linear: the curve bends because the restoring force depends on wavelength (a k⁴ term, a bead spacing, √(gk)). Piano String Inharmonicity's LTI offer says the same. Room modes: air carries every audio frequency at one speed, and a room's modes come from its walls. The one sense in which it is half right: an oblique mode travelling along a room's axis is waveguide dispersion, which is geometric. The suggested "waveguides whose cross-section matters" keeps that case, so dropping "room modes" loses nothing true.
3. **ω_n paired with f0 in Hz** — confirmed, no-claim-edit. f1 = f0·√(1 + B) is 600·log2(1.001) = **0.865 ¢** above f0 at B = 0.001, matching the offer's 0.87 and Piano String Inharmonicity's offer.
4. **Prism: glass slows the shorter wavelengths** — confirmed. N-BK7 group index: 1.53480 at 656 nm and 1.55758 at 486 nm, so over 1 km blue trails red by **76.0 ns**. At 700 against 410 nm the gap is **161.1 ns**. This matches Particle Synthesis's landed face and its own offer. The page's "phase velocities" should be group velocities for a flash. The phase order runs the same way in glass, so only the word changes, not the direction.
5. **Convolution: minimum-phase, maximum-phase, "nonlinear in ω"** — confirmed, every clause. (1) A first-order all-pass has its zero at radius 1/|a|, which is 1.111 at a = −0.9: outside the circle, so not minimum-phase. A minimum-phase filter with level one everywhere has zero phase by the Hilbert relation, so it is a wire. (2) a + z⁻¹ is maximum-phase, two taps, and causal. The page's clause confuses maximum-phase with anticausal. (3) The page asks for τ(ω) "nonlinear in ω". That rules out a delay rising in a straight line, and such a delay is dispersive. τ = β·ω (30 ms extra at 8 kHz) lands bands at 0.98, 3.89, 15.02 and 29.59 ms, a 28.6 ms chirp; the ideal is 0.94 to 30.0. Deep water is exactly this case: there arrival time is proportional to ω. Only a constant delay does not spread. **The page names the wrong boundary: it is the phase that must be nonlinear, and the delay need only vary.** This agrees with Categorizing Inharmonicity ("only the part beyond α·f bends") and with Dispersion Table ("a lag that grows in a straight line only retunes"). Note the vocabulary: in Dispersion Table "straight line" describes the phase lag; here it describes the delay. Both faces are right, but a reader holding both may trip over it. The suggested wording "τ varies with ω" avoids the clash. One narrowing to flag: "if the impulse response is all-pass" is sufficient, not necessary, since a lossy medium disperses and changes level too. "(for instance all-pass, …)" would keep it true.
6. **Convolution: fed forward it buys the chirp, not the stretch** — confirmed in substance: 0.0012 ¢ against 196.4 ¢, and the three faces agree. **Reword one clause.** "The cost is not small: 1024 multiplies a sample at B = 0.001 and 2688 at B = 0.0001" is this fit's cost, with first-order sections and a per-section price. It is not the recipe's floor. And 1024 multiplies a sample is about 49 M a second at 48 kHz, which is real-time on any current CPU, so it does not contradict the page's "at real-time cost". Suggested: drop "The cost is not small", or say "this fit used 512 to 1344 first-order sections".
7. **Open Questions Q1 partly answered** — confirmed in substance (+606.9, +196.4, +63.8 and +21.8 ¢ against 0.0, timing within 0.23 ms). **Reword one parenthesis.** "(Piano String Inharmonicity's click piece agrees with it to −228 dB)" cites as evidence a number that the piece's own caption calls "rounding, a check of the code, not of a piano". The string there is h(t) rebuilt as filters, so it agrees by construction. That a struck string is the convolution of the strike with the string's response is true by linearity alone. Drop the −228 dB or label it a code check.
