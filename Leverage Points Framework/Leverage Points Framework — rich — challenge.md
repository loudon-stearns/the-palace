## Same effort, three places · Meadows' Core Insight — **remake**

**Does it test or decorate?** It tests. The rule is written before the run, and in principle it could come out either way. But one side of Q is fixed by construction. Raising the yield scales every view by the same factor, the creator's comparisons are ratios, so the decisions do not change and only followers move: D for the parameter is exactly (4^e − 1)/2. That is 0.074 at 10% in every draw I ran (8 of 8), the same value whatever the constants. So the 64 draws vary only the goal side.

**Is it faithful?** This is where it falls down. Ask what kind of claim the sentence is: *"by intervening at high-leverage points, small efforts create massive systemic change."* This is Meadows' claim, and it is qualitative. The page reads it as a change in kind, in its own words: #3, *"isn't a parameter change — it changes every downstream decision"*; #2, *"a click of the mind"*. The piece tests a different reading, the goal as a continuous dial turned by the same share of its range. That fails, and then the piece's own "after the verdict" step confirms the page's reading. Taking views out of what the creator steers by gives D 2.46. The yield at the end of its stated range (2×) gives 0.50; at 0.5× it gives 0.25. The heading and caption still say "Fails in this model", and "the core insight fails". A refutation that confirms the page under the page's own reading is not a refutation of the page.

Two more points, both on the piece's explanation, not its numbers:
- *"That is the delay (#9) deciding whether the goal (#3) can act at all"* is not what the model shows. This piece runs with no delay on the slow signal (`Ds = 0` by default). Looking less often does not free the goal either: at 30-day looks, g = 0.5 moves D only 0.049.
- *"The daily lottery in views outshouts a day's change"* is only part of it. With the lottery all but gone (σ = 0.01), a 10% goal nudge still gives D 0.000, and decisions still follow views 99.9% of the time. The cause is how the model is built. Views answer the promotion share at once and in proportion (V ∝ u), and the slow stocks barely move in a day.

**Labelling.** The piece calls g "the goal (#3)", and at g = 1 the creator maximizes community and wellbeing. That is the page's own #3 shift, from "maximize reach" to "maximize wellbeing and right relationships", so the label matches. Note that in this model #3 and #6 are the same operation, because what the creator watches is what they maximize.

**Does it walk up?** Yes, and well. One stock (the bathtub, its settle point checked at 5e-15), then four, then the closed loop, then the three knobs, then the test.

**Remake, in one round:**
1. Retitle the verdict to what was tested: "a small nudge of the goal, as a dial, does nothing in this model." Do not write "the core insight fails". Change the caption to match.
2. Say on the test step that the parameter's D per unit effort is fixed by construction, the same in every draw.

## Who is steering · The 12 Leverage Points (#9) — **remake**

**Does it test or decorate?** It tests. The hijack rule is written first, both signals are scored, and the meter has a positive control (1.000) and a coin-flip null (0.4995). The maker found and named the rule's own flaw: monthly looks pass only because the slow-alone reference fell.

**Does it build the page's situation?** In kind, yes: an instant noisy signal beside a slow, more valuable one. But the explicit 30-day delay is not what makes the hijack. With no delay (Ds = 0, daily looks), decisions follow views 99.9% and the slow signal 49.2%, still hijacked. With the lottery almost gone (σ = 0.01) it is still hijacked (99.1%). The 99.9% comes from how the model is built: views answer the promotion share at once and in proportion. So the line *"Nothing in the rule favours views"* is true of the weights and misleading about the model. The structure favours views, and that structure is what the page means by "instant". The delay's real effect is on the reference. At monthly looks the slow-alone creator reaches K 2.17 with no delay and 0.73 with the 30-day delay. So "the reference fell" is an artefact of stacking a 30-day window on a 30-day delay. Without the delay, monthly looks are hijacked by the rule as well.

**Is "checking less often" the page's intervention?** Only part of it. The page says *"weekly reflection, monthly assessment, no daily metric checking"*, and it does not say views keep half the weight in the reflection. The piece keeps g = 0.5 and only stretches τ. Then it concludes *"the move that frees the slow signal is two levers higher on the page's list, not this one."* My rebuild says the two levers work together, and the cadence is not idle. With views demoted to 1% of what the creator watches (g = 0.99), across 64 draws of 20 creators:
- Daily looks lift the capture in 16 of 64 draws. Weekly looks lift it in **64 of 64**.
- Weekly looks raise K by more than 10% over daily in 63 of 64. Median K goes from 1.13 daily to 1.74 weekly.
- At the defaults: daily is hijacked (K 1.09); weekly is not (K 1.80, 91% of decisions follow the slow signal).
- At g = 0.95, weekly lifts the capture in 36 of 64 and daily in 0.

So demoting the metric and slowing the looks free the slow signal together. Neither does it alone at equal weights (0 of 64, which confirms the maker's number).

**What would have meant failure for each rule?** For the daily rule: agreement with views less than 0.20 above slow, or K above 90% of the reference. Neither could happen at g = 0.5 here, given how views are built. For the monthly rule: the same, but the reference moved too. The maker's commit already names that lesson.

**Does it walk up?** Yes. One slow signal, then a delay, then views beside it, then the meter, then the test.

**Remake, in one round:**
1. On the third step, replace "Nothing in the rule favours views" with what is true: the weights are equal, and views answer posting instantly. Add the no-delay line (99.9% / 49.2%), so the reader sees that the hijack comes from timescale, not from the 30-day lag.
2. Add one arm to the temporal-discipline step: views demoted (g = 0.99) at daily and at weekly looks, scored by the same rule.
3. Rewrite the after-verdict paragraph. Temporal discipline frees the slow signal when the metric is demoted beside it. It is not "two levers higher, not this one."
4. Optional: fix the reference in the rule so it does not move with τ (for example, slow-alone at daily looks). The commit already names that lesson.

## Offers

**Offer 1, Meadows' Core Insight, claim-change. Disputed.** The numbers are right: Q ≤ 1 in 64 of 64 at both sizes, and the switch gives D 2.46 against 0.50. The reading is not. The page already says that a goal or paradigm change is a change in kind (#3, "isn't a parameter change"; #2, "a click of the mind"). The model confirms the opportunity line under that reading. It refutes only the dial reading, which the page does not make. An edit adding "a change in kind" to the opportunity line restates what #3 already says, so it is a **no-claim-edit at most**, and Loudon may simply decline it. What the model cannot say, and the piece rightly says so, is whether a whole change of goal is a small effort. The section's own quote holds that tension on purpose (the higher the point, the more the system resists).

**Offer 2, #9, claim-change. Confirmed in part; overreaching as worded.** Confirmed: the hijack reproduces (99.9% / 50.3%, K 1.03 against 1.90). Looking less often while still weighing views equally does not lift it (0 of 64). Overreaching: "temporal discipline works only with the metric out of the loop". Demoting the metric is enough, and then the cadence does the work: at g = 0.99, weekly looks lift the capture in 64 of 64 draws against 16 of 64 daily. The right kind is a small **claim-change**. The #9 paragraph could say that temporal discipline frees the slow signal when the metric is also demoted in what you reflect on. "No daily metric checking" is the nearest phrase, and the edit sharpens it more than it reverses it. On the section field: the manifest already names `"The 12 Leverage Points (Lowest to Highest)"`, the page's own `##` heading, and that is correct. Do not write it as "→ #9": the arrow is for findings whose home is another page, and #9 is a paragraph inside this page's section.

## The face as a whole

This face is built with care. One honest model runs both pieces. The rules are written before the runs, the controls are real, the walk-ups are good, and the maker names its own weakest joints in its own words. It does make the page's idea felt in one place, and it is the place the page cares about most: the goal slider sitting dead until views leave what the creator steers by, then everything moving at once. That is Meadows' switch, made visible. But both pieces present that confirmation as a refutation. One tests the goal as a dial the page never claimed. The other tests temporal discipline without the half of it that makes it work. The fixes are in the wording and one added arm, not in the engine, so both are remakes and not retires. Every number here is the model's, from a structure the maker chose, and none of it is a measure of a person.
