What does the diagonal throw away?

The section says the diagonal of a Volterra kernel is a Hammerstein structure, a static curve then a filter, and that it keeps harmonic generation but discards intermodulation. Four steps, one idea each. The last one puts two tones into the second-order kernel, reads them on the diagonal and off it, and counts what comes out.

(the second-order output) = add up, over every pair of past moments τ₁ and τ₂, (the kernel's weight for that pair) × (the input at the first moment) × (the input at the second). The lag plane above is the floor h₂ stands on; this piece reads it at one point at a time.
on the diagonal, τ₁ = τ₂:   y = g ∗ x²  ·  H₂(f₁, f₂) = G(f₁ + f₂)
(on the diagonal) both moments are the same moment, so the input is squared and then filtered: a static curve and a filter, the Hammerstein shape. Its weight for any product depends only on the frequency the product lands on, f₁ + f₂, not on which two frequencies made it.
off it, h₂ = δ(τ₁) · δ(τ₂ − D):   y = x(t) · x(t − D)
(one point off the diagonal) the input now times the input D seconds ago.
two tones A·cos 2πf₁t + A·cos 2πf₂t:   each harmonic A²⁄2 ·   sum A²·|cos π(f₂ − f₁)D| ·   difference A²·|cos π(f₁ + f₂)D|
(what comes out) each octave, 2f₁ and 2f₂, at half the squared amplitude, whatever D is; the sum product at the squared amplitude times a cosine of the tones' spacing times the lag; the difference product the same with their total. At D = 0 both cosines are 1: intermodulation at full strength, 6 dB above the harmonics.
