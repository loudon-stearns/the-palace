One table: an oscillator, or an effect?

The page asks for one control surface that toggles a single stored field between a synth that scans it and an effect that reads it with its input. Here it is, in five steps. The field never changes; only the reader does. The last step tests where the two readers are really the same reader, and where they are not.

scan-read:   y(t) = F( f₁·t, f₂·t )
(the synth's output) = the stored field F, read at the place two phasors point to, each turning once around the circle per cycle of its frequency. The table wraps both ways, so the place lives on a torus.
input-read:   y(t) = F( arccos x(t) ⁄ 2π,  arccos x(t − D) ⁄ 2π )
(the effect's output) = the same field, read at the place the input's value now and its value D seconds ago point to. An amplitude has to be turned into a place on a circle; this piece uses the inverse cosine, which sends −1…+1 to the half-circle from ½ back to 0. An amplitude says how far round, never which way, so the effect only ever reads a quarter of the torus, and goes there and back without winding.
x(t) = cos 2πft  ⇒  effect = F( fold(ft), fold(ft − fD) )  =  synth at 1 : 1, lag fD,  when F(u, v) = F(−u, v) = F(u, −v)
(where they meet) a full-scale sine names its own phase, folded into the half-circle. So the effect fed that sine is the synth at ratio 1 : 1 with its second phasor lagging by f·D of a turn, exactly, whenever the field looks the same mirrored across each axis. Any other field is heard folded.
F(u, v) = Σ cjk · cos 2πju · cos 2πkv  =  Σ cjk · Tj(a) · Tk(b)
(the mirrored fields) a field built only from products of cosines is a polynomial in the two amplitudes a and b (the Chebyshev polynomials T). The one grid of numbers cjk is the synth's partial at j·f₁ ± k·f₂ and the effect's weight on Tj(x(t))·Tk(x(t − D)), a Volterra term of order up to j + k at the lags 0 and D.
