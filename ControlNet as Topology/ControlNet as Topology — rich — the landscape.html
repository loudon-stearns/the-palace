A terrain you can carve

The page's landscape, built up from one ball in one valley. Five steps, one idea each: the ball rolls downhill, the fog cools rough to fine, the commission is leaned on, a canyon is carved where no valley was, and last the test of denoise against weight. Nothing here runs a model: it is a made-up terrain of six valleys, simulated in your browser, and it moves exactly as the equations below say.

where the canyon lies
E(x) = −log p(x),   score(x) = −∇E(x)
(the energy at a point) = −log(how likely the model finds that image). The score is the steering force: (the downhill direction) = −(the slope of the energy). Likely images are valleys; the ball follows the score.
x ← x + (σᵢ² − σᵢ₊₁²) · score(x, σᵢ) + √(σᵢ² − σᵢ₊₁²) · z
one stroke of the carving: (where the ball goes next) = (where it is) + (how much fog this stroke removes) × (the downhill direction, seen through the fog σᵢ) + (a fresh random nudge the same size). The fog σ falls from 1.6 to 0.004 over 160 strokes. Seen through fog σ, every valley is widened by σ in each direction: that is what adding noise does.
score = score_all + w · (score_lion − score_all)
CFG: (the steering) = (the pull of all images) + w × (how much more the lion terrain pulls than all images do). w = 1 is the lion terrain itself; higher w overshoots it. It can only reweight what both terrains already hold.
score = score_terrain + λ · score_canyon
ControlNet, as drawn here: (the steering) = (the terrain's pull) + λ × (the canyon's pull). The canyon is a separate shape added in, so it can put a valley where the terrain had none.
σ_start = σ at (1 − d) of the way through the strokes
denoise strength d: image to image starts from the init, lifts it d of the way back up into the fog, and carves only the rest. d = 1 forgets the init; d = 0.2 keeps most of it.
A model of the page's picture, not of a network. The terrain is six Gaussian valleys in two dimensions, not a million; the probabilities are exact, so the fog is exact too. The ControlNet weight here multiplies the canyon's pull; in a real network it scales the ControlNet's residuals inside the U-Net, which the page's picture reads as carving deeper. The schedule is geometric, where Stable Diffusion's is not, so a denoise value here is the same idea at a different place on the dial. The canyon is drawn into the terrain at every fog level, the way CFG and compositional diffusion add scores; that is approximate even in the model. The canyon is a narrow well over a nearly flat floor (weight 0.02 against 0.98, spread 3), so its pull fades a few widths away: the denoise × weight map's low-denoise zeros depend on that choice. A ball counts as in a valley within three of that valley's spreads; otherwise it is between. Every run is seeded, so the same knobs give the same balls.
