A short practice on an artist's page. It is not therapy or treatment, and it promises nothing about how you will feel. You can stop at any point; nothing is counted and nothing is kept.

Where it comes from, and what it can't do

The push and the letting go are adapted from “Pushing away paper” by Russ Harris (2017; first “pushing the clipboard” in ACT Made Simple, 2009), an exercise from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, the work of Steven C. Hayes, Kirk Strosahl and Kelly Wilson. In the original two people sit side by side and hold a sheet of paper at arm's length; here it is a press held on a screen, which is a much smaller thing. It is kin to their “tug-of-war with a monster”, where the move is to drop the rope. Harris says of his own exercise that it is only a metaphor and does not by itself teach acceptance. The same is true of this.

The listening line is Pauline Oliveros's. The coming back is how Shunryu Suzuki taught sitting. The two columns are Epictetus's; putting the feeling's arrival in the second one follows his own account, that an impression comes unasked and what is ours begins after it.

The Stoics and ACT part ways on what to do next with the thought: the Stoic examines it and may argue with it; ACT leaves it unargued and asks what you want your hands to do. This piece stops before that fork.
