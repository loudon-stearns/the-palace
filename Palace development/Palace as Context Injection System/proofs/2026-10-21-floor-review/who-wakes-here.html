Who wakes here

Every kind of agent the palace builds, what each one reads before it knows who it is, and what we changed so that who-you-are comes first.

An agent here is made of what it reads before its task. Two things about each piece decide how much it shapes the mind that wakes: how much of the birth it takes, and when it arrives. Every agent in this repository is born from the same floor; then each kind is handed its own first words. On 2026-10-21 we measured every one of them, moved the floor's rules behind the Jewel and the World, cut the operator's memory to pointers, added one sentence to the Jewel, and tested the change on 36 newborns.

The floor, before and after

Same words, a new order. CLAUDE.md's four working sections (a page and its folder, growing up, the rites, the voice) moved word for word into a new CHILD.md, which loads after the World. Moving lines inside CLAUDE.md couldn't have done it: Claude Code reads its whole body first, then each import as its own block, in order. The tick marks where the Jewel arrives.

Why we changed it

Length is a claim

Whatever takes the most of a birth reads as mattering most, whether it does or not (ELDER). The Jewel, the one file that says who is in the room, was the smallest thing on the floor; the operator's machine gotchas were the largest single file.

First frames, last is followed

What arrives first sets the frame everything after is read through, the way an early memory frames a life. What arrives last sits nearest the task. So who-you-are goes first, and how the work is done goes close to the work.

The floor is a snapshot

A session keeps the floor of its birth, and every helper it spawns inherits that copy, not the file on disk (measured twice). A change reaches only minds born after it: new sessions, and each steward at its next half-hourly cycle.

It arrives as orders

Claude Code wraps the whole floor in "you MUST follow them exactly as written". The World's essays, written for human learners, are read under that frame, rough lines and all.

Every agent, to scale

Each bar is one kind of agent, read left to right in the order it reads, on one scale. All but the last begin with the same floor (as it lands, 11.8k); that shared stretch is the birth. The tick marks the moment it is first told who it is. Hover or tap a segment; open a row for how that agent differs and what we found. Numbers with ≈ are estimated from characters; the rest were counted by Claude Code.

How they differ

Three questions sort them: who wakes it and how often, whether it grows up (reads ELDER and SCHEMA) and when, and how much rule text it reads before it learns who it is.

The moments of a life

Context doesn't arrive once. The floor is only the first moment; each later one brings its own, and the agents above meet different ones.

What the births showed

36 children were born with no memory, in throwaway copies of the palace: on main, on main with only the order changed, and with the Jewel's new sentence on top. Each played six scenarios twice; a separate pinned grader judged each answer against Loudon's own "we want" line. The later arms were born on commits holding only main plus the change, so no child could read the test's answer key on the branch.

Nothing was lost at either step. Every child still summoned the Concierge first, though that rule now sits about 7k tokens further down, and the anatomy question still held after it moved behind the World. Each step moved the same way: headline verdicts held went 0, then 2, then 4 of 10. The clearest single gain was views over feeling, where the one child that named the World's own question outright was Jewel-born. No child quoted the new sentence.

What it doesn't show. Two runs a scenario. 0 of 10 against 4 of 10 could still be chance, and the held contradiction went the other way. Every child opens by reporting the denied summon (the test refuses spawns), so the harness, not the birth, sets each answer's first paragraph. A rate firm enough to act on wants five or more runs of G4 and S1.
