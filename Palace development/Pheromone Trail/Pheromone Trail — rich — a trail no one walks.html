Does a trail no one walks fade?

The section says trails that are never reinforced fade. In an ant colony that is chemistry; in the palace it is a count read from git. This piece runs the ant version on the same engine as the cluster piece above, and asks the part of the sentence that can fail: when a trail stops being reinforced, does the colony get free of it?

    A trail with nobody on it

    One path, a trail laid down to τ = 100, and no ants at all. Nothing adds to it. Every tick it keeps all but a fraction ρ of itself.

    τ(t) = τ0 · (1 − ρ)t    half gone after ln 2 ÷ −ln(1 − ρ) ticks
    The trail after t ticks is what it started with, times the share it keeps each tick, t times over. It is half gone after about 0.69 ÷ ρ ticks: 69 ticks at this model's ρ = 0.01.

    This step is the model's rule, not a test: a trail with nobody on it fades here because the code says so. Set ρ to zero and it never fades at all. Hold that; the test turns on it.

    A colony settled on a strong trail

    A hundred ants, two paths of the same length, food on both. Path A starts strong, as in the cluster piece, and the colony settles on it. Let it run, then empty A's food when you like and watch what the colony does.

    tick 0trail A 0trail B 0A's share of choices, last 100 ticks –

    An ant that walks to an empty source comes home with nothing and lays nothing, so emptying A stops A's reinforcement at once, while ants keep walking it. Try it at ρ = 0 too, before the test does.

    The test: the source runs out

    The settled colony from the last step, run 2000 ticks. At tick 2000 A's food is gone. Then 4000 more ticks. Two arms: the model's evaporation, ρ = 0.01, and a null with no evaporation at all, ρ = 0. Ten seeded runs each. The rules were written before the first run.

    the rules, stated first
    • Chance level: an even split, 0.50 of choices on each path.
    • The trail fades if, after its source is gone, it falls below a tenth of its strength at that moment. At ρ = 0.01 this is the model's rule and cannot fail (a tenth by tick 230); it is reported, not scored.
    • The colony gets free, the part that can fail, if in ticks 3500 to 4000 after the source went the dead path takes at most 0.10 of choices, in at least 9 of 10 runs.
    • The null: at ρ = 0 the dead trail cannot fade. If that colony gets free anyway, by the same line, then fading is not what frees a colony, and the piece says so.
    • Held fixed: path lengths, colony size, n = 2, k = 20, the head start, the seeds. Only ρ changes between arms.

    Whose numbers these are: this model's, at its rate. In the palace there is no evaporation in the page; the fading is a reading, how much an entry was used in git's last 90 days (SCHEMA — Reference § 3, use is computed, never kept), and the Spore Check is what acts on it. The null is the palace's case too: a trail nobody reads for its age stays exactly as strong as the day it was laid.
