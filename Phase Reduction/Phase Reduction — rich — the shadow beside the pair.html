The shadow beside the pair

Phase reduction says you can forget almost everything about a pair of weakly coupled oscillators: keep one number each, where it is round its loop, and the two numbers obey Kuramoto's equation. That forgetting is the shadow. This walk builds it a piece at a time and then measures, as the coupling grows, where the shadow stops telling the truth. Nothing plays until you press a sound button; headphones help.

Step 1 of 5 · one loop, one number

A phase is time round the loop, not an angle

One oscillator on its loop. Its phase is how far through the cycle it is, counted in time: it ticks round at a steady rate even when the point itself speeds up and slows down. On a nearly round loop the phase and the plain angle of the point almost agree. On the relaxation loop the point crawls, then jumps, and the angle races ahead of the phase and falls behind it. The phase is the one number the shadow keeps.

Step 2 of 5 · two loops, nothing between them

Without coupling, the gap only slides

Two copies of the loop, the second one running a little faster, and nothing connecting them. Each phase ticks round at its own rate, so the gap between them, ψ, slides steadily: one whole turn every 1 ÷ δ trips. In the sound, one oscillator is in each ear; the slide is the slow beat you hear between them.

Step 3 of 5 · the collapse

The pull, averaged round one cycle

Now let each oscillator feel ε times the other's x minus its own. Each push lands somewhere on the loop, and the phase response curve says how much a push there moves the phase. Multiply the curve (gold) by the push (blue) at every point of the cycle and average: that number, Γ(ψ), is the pull at gap ψ. Only part of it moves the gap. The part that is the same whichever oscillator is ahead speeds both up together; the part that flips sign draws them together or apart. Where that drawing part balances δ, the shadow locks.

TryCompare the bottom curve on the three loops. On the gentle loop it nearly vanishes: the pull is almost the same whichever way round the gap is, so it hardly draws at all. Hold that thought for step 5.

Step 4 of 5 · the pair and its shadow, side by side

Both lock, nearly together

Now run the full pair, every bit of both states, and the shadow, two phases and the pull, from the same start. Dots are the full pair's gap at each trip; the dashed line is the shadow's. At weak coupling they settle to nearly the same gap. Nearly: the shadow threw away how far each oscillator is pushed off its loop between kicks, and that leftover shows as a few degrees.

Step 5 of 5 · the test

The shadow is right only as the coupling goes to zero

The page's claim, in numbers: on the van der Pol loop at μ 1 the shadow's locking range matches the pair's up to a coupling of 0.1, and by 0.4 the two have parted; on the gentle loop the shadow misses even at 0.05.
