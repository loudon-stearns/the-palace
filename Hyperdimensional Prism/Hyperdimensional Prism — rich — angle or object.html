A new angle, or a new object?

The section says the Lissajous pattern changes with the frequency relationship of its oscillators, and that different angles of light give different projections. This rebuilds the trace from one oscillator, finds the four-dimensional curve the scope is drawing a shadow of, and last asks which changes are angles of light on that curve and which make a new curve.

Sound starts only on listen: the scope’s x in your left ear at 220 Hz, its y in your right at 220 Hz times the ratio. Headphones keep the two ears apart. Changing step stops it.
y = sin(bt + φ) = cos φ · sin bt + sin φ · cos bt
In words: turning the phase only mixes two coordinates the curve already has, sin bt and cos bt. So the scope at any phase is one light on one fixed curve in four dimensions, (sin at, cos at, sin bt, cos bt). Changing a or b changes those coordinates themselves.
S = [ L₁ ; L₂ ; L₃ ; L₄ ] · X   ⇒   rank S ≤ 4
In words: four figures that are all shadows of that one four-dimensional curve, stacked, never need more than four directions. The count in step 5 is that rank, the same count the opening’s piece uses.
