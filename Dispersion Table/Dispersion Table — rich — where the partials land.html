Where the partials land

A frequency rings in a loop when one trip round brings it back in step with itself: a whole number of turns. Count the turns a trip takes, frequency by frequency, and the partials are wherever that count crosses a whole number. First with nothing in the loop but delay, then with a lag that grows in a straight line, then with one that bends.

1 · The bare loop: turns in a straight line

A trip through N samples of delay turns a frequency f through f · N ⁄ fs whole cycles. That count rises in a straight line with frequency. Where it crosses 1, 2, 3 … the frequency comes back in step, and the loop rings there. Equal steps up the line give equal steps along the frequency axis: a harmonic series.

watchThe line crossing each whole number of turns. Each crossing drops to a gold tick, and every tick lands on the dashed harmonic grid.
hearA buzzy plucked tone. A longer loop, a lower pitch.
turns in one trip, against frequency · gold ticks: where the loop rings · dashed: whole multiples of the first
2π f τ = 2π k ⇒ f = k ⁄ τ, τ = N ⁄ fs
(the turns a frequency makes in one trip) must be a whole number k, so the loop rings at k divided by the trip time: every whole multiple of one frequency

2 · A lag that grows in a straight line

Put Karplus-Strong's average in the loop: each sample goes back as the mean of itself and the one before. That reads half a sample late, at every frequency, so its lag grows in a straight line, f · ½ ⁄ fs turns. The Karplus-Strong walk on Waveguide Synthesizer ends here: the pitch is fs ⁄ (N + ½), not fs ⁄ N. Watch what a straight lag does to the series.

doPluck with the average, then without it.
watchThe line gets a little steeper and every crossing slides down, but they stay on the dashed grid: equal steps still.
hearWith the average the pitch drops a little and the pluck turns soft. It stays a string, in tune with itself.
turns in one trip · faint: the bare delay alone · bright: delay plus the average
θ(f) = 2π f · ½ ⁄ fs ⇒ f = k ⁄ (τ + ½ ⁄ fs)
(the average's lag) is half a sample's worth of turns at every frequency, so the whole series moves down together and stays whole multiples of its first

3 · A lag that bends

Now a lag that does not grow in a straight line: eight first-order allpasses in the loop. They pass every frequency at full level and delay the low ones more than the high ones. The bare delay is shortened to make room, so the first partial keeps its pitch. Turn up the bend.

doPluck at no bend, then at more and more.
watchThe line bows away from the straight dashed one a harmonic loop would follow. Its crossings slide off the dashed grid, the high ones most. The bars count how far, in cents.
hearThe pluck goes from a plain string to a stretched, slightly metallic one.
turns in one trip · dashed line: a harmonic loop with the same first partial · bright: delay plus the allpasses
2π f τ + θ(f) = 2π k
(the turns through the bare delay) plus (the allpasses' lag, which bends) must come to a whole number: where the lag bends, the crossings no longer fall at equal steps

4 · The test: does the sound land on the crossings?

So far the ticks are the equation's. Now render each loop, measure where its partials really are, and hold them against the crossings. The page makes two claims to check. With no lag the series is harmonic. With a lag that is frequency-dependent and nonlinear the partials leave the grid. Step 2 adds a third thing to check: a lag that is frequency-dependent but straight only moves the pitch.
