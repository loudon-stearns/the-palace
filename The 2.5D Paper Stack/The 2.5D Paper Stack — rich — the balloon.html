A balloon has a place in the stack

The walker on the ridge says one line. Five steps: the balloon on the plate, a depth for it, the camera moving, the balloon breathing while its letters hold, and last every placement on the same camera move, measured. The panel border is ruled here; the sheets are track VI's plate, cut.

1 · One balloon

A balloon, its tail pointing at the speaker's mouth. On a still frame it doesn't matter where in the stack it lives: it only has a position on the page.

2 · A depth for it

Give the balloon a depth, and the stack decides what covers what. Behind the walker, he covers its tail; in front of him and behind the panel border is where the page puts it.

3 · The camera moves

Now the camera trucks sideways and every sheet slides by its depth. Watch the tail's tip against the walker's mouth.

When the camera steps c sideways, a sheet at depth p slides c·p the other way; the tail leaves the mouth by the camera's step times the difference in their depths.

4 · It breathes; the letters hold

The balloon's outline and its tail are drawn lines, so they may move with the same kind of warp that moves any other sheet. The letters are meaning to be read, so they stay locked.

Turn the letters' warp on and read the line while it moves: the same few pixels that make the outline feel drawn make the words swim.

5 · Every placement, one camera move

The test. The camera sweeps 40 pixels each way; each placement is measured on the same sweep. The page says a balloon has a place in the stack, in front of its speaker and behind the panel border, and that there it drifts with him. The narration sits outside, in the margin.
