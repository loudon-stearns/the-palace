The peak between bins

An FFT sorts a sound into bins, each 11.7 Hz wide here (4096 samples at 48 kHz). A sine that falls between two bins shows up as a few bars, not one, and the tallest bar can be off by up to half a bin. The page fits a parabola through the three tallest bars to find the peak between them. Four steps; the last tests the page's formula. Each time you play, the true tone sounds with an estimate beside it: the number of beats per second is how far off the estimate is, in hertz.

vertex: f̂ = f_k + (Δf ⁄ 2) · (m[k−1] − m[k+1]) ⁄ (m[k−1] − 2m[k] + m[k+1])
(the parabola's peak) = (the tallest bar's frequency) + (half a bin) × (left neighbour − right neighbour) ⁄ (the bars' bend, which is negative at a peak). The two lines differ only in the sign of the last bracket.
