Quadratic Interpolation in DSP · what it is · four steps

The joints

The page says quadratic interpolation "has changing slope but continuous first derivative": the curve bends, but never has a sharp corner. One parabola can't have a corner. The question is what happens where one parabola hands over to the next. Four steps; the last is the test.

samples per cycle Resets the samples to one cycle of a sine. Drag any sample to make your own.
y(x) = y₋₁(x² − x) ⁄ 2 − y₀(x² − 1) + y₁(x² + x) ⁄ 2
(the value between y₀ and y₁, a fraction x of the way) = each of the three samples, weighted by a parabola that is 1 at its own point and 0 at the other two
y′(x) = y₋₁(2x − 1) ⁄ 2 − 2x·y₀ + y₁(2x + 1) ⁄ 2
(the slope, how fast the value climbs per sample) = the same three samples, weighted by straight lines in x, so inside one parabola the slope slides and never jumps
jump at sample n = y′(x = 1, arriving) − y′(x = 0, leaving) = −½ (yₙ₊₁ − 3yₙ + 3yₙ₋₁ − yₙ₋₂)
(the corner at a sample) = (the slope the arriving parabola ends with) − (the slope the leaving parabola starts with) = minus half of the four samples' third difference, which is zero only when all four lie on one parabola
