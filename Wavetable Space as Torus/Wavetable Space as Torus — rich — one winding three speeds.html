One winding, three speeds

The listening experiment the page proposes, run. A scalar field on the three-circle torus T³ is read along a closed path: phase φ, frame θ and beat phase ψ, each a circle. Left, the path in the cube whose opposite faces are glued, which is T³ unrolled. Right, one cycle of the sound. Underneath, the energy on each harmonic of the cycle: the part of the timbre that belongs to one loop.

φ = p·w , θ = q·w + b·sin 2πu , ψ = r·w , w = u + (ε ⁄ 2π)·sin 2πu , u = F·t
(each circle's position, in laps) = (its winding number) × (how far round the loop the read is); u counts loops at the loop rate F. ε makes the speed along the loop uneven without moving the path; b bends the path through the frames. Both are zero until the last step, and neither changes a winding: the sine comes back to zero at the end of every loop
W(φ, θ, ψ) = Σ a·sin(2π(m·φ + n·θ + l·ψ) + c) → harmonic k = m·p + n·q + l·r of F
the field is a sum of ripples; read along the (p, q, r) path at an even speed, each ripple sounds at the whole number m·p + n·q + l·r times the loop rate, whatever the loop rate is
moved = 1 − Σ min(ek ⁄ Σe , e′k ⁄ Σe′)
(the share of the energy on different harmonics) = 1 − the overlap of the two spreads of energy over the harmonics; 0 when one cycle and the other put the same share on every harmonic
