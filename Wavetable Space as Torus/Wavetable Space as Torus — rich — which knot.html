Which knot do you hear?

One table, read along a closed path on the torus. The phase φ runs across a waveform cycle; the frame θ runs through the table's frames. Left, the path drawn on a donut, where a closed path can be a knot. Right, the same path on the torus unrolled, opposite edges glued. Underneath, the one thing the ear gets: how the loop's energy sits on its harmonics.

Sound starts only when you press play, and changing step stops it. Steps two and three hold the loop at 110 Hz. The test holds the phase rate at 220 Hz instead, so (3, 2) loops at 73.3 Hz: every path reads the waveform cycle at one rate, and what differs is the frames.
φ = p·u , θ = q·u , u = F·t
(where the read is across the cycle, in laps) = p × (how many loops so far); (where it is through the frames, in laps) = q × (the same count); the count runs at the loop rate F. After one loop the read is home: p laps of phase, q laps of frame
W(φ, θ) = Σ a·sin(2π(m·φ + n·θ) + c) → y(t) sounds at harmonic k = m·p + n·q of F
the table is a sum of ripples, each m times across the cycle and n times through the frames; read along the path, each ripple sounds at the whole number m·p + n·q times the loop rate. The table decides the ripples, the path decides where they land
crossings of the (p, q) torus knot = min(p(q − 1), q(p − 1)) , (p, q) and (q, p) are the same knot
(how many times the knot must cross itself in any drawing) = the smaller of p × (q − 1) and q × (p − 1); swap the two numbers and the knot does not change. With p or q equal to 1 it is no knot at all: the unknot
