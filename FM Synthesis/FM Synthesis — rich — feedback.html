Feedback — one operator, listening to itself

One sine at 200 Hz whose own output is fed back into its phase. No partner, no second oscillator. The left panel is two cycles of the wave; the right is its first sixteen harmonics, each against the first, with a sawtooth's levels marked as ticks. Walk the loop up a step at a time; the last step tests the section's claim across the whole range.

β = π · 2( fb − 7 )
(how hard the loop pushes, in radians) = π × 2 to the power of (the feedback setting − 7): each step up doubles it, and the DX7's top, 7, pushes by π. This is at the operator's full level; on the DX7 the loop shrinks with the operator's own volume.
Meters: this page's own engine rendered offline at 48 kHz on the 200 Hz note, 80 whole cycles after a settling tenth of a second. "Near a sawtooth" is the root-mean-square gap, in dB, between harmonics 2 to 10 and a sawtooth's 1 ⁄ k. "Repeats with the note" is the share of the sound's energy that is the same in every cycle. What you hear plays at your device's own sample rate.
