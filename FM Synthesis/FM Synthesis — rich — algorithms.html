Six operators — wirings of one pair

The DX7's six operators, wired by its own table. Carriers sit on the bottom row and are heard; everything above them pushes. A dashed box is an operator turned down to nothing. Under the diagram, the count: how many partials the patch makes that are within 60 dB of its loudest, below 5 kHz. Walk from one pair to three of the 32 wirings; the last step sets a stack against a pair.

y3 = sin( ω3t + I4 · sin( ω4t + I5 · sin ω5t ) )
(the carrier) = sine of ( its own phase + (operator 4's index) × (operator 4, which is itself a sine pushed by operator 5) ). A pair has one comb of partials, the carrier plus whole numbers of one modulator; a stack has the carrier plus whole numbers of each modulator, every combination of the two.
Counts are spectral peaks found on a 16384-point Blackman window at 48 kHz, so partials closer than about 12 Hz count as one: a floor, not an exact tally. Ratios, levels and indices are this piece's, not Yamaha's factory data.
