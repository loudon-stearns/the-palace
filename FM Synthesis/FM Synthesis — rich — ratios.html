Ratios — on the grid, and off it

One pair: a carrier you hear at 220 Hz and a modulator pushing it. Every partial the pair makes sits at the carrier plus a whole number of modulators. The faint lines are the note's harmonic grid, 220, 440, 660 and up; a partial drawn amber lands on it, one drawn violet falls off it. Walk the ratio one step at a time. The last step is the section's claim, set against any ratio you like.

f = fc + k · fm , k = … −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 …
(each partial) = (the carrier) + (a whole number, negative too) × (the modulator). A partial that comes out below 0 Hz folds back, and one at exactly 0 Hz is silent.
fm ⁄ fc = p ⁄ q ⇒ f0 = fc ⁄ q
(the modulator ÷ the carrier), written as two whole numbers with nothing in common, gives (the pitch the pair repeats at) = (the carrier) ÷ (the bottom number). The patch comes round again every 1 ÷ f0 seconds; below about 20 repeats a second that is no longer heard as a pitch.
The loudness of each partial is a Bessel function of the index, the sheet on Bessel Functions in Synthesis. Here it only sets how tall the lines are; where they stand is the ratio's doing.
