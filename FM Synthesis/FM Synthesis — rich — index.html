The index — brighter, or shimmering?

A carrier at 220 Hz and a modulator at the same speed, 1 : 1, as the section holds it. Only the index moves. The upper row is the sidebands as FM makes them, the carrier plus and minus whole numbers of the modulator, some of them below 0 Hz. The lower row is what reaches the ear, once those have folded back. Walk the index up; the last step sweeps it slowly and tests the section's claim.

(the pair's sound) = the sum, over every whole number k, of (the k-th Bessel function, at the index) × (a sine at the carrier plus k modulators). Each sideband's loudness rises and falls with the index in its own rhythm; that is the Bessel shape.
J0(I)² + 2 · Σk=1…I+1 Jk(I)² ≈ 0.98
(the carrier's share of the power) + (both sides of the first index-plus-one pairs) comes to about 98 % of all of it: Carson's rule, the section's "index-plus-one pairs".
The brightness centre is the power-weighted mean frequency of what reaches the ear. The sheet of the Bessel functions themselves is on Bessel Functions in Synthesis.
