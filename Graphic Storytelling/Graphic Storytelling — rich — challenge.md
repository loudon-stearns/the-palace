## The inherited grammar — `Graphic Storytelling — rich — time is space.html` · **remake**

**Test or decoration?** A test. It times the page's sentence (`Graphic Storytelling.md:35`, "a wider panel, or more gutter before the next, simply *lasts longer*") with a hold, and it can come out against the page. The geometry is what the commit says (check.mjs: 102 px along the line for both wide layouts, equal middle-panel area 46240 for wide and tall, one ground line, narrow fits).

**Is the hold a fair proxy for "lasts longer"?** Mostly. The question is about story time ("How long did he wait at the door?"), which is McCloud's sense of the word, and dividing by the same story's plain hold takes out each story's scale. Two limits, both stated honestly in the piece's own note (line 134): the reader sees the layout and has just read the claim, and step 2 primes it ("Notice what the wait does", line 313). The tall control can't catch a reader who follows the claim on purpose, because that reader produces the claim's pattern exactly. One more: the strip stays on screen during the hold, so a reader who re-scans while holding turns the hold into reading time. That is arguably McCloud's own mechanism, but the piece should know which one it measures. This is a note, not a remake.

**Does the tall control separate area from line?** Yes, well. I ran a size-reader (holds follow middle-panel area) through the piece's own engine with noise: the tall control came out "longer" 89% of runs at 15% scatter and 63% at 25%. A line-reader with a true 1.3× effect got a false size flag 5–10% of the time. One wording point: the tall panel adds the same panel area, but the whole sheet grows more, because the paper fills the viewBox above panels 1 and 3 (about 58,000 px² against the wide strip's 20,600). So the caption's "the same paper" is true of the panel, not of the sheet. That makes the control stricter, so the fix is only to say "the same panel area".

**Do the shuffle and jitter defeat memory?** Nothing can, with three stories each shown five times: the reader knows the story by its second showing and can anchor to their last hold for it. Anchoring pulls ratios toward 1, so it costs power and doesn't fake a result. The shuffle rule holds (no story or layout twice running, 2000 seeds). The 0.9–1.1 jitter hides absolute size only partly. A wide strip is about 18% wider than plain at the same scale, so the jitter never hides the layout, and it isn't meant to. The tall control is the real size control.

**The chance figure.** Confirmed per layout. Under no effect, the three stories' ratios are independent and each clears ×1.1 less than half the time, so the chance is ≤ (½)³ = 1/8. My runs: 0.035 at 15% hand scatter, 0.051 at 20%, 0.061 at 25% (the maker got 0.0588 at 25%). But the verdict's first line fires when *either* half clears the band, and that chance is 0.063 / 0.091 / 0.110, bounded by 1/4, not 1/8. The equation states the per-layout figure only.

**Can it split the panel half from the gutter half?** Not honestly, as it stands. This is the remake. The split line ("The page puts the two in one sentence; your holds split them", lines 209–210) fires whenever one half clears the band and the other doesn't. That isn't evidence that they differ. I gave hands a true, *equal* 1.3× effect for both halves, at 25% scatter: the verdict said "split" in 33% of runs and "both halves held" in only 15%. At 1.5× and 15% scatter it still said "split" 25% of the time. The failure line overclaims the same way. "Neither held … For your hands, this time, time was not drawn as space" (line 211) fires in 51% of runs where a true 1.3× effect is present. A driven run in headless Chromium, with holds of 200/260/320 ms cycling regardless of layout, produced "the wider gutter shorter in all three" and "the narrow panel … shorter in all three, as the claim would have it", from a hand that was only following its own rhythm.

**The remake, exactly.** (1) Split only on a direct comparison: per story, h(wide)/h(gutter). Say "your holds split them" only when that ratio clears ×1.1 in all three stories, one way or the other; chance ≤ 1/8, the same rule as the others. Otherwise say "one cleared the band and the other didn't; one run can't tell them apart". (2) Change line 211's close to "neither cleared the band in all three; one run can't tell a small effect from none", not "time was not drawn as space". (3) Under the equation, add one line saying that the chance that *either* half shows "longer" is at most 1 in 4. (4) In check.mjs, add a check that equal-effect hands (both 1.3×, 25% scatter) get the new split line at most 1 in 8, and keep the existing checks. Optional, as taste: a second plain hold per story (18 strips). Every ratio for a story rests on one plain hold, so one slip there moves all four rows. Under no effect, when either half came out longer, the tall control also came out longer in 16–20% of runs. That is this shared denominator at work.

**Faithful?** It tests the page's sentence as written. The caption and sub call it "McCloud's line". If offer 1 holds, the gutter half and the "simply" are the page's, not his, so "the page's line, after McCloud" would be more honest. That is a note for the remake, not a reason for it.

**Walks up?** Yes. Closure alone (two panels and the gap, with the in-betweens shown on request), then layout alone (drag width, gutter and height while the drawing stays still), then the instrument alone (hold one second, then four), then the test. Each step is one concept, and the test comes last.

**Runs clean?** Conform: 0 errors, 0 warnings, at 760 and 1130 px. Console clean in the face. No sound, so it has nothing to hush. It declares storage and uses storage. Both its files are tracked. Nothing licensed. Fonts come from Google Fonts, which conform passes.

## The face as a whole

It makes the idea felt, not just busy. Three pieces, each a test, two of them already sat by Loudon with results that moved the page. The made piece is careful work: a real control, an honest note about what blindness it can't have, and a walk up that a reader can follow. Its weak point is the one a careful test is most tempted by: it reads verdicts out of fifteen holds more confidently than fifteen holds allow. "Your holds split them" and "time was not drawn as space" are both the most likely outcomes under conditions where they're false. Fix the verdict lines and it says exactly what one sitting can say, which is the page's own standard for the colour roughs: a reading, not a law.
