One Strip, Six Stories

The same six panels, the same drawing, the same clock and sound, every time. Only the color changes. Play each take, then say what you think it is (a feeling, or where it sits in a longer story) and tell the story it told you. What each one was is hidden until you reveal it.

—

The six, side by side

The story under all six

  1. A city in the rain, at night.
  2. A figure under an umbrella, under a lamp.
  3. A hand, and a glove falling from it.
  4. He walks on. The glove stays, small, in the corner.
  5. The close-up: the glove in a puddle, held twice as long as any other panel.
  6. The street, empty, with the glove still there.

Underneath every take is a loss that nobody noticed, and the reader is the only one who knows. With no words, the strip never says whether he dropped it or let it go. That gap is the puzzle, and color is how each take answers it.
