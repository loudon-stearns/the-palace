Two walkers from the same doors

The page says the palace has well-worn paths, and that walking them leaves the entries beside them unvisited. Here are the palace's own roads, every body link between its entries, and two walkers on them. Four steps, one idea each; the test is the last.

Stand on one page. Every [[link]] in its body is a road out, and a walker here has to choose one. The two walkers differ only in how they choose.

the two ruleseach shown in symbols, then in words
Peven(j) = 1 ⁄ k
The even walker. Every road out of the page is as likely as any other; k is how many roads leave it. This is chance.
Pworn(j) = (uj + 1)α ⁄ Σi (ui + 1)α
The worn walker. A road is taken in proportion to how worn its destination already is. u is that entry's use over the last 90 days (days its page or bundle was edited, plus entries that formed a link to it), plus one, so an unused page is never impossible. α is how much the wear counts.

The rule, fixed before the first run. Budget: 200 walks of 15 hops from the seven doors, repeated over 100 seeds; the worn walker at α = 1. The country is the entries a body road reaches from the doors; the others are out of the test and named below. Chance is the even walker.
  1. A, wear hides country. Holds if the worn walker leaves at least 1.25 times as many entries unreached as the even walker in at least 95 of 100 seeds. Fails if it leaves no more than chance in more than 5 seeds. Anything between is weak.
  2. B, what is hidden is beside the path. Holds if, in the median seed, at least 80% of the entries the worn walker never reached are linked from a page it did stand on.
  3. The null. At α = 0 the worn walker's own code must behave as chance: a median ratio within 1.00 ± 0.05, ahead of the even walker in 30 to 70 seeds. Outside that, the engine is wrong and nothing above counts.
