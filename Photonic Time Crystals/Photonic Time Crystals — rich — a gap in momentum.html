Photonic time crystals · the key reversal

A gap in momentum

The page says a time crystal is a space crystal turned on its side: where a stack of layers makes a band of light die with depth, a medium that breathes in time makes a band grow with time. Five steps: one wave in a plain medium, the crystal in space, the crystal in time, the gap heard, and the test, where the space side and the time side are computed by methods that share no code.

1 · One wave, one medium

A plain medium, the same everywhere and always. A wave in it is a ripple with a wave number k, how many crests fit in a length, and a frequency ω, how many pass a point each second. In a plain medium the two are tied: ω is k times the speed. Every k travels, and keeps its size forever.

the frequency is the wave number times the speed of light, slowed by the medium’s index

2 · A crystal in space

Now stack the medium in layers: its permittivity ε rises and falls along x, one cell after another, and stays still in time. Send in light of one frequency. Most frequencies get through, reshaped cell by cell. Near the frequency whose half-wavelength fits one cell, nothing travels: the wave dies by the same factor every cell. That is the spatial band gap, the dielectric mirror.

ε(x) shaded · the wave across twelve cells (amber) and its size (blue)
its size at each cell boundary, on a log scale
E''(s) + ν² · (1 + 2m cos 2s) · E(s) = 0
the wave’s bend along the stack is set by its frequency squared times the local permittivity; one cell is s from 0 to π, and ν is the frequency in units of the one whose half-wavelength fits a cell

3 · A crystal in time

Now the other way. The medium is the same everywhere, a ring with no layers at all, but its 1/ε breathes in time, rising and falling at a rate Ω. Since every place is alike, a wave keeps its k forever; only its frequency can be pushed. Send one wave forward and watch. Near the k whose frequency is half the breathing rate, it grows, and a second wave appears running backward, the same k, the time-reflected partner, growing with it.

a stretch of the ring · its brightness is 1/ε now
forward (amber) and backward (blue) parts, per breathing period, log scale
D''(t) + (ck)² · (1 + 2m cos Ωt) · D(t) = 0 , ν = 2ck / Ω
each wave number on its own is a spring whose stiffness breathes; ν is the wave number in units of the one whose frequency is half the breathing rate

4 · Hear the gap

The same ring, made long enough to hold a mode every 2.5 Hz, from 2.5 Hz to 1.2 kHz, with a little loss, and a whisper of noise in every mode, the stand-in for the vacuum. Breathe it at a pump rate and listen. The modes near half the pump swell out of the noise. They have different k, so they ring at different pitches when nothing breathes; inside the gap they all sing at one pitch, half the pump. Put the pump between two modes and the pitch you hear belongs to neither. That could fail: two free modes at 440 and 442.5 Hz read about 442.25 Hz on the same zero-crossing count, with two lines and a 14 dB beat. Once the swell settles, pump depletion lets one mode through, still at half the pump.

each mode near half the pump, its size in dB · gap shaded · half the pump marked

The sound runs the ring as its modes, which is exact for a medium the same everywhere: step 5 shows on a full grid that no wave number feeds another. Pump depletion caps the swell: the depth the modes feel falls as they take energy. The loss is ζ = 0.002, so nothing grows below m = 0.004.

5 · The same gap?

The test. On the space side, a stack of 2000 thin uniform layers per cell, each carried by its exact matrix, gives the decay per cell at each frequency. On the time side, a full Yee grid of 8192 cells, uniform in space, its 1/ε breathing, gives its own one-period map for each wave number, with no equation for any single wave anywhere in it. If the page’s reversal holds, the decay per cell at ν in space equals the growth per period at ν in time. The pass rule, before the table: the stack within 2 × 10⁻⁴ of the Mathieu reference, the grid within 5 × 10⁻³ of it (the grid’s own step size). Every modulation here is sinusoidal; a square wave of the same m grows faster (the challenger measured 0.1996 a period at the centre, against 0.1569).

one cell deeper the field is smaller by the same factor; one breathing period later it is larger by the same factor; and the factor is the same number when frequency in space and wave number in time sit at the same place in their gap

Why the two equations are word for word the same: in space the stack varies ε, in time the ring varies 1/ε. Each is the stiffness of its own wave equation, ω²ε in space and k²/ε in time. Varying the same one on both sides gives the same equation only to first order in m.
