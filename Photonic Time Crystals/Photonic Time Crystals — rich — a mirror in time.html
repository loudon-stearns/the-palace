Photonic time crystals · open question one

A mirror in time

The page asks what time-reversed audio from a time-modulated system sounds like, and whether it is anything more than a reverse buffer. One phrase goes into a long uniform line, and comes back four ways: off a wall, off a jump of the whole line’s index, off a jump and a jump back, and out of a reverse buffer. Four steps, the last one the test.

1 · A mirror in space

A phrase of four notes goes into a long line from where you stand. At the far end a wall sends it back. The first note to reach the wall is the first to come home, so the echo keeps the phrase’s order. That is the ordinary mirror: it reverses direction in space, never order in time.

2 · A mirror in time

No wall now; the line runs on. Once the whole phrase is inside it, the whole line’s index jumps at one instant, from n₁ to n₂, everywhere at once. Nothing in space has changed, so every wave keeps its wave number; only its speed and frequency can change. Part of every wave turns round. The note that went in last is nearest, so it comes home first: the phrase returns reversed. And since the line is now slower, it comes back lower and longer.

3 · There and back

Jump the index up and, a moment τ later, back down. Now the returning copy travels at the old speed, so its pitch is the phrase’s own. But it is two turned copies, one from each jump, a hair apart in time and of opposite sign: a comb, a temporal Fabry–Pérot. Some frequencies come back strongly, some not at all, and the lowest hardly come back.

4 · Only a reverse?

The test. The same phrase, sent back by a reverse buffer, by the single jump of step 2 and by the there-and-back of step 3, each measured for order, pitch, length and loudness. Then the rule the engine runs on is checked against Maxwell’s equations on a Yee grid, which knows nothing of it: a pulse, the grid’s 1/ε switched, and what comes back read off the grid.

The line here is lossless and has no dispersion, and the jumps are instant; a real time interface takes a finite rise and a real material disperses, which shades every number above. The level is the electric field, read where the phrase went in. The page also says time-reflection happens “at bandgap frequencies”: the single jump turns every frequency round at once; only a periodic run of jumps, the crystal, makes it strong and selective, and there, in the momentum gap, it also amplifies (the piece above, step 3).
