Three rulers, one Q

The section says the peak's height, the peak's width and the ring's length are one number. Here each is measured on its own, from a swing the page's own equation moves, and each ruler gives back a Q. Nothing on the sheets is drawn from a formula: every curve, edge and time is read off a run. Nothing plays until you press a sound button.

Step 1 of 4 · how long

Strike it once and time the ring

A weight on a spring at 440 Hz, struck once and left alone. It rings at its own rate and forgets, a little each cycle. Time how long the ring takes to fall 60 dB, a thousandth of its first height, and turn that time back into a Q with the page's rule. Only Q moves here.

TryDouble Q and listen: the same pitch, twice as long. Then read the third box. At Q 100 the run loses about a sixteenth of its energy each cycle, as the page says. Pull Q down to 2 and the page's shortcut, 2π ÷ Q, would have it lose more than all of it; the run loses 96%. The shortcut is for a high Q.

Step 2 of 4 · how tall

Push it at its own rate and measure how far it swings

Now nothing strikes it. A smooth push at 440 Hz, its own rate, starts from rest, sized so that the same push held still would move the weight exactly 1. Every cycle adds to the last, until friction spends as much as the push gives. Measure where it stops growing. That height is the second ruler.

TryWatch the third box against step 1's ring time at the same Q: the swell takes the ring's own time to build. The sound is held level to keep your ears safe, so what you hear is the swell's shape, not its height; the sheet shows the height.

Step 3 of 4 · how narrow

Push it at every rate and measure the band it answers

Push at many rates, one run each, and wait every time for the start to die away. Record how far it swings and how fast it moves. Each makes a peak. Find the two rates where a peak has fallen to half its power, 1 ÷ √2 of its height, and the gap between them is the third ruler. The speed's peak is the band-pass filter's; the swing's is the weight's own.

TrySet Q to 10 and the two bands agree to a quarter of a percent. Bring Q down to 3, then 2: the swing's band runs 3%, then 8% wide, and its peak slides below 440 Hz. At Q 1 it has no lower edge at all, because the swing never falls to half power below its peak. The speed's band stays exactly f ÷ Q the whole way down.

Step 4 of 4 · the test

Three rulers, laid side by side

Choose any rate and any Q. The ring, the height and the width are measured again, each in its own run, and each gives back a Q. If the section is right they land on one line. Then the trade itself: the width times the time the ring takes to fall to 1/e should be 1 ÷ π, whatever the Q. Last, the page's own table, measured at 1 kHz.

TryDrag Q from 1 to 200 and watch the three dots ride the line together. Then run the table. If every box agrees, the section's claim stands as measured; if one doesn't, it says by how much.
