Q you can hear

One resonator, two numbers: where it rings (f) and how fast it forgets (Q). Strike it, feed it noise, or push it with a sine and sweep the push through the peak. The three pictures below are the same two numbers: how wide the peak is, how long the ring lasts, and how far the swing falls behind the push. New to this? Five steps up to it come first.

1 · the peak: how wide

Response against frequency, two octaves either side of f. The hatched band is f ÷ Q wide, the half-power width. The sketch holds the peak at one height to keep your ears safe; in the physics it stands Q times taller than the floor.

2 · the ring: how long

What one strike leaves behind, drawn from the same two numbers. The red line is where the ring has fallen 60 dB: about 2.2 × Q ÷ f seconds. While it plays, it strikes again each time the ring dies.

3 · the lag: how far behind

In sine mode: the push in amber, the swing in green, measured live. Below f they move together; at f the swing is a quarter cycle behind; above f it moves against the push.
