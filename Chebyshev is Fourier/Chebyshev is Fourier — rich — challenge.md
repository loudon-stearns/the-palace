## The offers

1. **The full-scale condition — confirmed, with prior art to credit.** Tₙ(a·x) has degree n and parity n, so its Chebyshev series holds only T_k with k ≤ n of n's parity, and its top coefficient is aⁿ (checked n = 1 to 8 at a = 0.7). T₃(0.5x) = −1.125·T₁ + 0.125·T₃, exact. Saw: miss 0.0664 at 0.99 (−23.6 dB), 0.179 at 0.97, 0.416 at 0.90, with h4 to h6 at −0.002, 0.006, −0.012 against 0.25, 0.2, 0.167. Above 1 with held ends: −17.5, −16.6, −17.8 dB above harmonic 8 at 1.05, 1.10, 1.25. All as the maker says. Two notes. For even n the spill includes harmonic 0, a steady offset. And the below-1 rule is already worked out on the paused Dissolutions branch (its sequence's step 6, T₃ at reach 0.6), so the offer should say it found the same thing, not claim it first. The proposed wording stands.
2. **"(Runge's phenomenon)" misnames Taylor's failure — confirmed.** 1/(1 + 25x²) has poles at ±i/5, so its Taylor series at 0 adds up only for |x| < 1/5. Worst gap inside |x| ≤ 0.15: 3.6e-2 at n = 9, 6.4e-5 at n = 31; at x = 0.25: 3.63 and 492.6. Evenly spaced interpolation: 1.045 at n = 9, 59.82 at n = 21; at the shadow points, 0.171 and 0.0153. The page itself files Runge's phenomenon among the Taylor survey under Lost Branches (`:80`), so the slip is consistent, not a one-off; the proposed wording ("the related failure of fitting through evenly spaced points") is right, since both failures come from the same complex poles.
3. **"The group zeroes out the rest" is partial — confirmed in the page's basis; the reach claim holds, tighten its words.** Group averaging gives pmm 49 free, 0 zeroed; cmm 24 zeroed, 0 tied, 25 free; p4m 0 zeroed, 21 pairs tied, 28 free; p4g 27 zeroed, 9 pairs tied (3 with a sign), 13 free; p4 about (0, 0) gives the same 28 as p4m. The reach: any cos×cos surface is fixed by both axis flips, so its group holds two perpendicular mirrors, which leaves pmm, cmm, p4m, p4g and p6m. p6m needs a hexagonal lattice, and a square lattice can't sit inside one (the Gram determinants would need c² = 3k²s² in whole numbers), so the four stand. Two tightenings for the wording: the zero-or-tie split is a fact about the cos×cos basis, not the group (in a symmetrised basis p4m zeroes 21); and of "the other thirteen", eight (p1, p2, pm, pg, cm, pmg, pgg, p4) need sine terms on a square torus, while the five three- and six-fold groups need a lattice no square torus has, sines or not.

## The face as a whole

It makes the idea felt. The page is an identity, and identities are easy to state and hard to feel; these walks make you hear the substitution x = cos θ three times over, as a waveshaper, as nodes and as a torus, and each walk ends somewhere the text hadn't fully said: the drive the sentence fixes, the radius Taylor never escapes, the ties a group makes instead of zeros. Three offers from three tests is the face doing the job, not getting busy. The one thing wrong is its edge with Dissolutions. A way up to the circle's shadow is owed, claimed and paused on Loudon's word, and this face built a second one beside it. Remade to the part only it has, the real lookup table past its ends, the drive stands on its own, and the face is better for leaving the proof to its own sequence.
