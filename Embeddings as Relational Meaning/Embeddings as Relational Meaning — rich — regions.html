Are categories regions, and what is in them?

The page’s three categories, twelve words each, fixed before anything was measured. Animals: dog, cat, horse, cow, sheep, lion, tiger, wolf, rabbit, mouse, eagle, whale. Emotions: joy, anger, fear, sadness, grief, love, pride, shame, envy, disgust, surprise, hope. Musical intervals: unison, semitone, tone, third, fourth, tritone, fifth, sixth, seventh, octave, ninth, interval.

1 · One axis, two words

Take two words and the line between them. Every other word gets one number: how much nearer it points to one end than the other. One direction already sorts the thirty-six.

s(w) = cos(w, B) − cos(w, A)
Left is nearer the first word, right is nearer the second. Amber are animals, rose emotions, teal intervals.

2 · Three regions

Now all 100 directions at once, flattened to the two that spread these words most (principal components, computed here). The diamonds are each category’s centre, the average of its twelve. Labels that would collide are moved aside, with a faint line back to their dot.

3 · Between regions

Walk a straight line from one word to another and watch which centre is nearest. The bars can only slide smoothly, since any straight path in a space like this does that. So the smoothness proves nothing, and the piece says so. The column worth reading is the real words that live along the way.

Two questions. Read both rules before you run. Question one’s rule was fixed before its first trial. Question two’s is its third version, and it says so: the first used probe words that are themselves interval names (a twelfth is one), and the second could not fail, since any word has to land with one of three centres.
