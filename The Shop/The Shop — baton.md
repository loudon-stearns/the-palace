# Baton: The Shop — shop specialist discovery

## Move

Find out what would make the Shop's full capabilities visible and available at the moment anything is made, in or with the palace, by looking at enough of the situations where making happens. Then bring Loudon a design that covers them, before anything foundational moves.

## Why this move matters

Loudon, 2026-11-14: "create a handoff 'shop specialist discovery' with the broad mandate to make sure that whenever anything is being made in or with the palace the full capabilities of the shop are easily visible and available. give our current situation as a single example, but be clear that there are other situations to consider. We have identified a clear gap in the palace/shop, but have not considered enough situations to come up with a full zoomed out solution."

**The goal, set at pickup.** Loudon, 2026-11-14: "We are building a shop mechanism so that the shop GETS USED! Our goal should be the constant use and development of the shop, these are our tools and though it may be inefficient to use these tools initially, we design them to improve themselves, so with more use they will get very good. We need to make it our easy default to know what the shop [is], how to use it, and encourage all making in the palace to leverage the shop, even when it seems inefficient because we have a goal of improving the palace and improving the shop that is equal to the work at hand."

So the design is judged by use, not by visibility: the Shop is used constantly, and use is what improves it. The bias is toward self-improvement, not a duty on every job. If a job worked really well, saying so is all that is owed; a rule that every job must leave a lesson would fill the ledgers with garbage.

The gap is real and it's showing up as work. Makers keep building sound, images and motion by hand while a Specialist that does it better sits unused. One morning turned up one example of this. It isn't the whole problem, and it isn't the design.

## The first example, and the candidate ideas

The walk is done and nothing is designed or built. What follows is the first example and the candidate ideas, carried so they aren't re-derived. None of it is decided.

**The one example: the Daily Page of 2026-11-14, Stable Audio Open.** Stable Audio 3 renders 20 s of sound locally in about a second. Loudon was impressed by the quality and said many small interactions should have used it. Record: `Shop/Stable Audio Open/Stable Audio Open — scroll.md` (trail entry `daily-2026-11-14-arc-or-texture`), the morning's commit `ops(The Daily Page): 2026-11-14 · Stable Audio Open`, and Loudon's earlier direction at `Light Enrichment.md:97-104`.

Two making sessions were asked to reflect on why, without doing any work. The woken Stable Audio Open page answered too.
- **Light enrichment proposals.** 70 light pieces, 16 of them sounds, none made through the Shop. The sound was numpy and scipy, the images SVG written from Python and PIL. That session never opened `The Shop.md` in three days.
- **Playful interface design enrichment plan.** A hook pilot and 6 rich-face pieces. The briefs said "synthesized with Web Audio". ComfyUI was named, but the makers drew SVG.

The causes they gave, all candidates:
1. The Shop is never in front of a maker when the tool is chosen. [[CHILD]] points at the design systems only. The Concierge's gatherer index lists Shop pages as subjects, not as means.
2. The wall is not knowing a Specialist's status, its command, how fast it is, or when it last ran.
3. Makers can't hear. So they trusted only what they could measure, and shipping generated mood material felt dishonest.
4. The default is one file of your own; a batch of thirty rewards one uniform script, and going through the Maker feels like handing the work away.
5. `_tools/` isn't in worktrees.

Pieces they named as worse for it: the Pauline Oliveros room; the Dub Lineage riddim; Rick Rubin's mix and "voice" ([[Kokoro]]); Cooking ↔ Mixing; Kick Drum ↔ Foundation; SUBSTRATE's bed and cues; the Dichotomy of Control room tone; the mycelial reverb's feeds; the p5.js page's piece, faked in numpy with the wrong recipe. Counts belonged in Observable Plot or D3, loops in Manim or p5, and LaMa and Depth Anything could have demonstrated themselves. They agreed that measurements you listen to (Leibniz's clocks, Endosymbiosis, Audition Gate) should stay hand-built.

**Candidate ideas floated, none settled:**
- **Roster columns.** Call me for / not yet shown to hold / last ran (from the tuning ledger) / one command by the main checkout's path. These would be read from a fixed first line on each Specialist page, with no new frontmatter.
- **A roster command.** `Shop/Maker/build-roster.mjs --print <medium>`, which agents run instead of reading `The Shop.md` (about 2,000 words; the roster is its sixth section). Or move the roster to the top of the page.
- **A CHILD line.** Widen "before anything visual" to "show, play or animate" and name the Shop. This is foundational, so only on Loudon's yes, and only once the roster can answer.
- **Honest captions.** The rich-face README and [[Light Enrichment]] would say generated material is welcome with an honest caption ("sets a mood, proves nothing"), with Loudon as the audition.
- **Per-piece checks.** A one-line tool note on each piece, and a challenger question: "was a Specialist passed over?"
- **Self-demonstration.** A piece on each Shop page, made with that page's own tool.
- **The hand as a Specialist.** Loudon, 2026-11-14: "I am curious if 'hand built' is really just another needed specialist, like a general purpose sound creator or general purpose svg creator, because even when you are building by hand you can get better at that, right, a ledger could still be useful, and there are some specifics about how we make things here that might help focus your hand built." Then nothing is made outside the Shop and the fallback keeps a ledger. Bears on `Shop/Maker.md:116`, which rejected raw Web Audio as a Specialist, and on where the p5 alpha mistake was recorded (Light Enrichment's Context, where no later sound-maker looks). Open: one page per material (sound from code, SVG from code) or one page for the hand.
- **Brainstorm cards.** The Shop as cards in brainstorms with Loudon.

## What the walk found

The eleven situations were walked on 2026-11-14 by read-only readers. Their reports, with file and line, are in [[The Shop — sketch — discovery walk 2026-11-14]]. Read as a whole:

**The Shop is missed in four different ways.**
1. **Silence.** The chooser's reading names no Shop: the stewards' prompts (`steward.md:150` says "Default to making" and names no tool), the Concierge's prompts, [[Search Before You Build]], `Projects/Loudon Live.md`, the Palace Guide, the app. Loudon has no trigger phrase for making.
2. **A spec that routes around it.** Light Enrichment step 4 is "Make it with a script that stays" (`Light Enrichment.md:66`). The Daily Page prep may build by hand alone, but "a Shop making waits for his yes" (`_ops/The Daily Page.md:100`). The Technical Diagram Standard sends figures straight to TikZ. The VST Template says "Sketch in numpy". The Concierge's gatherer is told to skip `_tools/`.
3. **The tool is used and the Specialist is bypassed.** Nine TikZ figures for DSP in Looping Dimensions (2026-11-09) while the LaTeX Specialist is a stub last run in June. ComfyUI plates for the Loudon Live looks (2026-10-25) with no line in ComfyUI's ledger; the recipes sit in the Design System's ledger, items 12–14. Matplotlib called directly in the Haptic Handwheel's feel-lab.
4. **Named and never run.** Blood Compressor and Retrospective Delay carry spec sheets that name a Specialist per row and say "not a dispatch order". The Loudon Live Design System's per-Specialist table is "held until first invocation". Four stubs have never had a real brief.

**Where it works, the Shop is in the path by construction.** The [[Hero and Avatar Maker]]'s Regen lane is a button that writes its own run line; its ledger holds 28 lessons. BLUELINE's steward manifest lists Shop pages in its neighborhood, and it uses Blender through [[Figure Rig]]. Wavetable Scanner lifted its gotchas into Web Audio Worklet and Three.js the same day. None of these rests on a maker remembering a sentence.

**Lessons land where the work was, not where the tool is.** Every situation's lessons went to its own ledger. A steward may not edit a Shop page at all (`shared.md:321-356`). So even real use has not been improving the Shop.

**The front door is not ready for traffic.** Fourteen of 32 Specialists have no route in the Maker's Selection Heuristics, Stable Audio Open among them: "I need a sound effect or a music bed" has no answer there. Six are missing from `host-capability.json`. The Maker's ledger has no run; its last Recipe is 2026-06-19. Most Specialist pages give no command a maker could paste.

**Availability is narrower than "the Mac has everything".** About ten Specialists run anywhere (the browser ones, Matplotlib, Mermaid, ffmpeg, Whisper). The rest need `_tools/`, a venv or a pod, and the app's own worktrees link none of them. Stable Audio's first render of a session took 334 s, then about 3 s.

## Calibrations from this session

- Loudon, 2026-11-14: "I don't want to settle this question in this session." The morning stopped at the example on purpose. Treat the candidate ideas as found, not chosen.
- Loudon, 2026-11-14, at pickup: improving the Shop is a goal "equal to the work at hand". Use the Shop "even when it seems inefficient". Efficiency is not a reason to go around a Specialist; a design that leaves the hand-built route as the easy one has missed.
- Loudon, 2026-11-14: "When we think that the shop will be worse we make through the shop and make handbuilt and compare and contrast and use that info help us in future decisions." A doubt about a Specialist is settled by making both and comparing, and the comparison is kept. The Maker's Comparison Mode (`Shop/Maker.md:211`) is the nearest existing form; it compares Specialist against Specialist, not Specialist against the hand.
- Loudon, 2026-11-14, on "every job owes": "'every' establishes a rule and encourages garbage. we want to bias ourselves toward self improvement, but if it worked really well, that is all that needs to be said."
- The example is one case. Don't fit the design to Stable Audio Open or to Light Enrichment.
- A CHILD edit is foundational: only on his yes, and only after the roster can actually answer what a maker would ask it.
- What a Specialist can't do is part of being visible. The morning's probe found small-sfx drops an arc it is told about, and small-music fades every clip. See the gotchas on [[Stable Audio Open]].

## On pickup (fixed — the catcher's checklist; do not rewrite per session)
*Identical in every baton. It rides along because the catching Claude loads the
baton and the entry, not this ceremony — so the catcher's obligations live where
the catcher will see them. Omit nothing here.*
A pickup has two beats: **claim** it when you catch it, **close** it when the move lands. The card stays visible in between — a claim that ages with no close is how a dropped baton (a "fumble") surfaces instead of vanishing. (A parent-entry baton that was never announced on the board has no card; skip the board posts — just remove the pointer and delete the file at close, step 8.)

**Catch it — claim:**
1. State the move back in one sentence. If you can't, the baton wasn't caught — stop and ask Loudon.
2. Check it may already be done before you commit to it. The baton is a snapshot from when it was written; the project may have moved past it. Re-read the parent entry and `git log` it since the baton's `born` date, and confirm the "Current state" the baton quotes still matches the file. For a board-announced baton, `node _ops/stigmergy/pickup-handoff.mjs <id>` prints exactly this reconciliation view — every commit that touched the entry since the baton posted — and then claims the card, so run it and read the list *before* you continue. If the move is already done, superseded, or no longer wanted, STOP — do not claim it; surface to Loudon, and if it plainly landed already, close it as a reconciler (step 7). A stale baton followed silently produces drift. (The auto-staleness heuristic is off by design — the freshness call is yours.)
3. If this baton or its board line is still uncommitted, commit them first. That commit is the git archive step 8 relies on.
4. Claim it. For a board-announced baton (it shows in `list-handoffs`), the `pickup-handoff.mjs` from step 2 has already posted the claim (`handoff_picked_up`, `lifecycle: claim`) — the card moves to **CLAIMED (in flight)**; it does *not* leave the board. Leave the "Active Baton" pointer and the baton file in place for now — they come out at close, so a fumble mid-move never erases the work.
5. If the baton names a receiving-surface capability delta or a worktree coordinate, confirm it holds before relying on it — for a worktree, check `git worktree list` and recreate it (`node _ops/worktree/new-worktree.mjs --name <branch> --profile <p>`) if it is gone. A build that was supposed to run here but can't is a finding to report, not a failure to hide.
6. Act on the move, holding the calibrations above.

**Close it — when the move lands:**
7. Post the close. `node _ops/stigmergy/close-handoff.mjs <id | entry> --commit <hash>` retires the card — an explicit close is the *only* thing that clears it (done is never inferred). Cite the commit that landed the move: it makes the close a checkable claim, not a self-report. **Complete, or re-baton the rest:** if you finished the whole move, close plain; if you did only part, `--partial --remainder "<what's left>"` posts the leftover as a fresh `handoff_ready` so it reappears as open work. Never let "in the spirit of the original" quietly drop scope — a gap becomes a new baton, not silence.
8. Delete the baton file (git is its archive) and remove the "Active Baton" section from the parent entry. Steward batons are the exception — updated in place, never deleted or closed, until their page ends and the steward leaves stewardship.
