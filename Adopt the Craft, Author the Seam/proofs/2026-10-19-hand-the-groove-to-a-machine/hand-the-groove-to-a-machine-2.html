Hand the Groove to a Machine

The chart says swing, laid back. A drummer reads that loosely and plays it with feel. A machine reads it literally. Between them is the seam, and at the far edges of the seam the groove stops being a groove. Find the voice, find the edge, then train your ear on it.

The chart
Medium swing · laid back · 96 bpm · | Dm7 | G7 | · ride, hat on 2 and 4, feathered kick, walking bass

A mystery groove
t(skip) = t(beat) + s × T
(when the skip note lands) = (when its beat starts) + (swing ratio) × (length of one beat)
t(hit) = t(grid) + L
(when the drum is actually struck) = (when the grid says) + (lay-back)

50% swing is straight eighths (the dashed line in each beat), 67% is a true triplet (the dotted line), and 75% is a dotted-eighth-and-sixteenth. Negative lay-back means the kit is pushing ahead of the bass. The bass always stays on the grid.

Why a game

Swing is hard to hear and easy to feel, which is the craft speaking. Match a hidden groove with nothing but your ear and the two numbers the drummer never needed, and you learn to hear where the skip note lands and where the kit sits against the bass. The game is the seam turned into ear training.

One record, three jobs

Whatever you set in the seam is the whole of what crossed from the drummer’s taste to the machine. The page says the right seam is the one where the same few words do three jobs at once. Here they are.

In both rows the machine isn’t simply a strict reader. It is a reader with defaults instead of taste. The new work at the seam is writing down what the human used to supply from taste, and then deciding how far past the norm to push it.

The question the rough is asking

The “rules” of swing are a history of what drummers did. The seam is where that history has to become numbers, and it is also where an author chooses how far to depart from it. Too little and it’s the machine. Too far and the drums come loose from the bass. Is that zone, between the norm and the breaking point, what the page means by author?

One honest note: the “drummer” here is itself a model, swing 64% and 22 ms behind, with jitter and ghosts. The real test is a real record.
