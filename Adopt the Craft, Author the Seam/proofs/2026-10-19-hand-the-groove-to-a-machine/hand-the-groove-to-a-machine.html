Hand the Groove to a Machine

The chart says swing, laid back. A drummer reads that loosely and plays it with feel. Give the same chart to a machine and listen to what comes back. Then write down the two numbers the drummer never needed.

The chart
Medium swing · laid back · 96 bpm · | Dm7 | G7 | · ride, hat on 2 and 4, feathered kick, walking bass

50% 0 ms
t(skip) = t(beat) + s × T

t(hit) = t(grid) + L
(when the drum is actually struck) = (when the grid says) + (lay-back)

A swing ratio of 50% is straight eighths (the dashed line in each beat). 67% is a true triplet (the dotted line). The bass stays on the grid, so you hear the drums sit behind it.

One record, three jobs

Whatever you set in the seam is the whole of what crossed from the drummer’s taste to the machine. The page says the right seam is the one where the same few words do three jobs at once. Here they are.

The question the rough is asking

Get the seam close to the drummer with two numbers and the page is right that the new work is small. Whatever you still can’t reach (the ghost notes, the hit that leans into the turnaround) is one of two things. Either it’s the third seam the page wonders about, or it’s the honest limit of “adopt the craft”, the part of the craft that won’t come off the shelf.

One honest note: the “drummer” here is itself a model, swing 64% and 22 ms behind, with jitter and ghosts. The real test is a real record, or Loudon playing against the machine.
