Where it stops being a groove

Loudon, in the Daily Page sit of 2026-10-19: a craft's norms are how art communicates clearly. The voice lives in breaking them. Break too far and the communication fails; break too little and it isn't art. Maybe authorship is the zone between the norm and the breaking point. The page doesn't say this, and this piece doesn't settle it. It lets you play the question.

The norm

Medium swing as the craft has it: the skip note near a triplet, 64% of the beat; the ride and hat 22 ms behind the bass; two and four leaning. These are the drummer's numbers from the page's other pieces, played exactly. The craft speaking clearly.

Push the swing

One number, pushed. Below 64% it heads back toward the machine's straight eighths. Above, it gets harder, then starts to limp. Somewhere the skip note stops sounding like a pickup into the next beat and starts sounding like a late hit. Listen for where that happens for you.

Drag the kit back

The other number, pushed. The ride and hat slide behind the bass and the feathered kick, from laid back to way back, until they come loose and sound like a second band a beat behind.

Find your edge

No right answer here. What's measured is whether you have a breaking point, and how blurred it is. Fourteen takes: seven swing amounts, 64, 68, 72, 76, 80, 84 and 88%, each played twice, in a shuffled order. The lay-back stays at 22 ms and two and four lean. For each, say whether it is still a groove or has broken.

What it finds. Only one end of the zone Loudon named: the breaking point. The other end, the norm, is fixed here at 64%, not measured. So this is not a test of his reading; it asks whether there is a breaking point to stand near at all.

The rule, fixed now. We find the cut that best splits your "still a groove" answers below it from your "broke" answers above it, and count the answers on the wrong side. 0 or 1: a sharp edge, at that cut. 2 or 3: a blurred edge, the amounts where you went both ways. 4 or more: fog, your answers don't sort by the number. No cut inside the range does best: no edge in this range.

Without listening. A coin flipper finds a sharp edge of the time, a blurred edge , fog and no edge . An answerer who doesn't listen but leans one way, saying "it broke" one time in ten or two in ten, finds a sharp edge and a blurred edge , and mostly no edge. So a sharp edge is hard to get without listening; a blurred one isn't.

Nothing else gives it away. Every take has the same hits, the same weights, the same length and the same loudness; only the swing moves. The grid stays dark.

You can play a take again before you answer.
