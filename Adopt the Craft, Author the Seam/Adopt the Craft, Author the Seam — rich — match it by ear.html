Match it by ear

The section's sign that you've found the right seam: one vocabulary serves the human read, the machine and the handoff between them. BLUELINE's vocabulary is staging. In music it is two numbers. The machine and the handoff read them by construction. Whether a person can read them back off the sound is the part that can be tested.

One record, three jobs

Set the groove. The machine's instructions and the file any other tool reads carry the same two numbers and a switch. The chart doesn't: a real chart says medium swing, laid back, never 64%. So in music the human's vocabulary is words and the machine's is numbers, and the one vocabulary the section prizes has to be built here; it isn't already there. Nothing crosses from the drummer's taste to the machine except what is in the last two boxes.

A fourth reader: hear one number

Before an ear can read a number back, it has to know what the number sounds like. Here is swing alone, at three settings, with the kit on the grid and nothing leaning. Listen to the little pickup on the ride into 2 and into 4.

Hear the other number

Now lay-back alone, swing held at 64%. The bass and the feathered kick stay on the grid; the ride and hat move behind them. Listen to where the hat on 2 and 4 sits against the bass note.

Practise: rough two's game

A hidden groove plays, its ride and hat hidden from the grid. Move your slider until yours sounds like it, then check. This is the game from the Daily Page rough, unscored: play as long as you like.

The test: does the ear read the record?

Two rounds of six. Round one, swing: each hidden groove's swing is a whole number from 50 to 72%, drawn at random; the kit is on the grid. Your slider starts at 61 every trial. Within 3 counts as locked in. Round two, lay-back: swing held at 64% in both; the hidden lay-back is a whole number from −10 to 40 ms; your slider starts at 15. Within 8 counts.

Chance. A reader who never listens does best by leaving the slider where it starts, and locks in 7 times in 23 in round one (30%) and 17 times in 51 in round two (33%). Locking in 5 or 6 of 6 that way happens of the time in round one and in round two.

The rule, fixed now: 5 or 6 of 6 in a round means your ear reads that number off the sound: the vocabulary does the human's job too. 4 or fewer, this didn't show it.

Nothing else gives it away. The hidden groove and yours have the same hits, the same weights, the same length and the same loudness; only the one number moves. Its ride and hat stay off the grid until you lock in. Each answer is shown after you lock it in, as in the game; the score waits for the end.
