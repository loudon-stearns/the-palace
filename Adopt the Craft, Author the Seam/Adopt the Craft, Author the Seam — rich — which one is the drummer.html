Which one is the drummer?

The page says its move works in any craft: the seam is where a reader who interpreted is replaced by one who needs it spelled out. Here is the craft it names and never works, music. A chart goes to a drummer, then to a machine; you author the seam; then you find out, blind, whether what you authored carries the groove.

The chart, read by a drummer

Medium swing · laid back · 96 bpm · | Dm7 | G7 | · ride, hat on 2 and 4, feathered kick, walking bass

This is the craft taken off the shelf whole: a lead sheet, a ride pattern, a walking bass, and two words of feel. A drummer reads swing, laid back loosely and supplies the rest from taste. The skip note on the ride lands late, near a triplet. The ride and hat sit a hair behind the bass. Two and four lean. No hit lands exactly where the last one did.

Coral dots are the drummer's hits; grey are the bass and the feathered kick, which stay on the grid. This drummer is a model, not a person: swing 64%, the ride and hat 22 ms behind, every hit wandering up to 7 ms and 15% in weight, as rough two had it. It plays only what the chart asks for, with no ghost notes, so what you hear is feel and not extra notes.

The same chart, read by a machine

Hand the same chart to a sequencer. Nothing in it reads swing or laid back, so it falls back on its default: straight eighths, dead on the grid, every hit the same weight. Nothing is wrong with what it plays. It just isn't the tune.

Amber dots are the machine's. Watch the skip note: the machine's sits on the dashed line, half the beat; the drummer's sits near the dotted one, two thirds. This is the seam. A reader with taste was replaced by a reader with defaults, and everything the drummer supplied is now missing.

One number: where the skip note lands

The first thing to author is the thing the drummer never had to be told. Swing is one number: how far into the beat the skip note falls. 50% is straight, 67% a true triplet, 75% a dotted eighth and sixteenth.

t(skip) = t(beat) + s × T
(when the skip note lands) = (when its beat starts) + (the swing ratio) × (the length of one beat, 0.625 s at 96 bpm)

Two numbers and a switch: the seam, authored

The second number is where the ride and hat sit against the bass, in milliseconds behind the grid. The switch is whether two and four lean. That is the whole record: the drummer's taste written down as two numbers and a switch. Set them yourself, or take the drummer's.

t(hit) = t(grid) + L
(when the ride or hat is struck) = (when the grid says) + (the lay-back)

With the drummer's numbers taken, the amber dots sit where the coral ones average. What the record has no field for is the wander: no two of the drummer's bars are alike, and the machine's are.

The test: which one is the drummer?

Sixteen trials. Each plays two takes of the same two bars, one after the other. One of them is the drummer. Say which.

Two kinds, shuffled together. In eight, the other take is the machine reading the chart as written: straight and on the grid. In the other eight, it is the machine reading the drummer's own seam: 64%, 22 ms back, leaning on 2 and 4, with nothing wandering. You aren't told which kind a trial is until the end.

Chance is 4 of 8 in each kind. Guessing gets 7 or more of 8 in 9 runs out of 256, 3.5%.

The rule, fixed now: in either kind, 7 or 8 of 8 means you can tell the drummer from that machine. 6 or fewer, the test didn't show that you can.

What the page predicts. The machine as written should be easy, 7 or more: the swap of readers costs something you can hear. If the authored seam is the only new work, the second kind should be hard, near 4. Near 4 on the first kind would fail the page: the test wouldn't have shown that the swap costs anything you can hear. 7 or more on the second means the seam is wider than two numbers and a switch, and what's left is the wander, the part the record has no field for.

Nothing else tells them apart. Every take's drums are set to the same loudness (the same RMS over the take), and the bass is the same in both. Each pair has the same length. The drummer comes first in four trials of each kind and second in the other four, shuffled. The grid stays dark. Every trial draws a fresh drummer.
