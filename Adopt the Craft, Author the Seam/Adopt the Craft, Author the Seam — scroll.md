### Where this stands

Dealt by [[The Daily Page]] on 2026-10-19 and kept for now. The morning tested the page's claim outside BLUELINE for the first time, in music, the craft the page names but never works through. Loudon called it great progress and enough for one morning, and said we will come back to it.

**Open, not decided:** Loudon's reading in the sit may supply the page's missing half. A craft's norms are how art communicates clearly: risers, the ii–V–I, the plagal cadence, comic frames. The artist's voice lives in breaking those norms, at the craft's edges and where crafts join. Break too far and the communication fails; break too little and it isn't art. Maybe authorship is that zone between the norm and the breaking point. Harmony's "rules" are historical studies of what people did, so you read the rules on purpose to find where authorship can happen. The page currently reads the seam as the place where a loose human reader is replaced by an exact machine reader. His reading is a second kind of seam: the edge of the norm, where the author works. Whether this becomes a `tune` of the page is his to say, later.

### 2026-10-19 — Hand the Groove, Loosened: the edges of the norm, and a match-by-ear game

The second rough, rebuilt from Loudon's notes on the first. The grid now updates the moment a parameter changes, so you can see the groove move while you hear it. ### 2026-10-19 — Hand the Groove to a Machine: the seam in sound

The morning's first rough, and the page's first test outside BLUELINE. A lead sheet says *swing, laid back*. A drummer reads that loosely and plays it with feel. Give the same chart to a machine and it comes back straight and on the grid, because nothing in the machine reads "swing". That is the centred portrait from [[Blocked, Not Prompted]], in sound. Then you author the seam with two numbers the drummer never needed: the swing ratio and how far the backbeat lays back. Those two numbers are also the page's "one vocabulary, three jobs": they are the chart's schema, the machine's conditioning and a format any tool can read. A second row shows the same swap at BLUELINE's board-to-render seam, so the music isn't the only case. The outside read the piece grew from, along with the page's own answers when it was woken, sits beside it as a workshop note.
