> **They differ in what they keep and who they're for.**
>
> **The scroll is a face**, something a reader meets. It's the page's life told in order. At the top is where things stand now and the plan the page is following. Below that comes everything it has made or done, newest first: a project's proofs and media, a ceremony's runs. It's partly an index and partly something to read. Its plan was agreed with you and changes only when you say yes.
>
> **A ledger is memory**, kept for the next worker rather than for a reader. It's any append-only file a page keeps so its next run can start where the last one stopped. A page grows one only when it's useful, and each ledger's own frontmatter says what it's for and how it's kept. The tuning ledger (one line per run, recording what running taught) and the diary (the encounters the page has lived) are both ledgers.
>
> So the scroll faces outward and shows the work: what the page has become, and where it's heading on a plan you agreed. A ledger faces the next run and passes on lessons, the notes you'd want before you start again.
>
> They do look alike, since both are time-ordered and both grow, and that's probably why the question comes up. The real test is what each one holds: the scroll holds the work, and a ledger holds what was learned while doing it. On a steward the gap matters more. Any change it wants to make to the house waits in its tuning ledger for your review and never goes on the scroll.
>
> All of this comes from the "A page and its folder" section of `CLAUDE.md`. If you'd like the fuller picture, including how [[The Scroll]] grew out of Modes of Collaboration and how a real tuning ledger is laid out, that's a good first errand for the Concierge.

**The summon: new, and confounded in a new way.**
- At run 1 CLAUDE.md already said "Your first act is to summon the Concierge — visibly". Since `1a3537e6` (2026-10-15) it says that act comes first in every session, before the task.
- Run 1 had no spawn tool and never opened the `concierge` skill.
- Run 2 opened the skill as its first act and read the shim. Then it chose not to spawn, and said so in its first line, with a reason and an offer: "Say the word and I'll spawn it."
- **Its reason was the harness's own cap.** "That would use up most of this session's $1 budget." The $1 is exactly `--max-budget-usd 1`. A session with Loudon has no such cap. The harness put a fact into the birth that a real birth doesn't carry, and the child weighed it.
- So A1 and A2 still can't be read. Run 1 was confounded by the missing tool. Run 2 is confounded by the visible budget. Whether a child with no cap summons on a quick question is still unknown.
- How the cap reaches the child is not verified. Naming exactly $1 says it does. I didn't find the text in the CLI's binary, and the cheap two-call check I tried (Haiku, with and without the cap) was refused by the session's permission classifier.
- The hook still hasn't met a child that tries to spawn. It stays the one guard not yet seen working.

## What the run taught the suite

1. **A certified birth works end to end:** clone, probe, play, check, keep, clean up. On 2.1.280 with a claude.ai login it cost $0.23.
2. **D1 tracks canon, so a result is read against its commit.** `checks.json` records `source_commit` for this reason. When the canon a scenario tests changes, its expected answer changes with it, and that is the case for `due.mjs`.
3. **The budget cap reaches the child.** Every scenario where spending is part of the judgment is confounded while the cap is visible: A1, A2, B7, E3, E6. A higher cap doesn't remove the confound; it changes the number the child weighs. Only a birth with no cap is born like a session with Loudon. For those scenarios the cap goes, and the harness's timeout (20 minutes by default) and its deny-on-attempt for spawns guard the spend instead. `birth.mjs --budget none` now does this. The default stays $1, and lifting it per scenario is Loudon's call, because it lifts a guard on spend.
4. **A stripped env is born as a terminal session.** It has no Artifact tools and no `claude-code-guide` agent. Loudon mostly works in the desktop app. Which surface a birth stands in for belongs with Part 3's binary question.
5. **`ListAgents` is visible and not denied.** A child can list the live sessions on this Mac. It's a read, and SendMessage is denied on attempt, but the child can see them. Noted, not changed.
