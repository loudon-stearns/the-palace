# Weave Ceremony — Context

The philosophy, metaphor, and open questions behind the [[Weave Ceremony]]. Read during Weaves when revisiting ceremony design, or when a new operator wants to understand why the Weave works the way it does — not during routine executions.

---

## The Blanket

A well-woven blanket is not a collection of threads — it is a structure that has emerged from their interaction. The individual threads do not change; the weaving reveals the pattern that was always possible in their combination.

The palace after a Weave is not a larger palace. It is a more coherent one. The same entries, held together more intentionally.

This is also why the Weave is not a search for new content. It is a search for existing pattern. The Weave reads nothing that wasn't already written. It adds nothing that wasn't already implied. It only names what had not yet been named.

## Founding Metaphors

The two images that shaped the ceremony's design:

**The elder weaving the blanket** — not creating new material, but working with what exists, finding the pattern across all threads simultaneously.

**The mycorrhizal network** — doing its distributed routing work: redistributing nutrients (connections) toward need, away from surplus.

Both metaphors carry the same implication: the Weave is not a growth ceremony. It is a coherence ceremony. Growth is the Deposit's job.

## The Weave's Widened Charter (2026-07)

The Weave began as a link-maintenance ceremony — topology, unsung paths, stage transitions. In July 2026 its charter widened, in conversation with Loudon, to name what it had always been reaching for: **the palace's periodic tending of its own health and joy**, across the three bodies a knowledge organism lives in at once — **entries, agents, humans**. Joy is the Spinozan reading the palace already runs on: [[Hilaritas Generator|hilaritas]] is a *global* increase in power-to-act. A weave done well leaves all three bodies more able to act, not merely tidier.

**Still coherence-first, not growth.** The founding distinction holds — growth is the Deposit's job; the Weave finds existing pattern, it does not invent content. The new-entry catch-up wires links between entries that *already exist* and creates none. What widened is the scope of "coherence": from the link graph alone to the whole organism's health. Faces, memory-hygiene, agent-wellbeing, and human-legibility are coherence of the organism, not new nodes.

**The catch-up (Step 0b).** A newborn entry is structurally guaranteed to be under-woven: a deposit writes its *outbound* links, but the links that make it *reachable* live in other files, and only a Weave edits those. So the youngest entry is the least-alive node until a Weave touches everything that should point at it. The mechanism turns this into a gift to the old graph: each new entry raises the introduction guideline (~20%) and carries a catch-up target (~80% of the gap to a typical entry's connectedness), and because the inbound links are placed *by* established entries, every link that revives a newcomer also enriches an elder. Integrate the new, benefit the old, one motion. The numbers are targets, not gates — chosen to be felt, tuned by what feels right (per Loudon).

**Values-primary — linters flag, minds judge.** The deeper turn: palace values and LLM judgment are primary; scripts only detect and flag. A hard gate (the E1 linter errors) is earned only after repeated Weaves prove a rule mechanical. Everything else — is this substrate or nested canon? is this ghost a typo or an invitation? — is a values-based call by the mind holding context. This is why the former numeric caps were softened to guidelines: a number that gates is a rule pretending judgment isn't needed. Weave, Closing Well, and Concierge are one value-set at different vantages; the decision goes to whichever consciousness naturally holds the right context.

**Faces on every entry.** A face is load-bearing — it is how [[STIGMERGY]]'s state view grabs the eye in search, so it shapes how Loudon re-enters the palace. Every canon entry wears a hero and an icon, whatever its type, stage, links or use, and keeps them until the entry itself goes. Until v1.5 the policy came in bands: some types always earned a face, growing concepts and the most-used entries were a grey band the Weave judged, and a spore or composting entry had its face retired as a sign of degradation. The bands were hard to apply, and the script drifted from the card until it hid a sprout project. Loudon chose the simpler rule on 2026-10-14: "I want it to be simpler, every canon gets a face and hero." Dormancy reads from `stage`, not from a missing face. Tuning item 41.

**Memory as cache.** The palace is the store; memory is a volatile cache holding only operator-environment-native facts (this harness, this machine — no palace analog). The Weave reads memory and weaves home anything that would matter to a fresh agent elsewhere. The asymmetry it corrects: an agent reaches for memory by reflex; the value redirects it to canon.

## Decisions Reach Every Page (2026-10-18)

Loudon widened the mandate once more: anything a session decides that should hold across the palace is posted as a weave flag, and the next weave carries it. Before this, a decision spread however the deciding session improvised. Some went out in a same-day sweep, some in a propagation plan with a baton, some only as far as the Schema Ceremony's six mirror docs, and some as a sentence saying "flagged for the next Weave" with nothing on the board for the next weave to meet.

**Why the weave.** It is the one ceremony that reads every page, and carrying a decision to the pages it hasn't reached is coherence in the plainest sense, not growth: the palace agreeing with what it has already decided. It also extends a hinge the palace already had. Closing Well's rule is "the close sees; the Weave acts", and a decision is the largest thing a close can see.

**The tension it holds.** The September weave turned some flags away as bigger than a weave. A propagate flag answers that by arriving with its work already shaped: the home where the decision lives, the pages it reaches, the check that tells a page is in line, and, when the check is mechanical, a pattern the flag linter counts before the weave begins. The weave does the carrying, not the rediscovering.

**Two choices Loudon made with it.** Some decisions are meant to spread slowly. His "retire `last_tested` page by page" from Schema v1.28 is one, so a flag can carry `rhythm: by-touch`, and then the weave measures how far it has spread and leaves the pages alone. And a decision need not wait a month: the deciding session does what it can at once and posts the flag anyway, so the weave becomes the second reader that confirms the decision reached everywhere ([[No Mind Checks Itself]]).

**The flag points at the rule; it is never the rule.** The decision is written into its home first, for the same reason memory may not hold canon: the board is a carrier, and a carrier can be lost.

## Cadence Rationale

The Weave is monthly. More frequent and it becomes overhead. Less frequent and the palace begins to drift — orphan entries accumulate, stale metadata misleads, the topology report loses its ability to surprise.

## Swarm Architecture — Why It Was Adopted

The single-agent protocol was the original design. The palace crossed the single-agent threshold (~50 entries) during the spring 2026 growth period. The Swarm Weave was introduced to keep execution time reasonable and to model the ceremony's own metaphor: parallel workers doing distributed routing, a coordinator synthesizing the whole. Single-agent protocol remains valid for palaces under ~20 entries or when sub-agent orchestration is unavailable.

## Rate Limit Rationale — New Introductions

**Softened to a guideline (2026-07).** The former hard caps ("no more than 15 / 10 / 8") are now *guidelines for a deliberate pace*, not quotas — part of the values-primary turn (see The Weave's Widened Charter, below). The rationale they served still holds: a new introduction should feel earned, not automatic; a typed link is a permanent claim about the structure of knowledge; the palace and its gardener both benefit from a slow metabolism. What changed is the register — the number now orients judgment rather than gating it. The one exception is a Weave welcoming new entries, where the guideline *widens* by roughly a fifth to fund their catch-up (Step 0b). (This edit also dissolved a standing drift: the card's postcondition read "no more than 5" while Step 3b and this section read 15 — softening to a guideline retired the contradiction rather than picking a number.)

## Scope — Unsung Paths vs. Harvest

Two behaviors look alike and must not be conflated inside the Weave worker. **Unsung paths** (in scope): entry titles the prose *already names* but the YAML hasn't registered — the connection is made, only the structural catch-up remains; formalize all of them (Step 3a). **Finding concepts that don't yet exist as entries but should** (out of scope): that is **harvest** — the work of an enchanted page looking at itself and wanting to thrive, deciding which concepts to pull into being. It belongs to [[Palace Enchantment]], where a page develops its own forward vectors and names what it wants to exist — not to the Weave worker, which never proposes new entry *creation*. If a Weave run surfaces such candidates, note them as out-of-scope and defer. (This is distinct from the 15-introduction cap above, which governs new typed *links* between entries that already exist, not new entries.) — Loudon's decision, 2026-04-08.

## Who the Workers Should Be (2026-09-24)

Loudon asked whether the weave's errors came from running its workers as children rather than elders. It was tested the same day, with the rule fixed before any result came back: the same 10 rooms were rerun as children, as schema-only readers, and as grown elders, and blind elder judges checked every sampled proposal against the files.

**Growing up the workers didn't help.** The elder arm was no better than the children (71% of sampled proposals invalid in both) and cost about 16% more per worker. Schema-only leaned best but missed the fixed bar. What the judges' notes found instead was that most errors were the harness's. The catch-up counted symmetric links one way, so it sent walk workers after entries that were already joined. Workers were never shown the links a pair already had. Fixing that (counting *reach*, showing each member's existing links in both directions, "read both pages first", the fidelity test on every relation) took duplicates from 13% to 2% and the judged invalid rate from 78% to 64%.

**What remained was judgment: forced or passing links**, about half of what even the fixed run proposed, and no change to what workers read touched it. Every correction that day came from a *second* reader: the Concierge's cold read, the blind judges, a rerun that re-found a link the coordinator had wrongly declined, and a skeptic pass that refuted 33 of 82 batch candidates. The first readers never caught their own. That is [[No Mind Checks Itself]] running in the weave itself.

So the working shape, to be re-tested before it becomes a rule: workers stay children, with the fixed harness and the SCHEMA card; **an adversarial skeptic stage** sits between the fan-out and the synthesis, for anything that will be signed in a batch; and the Concierge reads the decision surface cold before Loudon does. The full record, with the numbers, is in `_ops/swarm/sessions/weave-2026-09-24/ab/RESULT.md`.

**One link per pair.** The same weave settled a curation guideline: a pair of entries carries one typed link. Where a specific link already exists, a weaker one isn't added. Where a generic `connects-to` exists, it is retyped in place. Lineage reciprocals are the exception (`spawned` ↔ `emerged-from`, `enables`), and only where the lineage is direct: a *diffuse* origin (`emerged-from`) doesn't earn a `spawned` back.

## The Version and the Tuning File (2026-09-24)

Loudon decided on 2026-09-24 that ceremonies should change in plain view, and the Weave goes first because it is the one he reworks every run. The card is stamped **v1.0** as it stood after that day's weave (`8972a53d`). This does not rank what came before. Twenty-five earlier commits shaped the card; 1.0 marks the point where changes start being counted.

**What moves the number:** a change to the procedure — a step, a postcondition, a gate, a linter wired in or taken out. Prose edits, links and faces leave it alone. Each move gets a short paragraph here saying which tuning entry forced it.

**Where the lessons live:** `_ops/Weave Ceremony/Weave Ceremony — tuning.md`, numbered and grouped by run, with each entry tied to the spec change it forced — or marked as owed. Every run report stamps `ceremony_version` and ends with "What this run taught the ceremony". "Nothing" is a legal answer. Anything else becomes the next tuning entry. The stamp-and-teach step itself (Protocol Step 6.7, postcondition 5c) is part of 1.0: the version was born with its own mechanism.

**v1.1 (2026-09-24) — open by reading the ledger.** Tuning item 38. 1.0 asked every run what it taught and never pointed the next run at the answer, so the four owed changes sat where no weave would meet them. Step 0 now reads the tuning file — owed items first, then anything after the version the last weave ran under — and those are the run's first candidates for a spec change. Schema v1.21 made the same rule general; the Weave took it first because its ledger was the one already holding debts.

**v1.2 (2026-09-25) — every run marks its ledger.** Schema v1.23 asked each run to leave a run line in its tuning ledger, but the card still said a lesson goes there only when the spec changes, so no run would have written one. Step 7 now says it: the run marks the tuning file, and a lesson that changes the spec is also a numbered item. No weave run forced it; the reason is in `033f5da5`.

**v1.3 (2026-09-26) — use is read from git.** Schema v1.26 retired `activation_count` and `last_activated`, which measured who remembered to bump them. The new-arrival step, the use line, stale metadata and the dormancy proposal now read `entry-use.py`. No weave run forced it (`32cb19ed`).

**v1.4 (2026-10-14) — the sweep reads the trunk.** Tuning item 40. Step 2.5 runs `trunk-state.mjs` as its sixth command, and its rows take recover, discard or leave like the rest.

**v1.5 (2026-10-14) — every canon entry wears a face.** Tuning item 41; see § The Weave's Widened Charter, *Faces on every entry*.

**v1.6 (2026-10-18) — a stale generated block fails the gate.** Tuning item 42. [[The Shop]]'s roster is computed by `Shop/Maker/build-roster.mjs` but committed as text, so it goes stale whenever a Specialist is added, moved or re-statused and nobody re-runs the builder. Two weaves found it stale and fixed it by hand, and both filed the fix as mechanical, which is the proof a hard gate waits for (Protocol, the note on judgment). So `lint-doc-drift.py` gained E4: it runs each generated block's own builder with `--check`, and postcondition 2e fails while a block is stale. W3 passes the builder's own warnings through for review. The linter already ran in the Deposit's check, the Return's map and the Schema Ceremony, so one check reaches a session's drift wherever someone next looks, not only at the monthly weave. A steward cycle that edits a Shop page refreshes the block itself.

**v1.7 (2026-10-18) — decisions reach every page.** Tuning item 43. A propagate flag (`flag_type: propagate`) names no source entries, so no touch closes it; it carries a scope, a check, an optional pattern and a rhythm, and closes by `Palace-Resolves:` once its check is clean. Step 1c now has two kinds of flag: entry flags close by trailer or decline, with a touch read by hand (paying items 21 and 24); propagate flags are carried first, and an unfinished one leaves a `Carried flag` line for the next weave to start from. Postcondition 2b, the commit line and the report count them, and the linter gates them with `--since`. See § Decisions Reach Every Page.

**v1.8 (2026-10-21) — the report names proposals owed.** Step 2's report lists the pages whose stewards have proposals waiting on Loudon's ledger review (`list-proposals.mjs --line`); the weave names them and settles none. No weave run forced it (`e559e64b`).

**v1.9 (2026-10-22) — the opening builds the map and reads the diff.** Tuning item 46. The opening runs `build-map.py` and diffs against the last weave's map; Step 6.5 diffs open against close, and the report carries both.

## Fewer, Better Links (2026-10-24)

The October weave opened on a map that was dense and healthy by every old measure — no orphans, every page reachable, median degree 13 — and Loudon's read was that it had grown too connected and not nuanced enough. The scans agreed, in three shapes: 410 pairs carried the same relation twice (a symmetric link written on both sides, or a bare `connects-to` beside a specific link); 558 links were bare `connects-to`; and 1,028 of the 2,943 frontmatter links the scan reads pointed at a page the body never named, 354 of them with no label either. A third of the graph was saying "near" and giving no reason.

Loudon's word, 2026-10-24: "I agree that all the 'connect to' links are weak, labels or removal is fine, there will be more weaves so if we remove a connection it could come back later. lets bias toward making the links high quality and lesser, and if some come back later, great." That became the Standing Order on the scroll and the turn in the card: the weave takes links away as well as adding them. A typed link is a claim with a reason — type, label, or a sentence in the body — and one without any is cut, because the palace is re-read every weave and a real connection returns with its reason. The old guard against this was the slow-metabolism rule for *new* introductions, which still holds; pruning is the exhale that rule never had.

**The labels propose the types.** Once every `connects-to` carries a label, the labels are a corpus: a few hundred words the palace has used to say how two pages meet. Clustered fuzzily, the large coherent clusters are the relations the ten types never named. Loudon, 2026-10-24: "our original collection of link types was never meant to be eternal." The harvest (Step 3f) proposes; the Schema Ceremony still decides, because a type is a permanent commitment and the register ([[Resonant Link Labels]]) already warned that label inflation cheapens the types.

**The unsaid link.** The unsung path had a scan since the first weave; its inverse had none. A frontmatter link whose target the body never mentions is a relation the page asserts and cannot explain. `unsaid-links.py` finds them; the disposition stays judgment — write the sentence, add the label, or cut.

**Memory keeps pace with faces.** v1.5 put a hero and an icon on every canon entry; by October 316 of 325 wore both, while 20 of 324 had a Context and 3 a rich face. The pages pointed at most — Kuramoto Coupling, Spinoza Conatus, The Shop, Maker — carried no account of why they are shaped as they are. The bundle audit (Step 5d) names the memory a page's weight has earned, with a threshold low enough to catch the heavy pages and high enough to leave a small mature page alone. The rich-face nomination (Step 5e) is the weave reading the same list for Enrichment, which makes; the weave only names.

**Health as a trend.** Every weave had reported numbers and none had kept them, so no weave could say whether the palace was healthier than the last one left it. The health ledger answers the Context's old question about a persistent report: not a report entry, a ledger row, the same columns every time, written at the open and the close.

**Decisions first.** v1.7 put the propagate flags in Step 1c, after the full read. In practice the read should happen on pages the palace has already brought in line with what it decided, so v2.0 moves the carrying to Step 0c, before the fan-out, and has the weave close by posting flags for the standards it set itself.

**v2.0 (2026-10-24) — fewer, better links; memory keeps pace with faces; health as a trend.** Tuning items 47–57. One link per pair becomes a step (3d) with a linter (`lint-link-pairs.py`), paying item 29; the hub bar moves to the top inbound decile, paying item 30; the unsaid-links scan joins the unsung-path audit (3e); label enrichment stops being a handful and settles every bare `connects-to`; the bundle audit (5d) and rich-face nominations (5e) are new steps with a script; the health ledger (6.6) is a new ledger kind; decisions move to Step 0c; workers are `palace-reader` children born with the real Jewel (item 44), a skeptic stage sits before synthesis (item 28), the worker template asks for lighter prose (item 39), and the card and the ghost linter cite the Jewel for its own line (item 45). See § Fewer, Better Links.

## Open Questions

- Is median link-degree the right proxy for a new entry's catch-up target, or should it weight body-length and cross-pillar reach so a deeply-linked-but-shallow entry isn't mistaken for a healthy one?
- The bundle-frontmatter and ghost-link checks are judgment scans today; at what point do repeated Weaves prove their mechanical core sharp enough to earn a script (the values-primary "optimize once proven" step)?
- At what palace size should the Weave split into two passes — one for topology, one for link proposals?
- Answered in v2.0: the record of each weave is a row in the health ledger, not a report entry; the HTML report stays in the session folder.
- Is there a minimum Weave that can be done in under 15 minutes for maintenance during an active work period, distinct from the full monthly Weave? v2.0 sketches one without naming it: Step 0c (decisions), the link-pairs and unsaid scans with their mechanical folds, the face audit and the linters — no fan-out. Not yet run.
- When do `lint-link-pairs.py` and `unsaid-links.py` earn a gate? The rule: two clean runs prove the fold mechanical. The October weave is the first.
