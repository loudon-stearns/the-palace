# Loudon's Toolkit

![[Loudon's Toolkit — hero.png]]

Everything Loudon makes with — the hardware and software behind the music, the teaching, the building and the thinking. It is one entry with two inventories in its folder, split along the only division that holds up: what lives inside the computer, and what lives outside it.

Any Claude reading this page plus one of the two catalogs should have reliable orientation in that half of the toolkit, without reconstructing it from scattered conversation.

## The line

If you can pick it up, it lives outside. Everything else lives inside.

- **[[Loudon's Toolkit — catalog — outside the computer|Outside the computer]]** — synthesizers, drum machines, controllers, instruments, microphones and speakers, hardware effects, the computers and audio interfaces themselves, workshop tools.
- **[[Loudon's Toolkit — catalog — inside the computer|Inside the computer]]** — plugins, Ableton, Max and RNBO, design and art software, programming languages and environments.

Some tools reach across. Push and Move are objects on the desk that drive software; an audio interface is a box whose whole job is the crossing. Each is listed once, outside, and its line says what it drives inside.

Both catalogs grow by interview. The notes, and what has been covered so far, live in the working doc, [[Loudon's Toolkit — context — assessment]].

## The live rig

Target configuration for a live show:
- Keyboard (melodic synth)
- Electric bass
- Drum pads (Nord Drum 3P + Erica Synths)
- Vocal FX
- Ableton track control

The Nord was acquired specifically to serve the drum pad role while looking dynamic on stage. The full performance rig configuration is in active planning — space and consistent setup are the current constraints.

## Teaching

For broad online teaching, **software synthesizers are strongly preferred** — they are screenable, cheaper for students, and accessible without physical gear. This is a deliberate teaching philosophy, not a limitation.

Hardware synths are used for teaching in two specific contexts:

1. **One-on-one clients** — Hydrasynth Explorer is used with certain clients when hardware-specific instruction is appropriate.
2. **Manufacturer hire** — Teaching hardware synths is primarily envisioned as manufacturer-sponsored work, one of Loudon's own hopes.

## What the toolkit is working toward

**Preset Management** — Building comprehensive custom preset banks for each synth is a recognized critical project. Currently underinvested. Loudon notes this is work he would genuinely enjoy — it just needs dedicated time and commitment.

**Performance Configuration** — Designing and stabilizing a consistent hardware setup that can be left assembled (space is the current bottleneck). Would benefit from a diagram and deliberate routing design.

**Hardware Product (long-term)** — The aspiration to build and sell original synthesizer/effects hardware is a long-term goal of Loudon's own. Everything learned about hardware synthesis feeds this.

**Read The Manual** — whole manuals read, tested, and covered section by section, in Loudon's always-positive, always-supportive-of-makers voice. It is its own project now: [[LDN RTM]] holds the format and the queue, and the plugins marked as RTM candidates in the inside catalog feed it.

## How the tools relate

The catalogs sort by where a tool lives. The signal chain sorts by what it does:

- **Instruments** (acoustic and electronic) → generate or manipulate sound in the physical world
- **Controllers** → translate physical gesture into digital data
- **Synthesis environments** (hardware synths, Max/MSP, RNBO) → generate and transform sound digitally
- **DAW** (Ableton) → the central hub for arrangement, routing, and live performance
- **Plugins** → extend the DAW with processing, synthesis, and utility tools
- **Audio effects** (hardware and software) → color, sculpt, and destroy
- **Programming environments** → build new instruments, teach concepts, run research
- **Visual/design tools** → create educational materials, visualizations, documentation
- **Workshop tools** → maintain, modify, and build the physical world that houses all of the above

This layered view is how tools relate — not just what they are, but what role they play in the larger process.

## Four Pillars Alignment

- **Creation** — Every tool in this cluster is an extension of Loudon's creative capacity. The question is always: what does this instrument make possible that wasn't possible before?
- **Tools** — This entry is, itself, a meta-tool: a map of the instrument landscape that extends the capacity of any collaborating AI instance.
- **Philosophy** — Tool relationships reflect Spinozist conatus: each instrument tends toward its own maximum expressiveness. The signal chain is the ecology.
- **Practice** — Fluency is not uniform across the toolkit. The inventory captures relationship quality (fluent / learning / estranged / aspirational) — a map of current and possible practice.
