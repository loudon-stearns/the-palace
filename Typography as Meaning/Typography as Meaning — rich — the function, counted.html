The function, counted

The section says every text event is a point in four answers (who speaks, inside or outside the story, to whom, and the feeling) and that the lettering is a function of those four, not a free choice. Here is that function, the one whos-talking.html wrote down, copied unchanged. It starts from one balloon and ends by putting all 120 answer-sets through it and running it backwards: how many forms come out, and which answers can you read back off them?

One balloon

A speaks, inside the story, to B, calmly. Four answers go in; one balloon comes out. The blue line underneath is the function showing its work, rule by rule, in the order it applies them.

form = f(source, diegesis, addressee, emotion)
(how the words look: balloon, tail, face, case, size, where they sit) is decided by (who speaks, whether inside the story, to whom, how they feel), and by nothing else. Same four answers, same balloon.

Change one answer

Now the four answers are yours. Change one at a time and watch which part of the form moves. Each answer has its own job: the feeling picks the balloon and the face, the source can take them over, inside or outside picks frame or margin, and the listener moves the balloon and its size.

At A · inside · to B · calm this is step 1's balloon.

Two answers want the same balloon

A radio speaking with menace. Menace wants the icicle balloon and a dripping face; a radio wants its serrated balloon and a typewriter. A balloon has one outline and one face, so one answer has to give way. The page says source outranks emotion. Switch the order and see what each order costs.

The left panel is step 2 set to A · inside · to B · cold menace; the middle, the radio · inside · to B · calm.

The other order is made here, not in the proof: it runs the same rules, then lets the feeling take the balloon and the face back. Only the page's order is the function.

The test: all 120, run backwards

If the form is chosen by the four answers, then running it backwards should give the answers back. So put every answer-set through the function and count what comes out. Each tile is one form a reader could see; its number says how many answer-sets make exactly that form. A red number is a form that can't tell you which of them it was. Press a tile to see them.

Every tile is drawn by step 2's function; the answer-sets are every setting of step 2's four menus.

4 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 120 answer-sets  →  forms
(4 speakers) × (inside or outside) × (3 listeners) × (5 feelings) = 120 ways to answer the four questions. If every one drew a different form, all 120 could be read back. Each form shared by two or more is a place where running it backwards loses an answer.
forms answer-sets with a form of their own shared forms

Counted on the function, not on a reader. The function marks inside or outside every time; whether a reader sees the mark is the other test, the blind read in whos-talking.html, where a quiet margin caption was looked straight past. Tremor is drawn at random each time, so the tiles shake differently on each look; whether a form shakes is fixed. Two answer-sets count as one form when they differ only in what a reading can't see: under "to the eye", a 2 px shift of a margin caption, while a 3 px step in type size still counts as seen (merge those too and 73 forms remain); under "without size", every type size.
