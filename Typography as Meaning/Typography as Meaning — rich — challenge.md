All three counts match the piece. **What "to the eye" means.** It merges exactly one difference: `draw()` puts a margin balloon "to us" at cy 32 and one "to B" at cy 30 (`whos-talking.html:169–170`). That is all of the to-whom loss. To a ruler it reads back 120/120. At the eye it loses exactly the 40 margin answer-sets addressed to B or to us. Every type-size step is still counted as visible, including 21 against 24 px and, to oneself, 16.8 against 19.2 px. That is a 2.4 px type step called visible beside a 2 px shift called invisible. It's defensible, since a whole word 14% larger shows more than a balloon nudged 2 px, but it is a choice. Merge those two steps as well and you get **73 forms**, with the feeling reading back 77/120. The steps it would merge are real ones: a thinking A's calm and whisper are the same Caveat cloud at 24 and 21 px.

**Verdict: keep.** It is a test: the count could have come back 120, and it found where the grammar leaks. It walks up: one balloon, one answer changed, the collision, then all 120. It is faithful. ## "The grammar, blind"

**Chance levels are right.** Ordering: a random order gets n(n−1)/4 pairs on average and a perfect order once in n!. That is 3 of 6 and 1 in 24 for weight, and 1.5 of 3 and 1 in 6 for case and for hand. `check.mjs` brute-forces every order, and the formula is exact. Naming: seven outlines and seven meanings, with repeats allowed, average 7 × 1/7 = 1 named. A reader who uses each meaning once also averages 1, so the figure holds either way. **The commitment holds.** The key button stays disabled until every specimen is ranked or every balloon named. Once the key shows, ranking and selects are locked. The tally appears only after the balloon step is keyed.

**Nothing leaks by order.** The specimens and balloons are shuffled on every load. The meaning menu is shuffled independently of the balloons. Tags are display positions (A–D, 1–7). The truth labels render only after keying, and no shape id reaches the DOM. **Outline size** differs, but only as part of the outline: spiky's points reach 1.3× the radius, icicle's drips 1.45×, and wavy carries three drips. All seven share the centre, the radii, and the same "I'M STILL HERE." at 21 px, weight 600. Spiky being largest is the convention itself, not a stray cue.

**Verdict: keep.** It is a test, since a reader can fall to chance and the verdict says so. It is faithful to the page's list (`:38–41`), and the note admits that the menu gives each outline only its first meaning and that the hand step moves more than one detail. It walks up: one part per step, then the seven-way naming last. Notes:
1. *Start over* after keying lets a reader redo a step with the key in mind, and the tally doesn't mark the retry. Either reshuffle on start-over or tag the row "second try".
2. The test menu gives the rectangle "a narrator, or a machine", while the function piece next door draws the radio as the proof's serrated ellipse. Inside one face the radio has two outlines. [[Graphic Storytelling]] (`Graphic Storytelling.md:54`, Blambot) sides with the rectangle, and the page says "spiky … electronic". The palace already holds this tension, so it is not this piece's to fix, but the face shows both sides without naming it.
3. At 3 pairs, "held whole" happens by chance 1 time in 6. The note's "a sign, not a measurement" covers it.

## The offers

**Offer 1 (The three axes, claim-change).** **The numbers are confirmed.** 95 / 80 / 58. In/out reads back 120/120. To whom reads back 80/120 at the eye and 120 to a ruler, so all of the loss is the margin's 2 px. A menacing radio against a calm one: same serrated balloon, Special Elite italic, capitals and tail, differing only in **24 → 30 px**. The narrator inside the story makes 5 feelings into 2 forms (only weak's tremor survives).

**The proposed line is disputed.** "The source takes the balloon and the face, the feeling keeps size and case, and only the narrator takes everything" generalises from the radio. Run through `letter()`:
- the **radio** takes the balloon and the face; the feeling keeps size and case;
- **A thinking** takes the balloon and the **case** (always lowercase), and the face only on calm and fury (Caveat). Weak, whisper and menace keep Shadows Into Light, Caveat and Creepster;
- the **narrator** takes balloon, face, case and size, but not the tremor;
- **outside the story** the margin takes the balloon from every voice but the narrator's (a rectangle), so diegesis outranks both source and feeling;
- **to oneself** takes the case and scales the size by 0.8.

So the page's sentence (`:48`) is not wrong: where source and feeling want the same channel, source wins. "Per channel" is sharper than the page, but it is per channel *and per source*. What the page lacks is the cost. A possible line: *"Run as a function, the precedence is set per source: a radio takes the balloon and the face and leaves the feeling only its size, so a menacing radio is a calm one 6 px larger; a mind takes the balloon and the case; the narrator takes everything but a tremor."*

**A sharper finding for the same offer.** The 2 px is not a slip in the renderer; it is the grammar colliding with itself. The page uses the margin for two things: "Placement = diegesis … in the margin = *outside it*" (`:42`) and "a balloon breaking into the margin addresses *us*" (`:39`). A voice already outside the story has nowhere further to break, so "to us" has no mark of its own. That is worth offering beside the precedence line, and it is still a claim-change.

**Offer 2 (The letterform as material, claim-change).** **The observation is real** (I looked at all four renders: the tail is gone in each, and at 0.35 the body goes too). **But it isn't enough for a claim-change.** The end 0.4 and 0.7 pairs are nearly the same image, so it is effectively **two renders**, of one guide, one seed and one word, at strengths ≤ 0.5. BLUELINE's own findings never mention the tail, and the higher strengths it predicts will hold a cleaner rim (0.65, 0.8, 1.0) were never run. The guide's tail is also a thin open wedge (`sweep/guide.png`), which a canny lock may drop because it is thin, not because it is a tail. "Every time" overstates two images.

**Recommendation: downgrade to a no-claim-edit observation, not under "The letterform as material" but beside the Forward Vector the page already has** (`:71`, "the balloon as authored geometry — its tail anchored to a figure's mouth keypoint"). Something like: *"BLUELINE's canny sweep lost the guide's tail at both strengths tried; its montage draws the tails after the render."* The stronger evidence for "tail last" is already in the bundle: the montage's tails are composited by `balloon_lib`, never diffused. The test that would earn the claim is one more cell in BLUELINE's queued sweep: tail survival at cn 0.65, 0.8 and 1.0, with a filled tail as well as a thin one.

## The face as a whole

It makes the idea felt and stays well clear of being busy. The page's boldest sentence is that lettering is a function, so the form is chosen for you. The face takes that literally: it runs the page's own function backwards and counts it. What comes back is better than a confirmation. The grammar is a function, and it mostly runs backwards, but it leaks in two specific places. Ask a radio to sound menacing and it gains 6 px. Ask a voice outside the story to address us and the margin has already been spent. The blind piece puts the reader's own eye against the same grammar, part by part, and keeps score honestly against chance. The gathered renders let you see what "author the structure, let the model supply the material" looks like when it works. They also show where the model let go of the structure, which is the tail. The two remakes are bookkeeping, not judgment. The one thing to hold back is the second offer's leap from two images to "every time".
