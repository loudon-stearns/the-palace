The grammar, blind

The section lists what each part of the lettering means: weight is volume, case is intimacy, a hand belongs inside the story and type outside it, and the balloon's outline is the feeling. Read it here before the labels: in each step only one thing changes, you put the forms in order or name them, and only then does the key say what the grammar predicted. Nothing is kept once you leave.

Weight

One word, four times. Same family, same size, same case. Only the thickness of the strokes changes.

Press them in order, from the quietest voice to the loudest.

Case

One line, three times. Same weight, same family. Only the capitals change.

Press them in order, from the most private (said close, to one person) to the most public.

Hand or type

One sentence, three times. Same case, the weights as near as three families allow. What changes is how the letters were made: a face drawn for print, a face drawn for print to look lettered, and a hand. That takes three families, so more than one detail moves here; hand or type is itself a change of letterform.

Press them in order, from the one most like the author telling the story to the one most like someone in it saying it.

The test: the balloon alone

Seven balloons, and nothing else to go on. The words, the face, the size and the case are the same in all seven, and none has a tail, since a tail says who is speaking. Only the outline differs. The section's first line says the outline is the feeling, so name the feeling from the outline.

Give each balloon the meaning it reads as. A meaning may be used twice.

One reader, a handful of choices: a sign, not a measurement. The grammar is a convention, so a reader who knows comics will match more of it; the test is whether the convention reads to you, not whether it is natural. The section gives some outlines two or three meanings (spiky is a shout or electronic; the rectangle is formal, robotic or narration); the menu offers each outline its first one. Slant and tremor are left out: one change per step, and the walk stops at four.
