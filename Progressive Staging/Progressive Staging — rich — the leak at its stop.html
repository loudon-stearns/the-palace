Progressive Staging · the method · four steps

The leak at its stop

The page says Stage 2 wraps around Stage 1, so that a student can turn the leak off and get Stage 1 back exactly. Here are the two stages as the Action Potential Oscillator's dev plan writes them. Take each alone, run the leak to its stop and lay it over Stage 1, then run Stage 1's own frequency test on Stage 2.

1 · A capacitor and a threshold

one thing: threshold and reset

Stage 1 is a membrane that only stores charge. A steady current fills it by the same amount every sample. When it reaches the threshold it resets and starts again. The plan resets by subtracting the threshold rather than setting the voltage to zero, so whatever spilled past the threshold on that sample is kept as the start of the next climb.

2 · A leak

one thing: the time constant

Stage 2 lets the charge leak away while it fills. Every sample the voltage loses a share of itself, set by the membrane's time constant τ, and the drive is turned up just enough that the climb still reaches the threshold in one period. A short τ bends the climb into a curve that slows near the top. As in the plan, Stage 2 resets to zero.

doPlay, then slide τ while it plays.
watchThe climb bend away from the straight dashed line as τ shortens, and straighten as it lengthens.
hearA short τ is warmer, fewer upper harmonics; a long one comes back toward Stage 1's buzz.

3 · The leak at its stop, over Stage 1

one thing: what turning the leak off gives back

Now slide τ to its stop, where the leak is gone. At the stop the leak's two numbers become Stage 1's: the voltage keeps all of itself and the drive is one period's worth, f / fs, exactly what Stage 1 adds. The climb is Stage 1's climb. Lay the two over each other and find the first reset they don't share, then watch how far Stage 2 falls behind over two seconds.

doPlay both at once. Then their difference. Slide τ back off the stop and on again.

4 · The test: Stage 1's own, inherited

one thing: does the inherited test pass?

The page says testing criteria pile up: Stage 2's tests include Stage 1's frequency test, so a change that breaks Stage 1 is caught. Stage 1's test is in the plan: at 440 Hz the output measures 440 within a tenth of a hertz. Run it on Stage 2 with the leak at its stop, at five notes. Then give Stage 2 Stage 1's reset, keeping what spilled over, and run it again.
