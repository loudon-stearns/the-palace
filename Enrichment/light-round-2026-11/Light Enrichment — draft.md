## What a light enrichment is

One small thing set beside one sentence of a page, in its rich face: a still image, a diagram, a short loop, a picture drawn from the palace's own records, or a short sound. It makes that sentence clearer or more felt. It asks nothing of the reader: nothing to operate, nothing to learn first.

It is the small sibling of the heavy round, not a replacement. A heavy piece walks a reader up to a test. A light piece only has to make one sentence land.

It never changes the page's words. It lands as a proposal, and Loudon approves it in the palace, often when the Daily Page brings him to it.

## The round, step by step

1. **Open a workshop.** An elder opens `Enrichment/light-round-YYYY-MM/`. Everything is made there; nothing is written into a bundle until placement.
2. **Choose pages.** Start with pages that have no face. Leave alone any page a heavier effort has claimed (the hook pilot, the nominations baton), the floor pages unless Loudon names them, and anything whose record is private.
3. **Read the page whole, then pick one sentence.** The sentence is the anchor. Write down the claim the piece will make before making it. If the claim is not on the page, it is the maker's claim and the caption must say so.
4. **Let the sentence choose the form.** A sound if the idea can be heard. A diagram if it is a structure. A count if the palace's own records hold it. A loop if it is a change over time. Avatars and heroes are one material among these: reach for them when the idea is about pages themselves, and do not go looking for a way to use them.
5. **Let a neighbour leak in, when it helps.** A linked page's image or idea can shape the piece, as if the pages were speaking to each other. Check that the neighbour is really linked and really says what is borrowed, and name it.
6. **Make it with a script that stays.** The maker lives in the workshop beside its output and its data. A count names its source and its date. Randomness is seeded, or thrown once and kept.
7. **Write three lines for each piece.** A caption that states the claim, not a description of the picture. What is real and what is a choice. Which neighbour leaked in, if one did.
8. **Check it at the size it will be shown.** Look at every picture at band width. Check every sound by measurement, and say plainly that nobody has heard it until somebody has.
9. **Place it as a proposal.** An elder writes it into the rich face: in the band, sounds as m4a, the manifest noting it is a light piece and whether it has been challenged.
10. **Challenge before it is committed.** Batches of about ten, grouped by what kind of checking they need, each read by an agent that did not make them. The challenger reads the page whole, recomputes every number, and returns keep, remake or retire with a reason, plus any error it found on the page itself.
11. **Remake once, and record it.** The maker fixes what was found. The manifest keeps the verdict and what changed.
12. **Gather what the challenge found on the pages.** Stale numbers and missing links on the pages are offers for Loudon, and are often worth more than the pictures.
13. **Loudon reviews in the palace.** Section by section: approve, or leave a note for the next round.

## What the first round taught

Seventy pieces on sixty-eight pages. Of the first forty a challenger read, sixteen were kept as made and twenty-four needed work; none was retired. The faults came in a few kinds, and each has a guard.

- **The caption said more than the page.** Guard: write the claim first, and find it on the page.
- **The honesty line owned too little.** Guard: list every choice a reader could mistake for a finding: the selection, the scale, the thresholds, the mapping.
- **A count was off by one or two.** Guard: read frontmatter as YAML, not by pattern, and count again a second way.
- **A measure was diluted.** Guard: measure the thing the page measures, with the page's own tool where it has one.
- **The picture invented something.** Guard: draw only what a page describes; anything else is labelled as the maker's.
- **A leak was not on the neighbour.** Guard: open the neighbour and find the sentence.
- **It could not be read.** Guard: place in the band, and keep in-image words few and large.
- **A piece called the reader's companion "he".** Guard: a face a reader meets does not name the person in the room.
- **The maker was not reproducible as a plain run.** Guard: one seed per piece, not one shared stream.

Two things the round got right and should keep: the sounds each carry a picture drawn from what the sound did, and the challengers were asked to recompute, not to judge plausibility.

## What it cost

The first forty took four challengers, about 800 thousand tokens together, against roughly four million at the heavy round's one-challenger-per-page rate.
