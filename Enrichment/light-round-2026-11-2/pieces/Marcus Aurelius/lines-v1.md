# Marcus Aurelius — met-on-paper

**Title:** Epictetus, on paper

**Caption:** Why it was a book. Epictetus died around 135, teaching in Greece, when Marcus was about fourteen and in Rome. Marcus turned to philosophy at twenty-five, in 146 or 147, about eleven years later. He came to know Epictetus through notes of his lectures, which his teacher Rusticus lent him. The *Meditations* came later still, written on campaign, in the years of the plague and the northern war.

**What is real and what is a choice**
- **Real.** Every date drawn is taken from `dates.json`, and every one there names at least two sources. `make.py` refuses to draw a date with fewer. All sources were read on the web on 2026-11-17:
  - **Epictetus born, c. 50s.** SEP "Epictetus" says "sometime in the 50s". IEP "Epictetus" says "in about 55". Drawn as a fade from 50 to 60.
  - **Banished from Italy, c. 89.** SEP says "edict of Domitian (in 89)". IEP says "in about 89". Wikipedia's "Epictetus" says "Around AD 93". Drawn as a block from 89 to 95.
  - **Arrian writes down the lectures, c. 104–108.** SEP says "around the year 108 by Millar's (1965) dating". IEP says "some time around the years 104–107", and calls that a conjecture. Drawn as a block from 104 to 108.
  - **Epictetus dies c. 135, at Nicopolis.** SEP says "remained there teaching and lecturing until his death around 135". IEP's heading gives "55–135". Drawn as a fade from 130 to 140. The bracket's "c. 11 yrs" runs from 135 to 146, so the gap could be anything from about 6 to 17 years.
  - **Marcus born 121, in Rome.** SEP, IEP, and Livius ("121 Born in Rome").
  - **Adopted 138.** IEP says "adopted by the Emperor Antoninus Pius in 138". Livius gives 138 too.
  - **Turns to philosophy at 25, 146 or 147.** The primary source is Marcus's own letter to Fronto, *Ad M. Caes.* 4.13: "at twenty-five years of age". romanletters.org dates the letter c. 147 (145–147 in the 19th-century translation). Wikipedia's "Marcus Aurelius" says "between April 146 and April 147" and cites Birley, *Marcus Aurelius*, p. 69. I did not read Birley myself. Drawn as a block from 146 to 147.
  - **Rusticus lends him the notes.** The primary source is *Meditations* 1.7 in George Long's translation: Rusticus made him "acquainted with the discourses of Epictetus, which he communicated to me out of his own collection". Wikipedia's "Quintus Junius Rusticus" quotes the same passage and gives no year. It is drawn with no date: the dotted line stops at its words and does not touch Marcus's line.
  - **Emperor 161.** SEP, IEP, and Livius ("7 March 161").
  - **Martyrs at Lyon, 177.** Eusebius, *Church History* 5.1.1 (McGiffert's translation), says "the seventeenth year of the Emperor Antoninus Verus". Counting from 161, that year is 177; the arithmetic is mine. Livius has "177 Martyrs in Lyon".
  - **Dies 180.** Cassius Dio 72.33–34 gives "the seventeenth of March" and "nineteen years and eleven days" as emperor from 7 March 161. SEP and Livius also give 180.
  - **The Antonine plague, from 165/6.** Duncan-Jones, "The Antonine Plague Revisited", *Arctos* 52 (2018), § 2: "165 Plague in the east at Nisibis and Smyrna", "165/6 … brought back to Rome", and outbreaks in 182 and in 189 at Rome. Wikipedia's "Marcus Aurelius" says "broke out in 165 or 166". Livius has "165 Outbreak of the Plague".
  - **War on the northern frontier, c. 166–180.** Wikipedia's "Marcomannic Wars" says "from about CE 166 until 180". Livius says Marcus "leaves Rome for war" in 169, records "continued war" in 179, and has him die at Vindobona in 180.
  - **The *Meditations*.** IEP "Marcus Aurelius" says "probably written while Marcus was on campaign … c. C.E. 171–175". SEP says "written during the last years of his military campaigns". Livius has "174 Begins Meditations". The book endings in Long's translation place the writing at the front: "Among the Quadi at the Granua" (Book 1) and "This in Carnuntum" (Book 2).
- **Choices a reader could take for findings:**
  - **The soft ends and blocks are my drawing of uncertainty**, not data. They show the range the sources leave open; the shading inside a range means nothing. Two ranges are worth naming:
    - The *Meditations* band fades in across 170–172 and out across 177–180. That covers IEP's 171–175, Livius's start in 174, and SEP's "last years". Scholars do not agree on these dates, and the band does not settle them.
    - The war band fades in across 166–169, between the war's start and Marcus leaving Rome for it.
  - **The plague band is pale after 180**, fading out by 192. It ran past his death: Duncan-Jones records outbreaks in 182 and 189. The band does not mark an end, and its fade does not stand for any figure.
  - **The dotted line from Arrian's notes to the words "Epictetus's lectures reach Marcus on paper" is my link.** *Meditations* 1.7 calls them Epictetus's *hypomnemata*, "notes" or "memoirs". Whether Rusticus's copy was Arrian's *Discourses* as we have them is not settled. The page says "the *Discourses*".
  - **The shaded column, 121 to 135, is the years both were alive.** It does not mean they could have met. Marcus was a boy in Rome (Livius: born in Rome; adopted there in 138). Epictetus was teaching at Nicopolis, in north-western Greece, until his death (SEP; IEP). SEP says Marcus "was never in fact his pupil". No source I read records a meeting, and none rules one out, so the piece says neither. The column's right edge is drawn sharp at 135 for the sake of the shading; Epictetus's death is c. 135, and his line fades across 130–140.
  - **"Dies 180" gives no place.** Livius says Vindobona. Wikipedia lists Vindobona or Sirmium. Dio does not say.
  - **"a slave, later freed"** is SEP's account: "spent a portion of his life as the slave of Epaphroditus", and "the freedman philosopher". SEP does not say he was born a slave, though the palace's Epictetus page and Wikipedia do, so the picture does not say it either. **"Banished from Italy"** and **"teaches at Nicopolis, Greece"** are SEP's and IEP's wording, shortened. SEP places Nicopolis "in Epirus, on the Adriatic coast of northwest Greece".
  - **The frame runs from 50 to 195 CE**, so that the plague can be seen outliving him. Not drawn: Lucius Verus as co-emperor (161–169), Commodus as co-emperor from 177, and the deaths of Marcus's children. Their dates are less settled, and the page's sentence does not need them.
  - **The title and caption are mine.** "From a book" is the page's. Why it was a book (the teacher had died, and he wrote nothing) is the maker's, from these dates and from the Epictetus page.
- **No house rules are unmet** that I know of. It is a still picture, about 1000 px wide at 1:1 (PNG 2000 × 1080 at 2x), in the Drafting Table inks.

Its dates (c. 50–135) agree with SEP and IEP.

**Offers for the page** (found while checking the dates):
1. **"He learned his Stoicism from a book"** says more than the sources do. *Meditations* Book 1 thanks living Stoic teachers: Rusticus, Apollonius and Sextus. Cassius Dio 72.35 says Rusticus and Apollonius "both … professed Zeno's doctrines". What came from a book was Epictetus. Suggested: "He learned Epictetus from a book".
2. **People/Epictetus.md line 82** says Marcus "learned this philosophy directly from a former slave". That contradicts this page's "from a book" and SEP's "never in fact his pupil". Suggested: "from a former slave's recorded lectures".
3. **"The *Discourses* of Epictetus":** *Meditations* 1.7 says *hypomnemata* (notes). Long translates it "discourses", so this is a light touch at most.
4. **"At night, in a tent"** is not in the sources I read. The book endings place the writing on campaign, but not at night.
