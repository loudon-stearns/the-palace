## What went wrong, and what it teaches

1. **Evidence that agrees is not evidence that is independent.** Marcus Aurelius had two sources for every date, and both sources for the hinge date were summaries of one modern reading of a letter that says something else. Kokoro had forty takes, and ten seeds moved no timing, so it had four. In both the guard was real and the count it guarded was inflated.
2. **A translation is a summary.** "When he was twelve" was the English; the Latin is "having entered his twelfth year". The maker had noticed its date did not match and had not found why.
3. **An exception drawn without saying so where the eye lands.** Deposit Ceremony chose the one deposit of six that fits its sentence, and said so only in its notes. Then its counts on the picture added to five of six, and the missing one was the uncomfortable one.
4. **One claim attributed carefully, its neighbours stated as fact.** The same picture quoted one commit message as a commit's word and stated four others' claims plainly.
5. **The loudest thing in the picture was not the claim.** The wait in Deposit Ceremony; the one red mark on Marcus's line; the biggest shaded box in Kokoro, which showed the opposite of the sentence beside it. Each time the session's look or the challenger's cold read caught it, not the maker.
6. **A lost thing rebuilt when it had survived.** BLUELINE's shot was whole inside another proof image. The rebuild was exact, and unnecessary.
7. **A moving piece that shows nothing two-thirds of the time, and nothing at phone width.** True to the motion, and a reader has to be told when to look.
8. **A number credited to someone else is still a claim; a measurement with no script does not stay.** One piece's notes carried the challenger's figure underived, and six measured numbers lived only in prose.
9. **Too many words inside pictures.** 110 in one, 80 in another, at 11 to 13 px.
10. **A fault in the brief:** it pointed at Sound from Code's card for the house rules; they are on the page.

## What the process itself learned

- **Two pieces touched what is Loudon's to decide**, and the process had no step for it. One showed BLUELINE's boards on a concept page; that was caught at the line. One ended up stating a fact about his own reading of a deposit; that emerged in the making. A reader should say in its line when a piece would show a person's conduct or a project's unreleased work.
- **"Keep, with one fix" needs a name.** Three of four second verdicts were keeps with a small fix and no third verdict. The maker applied it and the session looked. That worked, and it should be written into the process, not improvised.
- **The session's own look earns its place** as a step. In three of four pieces it named, before the challenger reported, where the weight was wrong.

## What changes in the brief (v2 → v3)

1. The house rules for sound are on `Shop/Sound from Code.md`, § House rules a maker meets here.
2. **Independence.** Before you count repeats or sources, ask what actually differs between them. When a date or a fact is the hinge of your "why", read a primary text whole, look for a primary source that disagrees, and if you read a translation, check the number in the original.
3. **One instance.** Look at its neighbours in the same record. Say whether it is typical or the exception where the eye lands, not only in the notes. Counts on the face add up to the whole.
4. **Attribute evenly.** If one claim is given as its source's word, the claims beside it get the same care.
5. **Weight.** Name the first thing the eye lands on. It should be the claim. Aim for fifty words or fewer inside a picture.
6. **Before rebuilding something lost**, search the project's own proof images for it.
7. **A moving piece.** An animated WebP loops by itself in a face; a video plays only on a click. Count the frames where nothing shows, tell the reader when to look, and say what a phone shows.
8. **Every number in the notes comes from a script or data file in the folder.** A number you credit to someone else, you also derive.
9. **A model's own clock.** Check what a seed changes before you count seeds. If people heard a take, re-render that take and lay the clock on it.
10. **No year, no place on a time axis.** Any point on a line is a claim.
11. **Part one:** say in your line when the piece would show a person's own conduct or a project's unreleased work. The session stops for Loudon there.
12. The bench: the "once" is dropped. Iterate with your own script; run the bench when an output is final, and say how many lines you left.

## What changes in the challenger's brief (v2 → v3)

1. Ask what makes the evidence independent: what a repeat varies, whether two sources are two witnesses.
2. For a source in translation, check the number in the original where you can.
3. Name the first thing your eye lands on, and say whether it is the claim.
4. Check that counts on the face add up to the whole, and that claims side by side are attributed alike.
5. When you offer a number, say its scope (the peak frame, the whole loop).
6. For a moving piece: count the frames where nothing shows; look at phone width.
7. A keep may carry one small fix with no third verdict; say so plainly, and say what the maker must verify before applying it.
