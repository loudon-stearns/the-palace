## What went well

- **The numbers held.** Across five pieces the challengers recomputed some dozens of figures their own way and found two wrong as first made, both in one piece (three files where there were four; a count not computed in the script). The first round's commonest fault, a count off by one or two, did not recur. Makers counted two ways and pinned sources.
- **One challenger per piece went deep.** Each rebuilt the thing: an independent descent with an analytic gradient; a recompiled engine, byte for byte; an m4a decoded twice with its own detector. A batch of ten could not have done that.
- **The second verdict earned its place.** It was quick each time, and each time it either confirmed the fix against the files or found the last small things. Two of the five would have been placed with errors without it.
- **Two remakes became different, better pieces.** Walk Ceremony went from a count of the palace to one real step of a walk. Search Before You Build went from "how long the answer waited" to "it was said in commit messages and in no page", which is the page's own Forward Vectors sentence.
- **Every maker found things on its page.** About thirty-five offers across five pages, several of them substantial: a quote filed under a chapter it is not in; a table row that says one frost lengthens the room when it takes many; a ceremony called weekly whose ledger holds no run.
- **Makers and challengers corrected each other, and said so.** A maker tested its own reason and found it false. Twice a maker tested a challenger's explanation and overturned it, and the challenger then confirmed it had been wrong.

## What went wrong

- **The words, four times out of five.** Every first-verdict fault that mattered was in a caption, a title or a "because": a title that reused the page's own word for something else; "no instruction" when SCHEMA and the Jewel give one; "the same model keeps the rate" when the hand did; "all joined up" when 51 threads were not; two untested causes. The guard for numbers is working; there is none yet for words.
- **A challenger's better idea is a claim too.** Three times a challenger offered an explanation or a better piece that rested on something it had not verified, and each time part of it was false. It was caught only because the maker was told to check first.
- **Second routes that fail silently.** Maker and challenger both missed a spelling with spaces. The challenger's variant search used a pattern its git does not support and returned nothing, which read as confirmation.
- **The first pictures were made before the reader's trip was found.** In two pieces the working session's own look found the place a first-time reader would stumble (two near-identical plates; a curve that ends before its number). The makers had looked at size and not seen it.
- **Three of five were Drawn SVG alone.** Every challenger was asked whether a Specialist was passed over and every one said no. That is probably true of these five pages. It also means the round has not yet asked anything of generated image, motion or voice.
- **The bench took noise.** One job left four run lines on two ledgers because the maker iterated through the bench. A taught line carries a wrong number and a child cannot correct it.

## What the bench could not do (for the Shop)

1. A project's own instrument has no place in a chain. The bench refused "Mycelial Reverb plugin engine (Algorithm.h)" unless it began "hand:", so a project earns no ledger line.
2. `check` resolves a file against the first job that lists it. When a picture got its own job, `check` showed it as CHANGED though its job matched.
3. Provenance's chain text is not updated when a rerun adds a step; only hashes are compared.
4. Inputs are hashed only if the job lists them, and nothing says when they are missing.
5. A maker who iterates through the bench leaves a run line each time. There is no quiet rerun for the same job on the same day.
6. A wrong `--taught` line cannot be withdrawn by the one who wrote it.

## What changes in the brief (v1 → v2)

1. A maker knows only its own page. (One assumed a neighbour had been given to another maker.)
2. Before choosing the form: what does the page describe and never show? For a page about a process, usually one real instance of it. Find why a thing happened before drawing that it happened. Open the place the page sends you before borrowing from anywhere else.
3. A guard for words. Before a caption or title is final: every noun the page has already defined keeps the page's meaning; every "never", "no", "all", "same", "only" and "because" is tested or cut; when one side of a comparison is exact by construction, the caption says so and credits what did it; a caption states a count, not an extreme the method decides.
4. The honest line also owns the rule or model that was simulated, where the frame ends, how a count moves with the method's settings, and every house rule not met.
5. Sound: the house numbers are on Sound from Code's page. True peak at or under −1 dBTP measured on the m4a; say how far from −16 LUFS and why; say when two halves were matched by peak and so differ in loudness; say what a listener might hear in place of the claim.
6. Searching: every spelling; a pattern the tool supports; a second route that would fail loudly. Pin a count to the commit its source was built at, and copy the source data into the folder.
7. The bench: iterate with your own script; run the bench when the piece is finished, and again only when an output changes. One command reproduces exactly the files to be placed. Each output belongs to one job. Inputs are listed. A project's instrument is named `hand: <its name>` until the bench has a place for it. Workshop data is named as not for the bundle.
8. A challenger's explanation or better piece is a claim. Verify it your own way before you draw it or write it.
9. After looking at the picture at size, look once more as a stranger and name the place a first-time reader would stumble.

## What changes in the challenger's brief (v1 → v2)

1. When you offer a better piece or an explanation, say which parts you verified and which you did not. An unverified idea is welcome if it is marked.
2. A second route must be able to fail loudly. An empty result from a search is a reason to check the search.
3. Read the words as hard as the numbers: every "never", "no", "all", "same", "only", "because"; every noun the page has defined.
4. For a sound: true peak on the m4a against the house ceiling; loudness of each part when parts are compared; what a listener might hear in place of the claim.
5. Check that one command reproduces the files to be placed and that `check` reads clean.
6. Look for the place a first-time reader would stumble in the picture, before reading the maker's lines.
