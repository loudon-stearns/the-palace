## 2. Three hooks

### A. Be the wall

*"There's an artery on the screen with a heartbeat slamming through it, and you squeeze it with your hand to keep it from bulging. You're always a little late, and the page tells you how late."*

**The crossing.** Three pages met:
- **The page's own sentence:** "You'll never forget how a compressor works after pumping blood through arteries" (Projects/Biomechanical Synthesis.md:86), and its haiku, "the student's hand on the knob / finally feels it" (Projects/Biomechanical Synthesis/Biomechanical Synthesis — enrichment — grotesque-pedagogy-haiku.md:11-13).
- **Blood Compressor on attack:** "Cannot be set to 0 — the body has reaction time" (Projects/Blood Compressor.md:75), and "giving the body time to *notice* before *acting*. That is what attack actually is" (:89).
- **Playful Interface Design on the thing with mass:** "the metaphor is felt before it is seen" (Playful Interface Design.md:51).

The spark is that the reader's hand has a reaction time too. The manifest admits the present face is "a student *watching* a vessel narrow, not building one" (rich.json:114). This is neither: the reader is the smooth muscle.

**First five seconds.** A translucent vessel fills the frame, throbbing silently. The first press starts the sound, a close, heavy heartbeat (Blood Compressor.md:36: "The audio signal is the heartbeat").
- **Hand:** press and hold to clamp the wall. The clamp is real gain reduction on the audio.
- **Late:** the beat punches through loud, the wall bulges, and a bruise blooms where it got past.
- **Too long:** the wall blanches white under the press, like pressed skin, and the sound starves.

After eight beats one number appears: "you were 180 ms late. A compressor calls that attack." Then the reflex takes over at the reader's own setting.

**Proof or front.** The hook is the proof. The data is the reader's own lag, measured. The two-faces piece follows directly: its attack and release knobs arrive already holding a number the reader's hand made. Its owed remake (challenge.md:48-52) still stands.

**Caption.** "Press and hold to squeeze the artery. Every heartbeat is a loud hit. Your squeeze turns the sound down, and that is all a compressor does. The number is how late your hand was."

**Honest risk.**
- A steady beat lets the reader anticipate, so the lag falls toward zero. That is lookahead, which a plain compressor does not have. Either make the heart irregular (Blood Compressor already has heart-rate variability, :82) or say so and use it as the second beat. This is my inference, not tested.
- Browser pointer and audio latency inflates the number; the face must say so.
- It overlaps Blood Compressor's own face; the difference is hand against watching, and it must stay that.
- The bruise and blanching must each mean something about gain, or they are juice (Playful Interface Design.md:75).

### B. The fly

*"Your cursor is a fly on a Venus flytrap. Brush one hair and nothing happens; brush twice quickly and it snaps shut on you and won't let your pointer go until you hold still."*

**The crossing.** Four sources met:
- **The page's item 6 and its questions:** "how sensitive is the trigger? How long does the trap stay closed?" (Projects/Biomechanical Synthesis.md:60, :90).
- **The page's own negative-space piece,** whose node is "V ← 0.85V + w, fire at 1" (rich.json:149).
- **Action Potential Oscillator:** Stage 2 leaky integrate-and-fire (Projects/Action Potential Oscillator.md:51), and "During absolute refractory, no stimulus fires the cell" (:103).
- **Playful Interface Design:** "Let the character push back" (Playful Interface Design.md:79), and the oracle's eye that breaks a standard "when the break carries meaning" (:103).

The spark: one touch charges the trap, the charge leaks, and a second touch before it leaks crosses the line. That is the same leaky node the face already tests, met as a plant. Hold time is felt as being held.

**First five seconds.** An open trap, wet, hairs visible.
- **Hand:** the cursor buzzes as it moves, pitch following speed. One hair brushed gives a tick and a faint glow that fades.
- **Snap:** a second brush in time closes the trap with a wet clap. The buzz goes muffled and close, and the pointer is stuck. Struggling keeps it shut; stillness opens it.

**Proof or front.** It stands in front of the negative-space piece, the face's strongest. The reader arrives at "one node dividing the trigger" having already been caught by one. It also gives instrument six, a seed with no body anywhere, its first sketch.

**Caption.** "You are the fly. One touch is not enough; the plant forgets it. Two close together and it shuts. It holds while you struggle and opens when you go still. A noise gate asks the same two questions: how much is enough, and how long do I hold?"

**Honest risk.**
- **Polarity.** A gate passes the loud thing; the trap swallows it. The page never fixes the mapping, so mine is inferred, and the caption must say the gate is the trap run the other way or a stranger learns it backwards.
- **Biology.** The two-touch memory of a real flytrap is from my own knowledge, not the palace. Check it before the face claims it.
- **Hysteresis.** Two touches in a window is leaky integration, not hysteresis in the strict two-threshold sense the page names.
- **Access.** Capturing the pointer needs an escape and a reduced-motion form (Playful Interface Design.md:137).

### C. The chair

*"There's a body in a tattoo-shop chair with a sound running through it, and a steel tray of organs beside it. You drop the heart in and the sound gets squeezed; the organs nobody has built yet sit grey in jars."*

**The crossing.** Four sources met:
- **The page's DAW as Narrative World:** "a toroidal body-mod tattoo shop — bodies being transformed, inscribed, made strange" (Projects/Biomechanical Synthesis.md:94), and "the topology of the signal flow suggests the story" (:100).
- **The page's build instruction:** "Simplify mathematically to a synthesizer modular patch" (:66). Body-mod as modular patching is my reading of the two lines together, not Loudon's stated pun.
- **Tract Mirror:** "The tubes on screen are not a visualization of the filter — they **are** the filter" (Projects/Tract Mirror.md:35).
- **The ants roughs' panel:** "What is real here, and what is a guess" (Palace development/Pheromone Trail/proofs/2026-11-09-ants-on-topology/src.html:80-84).

**First five seconds.** The body hums a plain loop.
- **Hand:** drag the heart from the tray into the chest. The neck vessel starts pinching on every beat and the loop tightens.
- **Order:** drop a second organ before or after the heart and the sound differs, because order in the chain is order in the body.
- **Unbuilt organs:** grey, in jars, labelled with the date they were last only a seed.

**Proof or front.** It stands in front, as the map. It replaces the May hub SVG as the opening and keeps the SVG beneath as the dated key. Each installed organ is the door to its section or its sibling's face.

**Caption.** "Each organ is one of the eight instruments. Wet and beating means it is built, and you are hearing its real engine. Grey in a jar means it is only an idea so far. Drop one in and listen to what it does to the sound."

**Honest risk.**
- **Cost.** It is the most expensive by far.
- **Engines.** It is only honest if every live organ runs a real engine. I confirmed one by file: Blood Compressor's, at Projects/Blood Compressor/plugin/ui/blood_compressor.js. I did not check the Action Potential, Crystal or Slime Mold engines for browser use.
- **Too few organs.** With two or three live, it may read as a menu with a costume, a chart of the list after all.
- **Gore.** The open torso is where body-horror could tip into shock that teaches nothing.

## 3. Which one I would make

It also supplies the one thing the maker and the challenger both said the face lacks: a body you feel, not a red tube. B is the second I would make, because it fronts the face's best piece.

## 2. Hook ideas

### A. Thread the snail (from Logarithmic Interface Scaling)

- **A person would say:** "You push a wire into the ear's spiral, and the voice comes back one band at a time."
- **Crossing:** the cochlea lays pitch out along its length so equal distance is an equal ratio; an implant's electrode spacing is a log fader's tick marks, set in tissue.
- **First five seconds:** a pink coiled snail, cut open, fills the frame. A voice is speaking but you hear only a dull pulse. The hand drags a thin wire into the opening. Each electrode that touches the wall lights its stretch of membrane and one band of the voice switches on: one contact is a buzzing rhythm, a few more and words start to surface. Pull back and the voice falls apart again.
- **Second beat, the science after the play:** a switch re-spaces the contacts evenly in hertz instead of evenly along the flesh. Most of them crowd into the top of the spiral and the speech collapses. That is the linear slider's "cramped into 2.6% of slider space" (`Logarithmic Interface Scaling.md:33`), heard.
- **Hook or proof:** the hook is the proof. Counting bands until words appear is the vocoder.
- **Caption a stranger needs:** "This is the inside of your ear, uncoiled a little. Low sounds live deep in the spiral, high sounds at the door. Push the wire in. Each contact gives back one slice of the voice."
- **Honest risks:** (1) It needs a speech recording; a mic prompt would kill the first five seconds, and I did not check the palace for a usable voice file. (2) Facts from my own knowledge, not the palace, to be verified before any caption states them: the place-to-pitch map is only roughly logarithmic, speech becomes intelligible at around four bands, and real insertion depth shifts the pitch map. (3) A cochlear implant is how real people hear. The grotesque here should be the strangeness of the anatomy, not the device or its users. (4) It builds an instrument the page lists but, as far as the hub caption says, nobody has built (`rich.json:62`), so it is a bigger make than a hook in front of existing proofs.
- **Would the ring have caught it?** Partly. Item 8 is on the home page itself, so any reader could reach "a cochlea." The log-ruler turn, where spacing is the lesson, came from this room. The ring file cannot tell me whether Logarithmic Interface Scaling is inside the ring: its 2-hop list is cut off alphabetically at "Four Pillars of Enchanted Agency …" (ring file line 17). Inferred from frontmatter: it mirrors Boundary-Crossing Instruments (`Logarithmic Interface Scaling.md:10-11`), which the home page also mirrors, so it is probably two hops away. Round one walked the ring and did not bring it back.

### B. Knock (from Categorizing Inharmonicity)

- **A person would say:** "There's a body under a sheet. Knock on it and tell me what you hit."
- **Crossing:** each family of inharmonicity is a different kind of tissue: bone a stiff bar (type 1, `Categorizing Inharmonicity.md:62`), a drum-tight skin or eardrum a membrane with Bessel partials (`:73`), a full gut the stochastic scatter, "noisy, woody, organic" (`:118`). The taxonomy becomes an anatomy you learn by ear.
- **First five seconds:** a draped shape, no labels. Tap anywhere. A skull knock rings bright and metallic; the belly gives a dull, scattered thud; a stretched patch of skin gongs. Where you tap changes which partials speak. Then the sheet comes off one region at a time, only where you guessed right.
- **Hook or proof:** stands in front. The proofs behind it would be the partial sets themselves, shown after.
- **Caption:** "Every part of a body rings its own way, because of its shape and what it is made of. Knock, listen, and guess what is underneath."
- **Honest risks:** it teaches modal resonance, which is none of the page's eight instruments, so it may belong on Categorizing Inharmonicity's face more than this one. The tissue-to-type mapping is my inference, and a doctor's real percussion tells organs apart mostly by damping and pitch, not partial spacing (my knowledge, unverified here). It could slide into a guessing game with the lesson left out.
- **Would the ring have caught it?** It could have. Categorizing Inharmonicity is in the ring at two hops (ring file line 17). So this is a within-cluster draw, the kind Swarm Weave counts as waste (`Projects/Swarm Weave.md:290-291`). Round one did not find it, but chance deserves little credit.

## 2. The hooks

### A. "Only stop one" — from Ozu

- **What a person would say:** "I can't add a note. I can only stop one, and that's where the groove is."

- **First five seconds:** one fixed frame, no rail. A row of cells, every one firing on every trigger, sixteen to the bar: a flat wall of hits, no rhythm at all. The hand can do one thing: press and hold on a cell. While you hold, it fires and its neighbours are pushed down and go quiet, drawn as outlines (the existing piece already draws what was silenced, `… — rich.json:149`). Lift, and the quiet lasts a moment before they come back. Two fingers or two holds and a syncopated pattern stands out of the wall. You hear the groove arrive entirely from holes.

### B. "The kicked tent" — from Dillard

- **What a person would say:** "It took everything out of her voice and I can still understand every word."
- **The move that made it:** the water bug passage her page quotes, the frog's "skin emptied and drooped… like a kicked tent", staged "with the same intensity as the tree with the lights in it" (`People/Annie Dillard.md:70`); "the beauty and the horror in the same frame, at the same time" (`:74`); positioning, not causing: "put myself in the path of its beam" (`:68`). Turned on instrument 8, the cochlear implant vocoder (`Projects/Biomechanical Synthesis.md:64`), a seed with no piece anywhere in the palace (grep for "cochlear" in `.md` finds only this page and one challenge note in `Auditory Illusions/`).
- **The mapping, and it is my inference, not the page's:** a vocoder keeps each band's envelope and throws away what was inside it. The envelope is the skin; the fine structure is the frog. Outside knowledge, unverified here: noise-vocoded speech stays intelligible at a handful of bands, and an implant has on the order of twenty electrodes. A maker must check both before captioning.
- **First five seconds:** a throat, drawn alive and lovely, a voice speaking one plain sentence on a loop; the throat swells with each syllable. No sliders. A waterline sits across the frame and the hand drags it. Above the line the voice is whole. Below it the insides are gone and the skin keeps moving: the same syllables, the same rhythm, filled with hiss. Drag slowly and you watch and hear the emptying travel through one sentence, both halves in frame at once. The words survive, and that survival is the unsettling part.
- **Hook or proof:** stands in front. The proof behind it is the filter bank: how many bands, what each envelope keeps, where the words stop surviving as you drop the count.
- **Caption:** "Above the line, a voice. Below it, only the shape of the voice: how loud each band is, moment to moment, with noise poured in. This is what a cochlear implant sends. Drag the line."
- **Honest risk:** her own blind spot, named on her page: "She may aestheticize suffering" (`:78`). A beautiful emptied throat can be horror consumed as pleasure, and here there is a second edge: a cochlear implant is a real thing real people hear through, and it gives hearing. The caption must say the hiss is a simulation of the principle, not of anyone's experience. She is also "thin on other people" (`:78`), which is the same gap. It needs a recorded voice, an asset the bundle doesn't have. And I can't hear whether the dragged crossfade sounds like emptying or like a wet/dry knob; if it is only a wet/dry knob, it's a fader with a picture.

### C. "The next room" — from their disagreement

Every so often a hard cut: same hallway, same sentence, fewer bands. Nothing is shown. The hand has one act, and it is the reader's own: click the door, and a straight cut puts you inside with the throat and the waterline (hook B), then the filter bank. The piece can say on its face how many cuts you sat through before you looked.
- **Hook or proof:** the hook is the held shot; the proof is the cut the reader chooses. That is Loudon's order, play first and science after, with the reader's curiosity making the join instead of a Next button.
- **Caption:** "Someone is talking in the next room. Each cut takes something out of the voice. Open the door when you want to see what."
- **Honest risk:** it is quiet, and the hand does almost nothing for a while; a reader may leave before the second cut. Thinning by cuts could imply implants degrade with time, which is false as far as I know; the caption must say it is a count of bands, not a course of anything. It doubles the build (B plus a room). And both blind spots at once: resignation dressed as wisdom outside the door, beautiful cruelty inside it.
