## 2. Three hooks

### A. Coyote's Door

**In a sentence:** "There's a circle of drummers that always falls into one beat, and a ruler that stops at zero. Drag one drummer past zero and the village splits into two camps playing each other's offbeat."

**The crossing.** Three things met:
- **The Lens's best result.** `The Lens/proofs/2026-11-03-bold-mandate/readings/Trickster-Kuramoto-Coupling-bold.md:13-19`: "The surveyor held up his ruler. It started at zero." and "Two camps, each locked, each the other's shadow."
- **The Kuramoto ladder.** `Kuramoto Coupling.md:160-166` lists rungs from `K_receive = 0` upward. My grep found no negative rung, but two long lines (148, 172) were not shown to me, so this is not fully checked.
- **Loudon's Zen story.** The spark is line 31 of the same reading: "the crowd stays ordered, but it can never collapse into one voice, because some of the listening pushes back." That is his annoying student kept in the sangha, as a sign in an equation. The match is my inference; he named the story, not this line.

**First five seconds.** Eight or so drummers in a ring, each a tick with its own pitch, drift and then lock into one beat. Under each is a small ruler from 0 ("deaf") to 2 ("obedient"). One door has no mark and a coyote's ear showing. The hand drags that drummer's marker left, off the end of the ruler. The ruler grows a new red stretch below zero, the ring pulls apart into two clusters on opposite sides, and the single beat becomes a call and answer.

**Hook or proof.** It stands in front of the proof. The caption turn is the Lens's whole claim: this knob is on neither page, it came out of reading one through the other. "Move the glass" then follows as the natural doubt: how do we know that was Coyote talking and not the same model in a hat? "Nearest on the graph" follows as where to point the glass next.

**Loudon's note.** It answers it directly. It is built from the one glass of five that held him, in its bold form. It also makes his own association playable: take the contrarian out and the room is peaceful and flat, put him back and it has to hold its order against something.

**Caption a stranger needs.** "Each dot is a drummer who listens to the others. The number is how much they give way. Below zero means: listens fully, moves the other way. Real: the model (Hong and Strogatz, 2011). Invented by the glass: the village, the surveyor, Coyote. The glass's own bend: laying that paper's sign onto this page's knob."

**Honest risk.**
- It is a Kuramoto toy, and could look like it belongs on Kuramoto's face. It only earns The Lens's face if the "neither page said this" turn is unmissable.
- The two-camp state needs a real simulation with a real mix of conformists and contrarians. One contrarian among eight may not split the ring; it has to be run and the numbers shown, not drawn.
- The mapping onto `K_receive` is flagged as the glass's own in its distortion check (line 39), and the deposit is a candidate, not canon.
- I can't judge whether the split is audible as call and answer; someone has to hear it.

### B. The Duppy Glass

**In a sentence:** "A line from a page plays into a beat-up tape machine, and it comes back as an echo in another page's voice, wetter and stranger with each repeat."

**The crossing.** Three pages met:
- **The Lens** calls itself "a Trickster device in the literal palace sense — intercept the expected signal (the text as written) at a threshold (the glass edge) and return it transformed" (`The Lens.md:168`, § In STIGMERGY).
- **Semantic Delay** is that device already built for voice: "a beat-up tape machine haunted by trickster spirits... Each spirit is a radio button" (`Projects/Semantic Delay.md:33`), and "A short 'room' keeps words close to their original meaning; a long 'hall' lets them drift" (line 57).
- **Dub Lineage** gives the gesture: "The mixing board is the instrument. The engineer plays." (`Dub Lineage.md:21`).

The find is that careful-to-bold is dry-to-wet, and the page's rule that "around it the literal text stays" (`The Lens.md:83`) is the dry signal never leaving the mix.

**First five seconds.** A tape loop turns. One sentence of the subject page types out on the left, dry and grey. The hand pushes one fader up. The sentence comes back a beat later on the right in the glass's colour, first as the careful reading, then, pushed further, as the bold one: Coyote's tale, or Ohm's equations. Each tap drops a little in level like a real echo. The spirit buttons are the five woken glasses. Sound: tape hiss and a delay throw on a click or spoken syllable. Voiced readings would be better but cost far more.

**Hook or proof.** The hook is the operation itself, so it could replace steps 1 to 3 of "move the glass". The blind test stays as the proof behind it. The three taps are real material: passage, careful reading and bold reading for each of five pairs (`The Lens/proofs/2026-11-03-bold-mandate/readings/`).

**Loudon's note.** A partial answer. It makes "bold beat careful" something the hand does, and it uses Trickster's own machine. It does not touch the Zen connection.

**Caption a stranger needs.** "Left: the page's own words, untouched. Right: the same passage read by another page. Fader down is a careful reading, up is that page speaking in its own voice. Both readings are real runs from 3 November. Nothing on the right was ever written into the page on the left."

**Honest risk.**
- It is one fader beside text. That is the shape he said to be suspicious of, and only the tape machine and the sound keep it out of that bin.
- There are two real points per glass, not a continuum. A smooth fader would imply in-between readings that don't exist, so it should be a three-position throw.
- Long readings don't fit an echo. They would need cutting, and a cut bold reading loses the voice that made bold win.

### C. The Burning Glass

**In a sentence:** "Hold the lens still over the grey valley and the sun comes to a point: where two pages really spark, the paper smokes and a line burns in between them."

**The crossing.** Three things met:
- **The page's own fire words.** "which pairs spark", "if it lights up, you have found a candidate typed link" (`The Lens.md:114,118`).
- **The hero.** A handle-less glass lying on a grey engraved valley, colour only under it (`The Lens/The Lens — face.json:3-4`).
- **The sun.** The pillars are "cast by the light of curiosity" (`Hyperdimensional Prism.md:47`), whose open question is "What is the 'light of curiosity' in formal terms?" (line 114). So curiosity is the sun, the glass page focuses it, and the burn is the edge "shown *before the link exists*".

The sting comes from `The Lens/The Lens — tuning.md:44`: "The spark ran high. Nine of ten scored 4 or more." A glass that sets everything alight is not measuring anything.

**First five seconds.** The hero engraving fills the frame, with the ten real lensings as ten small landmarks. The hand drags the glass; colour blooms under it as in the hero. Hold still over a landmark and a bright point tightens, with a faint crackle. At a score of 4 or more it scorches a brown line to the glass's home page; at 3 it only warms. Within a minute the reader has burned nine of ten and the valley is covered in scorch lines.

**Hook or proof.** The hook is the proof of the page's open problem: the spark score can't yet be trusted. It leads straight into "nearest on the graph", which asks the same question of distance.

**Loudon's note.** It doesn't answer it. It is about the meter, not the voice, and no glass speaks.

**Caption a stranger needs.** "Each mark is one real reading of one page through another, scored 1 to 5 by the reader itself. 4 or more burns a line: a link someone could propose. Nine of these ten burned. That is the problem, not the result."

**Honest risk.**
- It is the nearest of the three to an optical pun, and fire is pretty enough to be decoration.
- The burn makes a self-scored 4 look like a discovery, the opposite of the page's honesty, unless the "everything burns" turn lands.
- Ten points is thin. The scores on record are eight 4s, one 5 and one 3, so there is almost no variation to show.

## 2. Hook ideas

### A. Turn It Round

- **What a person would say:** "It isn't the same looking back."
- **Where it was hiding:** `The Lens.md:124`: "A lensing has an **arrow:** A-through-B ≠ B-through-A (Granular *through* Kuramoto reads grains as oscillators; Kuramoto *through* Granular reads sync as texture)... they swap." Neither made piece's caption nor the challenge mentions the arrow, so the claim has no piece (inferred from those two files).
- **First five seconds:** one round glass with a name on each face. Press start and you hear a scattered patter of grains; lean on the glass and the patter pulls into a pulse. That is grains read as oscillators. Flick the glass over like a coin. The same material is now a held cloud with no beat, and leaning on it turns it from rough to smooth. That is sync read as texture. The hand flips and leans; the ear gets the answer before any text.
- **Hook or proof:** the hook is the proof. If the two sides sound alike, the arrow fails for this pair, and the piece should say so.
- **Caption a stranger needs:** "One glass, two sides. Granular Synthesis seen through Kuramoto Coupling, then flipped. Same two pages, different sound. Both sides were built by hand from one line of the page, not from a woken reading."
- **Honest risk:** the two directions may collapse into one sound in a lazy build, and I cannot hear to check. The page gives each direction only a six-word gloss, so the maker would be inventing most of both. It shows one pair only.

### B. The Amber Glass

Then a rack of spectacles, each named for a page. Put on Ohm's Law and the tributaries become branches meeting at a node (his reading says "Every voice hangs off that node as a branch", `Ohm-s-Law-Kuramoto-Coupling-bold.md:9`). Move it and the picture changes with the place. One frame has no glass in it, and the valley stays grey, since "a lensing that finds nothing is a valid result" (`The Lens.md:94`).
