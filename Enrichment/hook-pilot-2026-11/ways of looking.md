# Ways of looking

**Loudon, 2026-11-10:**

> I'd like this approach to be one of many, less rules and more options

**Loudon, 2026-11-10:**

> I am aiming for diversity when we establish rules we over-constrain. I know that this is difficult, but it is our challenge.

Everything below is the working session's reading of one day, 2026-11-10, on five pages. It is not a procedure, a brief, a ranking or a gate. Nobody is asked to use any of it. The words inside the quote marks are the session's, said to Loudon in the conversation and copied here at the close; they are not his, and he ruled on none of them.

## The deck, as the session offered it

Said right after his first sentence above. The session had turned his "show a specific interesting example of a broad concept" into the first question of the makers' brief, and took that back:

> The makers' brief stops being a procedure and becomes a deck: a handful of ways of looking, each one card, each with one example of what it found here. A maker picks one, draws one, or ignores them all. Yours goes in as a card, in your words. So far the deck would hold:
>
> - **One case of the broad idea.** Kuramoto as neurons firing; the proof widens from there. (Yours.)
> - **Walk the neighbors.** A painter's page gave the synth its ruled line.
> - **Take another seat.** Be the artery wall, not the person watching it.
> - **Harvest the verbs.** What is the page already describing without naming it?
> - **A random room.** A page drawn by chance, the draw accepted.
> - **An artist visits.** Eno, Cage, or whoever the coin sends.
> - **Read what the page already made, and what you said back.**

> New cards get added as makers find new ways.

His own card, in his words, is statement (a) in [[Loudon's words — 2026-11-10]].

## What each way of looking tended to find, as the session said it

Said when all twenty idea agents were back, about fifty-five ideas across the five pages. The reports themselves are parked beside this file, one per page (`hook ideas — <Page>.md`).

> - **Chance** gave the furthest ideas, and paid best when the drawn page was far away: a slider tutorial on the body-horror page, a film director on the Stoics. It wasted about one draw in three by landing inside the page's own neighborhood.
> - **The verb harvest** found what the page says and its face ignores, and found the empty sections.
> - **Artists** brought refusals. Ozu refused the Next button. Cage refused the word "home." Dillard called the meter toggle "a flinch into numbers." The coin-drawn artist usually gave more than the one chosen for fit, which mostly confirmed the page.
> - **Walking the neighbors** gave the most immediately playful scenes.
>
> Several agents said the same uncomfortable thing: none of them could see or hear anything. Every sound here is imagined.

And, of the faults the agents found on existing faces: "These came mostly from the artist visits, which turned out better at questioning a face than at decorating it."

## What this is not

It is one round, twenty read-only agents, five pages, read back by the session that briefed them. The session said of its own briefs that day: "Same-shaped questions return same-shaped answers." A comparison drawn from them carries that shape. Loudon then chose directions for four pages, some named from the spread and some in his own images; his picks are statement (d) in the same file.
