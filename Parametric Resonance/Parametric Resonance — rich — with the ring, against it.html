With the ring, against it

The page asks where the pump's phase went, since the stability chart never mentions it. Four steps: a ring watched once per cycle on the pump's clock; the pump switched on at twice the rate, where two lines appear; the pump at the wrong rate; and the page's own test, a ring started on the line that fades. The same 220 Hz resonator as the threshold piece, pumped at three times its threshold depth.

The ring, strobed

Look at the ring once per cycle, always at the same moment of the pump's clock. The pump is off; only its clock runs, at 440 Hz. A ring at exactly half that rate shows up in the same place at every look: its phase stands still and only its size shrinks. The dot's angle is the ring's phase; its distance from the middle is its size in decibels. Strike it at any phase. They all fade alike.

Pump at twice the rate

Switch the pump on: the spring stiffens and slackens by ε at 440 Hz. Two lines appear, worked out from one pump period of the equation before anything sounds. On the amber line a ring grows; on the indigo line it fades, faster than with no pump at all. Strike anywhere off the indigo line and watch the dot swing onto the amber one and ride out. Turn the dial: you change where the ring starts, not whether a way out exists.

ẍ + 2ζω₀ẋ + ω₀²(1 + ε cos 2ω₀t) x = 0,   ε = 3εc = 0.018
Follow every starting state through one pump period and you get a 2 × 2 matrix. Its two eigenvectors are the lines; its two eigenvalues are how much a ring on each line grows or shrinks per period. The stability chart keeps only the eigenvalues. The lines, the phase, are what it leaves out.

The wrong rate

Same depth, same ring, but run the pump at the ring's own rate, or at one and a half times it. Look at the lines. At once the rate, the two lines are both indigo: both fade. At one and a half there are no lines at all, the dot just spirals in. Only at twice the rate does a growing direction exist at this depth.

Pumping works near twice the natural rate divided by a whole number. n = 2, pumping at the ring's own rate, is the second tongue: real, but it needs a far deeper pump than this one, so at ε = 0.018 its lines both fade. 1.5 × is in no tongue at all.

Start on the fading line

The page's test: "Start exactly on the decaying mode and you sit in an unstable tongue and still decay (briefly), until noise leaks you onto the growing direction." Strike the ring exactly on the indigo line, then exactly on the amber one, and compare. Then make the room noisier and strike on indigo again.

On the fading line the pump takes energy out, on top of the damping, so the ring falls faster than if the pump were off. Nothing in the equation moves it off that line; only noise does. The noisier the room, the sooner the growing part takes over.
