Five voices, coupled

The mirror says each oscillator’s conatus is its natural frequency, and that coupling “doesn’t destroy conatus; it harmonizes” it, so that each voice is “amplified, not overridden.” That is an analogy, and an analogy can’t be proved. What can be checked is whether it holds inside the model it names. Five voices near 220 Hz, each with a pitch of its own. The palace’s picture of coupling comes first, as its own Kuramoto page draws it: too little and nothing cooperates, too much and one voice dominates. The last step measures both. This section’s sentence: “amplified, not overridden.” Overridden: while the crowd is locked, a voice’s sounding pitch is more than 0.1 Hz from its own. This follows from “locked”: every voice sounds the crowd’s pitch, so any voice whose own pitch isn’t the crowd’s is overridden; the rule can’t fail once the crowd locks. Amplified: a level rises by more than 1 dB from apart to locked at twice the lock. Per voice it can’t: each voice’s amplitude is fixed in this model (GAIN/5), so only the sum of the five can show it.

The Kuramoto page’s line, carried here because the mirror cites that model: “K too high → one oscillator dominates.” One voice dominates: the crowd locks, and its pitch lands within 0.1 Hz of one voice’s own pitch while the others move to it. Sameness: the crowd locks and its phases bunch to within 5° of each other (standard spread). The even arm is that line as drawn: the line fails if at twenty times its lock point no voice dominates. The anchor arm is the control: E stops listening (K_receive = 0) and pulls four times as hard (K_send = 4), the Kuramoto page’s own recipe for an anchor. If the rule doesn’t read domination there, the rule is broken, not the line.

One thing this test can’t do. In the even arm the locked pitch is the plain average of the five, by algebra: each pair’s pulls are equal and opposite, so they cancel in the sum. The run shows that; it couldn’t come out otherwise. What could have come out otherwise is the line’s picture of it.
