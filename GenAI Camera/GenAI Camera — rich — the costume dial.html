The costume dial: what depth's weight does to the cloth

The section's rule, that depth locks the surface and pose frees it, and its dial from 0.0 to 0.8, built up from the two plates the camera sends. Four steps: the plates, pose alone with the body's outline laid over it, the dial turned, and last a count of the light cloth each drawing grows beyond its body. The drawings are the bundle's own sweeps, renders 021 and 022: pose held at 0.9, denoise 0.9, seed 21, only depth's weight changing.

(cloth beyond the body) = (the light pixels of the drawing that fall outside the body’s outline, widened by four pixels, and outside what is set aside) ÷ (the body’s own area). Light means brighter than 128 and 60 levels above the cell’s background. The body is the depth plate brighter than 40 of 255. Set aside, grey on the drawing: the sorceress’s hair and hood (above her shoulders and her right arm), the barbarian’s sword (the strip right of his hand), and the cell’s 8-pixel margin, where the paper’s own edge shows. It counts white robes, fur and drips; it cannot see black cloth on a dark ground.
