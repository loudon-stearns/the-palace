How do a room's dimensions and its size work together?

The page's clap experiment, built up from one wall. Four steps, one idea each: an echo is late by its path, six walls write the room's dimensions in time, every echo together makes the decay, and last the test of what size does and what shape does. Clap with the button; after the first clap, every change claps again.

t = d ⁄ c,   c = 343 m/s
(how late a reflection arrives) = (how far it travelled) ÷ (the speed of sound). A wall x metres from you is a path of 2x: the sound goes there and comes back.
RT60 = 0.161 · V ⁄ A,   A = Σ Sᵢ · αᵢ
(seconds for the sound to fall 60 dB) = 0.161 × (the room's volume, m³) ÷ (its absorption: each surface's area times the share of sound it soaks up, added together). Make every side k times longer and V grows k³ while A grows k², so the decay grows k times.
The room is a shoebox with flat, mirror-like surfaces, one α for every surface and every frequency, and you a point half a metre from your own hands, 1.5 m up (lower when the ceiling is). Every image source is counted out to about 600,000 reflections (Allen and Berkley's method); after that a noise tail carries on the decay the images measured, and the sound is cut at 6 s. The darkening of the tail with time is a sound choice the single α does not contain. Real rooms scatter, so their late sound is nearer the even, diffuse field Sabine assumes than this bare box's.
