One kick, two views

The demonstration in the notes, built: one kick drum, made here from a falling sine and a click, drawn twice from the same samples. Each view answers one question plainly. The last step asks whether each one really can't answer the other's question. Nothing plays until you press Play.

The third view · a spectrogram
Before the first trial, the rule. Twenty-four silent trials, six in each of four arms, shuffled together. Each trial draws a fresh kick: its fundamental at random from 50, 55, 60, 65 or 70 Hz, and its onset at random from 0, 100, 200, 300 or 400 ms. You see one view only and answer one question, with five choices.

The four arms: waveform → fundamental and spectrum → onset, the two the notes say can't be done; waveform → onset and spectrum → fundamental, the two they say are plain.

Chance is 1 in 5 on every trial. Per arm: 4 or more right of 6 means the view told you (a guesser gets there 1.7% of the time); 2 or fewer is what guessing looks like (a guesser lands there 90.1% of the time); 3 is undecided (8.2%). The notes say 60 Hz and time 0. Here those are each one choice in five, drawn like the others, so answering 60 and 0 every time scores the same as guessing. You get no feedback until the end. The trials are silent, because your ear would answer the pitch question by itself.
