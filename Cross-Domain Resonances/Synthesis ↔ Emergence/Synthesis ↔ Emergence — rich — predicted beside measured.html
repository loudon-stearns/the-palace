Predicted beside measured — where the formula stops

The section says FM is deterministic but you can't predict the sound from its parameters alone. This piece puts a prediction made from the parameters beside a measurement of the sound, first for the page's two-sine pair, then for the DX7's feedback loop, one sine feeding back on itself, and looks for the place where prediction really fails. The last step is the test.

sin( ωct + I · sin ωmt ) = Σk Jk(I) · sin( (ωc + k ωm) t )
(the pair's sound) = the sum, over every whole number k, of (the k-th Bessel function, at the depth) × (a sine at the carrier plus k modulators). Two numbers in, every partial out, with no sound rendered.
y = sin( ωt + β · y )  ⇒  y = Σn≥1 ( 2 / nβ ) · Jn(nβ) · sin nωt,   while β ≤ 1
(a sine whose own output pushes its phase, as hard as β) = a sum of harmonics, the n-th as loud as 2·Jₙ(nβ)/(nβ). It is Kepler's equation for where a planet is on its orbit, with β in the place of the orbit's eccentricity, and this is the series Bessel wrote for it. Past β = 1 the equation has three answers at some instants, and the series no longer describes the sound.
The pair is measured at 48 kHz on its 200 Hz grid, the loop at 192 kHz on its 200 Hz note, each partial by an exact sum over whole cycles; at 192 kHz the loop's one-step delay sits close enough to the equation for the series to be a fair test. What repeats, the exponent and the twins are read at 48 kHz on a 200 Hz note. For the DX7's loop the exponent is negative to β 3.90, mixed in a narrow band, and positive at every β tested from 4.1; for a plain loop that feeds back only its last output it is positive from about 2.45. Both edges move a little with the sample rate and the pitch. Past the edge the exact figures differ from one JavaScript engine to the next (the same code in Node and in Chromium, on one Mac, disagrees), since the last bit of the arithmetic decides them; that is the point. The loop averages its last two outputs, as the DX7 does; β = π is the DX7's feedback 7, and FM Synthesis's feedback piece walks that loop to its edge.
