Two sines, one rule — and is it a bell?

The section's own pair: a carrier at 400 Hz and a modulator at 200 Hz. The walk is the demonstration the page scripts under Teaching Notes, one sine, then the other, then the two together, then the rule between them, and it ends on the section's claim that depth 5 sounds like a bell. Every line drawn below is measured from the sound itself, not drawn from a formula.

(what you hear) = a sine at the carrier's speed, whose phase is pushed back and forth by the modulator, as hard as the depth I says. At I = 0 nothing pushes, and the modulator is never heard at all.
f = fc ± n · fm,   n = 0, 1, 2, …
The partials sit at the carrier plus and minus whole numbers of the modulator; one that lands below 0 Hz folds back up. At 400 and 200 every one of them is a whole number of 200 Hz, whatever the depth.
The measurement renders the same engine you hear, offline at 48 kHz, and reads each partial by an exact sum over whole cycles of the grid. Whether a sound is brass or a bell is the ear's call; what the piece can measure is where the partials sit.
