# Synthesis ↔ Emergence — rich — challenge

Read against the page as it stands in the worktree (`Cross-Domain Resonances/Synthesis ↔ Emergence.md`, unchanged by the maker's commit d3c55a8b) and that commit's body, the only account taken of how the face was made. Nothing in a piece, the checks file or the page was changed.

One finding runs under everything below. The second piece's feedback loop is the DX7's as Dexed models it: it feeds back the **average of its last two outputs**. Everything the maker found about feedback holds for that loop. A plain loop that feeds back only its last output, y[n] = sin(θ[n] + β·y[n−1]), behaves differently: it turns chaotic from β ≈ 2.45, inside the DX7's range. The page's Advanced Applications names no particular loop ("Oscillator modulates its own frequency"). So two of the offers are true of one loop and not of feedback FM in general, and should say which loop they mean.

## Offers

1. **The Technical Side, the 400/200 bell — confirmed.** At c : m = 2 : 1 every sideband 400 ± 200n is a multiple of 200 Hz, so the spectrum is harmonic at every depth by construction. The measured 100.0000 % on the grid and the ten filled slots at depth 5 recompute. Is "like a bell" a timbre word or a spectrum claim? A timbre word. But the page itself says what makes a bell's timbre: "Bells: Metal resonance creates inharmonic partials (like FM ratios)". So by the page's own account a 2 : 1 pair can't sound like one at any depth. The side point holds too: n = −1 lands on 200 Hz, the modulator's own frequency, and n = −2 on 0 Hz, which is silent with a sine-phase carrier. One caution on the suggested wording: 1 : 1.4 is Chowning's bell ratio, but his bell also struck and decayed, amplitude and index together. A sustained 1 : 1.4 tone is more clang than bell, so "rings like a bell" wants "struck and left to ring" or an ear.
2. **Teaching Notes, depth 0 is one line — confirmed.** At depth 0, FM gives the carrier alone (measured: one partial, 400 Hz). "Just addition" is a teaching shorthand that teaches the wrong picture, because in two-operator FM the modulator's level is the depth, so at 0 it is silent. Addition is a different patch, both operators as carriers (the DX7's algorithm 32 at feedback 0). Part 1 step 2 says the same thing, and the split the offer suggests is right.
3. **The Deeper Principle — confirmed in its numbers, overstated in its frame, and in one place it needs the loop named.** The pair matches Bessel to 8.41e-12 dB, the series holds to 1.22 dB rms for β ≤ 1, and it is off by 10.0 / 14.3 / 15.8 dB rms at 1.5 / 2 / π. All of these recompute. But the page's next line is "Must calculate or experience it", and Bessel is a calculation. So the page's "predict" means "know without calculating", and the pair doesn't refute it. What the pair refutes is the weather analogy: weather is unpredictable even by calculation, and the pair has a closed form. The maker's suggested text already lands there ("what it can't tell you is what you'll hear it as"), so the first sentence of the offer should match it rather than say the sentence "fails". The two stages of feedback hold for the DX7's averaged loop. The second stage starts at β ≈ 3.93–4.1 (feedback 7.32–7.38), not "about 4.4". For a plain loop it starts at ≈ 2.45.
4. **Advanced Applications, feedback chaos only past the DX7's range — confirmed for the DX7's averaged loop, disputed as written.**
   - **The averaged loop.** The Lyapunov exponent is negative at every β to 3.90, at 48 kHz on 200 Hz. The first positive value is at 3.93 (fb 7.32). It is positive at 9 of 10 settings in 4.00–4.09 and at all 10 in every 0.1 band from 4.1 to 4.8. The same edge holds at 44.1, 96 and 192 kHz (3.92–3.96) and at 100 and 800 Hz (3.96, 3.88). At 1600 Hz it drops to 3.74 (fb 7.25), still above 7. So Yamaha's 0–7 is orderly, and the maker's "converges up to 3.85" is right.
   - **The plain loop.** y[n] = sin(θ[n] + β·y[n−1]) has a positive exponent from β 2.45 (fb 6.64) and at every β tested from 2.6. Across rates and pitches the edge sits between 2.34 and 2.56. At β = π the exponent is about +0.12 per sample, and only 78.5 % of the loop repeats, which matches the FM Synthesis face's own figure for the plain loop at 7.
   - **The twins can't see the plain loop's chaos.** I nudged twins by 1e-9 or 1e-3 at 24 points in a cycle. None stayed apart at any β up to 4.4, because the stretch near ±1 crushes every difference below the last bit. The maker's "a disturbance of a billionth shrinks below 1e-12 within 20 ms at every β from 0.05 to 3.85" is true of the averaged loop. A twin test cannot by itself establish "the opposite of chaotic".
   - **Suggested.** Keep the page's "chaotic behavior" and say which loop: "In the DX7's loop, which feeds back the average of its last two outputs, a little feedback leans the tone toward a sawtooth and it stays orderly to the top setting; chaos begins just past it. A plain loop that feeds back only its last output turns chaotic sooner, from a push of about 2.5 radians, inside the DX7's range."
   - **The two faces' scales.** The two faces use the same scale, β = π·2^(fb−7), and the same Dexed loop, so feedback 7 is β = π ≈ 3.14. FM Synthesis's 7.3 / 7.35 / 7.4 are β 3.87 / 4.00 / 4.15. This face's 3.85 / 3.9 / 4.4 are fb 7.29 / 7.31 / 7.49. They agree on the onset, about 7.3. They differ on where chaos is near-universal (7.4 against 7.5), and the exponent sides with 7.4.
5. **Common Student Discoveries, 1.0 vs 1.01 — confirmed.** The pair has no state to be sensitive with. Its output is an explicit function of time, so a 1e-9-cycle nudge to the modulator's phase moves the output by at most I·2π·1e-9 = 3.14e-8 at depth 5, and stays that size (recomputes). What 1.01 changes is a parameter near a whole-number ratio. Sideband pairs land 0.02·fc apart and beat, at 4.4 Hz at 220 Hz (0.02 × 220). "1.0 vs 1.01 = totally different spectrum" is itself loose, since the lines barely move and the sound gains a slow beat. The page's general sentence, "Non-linear systems are sensitive to initial conditions", is also false as a universal, because most non-linear systems are not chaotic. The offer's wording fixes both.

## The face as a whole

The two pieces make the page's idea felt, and better than the page does in two places. The first lets you hear the modulator vanish at depth 0, which is the whole of "interaction, not addition" in one click. The second turns "you can't predict it" from a slogan into a ladder: a formula that works, then one that stops working, then a loop that only running tells you, then a loop that running tells you about one copy only. The face isn't busy. Two walks, each with one test at the end, is the right weight for a page this long.

Its weakness is that it lets one loop stand for "feedback FM". The second piece's last rung is real, but it sits higher than it needs to, and it is called the only one. A plain loop reaches that rung inside the DX7's range, and the page's own Max exercise builds a loop that is neither of these. Name the loop, and the ladder is a true account of where emergence turns into chaos, which is the distinction the page blurs ("sensitive to initial conditions") and that this face is in a position to teach.
