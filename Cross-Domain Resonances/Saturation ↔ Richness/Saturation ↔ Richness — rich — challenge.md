## The pieces

### the bend and the lean · The Technical Side — **remake**

1. **Test or decoration?** The walk up is a test of Common Misunderstandings' EQ line, and it holds: a +12 dB bell at 400, 600 or 1000 Hz moves the sine by 1.470, 0.530, 0.171 dB (the bell's own gain there, to 0.001 dB) and adds nothing above −272 dB; on a sawtooth it lifts the line under it by 12.00 dB. The symmetric drive, the lean, and the split into bend and lean are exact (evens from tanh, drive 0.25 to 8, at or below −317 dB; the bend's evens −317, the lean's odds −329). The last step is meant to test the warmth claim, and here it has a hole. The even set is 2nd, 4th, 6th and the odd set 3rd, 5th, 7th at the same levels, so the even set always sits lower: its added lines centre near harmonic 3.1, the odd set’s near harmonic 4.1 (amplitude-weighted). A listener who hears "warmer" as "darker" will pick the even one with parity doing nothing. The test can come out "as the page says" when the page is wrong. That is the one thing a test must not do.
2. **Faithful?** Mostly. Step 4 says "a curve that is symmetric makes none, however hard you drive it". That is true of the sine on screen, not of curves in general: a symmetric tanh at drive 3, fed a wave that already has a 2nd (0.7 of the note, 0.3 of the 2nd), puts out a new 4th at −15.7 dB FS and a new 6th at −25.2. Any wave with only odd harmonics stays odd (−319 dB). A vocal, a guitar, a sawtooth carry evens.
3. **Walks up?** Yes: one sine, one line; the EQ; the drive; the lean; the test last.

Runs clean, sound only on a click, hushes through the kit, nothing hidden in the DOM. The pair is matched in K-weighted loudness to 0.000 LU; by A-weighting the residual is at most 0.22 dB (at −20 dB, 160 Hz), too small to be a cue. Order and note are random per trial and kept in a closure; curves and spectra are drawn only after the tenth answer; the chance (8 or more, 1 in 18) is in the step's text before Begin.

**What to change:** (a) Take brightness out of the answer. Half the ten pairs, chosen at random and said so before the first, use the 4th, 6th and 8th against the 3rd, 5th and 7th, so the even set is the brighter one there. Score parity over all ten and report the two halves. If evenness is warmth, the even one wins both halves; if it is only darkness, it flips. Say "as the page says" only when both halves agree. (b) In step 4 and the caption, say "on a sine" or "on a wave with no even harmonics of its own": "a symmetric curve makes no even harmonic from a sine, however hard you drive it".

### The vocal the nominator asked for

The nomination asked for "one vocal in a mix"; the maker used a synthesized sine bass. It matters to the claim in two ways. It is the most favourable source for "fuller": what light saturation adds to a sine bass is what lets small speakers play it at all, so a "light wins" here says less about a vocal than it seems to. And a vocal carries even harmonics, so on a vocal a symmetric curve makes evens too (above), which is the case offer 1's rewording leaves out. The piece is faithful to the page's assignment (synth is allowed); it should not be read as the page's general claim, and the caption fix (c) says so.

## The offers

1. **The Technical Side, claim-change — confirmed for a sine, disputed as worded.** The numbers hold: a symmetric curve makes no even harmonic from a sine at any drive (my FFT: at or below −317 dB, drive 0.25 to 8, at three input levels), and bend and lean split odd from even exactly. But "evens come only from a curve that leans" is true only for inputs with no evens. A symmetric curve fed a wave that has a 2nd makes a new 4th and 6th (−15.7 and −25.2 dB FS at drive 3). The suggested rewording would put a sine-only result into the page as a general one; it needs "on a pure tone" or should drop "only". **Tape:** the MOL definition is now checked against a primary source. RMGI's LPR35 technical data, §2.1: "MOL315/3, MOL1/3 (Maximum Output Level): Output level at 315 Hz (9.53 cm/s) or 1 kHz (19.05 cm/s) relative to reference level RL IEC, with a third harmonic distortion factor of 3 % or THD = −30.5 dB." The offer should cite that, not Wikipedia. What the sheet doesn't give is any 2nd-harmonic figure, so "tape is mostly odd" is still an inference from how the trade specifies it, not a measurement. The rewording states it as fact ("as tape, transformers and many solid-state clippers mostly do"); hedge it.
2. **Common Misunderstandings, no-claim-edit — numbers confirmed, two readings disputed, kind disputed.** At drive 3, 200 and 250 Hz at 0.5 each: harmonics of either note −31.4 dB re the notes, tones of neither −10.7 dB. Confirmed. But (a) the loudest tones of neither are four, not three: 150, 300, 650 and 700 Hz, each −17.0 dB FS, with no lean. The maker's list cut a four-way tie at three, and so "with a lean, 150 Hz, below both notes, is among the loudest" reads as if the lean made it. It didn't. 150 Hz is there without a lean, at the same level. What the lean adds is 50 Hz (−15.3) and 450 Hz (−15.4). (b) The gap depends on drive: 12.6 dB at drive 0.1 to 0.25 (third order alone: 10·log₁₀ 18 = 12.55), 14.2 at 1, 20.6 at 3, 17.7 at 4.5, 12.3 at 8; with lean 0.12, 9.4 to 17.4. "−10.7 against −31.4" is drive 3's number; say so, or give the range. (c) The suggested clause adds a claim the page doesn't make (what saturation does to a chord), so it belongs with claim-changes and the Deposit map, not with no-claim edits. A small point: the maker's "harmonics of either note" counts 1000 Hz (5 × 200 = 4 × 250), which is also an intermodulation product.
3. **Teaching the Resonance, no-claim-edit — confirmed.** Unmatched, light, medium and heavy are louder than clean by 0.90, 5.08, 11.73 dB (K-weighted, ungated); 0.90, 5.09, 11.75 gated (BS.1770-4 gates); 0.89, 5.03, 11.51 by RMS; 0.93, 5.94, 15.92 A-weighted. After matching, the stems agree in K-loudness, as built. The finding is real, and the residual above (heavy +4.2 dB A-weighted after K-matching) makes the suggested "each version turned to the same loudness" stronger, not weaker: what "the same loudness" means needs saying.

The chance figures all hold: P(8 or more of 10 | ½) = 56/1024 = 0.0547, 1 in 18.3; 9 or more, 1 in 93.1; all ten, 1 in 1024; one exact order of four, 1 in 24.

## The face as a whole

The face makes the page's technical idea felt, not just busy. The walk from one sine through the EQ to the bend and the lean is the clearest thing on it: you watch the EQ fail to make a line and the curve make one, and the split into bend and lean turns "even = warmth" from a slogan into a property of a shape. Both blind tests are honestly built. Nothing in the DOM gives them away, the chance is said before the first trial, and both can fail. What keeps them from being the page's tests is that each answers a slightly easier question than the page asks. The warmth test can't tell evenness from darkness, and the four-versions test scores hearing the steps but not the keep the page's students made. Both are one-round fixes. The life half of the page has no piece, and the manifest says why; that is the right call. I could not hear any of it; the mix, the warmth and whether light sits better are still waiting for Loudon's ear.
