## Remove one voice · The Structural Identity — **remake**

**Does it test or decorate?** Step 4 tests. Steps 1 to 3 measure, carefully, but step 3 calls two of its numbers the test's teeth, and neither can fail.

- *Rebuild under 80%.* For any line the engine accepts as written for the cantus, the most of it the cantus can give back is fixed by the rules, not found. Slide: a strong beat matched to the next at one fixed interval is a similar move. The search demands at least 60% contrary moves, which leaves at most 4 similar ones, so by counting alone at most 8 of 11 strong beats (72.7%) on Fux's cantus and 8 of 12 (66.7%) on the C major one. Mirror: the opening fifth or octave, the major sixth into the cadence and the closing octave pin the axis, and on these two canti at most 8 of 11 (72.7%) and 8 of 12 (66.7%) strong beats can sit on any mirror axis and stay consonant, whatever the melody. 80% needs 9 and 10. ## Offers

**Offer 1, The Technical Side, no-claim-edit. Disputed.** "Dissonances resolve on strong beats" already reads as second species: the dissonance passes on the weak beat, and the resolution lands on the strong one. Fourth species is the other way round, with the dissonance on the strong beat resolving on the weak one, so the sentence does not read as fourth species either. The proposed text, "dissonances fall on weak beats and pass by step into a consonance on the strong beat", narrows two-voice counterpoint to second species. It would then contradict the page's own Advanced Applications ("Fourth species: Suspensions") and its Bach and Inventions examples. That is a claim-change, not a clarity edit. The numbers are right (248 over 84 lines, 2.95 a line, all on weak beats), but they hold by construction, since the engine admits no other dissonance, so they show nothing about the page. If any edit is wanted, Part 2's own words do it without narrowing: "dissonances resolve by step, into a consonance on the strong beat."

**Offer 2, The Structural Identity, claim-change. Disputed.** The numbers reproduce: tenths narrow 1.0, written 0.723, octaves 0; contradictions 0 against 0.192 and 0.196; rebuild at most 0.5 against 1.0. The reading does not hold. First, ask what kind of claim the row makes. It says what happens to counterpoint when a voice is removed. It does not say that only co-designed lines lose harmony. Under the harmonic reading the row holds for the written line: it narrows the chord on 72.3% of strong beats, and removal loses that. Second, the two facts the offer rests on hold by the rules, not by measurement (see the remake above). Third, the proposed row, "Remove one voice → the one left can't rebuild it", carries only one of the two facts. It is just as true of the reordered line (rebuild at most 0.636) and of the line written for another cantus (at most 0.545), so it doesn't single out co-design either, which is the objection the offer brings against the current row. The page's own reading is felt ("the other feels naked"), and the blind test that could speak to it has had no listener. Leave the row as it stands until someone sits "apart".

**Offer 3, The Deeper Principle, claim-change. Confirmed, with one correction.** 19.2% reproduces. The offer's "matched move for move" is wrong: the reordered line matches the written line's count of contrary and similar moves, not the moves themselves. The sequences are identical in 1 of 84 pairs, and 4.57 moves differ on average. Say "matched in count". The finding holds and gets stronger with the third arm. The line written for another cantus has much less contrary motion (direction −0.114 against −0.452) and contradicts at almost the same rate (19.6% against 19.2%). Contrary motion doesn't move the contradiction rate; consonance on the strong beats does. The proposed sentence ("contrary motion keeps the two voices independent, and consonance on the strong beats locks them together") is right in Fux's terms, and the kind is right: it changes the mechanism the page asserts.

**Offer 4, The Deeper Principle, claim-change. Confirmed.** Fux forbids parallel fifths and octaves and allows parallel thirds and sixths in short runs, so "Counterpoint avoids: Parallel motion" is too broad, and the edit corrects a factual statement. The evidence that carries it is the rulebook, though, not the measure. The tenths' 100% narrowing is arithmetic, as the piece itself says, so "tenths support fully" should be credited to the rule, not to the count. The 100% rebuild by slide is by construction. The offer could also name Common Struggles ("My voices keep moving in parallel → They're not supporting, just doubling"): "doubling" fits octaves, not tenths, so that line needs the same narrowing.
