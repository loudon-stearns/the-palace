### Where this stands

Dealt by [[The Daily Page]] on 2026-11-03. The verdict is **tune**, and the vector was tuned and joined that day. In the afternoon sit Loudon added a last clause: notice when the better resolution is listening to each other. In the evening he took a new opening and, shown both, said "Join them". The page used to say that precision and flow are the same question at every scale. Now it says a deviation from the grid can carry intent: a lean chosen by someone who knows the standard well enough to step just outside it. That intent speaks at every span at once (beat, bar, phrase, form) and in the voice of the material it moves, and the better resolution is sometimes players listening to each other. The body still argues the old thesis. On 2026-11-08 the page also took a link to [[Adopt the Craft, Author the Seam]], and Loudon's reframe: we hear change in timing better than absolute distance, as relative pitch is easier than absolute pitch, so a quantize grid measures what we hear worst. A rich face already stands at proposal stage, with its own *mistake or feel* piece; the morning's roughs are to be reconciled with it, not laid beside it. The baton carries the rewrite, two small fact fixes, the page's own wish for a real sample matrix with timbre as a variable, and Loudon's research question: is a drummer's error lopsided, is feel the shifting of that spread, and do listeners hear timing change more easily than fixed error, and does that come with training or with being human?

### 2026-11-08 — Hear the Lift: four bars, a lift slider, and a drummer with no click

The third rough, made on Loudon's ask when he came back to finish the sit five days on. Four bars, a slider for how much the drummer lifts, and dots that light as they sound. Clean time on purpose: no swing, no stumbles, so that only the lift is left to hear. Two modes. In *Lean*, a fixed click plays and the drummer leans ahead of it (or behind) through bars 3 and 4 and lands back on the clock. In *Tempo*, added on his next ask, there is no click: the pulse itself speeds up (or slows) and eases back, so the loop comes round early. He then heard by ear that the push gave up at the turnaround, which the code and the pictures had not shown, and it was fixed so the push builds into an early downbeat.

It follows from his reframe in the sit: we hear change in timing better than absolute distance, as relative pitch is easier than absolute pitch. Three things said from memory along the way are leads, not facts: negative mean asynchrony, the fielder cancelling a ball's optical acceleration, and a timing just-noticeable difference of about 10 to 20 ms. Only Loudon has heard it.

### 2026-11-03 — Mistake or Feel, Phrased: a push into the cadence, and the bar groove that flattens it

The second rough, made in the sit. Eight bars, with a phrase-level push into bars 7 and 8. Five blind versions differ in timing only: as played, 50% grid, 100% grid, bar groove (the median of each slot across the bars), and bar groove plus phrase (the leftover deviations smoothed over about a bar). It is built on Loudon's correction to the first rough: feel speaks on many levels at once, and each measure sets up the next. Snapping to the bar groove keeps what repeats and throws away the push, so it flattens the phrase. Played on 2026-11-08, with the clips shortened to bars 5 to 8 on his "so much waiting!". Signs from one listener, not findings: he kept the bar groove plus phrase version blind; the 50% grid was the only one he heard lift; 100% was stiff; as played was too loose.

### 2026-11-03 — Mistake or Feel?: the page's bridge question, put to the ear

The morning's first rough. A four-bar synthetic take carries a feel that repeats every bar and mistakes that don't. A practice round, then six blind versions (0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% toward the grid, and the take snapped to its own groove), and an overlay explorer to see where each note moved. Loudon played it. He heard 100% as stiff and named it as the grid; 0% and 25% were too loose; 50%, 75% and the take's own groove all grooved. He kept 75%, and said that "keep" means nothing without the song's context, intent and timbre.

That was the finding. The rough held timbre fixed so it could isolate time, and that separation is itself the error: a mistake in prosody. Mechanical sounds suit mechanical time, so "too stiff" is not wrong for them. The page's "50% sounds more musical" partly held. Snapping to the take's own groove tied with the best; it did not win. The outside read the morning started from sits beside it as a workshop note.
