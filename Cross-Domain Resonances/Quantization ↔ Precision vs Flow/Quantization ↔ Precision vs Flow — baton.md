## Why this move matters
The vector moved twice in one day, and Loudon joined the two ("Join them"); the body did not move. The page used to argue that precision and flow are opposing forces, the same question at every scale. The new vector says something sharper: a deviation from the grid can carry intent, a lean chosen by someone who knows the standard well enough to step just outside it, and that intent speaks at every span at once (beat, bar, phrase, form) and in the voice of the material it moves. The morning's first rough found this by ear. Loudon heard 100% as the grid and kept 75%, then said "keep" means nothing without the song's context, intent and timbre. Holding timbre fixed to isolate time turned out to be the error itself. A reader who meets the page today gets the old thesis under the new vector. **The 2026-11-08 reframe, Loudon's:** we hear change in timing better than absolute distance. The parallel is relative versus absolute pitch: most ears track the interval, few hold the note. A quantize grid measures distance from a line, which is what we hear worst.

**Three things were said from memory in the sit and are leads, not facts; verify before the page leans on any:** negative mean asynchrony (players tending to sit slightly ahead of the beat, found in tapping work); the fielder's habit of cancelling a ball's optical acceleration to catch it, as a picture of tracking change rather than position; a timing just-noticeable difference of roughly 10 to 20 ms.

## Calibrations from this session
- Loudon's correction to the first rough: feel speaks on many levels at once, and each measure sets up the next. Snapping to the bar groove keeps what repeats and flattens the phrase. Rough two was built on that.
- The rough held timbre fixed to isolate time. That separation is itself the error, a mistake in prosody. Mechanical sounds suit mechanical time, so "too stiff" is not wrong for them. Do not build the next piece with one drum kit.
- The page's "50% sounds more musical" partly held: 50%, 75% and the take's own groove all grooved. Snapping to the take's own groove tied; it did not win. Do not write it up as the answer.
- The rough's model of the gesture can be wrong at its most important moment, and only the ear catches it: rough 3's no-click push gave up at the turnaround until Loudon heard it. Do not report a rough as working before someone plays it.
- A clip should be as short as the thing it tests. If a bar is scheduled ahead, Stop must cut a bus.
- Write HTML with Bash heredocs or `cp`, never the Write tool: Write opens the file in the browser pane ([[The Daily Page — tuning]] items 19 and 27).
