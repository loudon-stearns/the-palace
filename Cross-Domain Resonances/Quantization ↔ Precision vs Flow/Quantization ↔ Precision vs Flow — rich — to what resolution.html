To what resolution?

The section ends on a question: not whether to quantize, but to what resolution, and it says the answer depends on what you are trying to keep. Five steps on one note, a 330 Hz sine that fades from loud to 60 dB down over 3.5 s. The last step asks you two questions about it and compares your answers.

the attack, close up · 3 cycles
the tail at 3.0 s, close up · zoomed to its size
the whole note · its level in dB below full scale · dashed = one step of the grid
levels = 2N, Δ = 2 ⁄ 2N
(how many values a sample can take) = 2 to the power of (the number of bits); (one step of the grid) = (the full range, −1 to +1) ÷ (the number of levels). 24 bits is 16,777,216 levels; 1 bit is 2.
q = Δ · ( ⌊ x ⁄ Δ ⌋ + ½ )
(the value kept) = the middle of the step the true value x falls in. The error, q − x, is never bigger than half a step, and it is what you hear in step 3.
a fade stops fading when A(t) < Δ
once the note's own size A is smaller than one step, every sample rounds to plus or minus half a step: the note becomes a buzz of fixed size, however quiet it was meant to be. For this note that happens at t = τ · ln(0.8 ⁄ Δ), with τ = 3.5 s ⁄ ln 1000 = 0.507 s.
The grid here is symmetric, with no level at zero (mid-rise), so that 1 bit is exactly two levels, up and down; common PCM puts a level at zero, and its tail drops to silence where this one buzzes. Dither is triangular, two steps wide, added before rounding. Checked offline on this page's own engine (the script marked engine): the level counts, the time each bit depth's fade stops, that 12 bits is the fewest that carries this fade to −60 dB undithered, and that at 8 bits with dither the 330 Hz component in the tail still falls with the true fade, while without dither it is lost (held as a buzz on this grid).
