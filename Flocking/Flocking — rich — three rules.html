Take one rule away

The section says each of the three rules holds the flock against one way of failing: without separation it collapses, without alignment it mills, without cohesion it scatters. Here the rules come one at a time, then summed, and then you take one away and see how the flock fails. Every bird runs the bundle's one shared model, the same code the three lenses on this page run, to the last digit.

Step 1 of 5 · one rule alone

Separation: steer away from whoever is too close

Twelve birds and only the first rule. Each bird looks at everyone inside its reach, the dashed ring around the amber bird, and steers away from them, the nearest hardest. Drop them in a heap and watch them push apart until nobody is inside anyone's ring. Then they stop pushing and just fly on.

Step 2 of 5 · one rule alone

Alignment: steer toward the way your neighbours fly

Now only the second rule. Each bird turns, a small nudge a frame, toward the average heading of the birds in its reach. On the right every heading is a dot on a circle, and the amber arrow is their average. Scattered dots mean everyone flies their own way; one clump means one direction. Nobody tells the birds where to go, and still they agree.

TryDeal a few times. Birds that start out of each other's reach can't agree, so the dots gather into clumps first and one clump only if the reach chains them together.

Step 3 of 5 · one rule alone

Cohesion: steer toward the middle of your neighbours

Only the third rule. Each bird steers toward the average position of the birds in its reach, the small cross. They pull together, overshoot, swing back, and pile up. With nothing pushing them apart, the piles keep shrinking: half a minute in, neighbours are a few pixels apart.

Step 4 of 5 · the three combined

Each bird adds its three wants, and a flock appears that no bird holds

Eighty birds, the bundle's model as the three lenses run it: seed 7, a field that wraps at its edges, one reach of 50 px for all three rules, and weights 1.5, 1.0 and 1.0. Every frame each bird turns each want into a small steering force, weights them, adds them, and flies. The amber lines are each bird's summed force. Keep an eye on the count of birds with nobody in reach: you'll need it in the next step.
