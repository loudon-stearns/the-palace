Which part is the crystal?

The Crystal Synthesizer's own engine, ported from its Python: a cubic crystal's phonon spectrum, rendered as N partials, each decaying faster the higher it sits. We start with one partial and end on the section's question, measured: is the beating the crystal or the rendering, and is the sweep? Silent until you press a button.

One struck partial

One partial of the crystal, struck: a sine that starts loud and dies away. How fast it dies depends on how high it sits. That a high mode dies sooner than a low one is the physics, phonons shaking each other apart. How much sooner is a number the code chose, the exponent 0.45. Drag the partial up and strike it beside the lowest one.

Two neighbours

Beating needs a neighbour. Two partials struck together near 1 kHz: when they sit close, their sum swells and fades at the gap between them, in hertz. Pull them apart and the beats speed up into a roughness, then stop being beats at all and become two notes. The two marks on the slider are the gap between neighbouring partials when the crystal is rendered with 8 and with 300 of them.

The whole crystal at N

Now the whole cubic crystal. The code sorts its 64,000 modes into N bins spaced evenly in pitch and sounds one partial per bin that holds any. N is Decision Point 2, entirely ours. Few bins put the neighbours far apart, as in step 2's right end; many put them close. Below, each third-octave band's level over time: watch where it stays smooth and where it ripples.

r = (ftop / ffloor)1/N,   beats near f ≈ f · (r − 1)
(the ratio between neighbouring partials) = (the top partial over the lowest) to the power one over (the number of bins); and near any frequency the neighbours beat at about (that frequency) × (the ratio, minus one).

Step 2's pair, many times over. At the code's lowest-mode anchor, N = 8 puts every neighbour more than half an octave away.

Ripple is the spread of each band's level about its own slow decay, in dB, averaged over the 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz bands. Regular is how well that ripple repeats itself (its autocorrelation peak, 0 to 1); it is shown only where there is ripple to repeat.

Which part is the crystal?

The section says the beating is a rendering artifact, wrong in detail, and that the descending sweep of pitch character over the sustain is true, since high modes do decay first. So pull the two apart. Five renders of one crystal, two switches: the bins (8 or 300) and the decay exponent (0.45, or 0 for no decay law at all), and last the code's continuous path, band-limited noise per bin. For each, the beating (ripple, and how regular) and the sweep (the spectral centroid, the sound's centre of brightness, at three moments).
