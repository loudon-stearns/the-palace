Four choices, one crystal

The Crystal Synthesizer's four Decision Points, in the order the page's Forward Vectors ask a lesson to take them: the most audible first, the most conceptual last. Each step adds one choice and keeps the ones before. The engine is the instrument's own, ported from its Python. Silent until you press a button.

Resolution

A cubic crystal's phonon spectrum, sorted into N bins, one partial per bin that holds any modes. The physics has no opinion on N; a real crystal has some 10²² modes. Strike it at a few settings: few bins give a bell of separate tones, many give a dense shimmer.

Anchor

The physics gives ratios; somebody has to say where they land. Three answers, three claims. Lowest mode puts each crystal's softest phonon on 110 Hz and lets the rest spread up. Zone boundary puts each crystal's hardest phonon on 10 kHz, so they share a ceiling and their floors differ. Centroid puts the middle of each crystal's spectrum on 110 Hz, and the code then drops whatever falls below 88 Hz. Three crystals, so the claim can be heard as a comparison.

faudio = ω · (fanchor / ωanchor)
(each mode's pitch) = (its frequency in the crystal) × (the pitch we chose) ÷ (the frequency of the mode we chose to put there).

N stays where step 1 left it. Lowest mode is what step 1 used.

Decay

Phonons shake each other apart, the high ones faster: that direction is the physics. The rate is the exponent, and the exponent is ours. At 0 every partial rings as long as every other, a drone; at 2 the top is gone almost at once. The grey line is exponent 1, which is what the crystal-rings build gets when it gives every mode one Q from a measured Raman line width.

T60(f) = ln 1000 / (d₀ · (f / fa)p)
(how long a partial takes to fall 60 dB) = (the natural log of a thousand) ÷ ((the base rate) × (its frequency over the anchor) to the power (the exponent)).

A fifth?

The page counts four stretch points and asks whether that is the whole list. Hold all four where you left them and turn one thing none of them names: how many cells across the code computes its crystal. The engine works on a grid of wavevectors, 40 to a side for the cubic crystal. A real crystal a centimetre across is some ten million cells wide.
